FY1 · Physics as a natural science

Physics and the scientific method

Physics
An experimental natural science that studies the basic phenomena and structures of nature and the laws that describe them.
Empirical science
Knowledge is based on observations, measurements and experiments.
Scientific method
A way of building reliable knowledge that combines observations, assumptions, experiments and models.
Hypothesis
A justified assumption that can be tested by experiment.
Physical theory
A broad explanatory framework that links observations and makes it possible to predict new phenomena.
Model
A simplified description of reality used to explain phenomena or make predictions.
Range of validity
The conditions and limits within which a model can be used reliably.

Measurement and errors

Quantity
A measurable property, such as length, mass or time.
Unit
An agreed reference value in which a measured property is expressed.
Measurement result
The value of a quantity, made up of a number and a unit.
Measurement error
The difference between a measured value and the true value of the quantity.
Random error
A varying deviation whose effect can be reduced by repeating the measurement and taking the mean.
Systematic error
A deviation always in the same direction, caused for example by a faulty instrument or method.
Gross error
An obvious deviation caused for example by a misreading, a wrong entry or misuse of an instrument.
Significant figures
The digits of a number that show the precision available in a measurement result.
Resolution of an instrument
How small a change a measuring instrument can reliably detect.

The SI system and orders of magnitude

SI system
The internationally agreed system of units, in which all other units are formed from seven base units.
Base quantity
One of the seven measurable properties chosen as a basis, such as length, mass or time.
Base unit
The unit agreed for a base quantity, such as the metre, the kilogram or the second.
Derived unit
A combination of base units, such as m/s for velocity or N for force.
Multiple of a unit
A practical unit for very large or small values, formed with a prefix.
Prefix
A symbol in front of a unit that gives a power of ten, such as kilo $10^{3}$ or milli $10^{-3}$.
Power of ten
The form $10^{n}$ makes very large and very small numbers easier to write and compare.
Order of magnitude
The size of an object described with a suitable power of ten.

The basic structures of the universe

Elementary particle
One of the smallest known building blocks of matter, such as quarks and leptons.
Quark
A building block of protons and neutrons; ordinary matter contains mainly up and down quarks.
Lepton
A group of elementary particles to which the electron belongs.
Proton
A positively charged particle of the atomic nucleus, made of quarks.
Neutron
An electrically neutral particle of the atomic nucleus, made of quarks.
Atomic nucleus
The dense centre of an atom where the protons and neutrons are.
Atom
A building block of matter made up of a nucleus and the electrons around it.
Molecule
A bound group of two or more atoms.
Planetary system
A star and the planets, small bodies and other matter that orbit it.
Galaxy
A huge system of stars, gas, dust and other matter held together by gravity.
Cluster of galaxies
A large structure of many galaxies held together by gravity.

Space and the evolution of the universe

Big Bang
The model in which time and space evolved from a very hot and dense initial state.
Expansion of the universe
The average distances between galaxies grow with time.
Redshift
The spectral lines of a distant object are shifted to longer wavelengths, which shows that it is receding.
Exoplanet
A planet that orbits another star outside the Solar System.
Red giant
A stage in which a star expands as its hydrogen runs low and heavier nuclei begin to fuse.
White dwarf
The dense, hot core left by a medium-sized star, which slowly cools.
Neutron star
An extremely dense remnant of a collapsed massive star, made mostly of neutrons.
Black hole
An extremely dense concentration of mass whose gravity is so strong that not even light can escape.

Modelling and graphs

Physical model
A simplification based on measurements that describes a natural phenomenon or structure.
Graphical model
The relationship between quantities shown as a graph in a coordinate system.
Mathematical model
The relationship between quantities expressed as an equation.
Quantitative model
A description based on numerical values that allows numerical predictions.
Linear model
A straight line describing a steady change; its slope stays constant.
Line of best fit
A straight line fitted to the measured points that shows the general relationship in the data.
Slope
The change in the quantity on the vertical axis divided by the change in the quantity on the horizontal axis.
Physical slope
The steepness of a graph read as a quantity with its unit and its meaning in the phenomenon.
Interpolation
Using a model to estimate a value between the measured points.
Extrapolation
Extending a model beyond the measured range; the prediction is usually less certain.

Density and motion

Density
The mass of a substance per unit volume.$$\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}$$
Direct proportionality
When one quantity doubles, so does the other, and the graph passes through the origin.
Position
Where an object is in a chosen coordinate system at a given instant.
Uniform motion
The position changes by equal amounts in equal time intervals.
Velocity
The change in position per unit time, together with the direction of motion.
Average velocity
The whole displacement divided by the time it took.
Acceleration
The change in velocity per unit time.$$a = \dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$$
Free fall
Motion in which air resistance is neglected and only the weight acts on the object.
Acceleration of free fall
Near the Earth the speed changes by about $9.81\,\text{m/s}$ every second.$$g \approx 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$$

Experimental investigation

Research question
A precise question that an investigation aims to answer.
Experimental plan
A description, written in advance, of the measurements, the equipment and how the investigation is carried out.
Series of measurements
A set of measurement results collected systematically from the same phenomenon.
Repeatability
A well-described experiment can be done again with similar results.
Data
The values, observations, pictures or videos collected in an experiment for analysis.
Analysis of results
Processing the data with calculations and graphs to find relationships.
Conclusion
A justified answer to the research question drawn from the results.
Source of error
A factor that can cause a deviation or uncertainty in a measurement result.
Reliability
An assessment of how well the method and the results support the conclusion.
Report
A written account of the research question, method, results, conclusions and evaluation.

Finding information

Finding information
Searching for reliable information, for example in research, textbooks, databases and expert sources.
Source of information
The background and purpose of whoever publishes information affect how it should be assessed.
Source criticism
Assessing the reliability of a source by its author, reasoning, currency and other sources.
Material
A text, picture, table, graph or other source of information from which conclusions are drawn.
Objectivity
The aim of describing observations independently of the researcher's personal opinions.

FY2 · Physics, the environment and society

Energy in the environment and society

Energy source
A substance or phenomenon used to produce useful energy.
Renewable energy source
A natural resource or phenomenon that is replenished fast enough for human use.
Non-renewable energy source
A natural resource of limited amount that does not form again quickly.
Carbon-neutral energy source
Its use does not increase the concentration of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere in the long term.
Radiant energy
The form of energy that travels from the Sun to the Earth and drives most of the Earth's energy flows.
Infrared radiation
Electromagnetic radiation of longer wavelength than visible light, emitted by the warm ground.
Absorption
Radiation is taken up by matter and the energy it carries passes to the matter.
Reflection
Radiation changes direction at a surface and its energy is not absorbed there.

Climate change

Greenhouse effect
Gases in the atmosphere reduce how much of the Earth's thermal radiation escapes directly to space.
Greenhouse gas
A gas in the atmosphere that absorbs infrared radiation emitted by the Earth.
Carbon dioxide
A greenhouse gas whose concentration grows with the use of fossil fuels.
Methane
A strong greenhouse gas released for example by agriculture and the production of fossil fuels.
Climate change
A long-term change in the Earth's climate; human greenhouse gas emissions intensify the present warming.
Carbon sink
A system that takes up more carbon dioxide from the atmosphere than it releases.
Feedback
A change triggers an effect that can strengthen or weaken the original change.
Emission reduction
Releasing less greenhouse gas into the atmosphere, for example by changing energy production and use.

Energy and forms of energy

Energy
A central quantity of physics that is conserved in every phenomenon but can be transferred and transformed.
Joule
The SI unit of energy.
Form of energy
A way of describing in what form energy appears or is stored.
Kinetic energy
An object has it because it moves; it grows with the mass and the speed.
Potential energy
Stored energy of an interaction; its amount depends on the positions of the objects.
Mechanical energy
Kinetic energy and potential energy together.
Chemical energy
Energy stored in the interactions of atoms and molecules, released for example in burning.
Nuclear energy
Energy of the interactions within atomic nuclei, released in nuclear reactions.
Electrical energy
Energy of electric interactions, easy to transfer in the electricity grid.
Internal energy
Energy of the motion and interactions of the particles of matter.

Conservation of energy

Law of conservation of energy
Energy is neither created nor destroyed; it can be transferred or transformed from one form to another.
Energy transformation
The amount of one form of energy decreases while that of another increases.
Energy transfer
Energy passes from one object or system to another, and the total is conserved.
Energy diagram
A diagram that shows the forms of energy and how they transform and are conserved.
Heat
Energy transferred because of a temperature difference from a hotter object to a colder one.
Wasted energy
Energy that is transformed into a form useless for the device's purpose.
Useful energy
The part of the energy taken in by a device that serves the intended purpose.

Power and energy consumption

Power
How fast energy is transferred or transformed.$$P = \dfrac{\Delta E}{\Delta t}$$
Watt
The SI unit of power, one joule per second.
Input power
How fast a device takes energy from its energy source.
Output power
How fast a device transfers energy to its intended purpose.
Energy consumption
The energy used in a given time can be calculated from the power.$$E = P\Delta t$$
Kilowatt-hour
The energy transferred at a power of one kilowatt in one hour.
Rating plate
A label on a device that shows for example the power it takes from the grid.
Price of electricity
The cost of the electrical energy used is the number of kilowatt-hours times the price per kilowatt-hour.

Efficiency and heating value

Efficiency
The fraction of the energy taken in that is put to use.$$\eta = \dfrac{E_{\text{a}}}{E_{\text{o}}}$$
Efficiency from power
The ratio of output power to input power.$$\eta = \dfrac{P_{\text{a}}}{P_{\text{o}}}$$
Input energy
The total energy a device takes in to operate.
Output energy
The energy a device transfers or transforms for its intended purpose.
Transmission loss
In transmission part of the energy is transformed into an unused form, usually heat.
Heating value
The energy released per unit mass of a fuel when it burns completely.
Energy of combustion
The energy released by a fuel can be calculated from its mass and heating value.$$E = Hm$$
Energy efficiency
The desired benefit is achieved with as little energy and as few losses as possible.

Energy production

Energy production
Turning energy found in nature into a form people can use, such as electricity or heat.
Power plant
A plant where the energy of an energy source is turned into electricity, heat or both.
Generator
A device that turns the energy of rotation into electrical energy.
Turbine
Flowing water, air or steam turns this device, which drives a generator.
Thermal power plant
Thermal energy produces steam, which turns a turbine and a generator.
Condenser
Cools the steam from the turbine back into water and at the same time transfers heat to the surroundings.
Combined heat and power
The same plant produces both electricity and useful heat, which improves the efficiency.
District heating
Centrally produced thermal energy carried by hot water to heat buildings.
Condensing power
Electricity production in which the heat left after the turbine is usually not used for district heating.

Ways of producing energy

Solar power
The Sun's radiant energy is turned directly into electrical energy, for example in a solar panel.
Wind power
The kinetic energy of the air turns a turbine and a generator, which produces electricity.
Hydropower
The potential and kinetic energy of water is turned into electricity with a turbine and a generator.
Bioenergy
Chemical energy bound in organic matter, used for example by burning it.
Fossil fuels
Coal, oil and natural gas are non-renewable fuels whose use increases carbon dioxide emissions.
Nuclear power
Energy released in nuclear reactions boils water, and the steam is used to produce electricity.
Ground-source heat
Thermal energy stored in the soil or bedrock is transferred to heat buildings.
Geothermal energy
Energy from the Earth's internal heat used to produce heat or electricity.

Environmental impacts

Life cycle
The impacts of a way of producing energy are examined from construction and use to dismantling the plant.
Carbon dioxide emission
The climate impact of energy production can be compared by the amount of carbon dioxide released.
Land use
Different ways of production need different areas for plants, fuels and transmission lines.
Natural resources
Energy production uses materials and fuels whose availability and renewal vary.
Impact on ecosystems
Energy production can change habitats, waters and biodiversity.
Health impact
Emissions, accident risks and other factors of production can affect people's health.
Order of magnitude
Ways of production can be compared by the scale of their power, emissions, costs or area.

A flexible energy system

Energy system
The whole made up of the production, transmission, storage and consumption of energy.
Distributed generation
Energy is produced at many small sites close to consumers as well as in large central plants.
Smart grid
A digital grid controls production and consumption and passes information both ways.
Balancing power
Electricity production whose output can be changed quickly to balance production and consumption.
Energy storage
Energy is turned into a form that can be kept and released later when needed.
Thermal storage
Thermal energy is kept for later use, for example in water, soil or the structures of buildings.
Pumped-storage plant
Surplus electricity pumps water uphill, and the energy is stored as potential energy of the water.
Flywheel
Kinetic energy is stored in a rotating body and can later be turned back into electricity.
Microgrid
A local energy grid that links nearby producers, storage and consumers.

A sustainable energy economy

Sustainable energy economy
The ecological, economic and social impacts are considered in the production and use of energy.
Ecological sustainability
Natural resources are used so that the environment's capacity to renew and to carry the load is not put at risk.
Economic sustainability
Energy solutions must be workable in the long run in cost and in the use of resources.
Social sustainability
Energy solutions take into account people's well-being, safety and fair access to energy.
Saving energy
Reducing needless consumption by changing habits and using energy only as needed.
Waste heat
Heat that would otherwise go unused can be recovered and used elsewhere.
Storing energy
Keeping energy for later use evens out the difference in time between production and consumption.
Sustainable consumption
Energy is used efficiently for real needs and its environmental impacts are reduced.

FY3 · Energy and heat

Temperature and the thermodynamic system

Temperature
Describes how vigorous the thermal motion of the particles of matter is.
Kelvin scale
The absolute temperature scale, whose zero $0\,\text{K}$ is the lowest possible temperature.
Absolute zero
The lowest possible temperature, $0\,\text{K}$ or $-273.15\,{}^{\circ}\text{C}$.
Thermal motion
The constant random motion and vibration of the particles of matter.
State variable
A quantity that describes the state of a system, such as temperature, pressure or volume.
Thermodynamic system
The matter or region marked off for study, whose energy changes are examined.
Open system
Both matter and energy can be exchanged with the surroundings.
Closed system
Energy but not matter can be exchanged with the surroundings.
Isolated system
Neither matter nor energy is exchanged with the surroundings.
Thermal equilibrium
The temperatures of systems in contact have become equal.

Thermal expansion and heat transfer

Thermal expansion
Most substances grow when they warm up and shrink when they cool.
Linear thermal expansion
The change in an object's length depends on its original length, the change in temperature and the material.$$\Delta L = \alpha L_0 \Delta T$$
Volume thermal expansion
The change in an object's volume depends on its original volume, the change in temperature and the material.$$\Delta V = \gamma V_0 \Delta T$$
Heat
Energy transferred because of a temperature difference from a hotter system to a colder one.
Conduction
Energy is transferred through the interactions of the particles of matter without any bulk motion of the matter.
Convection
Energy is carried along with a moving liquid or gas.
Thermal radiation
Energy is transferred as electromagnetic radiation and needs no medium.
Thermal conductor
A material in which energy is transferred efficiently by conduction.
Thermal insulator
A material in which energy is transferred slowly by conduction.
Energy balance
The energy of a system stays constant when as much energy enters it as leaves it.

Force and pressure

Force
Describes the strength of the interaction between two objects.
Weight
The strength of the gravitational interaction between the Earth and an object.$$G = mg$$
Pressure
The force perpendicular to a surface per unit area.$$p = \dfrac{F}{A}$$
Pascal
The SI unit of pressure, one newton per square metre.
Air pressure
The pressure due to the weight of the atmosphere and the collisions of gas molecules.
Standard pressure
An agreed reference pressure of about $101\,325\,\text{Pa}$.
Hydrostatic pressure
The pressure due to the weight of the liquid itself grows with depth.$$p_{\text{h}} = \rho g h$$
Total pressure
The pressure in a liquid is the sum of the pressure at the surface and the hydrostatic pressure.$$p = p_0 + \rho g h$$
Pressure difference
Different pressures in two regions can exert a force on an object.

The state of a gas and its changes

Gas pressure
The collisions of the particles with the container walls cause a pressure observed on the large scale.
Amount of substance
Describes the number of particles of a substance; its unit is the mole.
Mole
The amount of substance that contains about $6.02 \cdot 10^{23}$ particles.
Ideal gas
A simplified model of a gas that neglects the volume of the particles and their interactions.
Ideal gas law
Links the pressure, volume, amount of substance and temperature of a gas.$$pV = nRT$$
Isochoric change
The volume stays constant, so the pressure is directly proportional to the absolute temperature.
Isobaric change
The pressure stays constant, so the volume is directly proportional to the absolute temperature.
Isothermal change
The temperature stays constant, so the pressure and the volume are inversely proportional.$$pV = \text{constant}$$
Boyle's law
At constant temperature the pressure of a gas rises as its volume decreases.$$p_1 V_1 = p_2 V_2$$
Combined gas law
The states of a fixed amount of gas can be compared with an equation in which pressure times volume is divided by temperature.$$\dfrac{p_1 V_1}{T_1} = \dfrac{p_2 V_2}{T_2}$$

Warming and cooling

Heat capacity
The energy needed to raise the temperature of an object by one degree.
Specific heat capacity
The energy needed to raise the temperature of one kilogram by one degree.
Quantity of heat
The energy transferred in a change of temperature can be calculated from the mass and the specific heat capacity.$$Q = cm\Delta T$$
Specific heat capacity of water
Its large value means that much energy is needed to change the temperature of water.
Mixing experiment
In an isolated system the energy received by the parts that warm up equals the energy given up by the parts that cool.
Heating power
How fast energy is transferred to the object being heated.$$P = \dfrac{Q}{\Delta t}$$
Cooling
The temperature of a system falls when energy passes from it to the surroundings.
Warming
The temperature of a system rises when its internal energy grows without a change of state.

Changes of state

Solid
The particles are bound in place and vibrate about their equilibrium positions.
Liquid
The particles are close together but can move relative to each other.
Gas
The particles move almost freely and fill the space available.
Melting
A solid turns into a liquid and takes in energy.
Freezing
A liquid turns into a solid and gives up energy to the surroundings.
Vaporisation
A liquid turns into a gas and takes in energy.
Condensation
A gas turns into a liquid and gives up energy to the surroundings.
Sublimation
A solid turns directly into a gas without a liquid stage.
Deposition
A gas turns directly into a solid without a liquid stage.
Specific latent heat of fusion
The energy needed to melt one kilogram at the melting point.$$Q = sm$$
Specific latent heat of vaporisation
The energy needed to vaporise one kilogram at the boiling point.$$Q = rm$$

Phase diagram and humidity

Phase diagram
Shows the state of a substance as a function of temperature and pressure.
Triple point
The temperature and pressure at which solid, liquid and gas can be in equilibrium at the same time.
Critical point
Above this temperature a gas cannot be liquefied by raising the pressure alone.
Boiling
Vaporisation throughout the liquid, when the vapour pressure reaches the pressure of the surroundings.
Evaporation
Vaporisation from the surface of a liquid, which can happen at any temperature.
Saturated vapour
The gas holds the largest possible amount of the substance's vapour at that temperature.
Relative humidity
The amount of water vapour compared with the largest possible amount at that temperature.
Dew point
The temperature to which air must be cooled for water vapour to start condensing.
Boiling point
The temperature depends on the pressure of the surroundings and rises as the pressure rises.

Mechanical work and internal energy

Mechanical work
The energy transferred by a force is the product of the force and the distance moved in its direction.$$W = Fs$$
Internal energy
The energy of a system made up of the kinetic and interaction energies of its particles.
First law of thermodynamics
Internal energy changes through the heat transferred and the work done on the system.$$\Delta U = Q + W$$
Joule's experiment
Mechanical work turning into internal energy is seen as a rise in the temperature of water.
Work done by a gas
An expanding gas does work on the surroundings as its volume grows.
Work at constant pressure
At constant pressure the work done by a gas depends on the pressure and the change in volume.$$W = p\Delta V$$
Adiabatic change
No heat is transferred between the system and the surroundings, so work changes the internal energy.
Isothermal process
The temperature and the internal energy of an ideal gas stay constant during the process.
Conservation of energy
Energy is neither created nor destroyed; it is transferred and transformed into different forms.

Heat engines

Heat engine
Turns part of the energy received from a hot system into mechanical work.
Hot reservoir
The system at higher temperature from which the engine receives energy as heat.
Cold reservoir
The system at lower temperature to which the engine gives up part of the energy it received.
Waste heat
Energy passed to the colder surroundings that cannot be turned into useful work.
Efficiency
The fraction of the energy received that is turned into useful work.
Cycle
A system goes through changes of state and finally returns to its original state.
Refrigerator
Uses work to move energy from a colder space to warmer surroundings.
Heat pump
Uses external work to move energy from cooler surroundings to the space being heated.
Compressor
Compresses the refrigerant, so its pressure and usually its temperature rise.

Climate and heat

Radiative equilibrium
The Earth's temperature stays steady when the power received equals the power radiated to space.
Greenhouse effect
The atmosphere absorbs part of the Earth's thermal radiation and raises the mean surface temperature.
Greenhouse gas
A gas in the atmosphere that absorbs and emits infrared radiation.
Carbon dioxide
A long-lived greenhouse gas whose concentration grows with the use of fossil fuels.
Albedo
The fraction of the radiation arriving at a surface that is reflected back.
Ice–albedo feedback
Less ice means less reflection and more of the Sun's energy absorbed.
Ocean warming
The large specific heat capacity of water lets the oceans store a large amount of energy.
Sea-level rise
Caused mainly by melting land ice and the thermal expansion of sea water.
Melting of sea ice
Floating ice melting does not in itself raise the sea level significantly.
Climate change
The long-term warming of the Earth changes the energy balance, glaciers, oceans and other parts of the climate system.

FY4 · Force and motion

Quantities of motion

Position
Where an object is in a chosen coordinate system at a given instant.
Distance travelled
The total length of the path an object has travelled.
Displacement
The change in an object's position, with its direction.$$\Delta x = x_2 - x_1$$
Velocity
The rate of change of position, with the direction of motion.
Speed
The magnitude of the velocity, without its direction.
Average velocity
The whole displacement divided by the time it took.$$v_{\text{avg}} = \dfrac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$$
Instantaneous velocity
The velocity of an object at a given instant.
Uniform motion
The velocity stays constant, so the position changes by equal amounts in equal time intervals.

Graphs of motion

Position–time graph
Shows an object's position as a function of time.
Tangent to a graph
A straight line that touches the curve at the point studied and gives the instantaneous rate of change.
Slope of the tangent
On a position–time graph it gives the object's instantaneous velocity.
Velocity–time graph
Shows an object's velocity as a function of time.
Area under a graph
The signed area between the velocity–time graph and the time axis gives the displacement.
Acceleration–time graph
Shows an object's acceleration as a function of time.
Change in velocity
The signed area under the acceleration–time graph gives the change in velocity.
Graphical differentiation
A new graph of the rate of change is made from the slopes of a graph.

Accelerated motion

Acceleration
The rate of change of velocity.$$a = \dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$$
Average acceleration
The total change in velocity divided by the time it took.
Instantaneous acceleration
The rate of change of velocity at a given instant.
Constant acceleration
The acceleration stays the same, so the velocity changes steadily.
Velocity in uniformly accelerated motion
The velocity changes steadily with time.$$v = v_0 + at$$
Position in uniformly accelerated motion
The position follows from the initial position, the initial velocity and the acceleration.$$x = x_0 + v_0 t + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2$$
Acceleration of free fall
The magnitude of the acceleration of a freely falling object near the Earth.$$g \approx 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$$
Free fall
Motion in which only the weight acts on the object and air resistance is neglected.

Interaction and Newton's laws

Interaction
Something between two objects in which they can change each other's motion or shape.
Force
Describes the strength of an interaction; its unit is the newton.
Newton's first law
An object keeps its state of motion if the net force on it is zero; the law of inertia.
Inertia
The property of an object to resist a change in its state of motion.
Newton's second law
A net force gives an object an acceleration; the fundamental law of dynamics.$$\Sigma\overline{F} = m\overline{a}$$
Net force
The vector sum of the forces acting on an object.
Newton's third law
In an interaction the objects exert equal but opposite forces on each other.
Action and reaction
Two equal and opposite forces acting on different objects in the same interaction.

Forces and the equation of motion

Free-body diagram
A drawing that shows every force acting on the object studied as an arrow.
Weight
The gravitational force the Earth exerts on an object.$$G = mg$$
Normal force
The force a surface exerts on an object, perpendicular to the surface.
Tension
The force a taut string or rope exerts on an object, along the string.
Equation of motion
Newton's second law applied: the sum of the forces on an object is linked to its acceleration.
Component of a force
The part of a force in a chosen direction of the coordinate system.
Component form
The equation of motion is written separately, for example in the horizontal and vertical directions.
Condition of equilibrium
In translational equilibrium the vector sum of the forces on an object is zero.$$\Sigma\overline{F} = \overline{0}$$
Inclined plane
The situation becomes easier when the axes are chosen along the plane and the forces are split into components.

Friction, drag and buoyancy

Friction
A force between surfaces in contact that opposes their relative motion or the start of it.
Static friction
Prevents surfaces from sliding over each other and adjusts to the size needed, up to its maximum.
Kinetic friction
Opposes the motion of surfaces sliding over each other (sliding friction).$$F_{\mu} = \mu N$$
Coefficient of friction
A dimensionless number that depends on the pair of materials and describes the friction between the surfaces.
Drag of the medium
A gas or liquid exerts a force on a moving object that opposes its motion.
Air resistance
The drag force of the air on an object usually grows with the speed.
Terminal velocity
The constant velocity reached when the drag and the other forces along the motion cancel.
Buoyancy
The upward force a liquid or gas exerts on an object.

Work and mechanical energy

Mechanical work
A force transfers energy to or from an object during a displacement.$$W = Fs\cos\alpha$$
Kinetic energy
The energy of motion depends on the mass and the speed.$$E_{\text{k}} = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$$
Potential energy
The energy of a conservative interaction depends on the positions of the objects.
Gravitational potential energy
The energy related to gravity near the Earth.$$E_{\text{p}} = mgh$$
Conservative force
The work done by the force depends only on the start and end positions, not on the path.
Mechanical energy
The sum of kinetic energy and potential energy.$$E_{\text{mech}} = E_{\text{k}} + E_{\text{p}}$$
Conservation of energy
In an isolated system the total energy stays constant.
Conservation of mechanical energy
Mechanical energy is conserved when only conservative forces do work.
Work–energy principle of mechanics
The change in mechanical energy equals the work done by the non-conservative forces.$$\Delta E_{\text{mech}} = W_{\text{other}}$$
Work–kinetic energy principle
The work done by the net force on an object equals the change in its kinetic energy.$$W_{\text{total}} = \Delta E_{\text{k}}$$

Momentum, impulse and collisions

Momentum
The product of an object's mass and velocity, a vector quantity.$$\overline{p} = m\overline{v}$$
Impulse
Describes the total effect of a force over a time; for a constant force:$$\overline{I} = \overline{F}\Delta t$$
Impulse–momentum principle
The impulse an object receives equals the change in its momentum.$$\overline{I} = \Delta\overline{p}$$
Force–time graph
The area between the graph and the time axis gives the impulse the object receives.
Conservation of momentum
The total momentum of an isolated system does not change in an interaction.
Collision
A short interaction in which the velocities and momenta of the objects can change quickly.
Elastic collision
Besides the total momentum, the total kinetic energy of the objects is also conserved.
Inelastic collision
The total momentum is conserved, but part of the kinetic energy turns into other forms.
Perfectly inelastic collision
After the collision the objects move together at the same velocity.
One-dimensional collision
The objects move along one line, so the directions of momentum can be handled with signs.

FY5 · Periodic motion and waves

Moment and equilibrium

Statics
The part of mechanics that studies the equilibrium of objects at rest.
Equilibrium
A state in which neither the translational nor the rotational motion of an object changes.
Moment
A quantity that describes the turning effect of a force.$$M = Fr$$
Turning effect
The effect of a force that tends to rotate an object.
Axis of rotation
The line about which an object rotates or could rotate.
Line of action
The line through the point of application of a force, in the direction of the force.
Lever arm
The perpendicular, that is shortest, distance between the axis of rotation and the line of action of the force.
Net moment
The sum of the moments of the forces on an object about the chosen axis.
Equilibrium condition for translation
An object does not accelerate when the vector sum of the forces on it is zero.$$\Sigma\overline{F} = \overline{0}$$
Equilibrium condition for rotation
The rotation of an object does not change when the sum of the moments on it is zero.$$\Sigma M = 0$$
Centre of gravity
The point at which the whole weight of an object can be thought to act.
Base of support
The area bounded by the points where an object touches its support; the centre of gravity must be above it for the object not to topple.

Circular motion

Circular motion
Motion along a circular path.
Uniform circular motion
Motion on a circle in which the speed stays constant but the direction keeps changing.
Speed along the path
The speed of an object moving along a circular path.$$v = \dfrac{2\pi r}{T}$$
Centripetal acceleration
The acceleration towards the centre of the circle.$$a_{\text{n}} = \dfrac{v^2}{r}$$
Radius of the path
The distance from the centre of the path to the object moving on it.
Revolution
One full turn on a circular path.
Net force in circular motion
In uniform circular motion the sum of the forces points to the centre and causes the centripetal acceleration.$$\Sigma F = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$$

Gravitation and planetary motion

Gravitational interaction
The always attractive interaction between objects with mass.
Gravitational force
The force with which two objects with mass attract each other.
Law of universal gravitation
The gravitational force is proportional to the product of the masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance.$$F = \gamma\dfrac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}$$
Gravitational constant
A constant of nature that sets the strength of the gravitational interaction.$$\gamma \approx 6.67 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$$
Gravitational field
The region around an object with mass in which another object feels a gravitational force.
Gravitational field strength
The gravitational force on an object per unit mass.$$g = \dfrac{F}{m}$$
Planetary motion
The motion of a celestial body or satellite governed by the gravitational interaction.
Satellite
An object that orbits another object because of gravity.
Orbit
The path a satellite or celestial body follows around its central body.
Orbital period
The time for one full orbit around the central body.
Geostationary orbit
A circular orbit above the equator on which a satellite's period is one day, so it seems to stay still in the sky.

Oscillation

Periodic motion
Motion in which the same state of motion repeats at regular intervals.
Oscillation
Periodic back-and-forth motion about an equilibrium position.
Swinging motion
Periodic motion in which an object moves back and forth along a curved path.
Oscillator
An object or system that oscillates.
Equilibrium position
The position in which the net force on the oscillator is zero.
Displacement
The oscillator's distance from the equilibrium position, with its direction.
Amplitude
The largest displacement of the oscillator from the equilibrium position.
Period
The time for one full oscillation.$$T = \dfrac{1}{f}$$
Frequency
The number of full oscillations per unit time.$$f = \dfrac{1}{T}$$
Damping
The decrease of the amplitude as energy passes to the surroundings, for example through friction or drag.

Simple harmonic motion

Spring force
The restoring force produced by the deformation of a spring.
Hooke's law
The spring force is directly proportional to the extension or compression of the spring.$$F = -kx$$
Spring constant
A quantity that describes the stiffness of a spring; the larger $k$, the stiffer the spring.
Harmonic force
A restoring force directly proportional to the displacement and directed towards the equilibrium position.
Simple harmonic motion
Oscillation in which the displacement varies sinusoidally with time.
Harmonic oscillator
An oscillator whose period does not depend on the amplitude, because the restoring force is directly proportional to the displacement.
Mass–spring oscillator
An oscillating system made of a spring and an object attached to it.$$T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}}$$
Pendulum
A suspended object that swings to both sides of its equilibrium position under its weight.
Simple pendulum
A point-like object swinging on a massless string; for small angles:$$T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g}}$$
Natural frequency
The frequency at which a system oscillates freely after a disturbance.
Resonance
A strong growth of the amplitude when the frequency of an external drive matches the system's natural frequency.
Spring potential energy
The energy stored in a stretched or compressed spring.$$E_{\text{p}} = \tfrac{1}{2}kx^2$$

Basics of wave motion

Wave motion
An oscillation travelling from place to place so that energy is transferred but the matter does not travel with it.
Mechanical wave
An oscillation passing through a material medium from particle to particle.
Source of a wave
An oscillator that starts a wave in the surrounding medium.
Medium
A gas, liquid or solid in which a mechanical wave travels through the interactions of its particles.
Pulse
A single disturbance travelling through a medium.
Transverse wave
A wave in which the medium oscillates perpendicular to the direction of travel.
Longitudinal wave
A wave in which the medium oscillates along the direction of travel.
Wavelength
The distance between two neighbouring points in the same phase of oscillation.
Wave speed
The speed at which a disturbance travels through a medium; it depends on the properties of the medium.
Fundamental equation of wave motion
The link between the wave speed, the frequency and the wavelength.$$v = \lambda f$$

Wave phenomena

Reflection
A wave changes direction at a boundary so that it returns into the original medium.
Diffraction
A wave bends and spreads at the edge of an obstacle or a narrow opening.
Interference
The combined effect of two or more waves where they meet.
Constructive interference
Waves that meet in phase have displacements in the same direction, so the amplitude of the resultant wave grows.
Destructive interference
Waves that meet in opposite phase have opposite displacements, so the amplitude of the resultant wave shrinks.
Resultant wave
The wave formed by the combined effect of two or more waves that meet at the same place.
Spectrum
A presentation of which frequencies an oscillation or wave contains and how strong they are.
Beats
A periodic variation in loudness caused by the interference of two waves of nearly equal frequency.

Standing waves

Standing wave
A wave pattern formed by the interference of two identical waves travelling in opposite directions; its nodes stay in place.
Node
A point of a standing wave where the amplitude of oscillation is zero.
Antinode
A point of a standing wave where the amplitude of oscillation is largest.
Fundamental frequency
The lowest possible natural frequency of an oscillating system.
Overtone
A natural frequency of the system higher than the fundamental frequency.
Natural wavelength
A wavelength at which a standing wave that fits the system's boundary conditions can form.
Pipe closed at one end
A pipe with one end closed and one open; a node forms at the closed end and an antinode at the open end.
Pipe open at both ends
A pipe with both ends open, so an antinode of the standing wave forms at each end.

Sound

Sound
A mechanical wave that travels in a gas, a liquid or a solid but not in a vacuum.
Sound wave
A longitudinal wave of compressions and rarefactions travelling through a medium.
Pitch
The perception determined mainly by the frequency of the sound.
Loudness
The perception affected mainly by the sound intensity level and the frequency.
Timbre
The property that tells apart different sound sources playing at the same pitch.
Partial
A single oscillation component of a sound with a given frequency.
Doppler effect
The change in the observed frequency when the source and the observer move relative to each other.

Sound intensity and hearing

Sound intensity
The sound energy passing through unit area per unit time.$$I = \dfrac{P}{A}$$
Sound intensity level
A logarithmic quantity in decibels that compares the intensity of a sound with the threshold of hearing.$$L = 10\,\text{dB} \cdot \lg\dfrac{I}{I_0}$$
Threshold of hearing
The lowest sound intensity a person can hear at a given frequency; at $1\,\text{kHz}$:$$I_0 \approx 10^{-12}\,\text{W/m}^2$$
Range of hearing
The range of frequencies a person can hear; about $16\,\text{Hz}$–$20\,\text{kHz}$.
Noise
Unwanted or disturbing sound.
Infrasound
Sound whose frequency is below the lower limit of human hearing.
Ultrasound
Sound whose frequency is above the upper limit of human hearing.

FY7 · Electromagnetism and light

Magnetism

Magnetic field
A region in which a force acts on a magnetic material or a moving charge.
Magnetic flux density
The quantity $B$ that describes the strength of a magnetic field; its unit is the tesla.
Lorentz force
The force that a magnetic field exerts on a moving charge.$$F = qvB\sin\alpha$$
Force on a current-carrying wire
The force on a wire carrying a current in a magnetic field.$$F = IlB\sin\alpha$$
Magnetic flux
The amount of magnetic field passing through a surface, when the field is perpendicular to the surface.$$\Phi = BA$$
Solenoid
A coil inside which a uniform magnetic field forms when a current flows.

Electromagnetic induction

Faraday's law of induction
A changing magnetic flux induces a voltage.$$e = -N \cdot \dfrac{\Delta\Phi}{\Delta t}$$
Lenz's law
An induced current opposes the change that caused it.
Generator
A device that turns kinetic energy into electrical energy by induction.
Transformer
A device that changes the size of an alternating voltage in the ratio of the numbers of turns in its coils.
Alternating current
A current whose direction and size change periodically.
RMS value
The value of an alternating current that has the same heating effect as an equal direct current.

Light as an electromagnetic wave

Electromagnetic wave
A travelling oscillation of electric and magnetic fields that needs no medium.
Speed of light
The speed of electromagnetic radiation in a vacuum.$$c \approx 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$$
Refractive index
The ratio of the speed of light in a vacuum to its speed in a material.$$n = \dfrac{c}{v}$$
Snell's law
The law of refraction: the angles of incidence and refraction depend on the refractive indices of the materials.$$n_1\sin\alpha_1 = n_2\sin\alpha_2$$
Total internal reflection
Light is reflected entirely back into the denser material when the critical angle is exceeded.
Diffraction grating
An optical component whose slits diffract light and split it into a spectrum.
Polarisation
Restriction of the direction of the light’s oscillation to one plane.
Focal length of a lens
The distance at which rays parallel to the optical axis are brought to a point.
