**Principle:** In the glass light travels at the speed $v = c : n$, so travelling the same distance takes longer in the glass than in the air. The travel time is the distance divided by the speed. **Substitution:** $\Delta t = \dfrac{(1.52 - 1) \cdot 0.020\,\text{m}}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}} = 3.467 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\text{s} = 34.67\,\text{ps}$
**Answer:** $\Delta t \approx 35\,\text{ps}$, two significant figures like the thickness $2.0\,\text{cm}$.
**Reasonableness:** In air the distance takes $0.020\,\text{m} : 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s} \approx 67\,\text{ps}$, and in glass about $1.5$ times longer, that is, about $100\,\text{ps}$. The difference is about half of the travel time in air, as follows from the refractive index $1.52$.

**Formula:** $d\tan\alpha_1 = h'\tan\alpha_2$, so $\tan\alpha_1 = \dfrac{h'\tan\alpha_2}{d}$; from the law of refraction $n \sin\alpha_1 = n_2 \sin\alpha_2$ follows $n = \dfrac{n_2 \sin\alpha_2}{\sin\alpha_1}$ and the mean $n_\text{mean} = \dfrac{n_{10} + n_{20} + n_{30} + n_{40}}{4}$

**Substitution:** In the row $10.0^\circ$: $\tan\alpha_1 = \dfrac{44.9 \cdot \tan 10.0^\circ}{60.0} = 0.1320$, $\alpha_1 = 7.517^\circ$, $n = \dfrac{\sin 10.0^\circ}{\sin 7.517^\circ} = 1.3274$. In the row $20.0^\circ$: $\alpha_1 = 14.880^\circ$, $n = 1.3319$. In the row $30.0^\circ$: $\alpha_1 = 22.006^\circ$, $n = 1.3344$. In the row $40.0^\circ$: $\alpha_1 = 28.978^\circ$, $n = 1.3268$. The sum is $1.3274 + 1.3319 + 1.3344 + 1.3268 = 5.3205$, so $n_\text{mean} = \dfrac{5.3205}{4} = 1.3301$
**Answer:** $n \approx 1.33$, three significant figures like the depth and the values of the table. Justification: light is refracted from water into air away from the normal, so the ray arriving at the eye is less steep than the ray in the water, and its extension meets the vertical line through the object above the real depth, so $h' < d$. As the viewing angle grows, the difference between the rays grows, because the refraction is stronger the more oblique the ray is, and therefore $h'$ decreases according to the data from $44.9\,\text{cm}$ to $39.6\,\text{cm}$.
**Reasonableness:** The result corresponds to the tabulated refractive index of water, $1.33$. At a small angle the apparent depth approaches $d : n = 60.0\,\text{cm} : 1.33 = 45.1\,\text{cm}$, and the reading $44.9\,\text{cm}$ at the smallest angle $10.0^\circ$ is close to this. In every row $h' < 60.0\,\text{cm}$.

## fy7-10-L3-05
**Given:** angle of incidence $\alpha_1 = 55.0^\circ$, thickness of the glass plate $t_2 = 6.0\,\text{mm}$ and refractive index $n_2 = 1.52$, thickness of the water layer $t_3 = 20.0\,\text{mm}$ and refractive index $n_3 = 1.33$, refractive index of air $n_1 = 1.00$
**Find:** the horizontal distance $x$ through the glass and the water in millimetres
**Principle:** The ray is refracted at both boundaries according to the law of refraction. The boundaries are parallel, so the angle in the glass is the same with respect to the normal of the boundary between air and glass and the normal of the boundary between glass and water. Therefore $n_1 \sin\alpha_1 = n_2 \sin\alpha_2 = n_3 \sin\alpha_3$, and the refractive index of the glass cancels out when the angle in the water is calculated. In each layer the ray travels straight, so the horizontal distance is the thickness of the layer multiplied by the tangent of the angle.
**Formula:** $\sin\alpha_2 = \dfrac{n_1 \sin\alpha_1}{n_2}$ and $\sin\alpha_3 = \dfrac{n_1 \sin\alpha_1}{n_3}$; $x = t_2 \tan\alpha_2 + t_3 \tan\alpha_3$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the thicknesses are in millimetres, so $x$ is obtained in millimetres.
**Substitution:** a) $\sin\alpha_2 = \dfrac{1.00 \cdot \sin 55.0^\circ}{1.52} = 0.5389$, so $\alpha_2 = 32.61^\circ$. b) $\sin\alpha_3 = \dfrac{1.00 \cdot \sin 55.0^\circ}{1.33} = 0.6159$, so $\alpha_3 = 38.02^\circ$; without the glass plate the result would be the same, because for a ray passing directly from air into water $1.00 \cdot \sin 55.0^\circ = 1.33 \cdot \sin\alpha_3$. c) $x = 6.0\,\text{mm} \cdot \tan 32.61^\circ + 20.0\,\text{mm} \cdot \tan 38.02^\circ = 3.839\,\text{mm} + 15.636\,\text{mm} = 19.47\,\text{mm}$
**Answer:** $x \approx 19.5\,\text{mm}$, three significant figures like the angle of incidence and the refractive indices; the term of the glass ($3.8\,\text{mm}$) is small, so the $20.0\,\text{mm}$ water layer limits the accuracy of the result.
**Reasonableness:** If the ray travelled the whole distance of $26.0\,\text{mm}$ in the direction of the air, $55.0^\circ$, the horizontal distance would be $26.0\,\text{mm} \cdot \tan 55.0^\circ = 37.1\,\text{mm}$. In denser materials the ray is closer to the normal, so the distance is shorter than this, and $19.5\,\text{mm} < 37.1\,\text{mm}$. The angles $32.6^\circ < 38.0^\circ < 55.0^\circ$ are in the order of the refractive indices $1.52 > 1.33 > 1.00$.
