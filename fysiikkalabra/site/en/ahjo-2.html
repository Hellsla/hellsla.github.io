Goals

Draft: the goals and the lesson outline await the teacher's approval.

In this course, you will learn to

  • define a problem and the user's needs
  • come up with possible solutions and choose between them with reasons
  • plan and schedule a project in a group
  • build a prototype with Ahjo's equipment and methods
  • test a solution and improve it on the basis of tests and feedback
  • document and present the project and assess your own and the group's work

16 lessons

  1. Starting the project How Ahjo 2 runs, teams and finding a project topic.Example: Look at a few student invention and enterprise projects and collect problems that matter to you, the school or the neighbourhood.
  2. Defining the problem Whose problem, what need, and how success is recognised.Example: Interview at least three users and write a precise problem definition: who, in what situation, and by what measurable criterion the solution succeeds.
  3. Ideation Ideation methods and producing several possible solutions.Example: Ideate through constraints: what if it cost at most €20, worked without electricity, or had to be repairable by the user?
  4. Judging and choosing ideas Criteria, comparison and a justified choice.Example: Rank the ideas with a weighted decision matrix that also covers sustainability: materials, energy use and life cycle.
  5. Project plan Goals, roles, schedule, materials and risks.Example: Write a plan with milestones, responsibilities, a budget and a risk assessment, as in a real product development project.
  6. Design Sketches, dimensioned drawings and digital models.Example: Make a dimensioned drawing and a 3D model from which another group could build the same part without explanations.
  7. Materials and methods Choosing suitable materials, equipment and ways of making.Example: Choose a material with reasons: compare strength, cost, making time and recyclability for at least two options.
  8. A first prototype A quick, simple model to try out the idea.Example: Build the smallest working prototype that tests the riskiest assumption of your project.
  9. Testing the prototype A test plan, measuring and recording observations.Example: Write a test plan: what is measured, how precisely, and what result counts as success. Collect the data and present it as a graph.
  10. Feedback and improvement Feedback from users and the group; planning changes.Example: Run a user test in which an outsider uses the prototype without instructions. Record what you see and decide what to change and why.
  11. Building 1 Making the parts of the solution according to the plan.Example: Make the parts with different machines and share the work so that every member masters at least one new method.
  12. Building 2 Assembling the parts; electronics, programming or finishing as needed.Example: Add a sensor, a microcontroller or a printed textile part that measurably improves how it works.
  13. Final testing Does the solution work: tests, measurements and comparison with the goals.Example: Test the final solution with the same measures as the prototype and judge whether the original goal was met.
  14. Finishing Last fixes, appearance and usability.Example: Finish it for presentation: a case, labels and instructions that let the user manage without the makers.
  15. Documentation The project's stages, decisions and results into a portfolio.Example: Put together a portfolio that tells your decisions and failures honestly; it also serves an upper secondary diploma and applications to further studies.
  16. Presentation and assessment Presenting the project and assessing your own and the group's work.Example: Pitch the project to an audience or a partner company and answer questions. Finally, assess your own and the group's work.
