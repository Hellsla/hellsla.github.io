Density: m = ρV

Why can two metal cubes of the same size feel completely different in weight in your hand, and how can you work out what an unknown object is made of using only a balance and a measuring cylinder?

In this lesson, you will learn to

  • calculate the mass, volume or density of an object with the formula $m = \rho V$.
  • explain why mass is directly proportional to volume and what the slope of the line tells you.
  • determine the density of a substance from measurement results and identify the substance using a table.

Prior knowledge

  • In direct proportionality the measurement points lie, within the measurement uncertainty, on a straight line through the origin, and its slope is the ratio $y : x$chapter Graphs and fitting a straight line
  • Measurement results always contain error, and a result is given with a suitable number of significant figureschapter Measuring and the reliability of a measurement
  • Quantities are calculated in SI units, and units are converted using powers of tenchapter Quantities, units and the SI system

Core path: follow the steps in order. Tasks marked Optional give extra practice. Optional tasks: 3.

1Think about it

At a recycling point there are two grey metal cubes on the table. They are exactly the same size, but one feels much heavier in your hand than the other. One is aluminium and the other is iron, but the cubes have no markings. On the table there are a balance and a ruler.

Which quantities of the cubes should you measure so that you can work out what they are made of?

Optional Recap: direct proportionality
In an experiment the quantities $x$ and $y$ are measured. Within the measurement uncertainty, the measurement points lie on a straight line through the origin.What does this tell you about how the quantities depend on each other?

  • The slope can be any number, not only 1. At the points of the line $y = kx$, $y$ is $k$ times $x$ (chapter Graphs and fitting a straight line).

  • This describes the line $y = kx + b$, where the constant $b$ is the intercept on the vertical axis. When the line passes through the origin, $b = 0$, and then $y$ is also zero when $x$ is zero.

  • Correct. A line through the origin has the form $y = kx$, so the ratio $y : x = k$ stays the same and doubling $x$ doubles $y$ (chapter Graphs and fitting a straight line). Today $y$ is mass, $x$ is volume and $k$ is density.

  • If the quantities did not depend on each other, the points would lie on a horizontal line or be scattered. A line whose slope is not zero shows a clear dependence.

2Predict

Make your predictions before investigating 2 questions

Prediction 1: a piece three times as big

A piece of aluminium has a volume of $20\,\text{cm}^3$ and a mass of $54\,\text{g}$. What is the mass of a $60\,\text{cm}^3$ piece made of the same aluminium?

  • Correct. When mass is directly proportional to volume, three times the volume gives three times the mass: $3 \cdot 54\,\text{g} = 162\,\text{g}$. You get the same result with density: $54\,\text{g} : 20\,\text{cm}^3 = 2.7\,\text{g/cm}^3$, and $2.7\,\text{g/cm}^3 \cdot 60\,\text{cm}^3 = 162\,\text{g}$.

  • Shape does not affect mass when the substance and the volume are the same. The density $2.7\,\text{g/cm}^3$ is a property of the substance, so $60\,\text{cm}^3$ of aluminium has a mass of $162\,\text{g}$ whatever its shape.

  • In an object made of one substance, every cubic centimetre has the same mass, $2.7\,\text{g}$. Three times as many cubic centimetres have three times the mass, so the mass grows as fast as the volume: $162\,\text{g}$.

  • The mass does not increase by as many grams as the volume increases in cubic centimetres. Each cubic centimetre of aluminium has a mass of $54\,\text{g} : 20\,\text{cm}^3 = 2.7\,\text{g/cm}^3$, so $40\,\text{cm}^3$ adds $108\,\text{g}$ of mass, which gives $162\,\text{g}$ in total.

Prediction 2: two substances in the same coordinate system

The masses and volumes of pieces of aluminium and iron are measured, and the results are plotted in the same coordinate system with volume on the horizontal axis and mass on the vertical axis.What kind of lines do you get?

  • What matters is the kind of substance, not whether it is a metal. The density of aluminium is $2.70\,\text{g/cm}^3$ and that of iron is $7.87\,\text{g/cm}^3$, so for the same volume the mass of iron is almost three times as large and its line is steeper.

  • The vertical axis shows mass. A dense substance has a lot of mass per unit of volume, so its mass grows quickly with volume and the line is steep, not shallow.

  • Correct. At zero volume the mass is zero, so the lines start from the origin. The slope is the density, and the density of iron, $7.87\,\text{g/cm}^3$, is greater than that of aluminium, $2.70\,\text{g/cm}^3$, so for the same volume the iron piece has a greater mass and its line is steeper.

  • An object with zero volume contains no matter, so its mass is zero and the line passes through the origin. Only if a container or packaging is included do the measurement points shift upwards; this is covered later.

Aluminium pieces: is mass proportional to volume?

Simulated example data

The table shows example data for five aluminium pieces of different sizes. The volume has been calculated from the dimensions and the mass measured with a balance. The numbers are example values, not real measurements, but they include the usual measurement error.

Piece $V$ ($\text{cm}^3$) $m$ (g)
1 10.0 26.8
2 20.0 54.4
3 30.0 80.6
4 40.0 108.4
5 50.0 134.6

Calculate the ratio $m : V$ for each row.

What can you conclude from the table?

  • Correct. The ratios are $2.68$, $2.72$, $2.69$, $2.71$ and $2.69\,\text{g/cm}^3$, and they differ from their mean $2.70\,\text{g/cm}^3$ by less than one per cent. The constant ratio is the density of the substance, which for aluminium is about $2.7\,\text{g/cm}^3$.

  • The differences are $16.8$, $34.4$, $50.6$, $68.4$ and $84.6$, so they increase. In addition, mass and volume have different units, and you cannot subtract one from the other. The correct comparison is the ratio $m : V$.

  • Measurement results always show small variation (chapter Measuring and the reliability of a measurement). Each ratio differs from the mean by less than one per cent, and the differences are not systematic, so the ratio is in practice constant.

  • Mere increase is not enough for proportionality. The line $m = V + 5$ would also increase with volume. In proportionality the ratio $m : V$ is constant, and you have to calculate it: here it is about $2.7\,\text{g/cm}^3$ in every row.

Formula

Liquid in a container: what does the mass consist of?

Simulated example data

Liquid is poured into a measuring flask, and the balance shows the total mass $m_\text{tot}$ of the flask and the liquid. The table has example data. The numbers are example values.

$V$ ($\text{cm}^3$) $m_\text{tot}$ (g)
0 42.1
20 57.9
40 73.5
60 89.6
80 105.1

Investigate how much the mass increases for every $20\,\text{cm}^3$.

Which statement is correct?

  • The mass in the last row includes the flask. If the mass of the flask, $42.1\,\text{g}$, is subtracted, the mass of the liquid is $63.0\,\text{g}$ and the density is $63.0\,\text{g} : 80\,\text{cm}^3 \approx 0.79\,\text{g/cm}^3$.

  • Correct. The increases are $15.8$, $15.6$, $16.1$ and $15.5\,\text{g}$. Volume and mass depend on each other linearly: $m_\text{tot} = \rho V + m_0$. The slope of the line, $(105.1 - 42.1)\,\text{g} : 80\,\text{cm}^3 \approx 0.79\,\text{g/cm}^3$, is the density of the liquid, and the intercept $m_0 \approx 42\,\text{g}$ is the mass of the flask.

  • The mass of the empty flask is the intercept of the line on the vertical axis, that is, the mass when $V = 0$. The slope tells you how much the mass increases per unit of volume, and it is the density of the liquid.

  • When the volume is zero, the total mass is still $42.1\,\text{g}$, and the ratios $m_\text{tot} : V$ are $2.9$, $1.8$, $1.5$ and $1.3\,\text{g/cm}^3$, so they are not constant. In a proportional dependence the line would pass through the origin.

Write based on the three tables: How do you know that the mass of the aluminium pieces is directly proportional to volume? How was the density of the unknown metal obtained? Model answer

For the aluminium pieces the ratio $m : V$ was about $2.7\,\text{g/cm}^3$ in every row, so mass is directly proportional to volume and the ratio is the density of the substance. The density of the unknown metal was obtained from the differences of two points far apart, $\rho = \Delta m : \Delta V = 179.2\,\text{g} : 20.0\,\text{cm}^3 = 8.96\,\text{g/cm}^3$, and it matched copper. The line of copper is steeper than that of aluminium, because its density, that is its slope, is greater. The total mass of a liquid with its container is not proportional to volume, because the mass of the empty container is included. So the mass of the container must be subtracted from the total mass, or the density must be read from the slope of the line, in which case the mass of the container is the intercept. The data were example values with small measurement error. So you can work out what the cubes are made of by measuring their mass and volume and calculating the density from them.

    4Theory summary

    Key idea

    The mass $m$ of an object tells you how much matter the object contains, and its unit is the kilogram. The volume $V$ tells you how much space the object takes up; its SI unit is the cubic metre ($\text{m}^3$). Two objects of the same size may have different weights if they are made of different substances: a ten-cubic-centimetre pine cube is light, an aluminium cube is clearly heavier and an iron cube is the heaviest.

    The difference comes from how tightly the matter is packed inside the volume. This is described by density $\rho$: it tells you how much mass a substance has per unit of volume. Iron is about sixteen times as dense as pine, so the same volume contains about sixteen times as much matter.

    Mass is directly proportional to volume

    When you measure pieces of different sizes made of the same substance, you notice a regularity: twice the volume gives twice the mass, and half the volume gives half the mass. So mass is directly proportional to volume, which means the ratio $m : V$ stays the same. That constant ratio is the density of the substance.

    If the measurement results are plotted in a coordinate system with volume on the horizontal axis and mass on the vertical axis, the points lie, within the measurement uncertainty, on a straight line through the origin (chapter Graphs and fitting a straight line). The slope of the line is the density.

    Density tells you what the substance is

    Every pure substance has its own density in a given state of matter and at a given temperature, so density is like a fingerprint of the substance: once the mass and volume of an object have been measured, the calculated density can be compared with table values. The conclusion is not certain. Measurements always contain error (chapter Measuring and the reliability of a measurement), and some substances have almost the same density, so the result only tells you which substances the object may be made of.

    Density

    The density of a substance is the ratio of mass to volume. Solving this ratio for mass and for volume gives two other forms:

    $$\begin{aligned} \rho &= \frac{m}{V} \\ m &= \rho V \\ V &= \frac{m}{\rho} \end{aligned}$$

    where $\rho$ is the density (SI unit $\text{kg/m}^3$), $m$ the mass (kg) and $V$ the volume ($\text{m}^3$). The form $m = \rho V$ is a model of direct proportionality $y = kx$: the mass $m$ is $y$, the volume $V$ is $x$ and the density $\rho$ is the slope $k$.

    In the figure, the points of each substance lie on their own line. The steeper the line, the greater the density. You can get the density from a graph by reading two points far apart on the line and calculating $\rho = \Delta m : \Delta V$. The result obtained this way is more accurate than the ratio $m : V$ of a single point, because it uses the whole line.

    Units

    The SI unit of density is $\text{kg/m}^3$, but for small objects the unit $\text{g/cm}^3$ is often used. The relationship between the units is

    $$1\,\tfrac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3} = \frac{10^{-3}\,\text{kg}}{10^{-6}\,\text{m}^3} = 1000\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$$

    In a calculation, keep all quantities in the same system of units. The volume conversions are $1\,\text{cm}^3 = 10^{-6}\,\text{m}^3$ and $1\,\text{dm}^3 = 10^{-3}\,\text{m}^3$. In addition, $1\,\text{dm}^3$ is one litre, and $1\,\text{ml} = 1\,\text{cm}^3$.

    Substance $\rho$ ($\text{kg/m}^3$) $\rho$ ($\text{g/cm}^3$)
    pine 500 0.50
    ethanol 790 0.79
    cooking oil 920 0.92
    ice 920 0.92
    water 1000 1.00
    aluminium 2700 2.70
    iron 7870 7.87

    Measuring volume

    The volume of a regular object is calculated from its dimensions: the volume of a cube is $a^3$ and the volume of a rectangular block is the area of the base times the height. The volume of an irregular object is measured by displacement of water: the object is lowered into a measuring cylinder, and the volume is the rise of the water level, $V = V_2 - V_1$.

    A reading of a measuring cylinder contains uncertainty, for example from reading the scale and the curvature of the liquid surface. The difference of two readings is a smaller number than the readings themselves, but it includes the uncertainty of both readings, so the difference may have fewer significant figures than either reading. This limits the accuracy of the density.

    The mass of a container in a measurement

    If a liquid is measured in a container and the balance shows the total mass of the container and the liquid, the measurement points do not pass through the origin: the mass $m_0$ of the empty container is included. The total mass follows a linear model $m_\text{tot} = \rho V + m_0$. The density is still the slope of the line, and the constant $m_0$ is the point where the line crosses the vertical axis, that is, the mass of the empty container. The total mass is not directly proportional to volume, but the mass of the liquid itself is.

    Common misconception

    Many people think that a bigger object is denser because it is heavier. A bigger piece of iron is indeed heavier than a small one, but their density is the same: mass and volume grow together so that the ratio $m : V$ does not change. Density is a property of the substance, not of the size of the object.

    Another mistake is to mix up units. If the mass is in grams and the volume in cubic metres, the result is a thousand times too large or too small. First convert everything to one system of units: either $\text{kg}$ and $\text{m}^3$ or $\text{g}$ and $\text{cm}^3$.

    A third mistake is to think that the mass of an empty container belongs to the liquid. It must be subtracted from the total mass before the density is calculated.

    Summary

    • Density is mass per unit of volume: $\rho = m : V$, SI unit $\text{kg/m}^3$. Water 1000 $\text{kg/m}^3$, aluminium 2700 $\text{kg/m}^3$, iron 7870 $\text{kg/m}^3$.
    • The mass of a given substance is directly proportional to volume: $m = \rho V$. In a graph, $m(V)$ is a straight line through the origin whose slope is the density.
    • Solve the formula for the quantity you need: $\rho = m : V$, $m = \rho V$ or $V = m : \rho$. Keep the units in one system: $1\,\text{g/cm}^3 = 1000\,\text{kg/m}^3$.
    • The volume of an irregular object is measured by displacement of water, $V = V_2 - V_1$. The mass of a container must be subtracted, or it shows up as the constant term $m_0$ of the line.

    5Apply

    Worked example

    Problem

    A solid iron beam is $1.20\,\text{m}$ long, and its cross-section is a square with a side of 4.0 cm. What is the mass of the beam?

    Step 1/7

    Given and find. The length is $L = 1.20\,\text{m}$ and the side of the cross-section is $a = 4.0\,\text{cm}$. From the table, the density of iron is $\rho = 7870\,\text{kg/m}^3$. We are asked for the mass $m$ of the beam.

    Step 2/7

    Principle. The beam is iron all the way through, so its mass is directly proportional to volume, and the constant of proportionality is the density of iron. The volume is obtained as the product of the area of the cross-section, $a^2$, and the length.

    Step 3/7

    Rearranged formula. $$\begin{aligned} V &= a^2 L \\ m &= \rho V = \rho a^2 L \end{aligned}$$

    Step 4/7

    Unit conversions. The density is in $\text{kg/m}^3$, so the lengths are converted to metres: $a = 4.0\,\text{cm} = 0.040\,\text{m}$. The length is already in metres, and the mass comes out directly in kilograms.

    Step 5/7

    Substitution. $$\begin{aligned} m &= \rho a^2 L \\ &= 7870\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3} \cdot (0.040\,\text{m})^2 \cdot 1.20\,\text{m} \\ &= 7870\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3} \cdot 1.92 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{m}^3 \\ &= 15.11\,\text{kg} \end{aligned}$$

    Step 6/7

    Result. The mass of the beam is about $15\,\text{kg}$. The result is given with two significant figures, because the side $4.0\,\text{cm}$ is known to two figures; the table density $7870\,\text{kg/m}^3$ would be more precise.

    Step 7/7

    Reasonableness. The volume of the beam is $1.92\,\text{dm}^3$, about two litres, and the same volume of water would have a mass of about $1.9\,\text{kg}$. Iron is about 7.9 times as dense as water, so the estimate is $7.9 \cdot 1.9\,\text{kg} \approx 15\,\text{kg}$. The result matches.

    Optional Find the error
    A student calculates the mass of a $250\,\text{cm}^3$ piece of aluminium: $m = \rho V = 2700\,\text{kg/m}^3 \cdot 250\,\text{cm}^3 = 675\,000\,\text{kg}$. The result is far too large. Where is the error?

    • Both units are acceptable as long as they are consistent: $2700\,\text{kg/m}^3 = 2.70\,\text{g/cm}^3$. The error arises only when the units of density and volume are from different systems.

    • Correct. The units must be converted to the same system: $250\,\text{cm}^3 = 250 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m}^3 = 2.5 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\text{m}^3$, and $2700\,\text{kg/m}^3 \cdot 2.5 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\text{m}^3 = 0.675\,\text{kg}$. You could have spotted the wrong result with an estimate: a quarter of a litre of aluminium cannot weigh hundreds of tonnes.

    • The formula $m = \rho V$ is correct, because density is mass per volume. Check the units: $V : \rho$ would give the unit $\text{m}^3 \cdot \text{m}^3/\text{kg}$, which is not a unit of mass.

    • Estimate the result: $250\,\text{cm}^3$ is a quarter of a litre, so the piece is about the size of a palm. That much water would have a mass of $250\,\text{g}$, and aluminium is 2.7 times as dense, so the mass is about $0.7\,\text{kg}$.

    Apply to a new situation

    A stone found in a river has a mass of $187\,\text{g}$. A measuring cylinder contains $120\,\text{ml}$ of water, and when the stone is lowered in, the reading is $190\,\text{ml}$.

    What is the density of the substance of the stone? Give your answer in $\text{kg/m}^3$.

    kg/m³
    Hint 1: a guiding question

    What is the volume of the stone? The stone displaces its own volume of water.

    Hint 2: what is the connection

    The volume is

    $V$$= V_2 - V_1$
    $= 190\,\text{ml} - 120\,\text{ml}$

    Then convert the mass to kilograms and the volume to cubic metres:

    $1\,\text{ml}$$= 1\,\text{cm}^3$
    $= 10^{-6}\,\text{m}^3$
    Hint 3: an intermediate step
    $\rho$$= \dfrac{0.187\,\text{kg}}{70 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m}^3}$
    Show the solution

    Given

    mass of the stone $m = 187\,\text{g}$; water reading before $V_1 = 120\,\text{ml}$ and after $V_2 = 190\,\text{ml}$

    Find

    the density $\rho$ of the substance of the stone in $\text{kg/m}^3$

    Principle

    A submerged stone displaces its own volume of water, so the volume of the stone is the difference of the readings. Density is mass divided by volume.

    Formula

    $V = V_2 - V_1$ and

    $\rho$$= \dfrac{m}{V}$
    $= \dfrac{m}{V_2 - V_1}$

    Conversions

    $V$$= 190\,\text{ml} - 120\,\text{ml}$
    $= 70\,\text{ml}$
    $= 70\,\text{cm}^3$
    $= 70 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m}^3$
    $= 7.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\text{m}^3$

    and $m = 187\,\text{g} = 0.187\,\text{kg}$.

    Substitution

    $\rho$$= \dfrac{0.187\,\text{kg}}{7.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\text{m}^3}$
    $= 2671\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$

    Answer

    $2.7 \cdot 10^3\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$

    Two significant figures, because the difference of the readings, $70\,\text{ml}$, is known to two figures.

    Reasonableness

    Rocks are usually about 2.5 to 3 times as dense as water, and $2.7 \cdot 10^3\,\text{kg/m}^3$ is in this range. Estimate: $190\,\text{g}$ and $70\,\text{cm}^3$ would give about $2.7\,\text{g/cm}^3$.

    6Check your understanding

    1. Concept

    Two identical bottles are filled so that each contains $0.50\,\text{kg}$ of liquid: one with water and the other with cooking oil, whose density is $920\,\text{kg/m}^3$.Which liquid takes up more space?

    • The solution does not need a particle model. The densities $1000\,\text{kg/m}^3$ and $920\,\text{kg/m}^3$ and the mass are enough: $V = m : \rho$ gives the volumes.

    • A dense substance packs into a small space: density is mass per volume, so with a high density the same mass takes up less space. From the formula $V = m : \rho$ you can see that a greater $\rho$ gives a smaller $V$.

    • The same mass does not mean the same volume. The volume is $V = m : \rho$, and it also depends on the density, which is different for different substances.

    • Correct. The volume of the water is $0.50\,\text{kg} : 1000\,\text{kg/m}^3 = 5.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\text{m}^3$ and that of the oil is $0.50\,\text{kg} : 920\,\text{kg/m}^3 = 5.4 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\text{m}^3$. The lower the density, the more volume per unit of mass.

    Suggestion: one answer does not show everything you can do, but review the theory summary and do the core exercises.

    2. Density is a property of a substance

    The volume of an iron cube doubles, but the substance stays iron. What happens to the mass and the density of the cube?

    • The mass doubles, but the density does not. If both the mass and the volume double, the ratio $m : V$ stays the same. Density does not tell you the size of an object but how much mass there is in one unit of volume.

    • Correct. Mass is directly proportional to volume, $m = \rho V$, so double the volume means double the mass. The density $\rho = m : V$ does not change, because the ratio stays the same: it is a property of iron.

    • The density does not halve. When the volume doubles and the mass doubles, the ratio $\rho = m : V$ stays the same. The density would decrease only if the volume grew without the mass growing.

    • The mass does not stay the same: a cube of iron with double the volume contains twice as much iron, so its mass doubles. The density does not halve, because the ratio $m : V$ stays the same.

    Suggestion: one answer does not show everything you can do, but review the theory summary and do the core exercises.

    3. Calculation exercise

    The edge of a pine cube is $20.0\,\text{cm}$ and the density of pine is $500\,\text{kg/m}^3$. What is the mass of the cube? Give your answer in kilograms.

    kg
    Hint 1: a guiding question

    First calculate the volume of the cube. The volume of a cube is the edge to the power of three.

    Hint 2: what is the connection

    Convert the edge to metres: $20.0\,\text{cm} = 0.200\,\text{m}$. The volume is $V = a^3$.

    Hint 3: an intermediate step
    $m$$= \rho a^3$
    $= 500\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3} \cdot (0.200\,\text{m})^3$
    Show the solution

    Given

    edge of the cube $a = 20.0\,\text{cm}$; density of pine $\rho = 500\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$

    Find

    the mass $m$ of the cube in kilograms

    Principle

    The cube is pine all the way through, so the mass is density times volume. The volume of a cube is the edge to the power of three.

    Formula

    $V = a^3$ and $m = \rho V = \rho a^3$

    Conversions

    The density is in $\text{kg/m}^3$, so the edge is converted to metres: $a = 20.0\,\text{cm} = 0.200\,\text{m}$.

    Substitution

    $m$$= 500\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3} \cdot (0.200\,\text{m})^3$
    $= 500\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3} \cdot 0.00800\,\text{m}^3$
    $= 4.00\,\text{kg}$

    Answer

    $4.00\,\text{kg}$

    Three significant figures like the edge $20.0\,\text{cm}$.

    Reasonableness

    The volume of the cube is $8.00\,\text{dm}^3$, that is, eight litres. The same volume of water would be $8\,\text{kg}$, and pine is about half as dense as water, so the mass is about $4\,\text{kg}$.

    Optional 4. Explain and justify

    A student measures the mass and volume of an unknown piece of metal and calculates a density of $8.9\,\text{g/cm}^3$. The student says: "The piece is copper." Explain why density is a good way to identify a substance and why the conclusion is still not completely certain.

    Model answer

    Density $\rho = m : V$ is a property of the substance: a large and a small piece of the same substance give the same result. That is why a measured density can be compared with table values. The result $8.9\,\text{g/cm}^3$ is very close to the value for copper, $8.96\,\text{g/cm}^3$, so copper is likely. The conclusion is not certain, because measurements always contain error, especially in the volume, and because other substances or alloys may have a density close to the same value. Other tests, such as colour or magnetism, add certainty.

        The volume of an aluminium plate is $35.0\,\text{cm}^3$, and the density of aluminium is $2.70\,\text{g/cm}^3$. What is the mass of the plate? Give your answer in grams.

        g
        Answer

        94.5 g

        Given

        volume of the aluminium plate $V = 35.0\,\text{cm}^3$ and density $\rho = 2.70\,\tfrac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3}$

        Find

        the mass $m$ of the plate in grams

        Principle

        The mass of a given substance is directly proportional to volume, and the constant of proportionality is the density. The plate is aluminium all the way through.

        Formula

        Solve for the mass from the definition of density $\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}$: $m = \rho V$

        Conversions

        Not needed: the density is in $\text{g/cm}^3$ and the volume in $\text{cm}^3$, so the mass comes out directly in grams.

        $m$$= 2.70\,\tfrac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3} \cdot 35.0\,\text{cm}^3$

      3. In the chemistry classroom, $316\,\text{g}$ of ethanol is measured on a balance. Its density is $0.79\,\text{g/cm}^3$.

        Answer

        400 cm³

        Given

        mass of the ethanol $m = 316\,\text{g}$ and density $\rho = 0.79\,\tfrac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3}$

        Find

        the volume $V$ of the liquid in cubic centimetres

        Principle

        Density is mass per unit of volume, so when the mass and the density are known, the volume is obtained by dividing the mass by the density.

        Formula

        Solve for the volume from $\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}$: $V = \dfrac{m}{\rho}$

        Conversions

        Not needed: the mass is in grams and the density in $\text{g/cm}^3$, so the gram cancels and $\text{cm}^3$ remains.

        Substitution

        $V$$= \dfrac{316\,\text{g}}{0.79\,\tfrac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3}}$
        $= 400\,\text{cm}^3$

        $400\,\text{cm}^3$

        Two significant figures, because the density $0.79\,\tfrac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3}$ is known to two figures.

        Reasonableness

        Ethanol is less dense than water, so $316\,\text{g}$ takes up more space than $316\,\text{g}$ of water, which is $316\,\text{cm}^3$. The result $400\,\text{cm}^3$ is greater, as it should be.

      1. Core

        The dimensions of a gold ingot are $10.0\,\text{cm} \cdot 5.0\,\text{cm} \cdot 2.0\,\text{cm}$. The density of gold is $19\,300\,\text{kg/m}^3$. What is the mass of the ingot? Give your answer in kilograms.

        kg
        Answer

        1.9 kg

        Show the solution

        Given

        dimensions of the ingot $10.0\,\text{cm}$, $5.0\,\text{cm}$ and $2.0\,\text{cm}$; density of gold $\rho = 19\,300\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$

        Find

        the mass $m$ of the ingot in kilograms

        Principle

        The ingot is gold all the way through, so the mass is density times volume. The volume of a rectangular block is the product of the three dimensions.

        Formula

        $V = a\,b\,c$ and $m = \rho V = \rho\,a\,b\,c$

        Conversions

        The density is in $\text{kg/m}^3$, so the dimensions are converted to metres: $10.0\,\text{cm} = 0.100\,\text{m}$, $5.0\,\text{cm} = 0.050\,\text{m}$ and $2.0\,\text{cm} = 0.020\,\text{m}$.

        Substitution

        $m$$= 19\,300\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3} \cdot 0.100\,\text{m} \cdot 0.050\,\text{m} \cdot 0.020\,\text{m}$
        $= 19\,300\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3} \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\text{m}^3$
        $= 1.93\,\text{kg}$

        Answer

        $1.9\,\text{kg}$

        Two significant figures, because the dimensions $5.0\,\text{cm}$ and $2.0\,\text{cm}$ are known to two figures.

        Reasonableness

        The volume of the ingot is $100\,\text{cm}^3$, and the same volume of water would be $100\,\text{g}$. Gold is about 19 times as dense, so $19 \cdot 100\,\text{g} \approx 1.9\,\text{kg}$.

      2. Core

        In a laboratory there is a bottle containing $2.5\,\text{dm}^3$ of ethanol. The density of ethanol is $790\,\text{kg/m}^3$.

        What is the mass of the liquid? Give your answer in kilograms.

        kg
        Answer

        2.0 kg

        Show the solution

        Given

        volume of the ethanol $V = 2.5\,\text{dm}^3$ and density $\rho = 790\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$

        Find

        the mass $m$ of the liquid in kilograms

        Principle

        The liquid is one substance, so the mass is directly proportional to volume and the constant is the density.

        Formula

        $\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}$, so $m = \rho V$

        Conversions

        The density is per cubic metre, so

        $2.5\,\text{dm}^3$$= 2.5 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{m}^3$
        $= 0.0025\,\text{m}^3$

        because $1\,\text{dm}^3 = 10^{-3}\,\text{m}^3$.

        Substitution

        $m$$= 790\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3} \cdot 0.0025\,\text{m}^3$
        $= 1.975\,\text{kg}$

        Answer

        $2.0\,\text{kg}$

        Two significant figures like the volume $2.5\,\text{dm}^3$ and the density $790\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$.

        Reasonableness

        Two and a half litres of water has a mass of $2.5\,\text{kg}$. Ethanol is less dense than water, so its mass is slightly smaller, about $0.79 \cdot 2.5\,\text{kg} \approx 2.0\,\text{kg}$.

      3. The mass of a metal object is $89.0\,\text{g}$. The figure shows a measuring cylinder before and after the object is lowered into it.

        What is the density of the substance of the object? Give your answer in $\text{kg/m}^3$.

        kg/m³
        Answer

        8900 kg/m³

        Show the solution

        Given

        mass of the object $m = 89.0\,\text{g}$; a figure of the measuring cylinder before and after the object is lowered in

        Find

        the density $\rho$ of the substance of the object in $\text{kg/m}^3$

        Principle

        A submerged object displaces its own volume of water, so the volume of the object is the difference of the water readings. Density is mass per unit of volume.

        Reading the graph

        The numbers on the scale are at $20\,\text{ml}$ intervals, the long marks are at $10\,\text{ml}$ intervals and the small marks at $2\,\text{ml}$ intervals. Before the object is lowered in, the water reaches the mark $V_1 = 40\,\text{ml}$. After that, the water reaches the long mark halfway between the numbers $40$ and $60$, $V_2 = 50\,\text{ml}$. An answer is accepted if it deviates by at most $3\,\%$.

        Formula

        $V = V_2 - V_1$ and $\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}$

        Conversions

        $V$$= 50\,\text{ml} - 40\,\text{ml}$
        $= 10\,\text{ml}$
        $= 10\,\text{cm}^3$
        $= 1.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\text{m}^3$

        and $89.0\,\text{g} = 0.0890\,\text{kg}$.

        Substitution

        $\rho$$= \dfrac{0.0890\,\text{kg}}{1.0 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\text{m}^3}$
        $= 8900\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$

        Answer

        $8900\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$

        Two significant figures, because the volume $10\,\text{ml}$ is read from the scale to about $1\,\text{ml}$.

        Reasonableness

        Estimate: $89\,\text{g} : 10\,\text{cm}^3 \approx 9\,\tfrac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3}$, which is $9000\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$. The result is close to the density of copper, $8960\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$, so the object may be copper.

      4. The mass of an empty measuring flask is $215\,\text{g}$. When $250\,\text{cm}^3$ of an unknown liquid is poured into the flask, the balance shows $412\,\text{g}$.

        What is the density of the liquid? Give your answer in $\text{kg/m}^3$.

        kg/m³
        Answer

        788 kg/m³

        Show the solution

        Given

        mass of the empty flask $m_0 = 215\,\text{g}$, volume of the liquid $V = 250\,\text{cm}^3$ and total mass of the flask and the liquid $m_\text{tot} = 412\,\text{g}$

        Find

        the density $\rho$ of the liquid in $\text{kg/m}^3$

        Principle

        The balance shows the total mass of the flask and the liquid, but the density is calculated from the mass of the liquid alone. The mass of the flask is subtracted, and the rest is the mass of the liquid.

        Formula

        $m = m_\text{tot} - m_0$ and

        $\rho$$= \dfrac{m}{V}$
        $= \dfrac{m_\text{tot} - m_0}{V}$

        Conversions

        $m$$= 412\,\text{g} - 215\,\text{g}$
        $= 197\,\text{g}$
        $= 0.197\,\text{kg}$

        and

        $250\,\text{cm}^3$$= 250 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m}^3$
        $= 2.50 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\text{m}^3$

        Substitution

        $\rho$$= \dfrac{0.197\,\text{kg}}{2.50 \cdot 10^{-4}\,\text{m}^3}$
        $= 788\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$

        Answer

        $788\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$

        Three significant figures, because the difference of the masses, $197\,\text{g}$, and the volume, $250\,\text{cm}^3$, have been measured to three figures.

        Reasonableness

        The result is close to the density of ethanol, $790\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$, so the liquid is probably ethanol. If the mass of the flask had been forgotten, the wrong result would be $1650\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$, which would be more than water.

      1. A copper object and an aluminium object have the same mass.

        How many times as large is the volume of the aluminium object compared with the volume of the copper object? Use the densities in the table.

        kertaa
        Answer

        3.32 kertaa

        Show the solution

        Given

        the copper object and the aluminium object have equal masses, $m_\text{Cu} = m_\text{Al} = m$; from the table $\rho_\text{Cu} = 8960\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$ and $\rho_\text{Al} = 2700\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$

        Find

        the ratio of the volumes $V_\text{Al} : V_\text{Cu}$

        Principle

        The mass of each object is density times volume. Because the masses are equal, the products of density and volume are equal, so a greater density means a smaller volume.

        Formula

        $\rho_\text{Al} V_\text{Al} = \rho_\text{Cu} V_\text{Cu}$, from which
        $$\frac{V_\text{Al}}{V_\text{Cu}} = \frac{\rho_\text{Cu}}{\rho_\text{Al}}$$

        Conversions

        Not needed: the densities are in the same unit and the mass $m$ cancels.

        Substitution

        $\dfrac{V_\text{Al}}{V_\text{Cu}}$$= \dfrac{8960\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}}{2700\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}}$
        $= 3.32$

        Answer

        $3.32$

        Times, three significant figures like the densities $8960\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$ and $2.70\cdot10^{3}\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$.

        Reasonableness

        Aluminium is less dense, so more volume of it is needed to get the same mass. With rounded numbers $3 \cdot 2700 = 8100$ and $4 \cdot 2700 = 10\,800$, so the ratio $8960 : 2700$ is between three and four, closer to three, like $3.32$.

      2. An object is made by casting together $100\,\text{cm}^3$ of aluminium and $50\,\text{cm}^3$ of iron. The volumes are assumed to add up.

        What is the average density of the object? Give your answer in $\text{g/cm}^3$.

        g/cm³
        Answer

        4.4 g/cm³

        Show the solution

        Given

        aluminium $V_1 = 100\,\text{cm}^3$, $\rho_1 = 2.70\,\tfrac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3}$ and iron $V_2 = 50\,\text{cm}^3$, $\rho_2 = 7.87\,\tfrac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3}$; the volumes add up

        Find

        the average density $\bar{\rho}$ of the object in $\text{g/cm}^3$

        Principle

        Densities are not added or averaged directly. The masses and the volumes are added, and the density of the whole object is the total mass divided by the total volume.

        Formula

        $m_i = \rho_i V_i$, and
        $$\bar{\rho} = \frac{m_1 + m_2}{V_1 + V_2} = \frac{\rho_1 V_1 + \rho_2 V_2}{V_1 + V_2}$$

        Conversions

        Not needed: all quantities are in the units $\text{g}$ and $\text{cm}^3$.

        Substitution

        The masses are

        $m_1$$= 2.70\,\tfrac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3} \cdot 100\,\text{cm}^3$
        $= 270\,\text{g}$

        and

        $m_2$$= 7.87\,\tfrac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3} \cdot 50\,\text{cm}^3$
        $= 393.5\,\text{g}$

        so

        $\bar{\rho}$$= \dfrac{270\,\text{g} + 393.5\,\text{g}}{150\,\text{cm}^3}$
        $= 4.42\,\tfrac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3}$

        Answer

        $4.4\,\tfrac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3}$

        Two significant figures like the volumes $50\,\text{cm}^3$.

        Reasonableness

        The result is between the densities of aluminium and iron, and closer to aluminium, because there is twice as much aluminium by volume. The ordinary mean of the two densities, $5.3\,\tfrac{\text{g}}{\text{cm}^3}$, would be too large.

      3. A round copper wire has a diameter of $1.0\,\text{mm}$ and a mass of $0.50\,\text{kg}$.

        How long is the wire? The cross-section of the wire is a circle with area $\pi r^2$, and the volume of the wire is the area times the length.

        m
        Answer

        71 m

        Show the solution

        Given

        mass of the wire $m = 0.50\,\text{kg}$, diameter $d = 1.0\,\text{mm}$, so radius $r = 0.50\,\text{mm}$; from the table $\rho = 8960\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$

        Find

        the length $L$ of the wire in metres

        Principle

        The volume is obtained from the mass and the density. The wire is a long cylinder whose volume is the area of the cross-section times the length, so the length is obtained by dividing the volume by the area.

        Formula

        $V = \dfrac{m}{\rho}$, $A = \pi r^2$ and $V = A L$, so
        $$L = \frac{V}{A} = \frac{m}{\rho\,\pi r^2}$$

        Conversions

        The radius is

        $r$$= 0.50\,\text{mm}$
        $= 0.50 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{m}$

        so $r^2 = 2.5 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\text{m}^2$.

        Substitution

        $V$$= \dfrac{0.50\,\text{kg}}{8960\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}}$
        $= 5.58 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\text{m}^3$

        and

        $A$$= \pi \cdot 2.5 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\text{m}^2$
        $= 7.85 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\text{m}^2$

        so

        $L$$= \dfrac{5.58 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\text{m}^3}{7.85 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\text{m}^2}$
        $= 71.1\,\text{m}$

        Answer

        $71\,\text{m}$

        Two significant figures, because the diameter $1.0\,\text{mm}$ is known to two figures.

        Reasonableness

        Half a kilogram of copper has a volume of only about $56\,\text{cm}^3$, that is, half a decilitre. Drawn into a thin wire one thousandth of a metre thick, it is enough for tens of metres of length, so $71\,\text{m}$ is believable.
