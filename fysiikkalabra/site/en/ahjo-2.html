Your own rhythm: Projects differ, and not everyone has to make the same kind of solution. The lessons are a guiding model: the hand-ins are the same for everyone, but groups can move at different paces with the teacher's guidance. Building and testing can partly run in parallel, for example the test plan is written during a long building stage. The journal records what the group actually did.

Opening challenge: The slowest ball run

35 minAhjo 2, lesson 1

Ahjo 2 starts with a quick build where failing is part of the job. The goal is not a polished result but the courage to try, test and change direction. All the materials are worked by hand, so no device induction is needed.

You need: cardboard, masking tape, string, paper cups, straws, scissors, a ping-pong ball

  1. The teacher shows the task: build a run on which a ping-pong ball travels from the edge of the desk to the floor as slowly as possible. (2 min)
  2. Build the first version as a group. Test as soon as the ball travels even part of the way. (15 min)
  3. Test round: time the ball's trip with a stopwatch and tell the others what did not work. (3 min)
  4. The teacher gives each group a user card, such as “a small child must understand the run without instructions” or “the run must fit in a shoebox”. Change the run for the user on the card. (10 min)
  5. Debrief: what failed, what did you learn from it and what did the user card change? (5 min)

What you take from it: The same loop of building, testing, listening to the user and changing repeats all through Ahjo 2. Write in the project journal one thing that did not work and what you learnt from it.

Maker snacks

Optional 10–15 minute experiments for the middle of the project work, lessons 8–12. The teacher can bring them out when the work is stuck or a group needs a new idea. Nothing is handed in, and your own project stays the main thing.

  • A prototype from an odd material 15 min Make a model of the most important part of your project from a material you have not used yet: foil, straws, clothes pegs, fabric or modelling clay. What did the new material reveal about your solution?
  • Mechanism try-out 15 min Build one mechanism from cardboard that you have not tried yet: a lever, a cam, a hinge or a crank. Could it work in your project?
  • The ten-object challenge 10 min The group gets a bag of random materials and in ten minutes builds from them a solution to one part of the project's problem.
  • Paper user interface 15 min Draw your device's buttons, display or instructions on paper and let someone from another group try them. You act as the device and react to the presses. Where do they hesitate?
More about the courseGoals, contents and assessment.

Goals

Draft: the goals and the lesson outline await the teacher's approval.

In this course, you will learn to

  • define a problem and the user's needs
  • come up with possible solutions and choose between them with reasons
  • plan and schedule a project in a group
  • build a prototype with Ahjo's equipment and methods
  • test a solution and improve it on the basis of tests and feedback
  • document and present the project and assess your own and the group's work

Assessment

The assessment is based on the project journal, the five hand-ins and the teacher's observations. The levels are described in the project journal guide. It takes into account:

  • defining the problem and the user's need
  • ideation and a justified choice of solution
  • planning and carrying out the project
  • testing, measurements and using feedback
  • improving the solution
  • documentation and showing your own skills
  • collaboration and personal responsibility

Technical flair or a successful end product alone does not decide the assessment. A failed prototype can show excellent skills when the design, the testing, the analysis of observations and the development decisions are justified. Everyone shows their own part in the hand-ins. The assessment is a draft, and the teacher decides on the weighting.
