## fy4-7-L1-01
**Given:** mass $m = 1200\,\text{kg}$, speed $v = 20.0\,\text{m/s}$
**Find:** the kinetic energy $E_\text{k}$ of the car in kilojoules
**Principle:** A moving object has kinetic energy, which is proportional to the mass and the square of the speed. The speed and the mass of the car are known, so the kinetic energy is obtained directly.
**Formula:** $E_\text{k} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,mv^2$. The formula is already solved for the quantity asked.
**Conversions:** The mass and the speed are in SI units, so the result comes in joules. Finally $1\,\text{kJ} = 1000\,\text{J}$.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{k} = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 1200\,\text{kg} \cdot (20.0\,\text{m/s})^2 = 600\,\text{kg} \cdot 400\,\text{m}^2/\text{s}^2 = 240\,000\,\text{J} = 240\,\text{kJ}$
**Answer:** $240\,\text{kJ}$, two significant figures like the mass $1200\,\text{kg}$.
**Reasonableness:** With rounded numbers $\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 1000\,\text{kg} \cdot 400\,\text{m}^2/\text{s}^2 = 200\,\text{kJ}$, so the order of magnitude is right. The speed $20\,\text{m/s} = 72\,\text{km/h}$ is a normal speed on a country road, and the kinetic energy of a car is then hundreds of kilojoules.

## fy4-7-L1-02
**Given:** mass $m = 2.50\,\text{kg}$, height $h = 8.00\,\text{m}$ above the zero level, that is, the ground, acceleration of free fall $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the potential energy $E_\text{p}$ of the pot in joules
**Principle:** Gravitational potential energy depends on the mass and the height above the chosen zero level. The difference in height is small, so $g$ is constant and $E_\text{p} = m g h$ holds.
**Formula:** $E_\text{p} = m g h$. The formula is already solved for the quantity asked.
**Conversions:** All quantities are in SI units, and $\text{kg} \cdot \text{m/s}^2 \cdot \text{m} = \text{J}$. No conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{p} = 2.50\,\text{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 8.00\,\text{m} = 196.2\,\text{J}$
**Answer:** $196\,\text{J}$, three significant figures like the mass and the height.
**Reasonableness:** With rounded numbers $2.5\,\text{kg} \cdot 10\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 8\,\text{m} = 200\,\text{J}$. The weight of the pot is about $25\,\text{N}$, and lifting it to eight metres takes an energy of a few hundred joules. The result is positive, because the pot is above the zero level.

## fy4-7-L1-03
**Given:** mass $m = 1.20\,\text{kg}$, speed $v = 10.0\,\text{m/s}$, height $h = 20.0\,\text{m}$ above the zero level, that is, sea level, $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the mechanical energy $E_\text{mech}$ of the gull in joules
**Principle:** Mechanical energy is the sum of kinetic energy and potential energy. Kinetic energy is obtained from the speed and potential energy from the height above sea level.
**Formula:** $E_\text{mech} = E_\text{k} + E_\text{p}$, so $E_\text{mech} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,mv^2 + m g h$
**Conversions:** All quantities are in SI units, so both terms come in joules. No conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{mech} = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 1.20\,\text{kg} \cdot (10.0\,\text{m/s})^2 + 1.20\,\text{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 20.0\,\text{m} = 60.0\,\text{J} + 235.4\,\text{J} \approx 295\,\text{J}$
**Answer:** $295\,\text{J}$, three significant figures like the initial data.
**Reasonableness:** With rounded numbers $60\,\text{J} + 1.2\,\text{kg} \cdot 10\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 20\,\text{m} = 60\,\text{J} + 240\,\text{J} = 300\,\text{J}$. The potential energy is greater than the kinetic energy, which suits a gull flying high but slowly.

## fy4-7-L1-04
**Given:** mass $m = 72\,\text{kg}$, change in height $\Delta h = 12\,\text{m}$ upward, $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the change in potential energy $\Delta E_\text{p}$ in kilojoules
**Principle:** Potential energy is proportional to height, so its change depends only on the change in height, not on the zero level. The climber goes up, so the potential energy increases.
**Formula:** $E_\text{p} = m g h$, so $\Delta E_\text{p} = m g h_2 - m g h_1$, that is, $\Delta E_\text{p} = m g\,\Delta h$
**Conversions:** All quantities are in SI units, so the result comes in joules. Finally $1\,\text{kJ} = 1000\,\text{J}$.
**Substitution:** $\Delta E_\text{p} = 72\,\text{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 12\,\text{m} \approx 8476\,\text{J} \approx 8.48\,\text{kJ}$
**Answer:** $8.5\,\text{kJ}$, two significant figures like the mass $72\,\text{kg}$ and the height $12\,\text{m}$.
**Reasonableness:** The climber's weight is about $700\,\text{N}$, and $700\,\text{N} \cdot 12\,\text{m} \approx 8400\,\text{J}$. The result does not depend on the height from which the climber started, because only the change is asked.

## fy4-7-L1-05
**Given:** mass $m = 4.0\,\text{kg}$, height $h = -6.5\,\text{m}$ (the bucket is $6.5\,\text{m}$ below the zero level, that is, the ground), $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the potential energy $E_\text{p}$ of the bucket in joules
**Principle:** Potential energy is measured from the chosen zero level. Below the zero level the height is negative, so the potential energy is negative too: the bucket has less potential energy than at ground level.
**Formula:** $E_\text{p} = m g h$. The formula is already solved for the quantity asked.
**Conversions:** All quantities are in SI units, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{p} = 4.0\,\text{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot (-6.5\,\text{m}) \approx -255\,\text{J}$
**Answer:** $-255\,\text{J}$, that is, to two significant figures $-2.6 \cdot 10^{2}\,\text{J}$, like the mass $4.0\,\text{kg}$ and the depth $6.5\,\text{m}$.
**Reasonableness:** Lifting the bucket to ground level takes about $40\,\text{N} \cdot 6.5\,\text{m} = 260\,\text{J}$, and that is exactly how much lower the potential energy of the bucket is than its value zero at ground level. The sign is negative, as it should be below the zero level.

## fy4-7-L2-01
**Given:** mass $m = 1350\,\text{kg}$, speed $v = 90\,\text{km/h}$
**Find:** the kinetic energy $E_\text{k}$ of the van in kilojoules
**Principle:** Kinetic energy is proportional to the mass and the square of the speed. The joule is $\text{kg} \cdot \text{m}^2/\text{s}^2$, so the speed must first be converted to the unit $\text{m/s}$.
**Formula:** $E_\text{k} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,mv^2$. The formula is already solved for the quantity asked.
**Conversions:** $90\,\text{km/h} = \dfrac{90 \cdot 1000\,\text{m}}{3600\,\text{s}} = 25\,\text{m/s}$ and finally $1\,\text{kJ} = 1000\,\text{J}$.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{k} = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 1350\,\text{kg} \cdot (25\,\text{m/s})^2 = 675\,\text{kg} \cdot 625\,\text{m}^2/\text{s}^2 = 421\,875\,\text{J} \approx 422\,\text{kJ}$
**Answer:** $422\,\text{kJ}$, that is, to two significant figures $4.2 \cdot 10^{2}\,\text{kJ}$, like the speed $90\,\text{km/h}$.
**Reasonableness:** With rounded numbers $\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 1400\,\text{kg} \cdot 600\,\text{m}^2/\text{s}^2 = 420\,\text{kJ}$. If the speed were substituted in kilometres per hour, the result would be almost $13$ times too large, because $3.6^2 \approx 13$.

## fy4-7-L2-02
**Given:** mass $m = 57\,\text{g}$, kinetic energy $E_\text{k} = 82\,\text{J}$
**Find:** the speed $v$ of the ball in $\text{m/s}$
**Principle:** Kinetic energy depends on the mass and the speed. When the energy and the mass are known, the speed is solved from the expression for kinetic energy. The speed is positive, because we ask for its magnitude.
**Formula:** $E_\text{k} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,mv^2$, so $v^2 = \dfrac{2E_\text{k}}{m}$ and $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2E_\text{k}}{m}}$
**Conversions:** $m = 57\,\text{g} = 57 : 1000\,\text{kg} = 0.057\,\text{kg}$
**Substitution:** $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2 \cdot 82\,\text{J}}{0.057\,\text{kg}}} = \sqrt{2877\,\text{m}^2/\text{s}^2} \approx 53.6\,\text{m/s}$
**Answer:** $54\,\text{m/s}$, two significant figures like the mass $57\,\text{g}$ and the energy $82\,\text{J}$.
**Reasonableness:** $54\,\text{m/s} \approx 190\,\text{km/h}$, which is the speed of a hard serve by a top player. Calculated back, $\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 0.057\,\text{kg} \cdot (54\,\text{m/s})^2 \approx 83\,\text{J}$, close to the given energy.

## fy4-7-L2-03
**Given:** mass $m = 1.20\,\text{kg}$, height of the shelf from the floor $1.90\,\text{m}$, height of the table surface from the floor $0.75\,\text{m}$, $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$; the zero level is the surface of the table
**Find:** the potential energy $E_\text{p}$ of the book relative to the surface of the table in joules
**Principle:** In potential energy the height is measured from the chosen zero level. The zero level is the surface of the table, so the height is the difference between the heights of the shelf and the table, not the height of the shelf from the floor.
**Formula:** $E_\text{p} = m g h$, where $h = h_\text{shelf} - h_\text{table}$. So $E_\text{p} = m g\,(h_\text{shelf} - h_\text{table})$
**Conversions:** All quantities are in SI units, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{p} = 1.20\,\text{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot (1.90 - 0.75)\,\text{m} = 1.20\,\text{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 1.15\,\text{m} \approx 13.5\,\text{J}$
**Answer:** $13.5\,\text{J}$, three significant figures like the mass and the difference in height $1.15\,\text{m}$.
**Reasonableness:** Relative to the floor the potential energy would be $1.20\,\text{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 1.90\,\text{m} \approx 22.4\,\text{J}$. Relative to the table it is smaller, because the book is lower relative to the surface of the table than relative to the floor. The difference $8.8\,\text{J}$ is exactly the share of the height of the table, $0.75\,\text{m}$.

## fy4-7-L2-04
**Given:** mass $m = 1.80\,\text{kg}$, height $h = 40.0\,\text{m}$ above the ground, mechanical energy $E_\text{mech} = 905\,\text{J}$ relative to the ground, $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the speed $v$ of the plane in $\text{m/s}$
**Principle:** Mechanical energy is the sum of kinetic and potential energy. When the potential energy is calculated from the height and subtracted from the mechanical energy, the kinetic energy is left, and the speed is solved from it.
**Formula:** $E_\text{mech} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,mv^2 + m g h$, so $\tfrac{1}{2}\,mv^2 = E_\text{mech} - m g h$ and $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2\,(E_\text{mech} - m g h)}{m}}$
**Conversions:** All quantities are in SI units, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $m g h = 1.80\,\text{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 40.0\,\text{m} \approx 706.3\,\text{J}$, so $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2 \cdot (905 - 706.3)\,\text{J}}{1.80\,\text{kg}}} = \sqrt{220.8\,\text{m}^2/\text{s}^2} \approx 14.9\,\text{m/s}$
**Answer:** $14.9\,\text{m/s}$, three significant figures like the initial data.
**Reasonableness:** Calculated back, $\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 1.80\,\text{kg} \cdot (14.9\,\text{m/s})^2 \approx 200\,\text{J}$ and $200\,\text{J} + 706\,\text{J} \approx 905\,\text{J}$. The speed $15\,\text{m/s} \approx 54\,\text{km/h}$ is normal for a model plane.

## fy4-7-L3-01
**Given:** mass of the lorry $m_2 = 12.0\,\text{t}$ and speed $v_2 = 40.0\,\text{km/h}$, mass of the passenger car $m_1 = 1.20\,\text{t}$
**Find:** the speed $v_1$ of the passenger car in $\text{km/h}$ when the kinetic energies are equal
**Principle:** Kinetic energy is proportional to the mass and the square of the speed. The lighter car has to drive faster, but because the speed is squared, ten times the mass is made up for by only $\sqrt{10}$ times the speed.
**Formula:** $\tfrac{1}{2}\,m_1 v_1^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}\,m_2 v_2^2$, so $v_1^2 = \dfrac{m_2}{m_1}\,v_2^2$ and $v_1 = v_2\sqrt{\dfrac{m_2}{m_1}}$
**Conversions:** The formula has only the ratio of the masses and the speed is multiplied by it, so the tonnes need not be converted to kilograms nor the speed to $\text{m/s}$: the units are preserved.
**Substitution:** $v_1 = 40.0\,\text{km/h} \cdot \sqrt{\dfrac{12.0\,\text{t}}{1.20\,\text{t}}} = 40.0\,\text{km/h} \cdot \sqrt{10.0} \approx 126.5\,\text{km/h}$
**Answer:** $126\,\text{km/h}$, three significant figures like the initial data.
**Reasonableness:** Check in joules: $40.0\,\text{km/h} = 11.11\,\text{m/s}$ and $126.5\,\text{km/h} = 35.14\,\text{m/s}$, so $\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 12\,000\,\text{kg} \cdot (11.11\,\text{m/s})^2 \approx 741\,\text{kJ}$ and $\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 1200\,\text{kg} \cdot (35.14\,\text{m/s})^2 \approx 741\,\text{kJ}$. A lorry at city speed thus carries as much kinetic energy as a passenger car well over the motorway speed.

## fy4-7-L3-02
**Given:** mass $m = 0.500\,\text{kg}$, length of the string $L = 1.20\,\text{m}$, angle of the string to the vertical $\alpha = 40^\circ$, $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$; the zero level is the lowest point of the bob
**Find:** the potential energy $E_\text{p}$ of the bob in joules
**Principle:** For potential energy we need the height of the bob above its lowest point. The bob moves along a circular arc whose radius is the length of the string. Measured from the point of suspension, the bob is at its lowest a distance $L$ below it, and when pulled aside a distance $L\cos\alpha$ below it, so the height is the difference of these.
**Formula:** $h = L - L\cos\alpha = L\,(1 - \cos\alpha)$ and $E_\text{p} = m g h$, so $E_\text{p} = m g L\,(1 - \cos\alpha)$
**Conversions:** All quantities are in SI units, so no conversions are needed. The calculator must be in degrees.
**Substitution:** $h = 1.20\,\text{m} \cdot (1 - \cos 40^\circ) \approx 1.20\,\text{m} \cdot 0.2340 \approx 0.2808\,\text{m}$, so $E_\text{p} = 0.500\,\text{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 0.2808\,\text{m} \approx 1.377\,\text{J}$
**Answer:** $1.38\,\text{J}$, three significant figures like the mass $0.500\,\text{kg}$ and the length of the string $1.20\,\text{m}$.
**Reasonableness:** The height $0.28\,\text{m}$ is clearly smaller than the length of the string, as it should be when the angle is under $90^\circ$; at the angle $90^\circ$ the height would be the whole $1.20\,\text{m}$. With rounded numbers $5\,\text{N} \cdot 0.28\,\text{m} \approx 1.4\,\text{J}$.

## fy4-7-L3-03
**Given:** mass $m = 55.0\,\text{kg}$, speed at the foot of the ramp $v = 7.00\,\text{m/s}$, height at the highest point $h = 2.10\,\text{m}$, where the speed is zero, $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$; the zero level is the foot of the ramp
**Find:** the mechanical energy that changed into other energy, in joules
**Principle:** Without friction and air resistance the mechanical energy would stay the same. Friction and air resistance are not conservative, and they change part of the mechanical energy into internal energy. The amount changed is the decrease in mechanical energy from the foot to the highest point. At the foot there is only kinetic energy ($h = 0$), at the highest point only potential energy ($v = 0$).
**Formula:** $E_\text{other} = E_\text{mech,1} - E_\text{mech,2}$, where $E_\text{mech,1} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,mv^2$ and $E_\text{mech,2} = m g h$. So $E_\text{other} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,mv^2 - m g h$
**Conversions:** All quantities are in SI units, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $\tfrac{1}{2}\,m v^2 = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 55.0\,\text{kg} \cdot (7.00\,\text{m/s})^2 = 1347.5\,\text{J}$ and $m g h = 55.0\,\text{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 2.10\,\text{m} \approx 1133.1\,\text{J}$, so $E_\text{other} = 1347.5\,\text{J} - 1133.1\,\text{J} \approx 214\,\text{J}$
**Answer:** $214\,\text{J}$, three significant figures like the initial data.
**Reasonableness:** Without friction the skater would rise to the height $\tfrac{v^2}{2g} = \tfrac{49\,\text{m}^2/\text{s}^2}{19.62\,\text{m/s}^2} \approx 2.50\,\text{m}$. She rises $2.10\,\text{m}$, about $16\,\%$ less, and the changed energy $214\,\text{J}$ is about $16\,\%$ of the original $1347.5\,\text{J}$. The result is positive, because friction only decreases the mechanical energy.
