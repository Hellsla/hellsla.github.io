## fy2-2-L1-01
**Given:** energy $E = 54\,\text{kJ}$
**Find:** energy $E$ in joules
**Principle:** The prefix kilo (k) means a thousand, so a kilojoule is a thousand joules. The energy stays the same; only the way of writing it changes.
**Formula:** $1\,\text{kJ} = 1000\,\text{J}$, so $E = \text{value} \cdot 1\,\text{kJ} = \text{value} \cdot 1000\,\text{J}$
**Conversions:** The conversion is the task itself: $\text{kJ}$ is replaced with the value $1000\,\text{J}$.
**Substitution:** $E = 54 \cdot 1000\,\text{J} = 54\,000\,\text{J}$
**Answer:** $54\,000\,\text{J}$, two significant figures as in the given value $54\,\text{kJ}$.
**Reasonableness:** The joule is a much smaller unit than the kilojoule, so the number must grow: $54$ is less than $54\,000$. The battery contains tens of thousands of joules, which fits the fact that the energy of a match is about $1000\,\text{J}$.

## fy2-2-L1-02
**Given:** chocolate bar ($200\,\text{g}$) $E_\text{chocolate} = 4.4\,\text{MJ}$ and a day's food $E_\text{food} = 10\,\text{MJ}$
**Find:** number of chocolate bars $N$
**Principle:** Every bar has the same energy, so the number is obtained by dividing the energy of a day's food by the energy of one bar. The energies are in the same unit, so they can be divided directly.
**Formula:** $N \cdot E_\text{chocolate} = E_\text{food}$, so $N = E_\text{food} : E_\text{chocolate}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: both energies are given in megajoules, and the unit cancels.
**Substitution:** $N = 10\,\text{MJ} : 4.4\,\text{MJ} = 2.27$
**Answer:** About $2.3$ chocolate bars, two significant figures as in the data $4.4\,\text{MJ}$ and $10\,\text{MJ}$.
**Reasonableness:** Estimate with round numbers: $10 : 5 = 2$, so a little over two bars is the right order of magnitude. Two bars ($8.8\,\text{MJ}$) fall slightly short of a day's food, and three ($13.2\,\text{MJ}$) go over it.

## fy2-2-L1-03
**Given:** energy $E = 0.14\,\text{TJ}$
**Find:** energy $E$ in gigajoules
**Principle:** The prefix tera (T) is a thousand times giga (G), so one terajoule is a thousand gigajoules. The energy stays the same.
**Formula:** $1\,\text{TJ} = 1000\,\text{GJ}$, so $E = \text{value} \cdot 1000\,\text{GJ}$
**Conversions:** $\text{TJ}$ is replaced with the value $1000\,\text{GJ}$.
**Substitution:** $E = 0.14 \cdot 1000\,\text{GJ} = 140\,\text{GJ}$
**Answer:** $140\,\text{GJ}$, two significant figures as in the given value $0.14\,\text{TJ}$.
**Reasonableness:** The terajoule is a larger unit than the gigajoule, so the number grows. A tank of petrol is about $1.6\,\text{GJ}$, so $140\,\text{GJ} : 1.6\,\text{GJ} \approx 90$, that is, $140\,\text{GJ}$ corresponds to about $90$ tanks. That is the daily output of a small wind farm; a nuclear power plant unit produces about a thousand times more in a day, $140\,\text{TJ}$.

## fy2-2-L1-04
**Given:** chemical energy $E_\text{start} = 12\,\text{MJ}$ and energy transferred to the water $E_\text{water} = 9.0\,\text{MJ}$
**Find:** energy transferred to the air $E_\text{air}$ in megajoules
**Principle:** Energy is conserved, so the chemical energy of the gas is transferred entirely to the water and the air. The chemical energy does not disappear anywhere.
**Formula:** $E_\text{start} = E_\text{water} + E_\text{air}$, so $E_\text{air} = E_\text{start} - E_\text{water}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: both energies are given in megajoules.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{air} = 12\,\text{MJ} - 9.0\,\text{MJ} = 3.0\,\text{MJ} \approx 3\,\text{MJ}$
**Answer:** $3\,\text{MJ}$, to the accuracy of a whole megajoule: in a subtraction the least precise value decides the accuracy, and the energy of the gas, $12\,\text{MJ}$, is given only in whole megajoules.
**Reasonableness:** Most goes to the water, $9.0\,\text{MJ}$ out of twelve. Check: $9.0\,\text{MJ} + 3\,\text{MJ} = 12\,\text{MJ}$, which is the same total as at the start.

## fy2-2-L1-05
**Given:** a day's food $E_\text{food} = 10\,\text{MJ}$ and an apple $E_\text{apple} = 300\,\text{kJ}$
**Find:** number of apples $N$
**Principle:** Every apple has the same energy, so the number is obtained by dividing the energy of a day's food by the energy of one apple, when both are given in the same unit.
**Formula:** $N \cdot E_\text{apple} = E_\text{food}$, so $N = E_\text{food} : E_\text{apple}$
**Conversions:** Replace the prefix mega with its value: $10\,\text{MJ} = 10 \cdot 1000\,\text{kJ} = 10\,000\,\text{kJ}$
**Substitution:** $N = 10\,000\,\text{kJ} : 300\,\text{kJ} = 33.3$
**Answer:** About $33$ apples, two significant figures as in the energy of a day's food, $10\,\text{MJ}$.
**Reasonableness:** Estimate: $300\,\text{kJ}$ is about a third of a megajoule, so one megajoule takes three apples and ten megajoules about $30$. The units cancel, so the result is just a number.

## fy2-2-L2-01
**Given:** chocolate bar ($100\,\text{g}$) $E_1 = 2.2\,\text{MJ}$ and nuts $E_2 = 800\,\text{kJ}$
**Find:** total energy $E$ in megajoules
**Principle:** Energies are added together, because energy is additive. Addition works only in the same unit, so the kilojoules are converted to megajoules.
**Formula:** $E = E_1 + E_2$
**Conversions:** Write the number using a thousand and replace $1000\,\text{kJ}$ with a megajoule: $800\,\text{kJ} = 0.80 \cdot 1000\,\text{kJ} = 0.80\,\text{MJ}$
**Substitution:** $E = 2.2\,\text{MJ} + 0.80\,\text{MJ} = 3.0\,\text{MJ}$
**Answer:** $3.0\,\text{MJ}$, to the accuracy of tenths of a megajoule, because the energy of the chocolate, $2.2\,\text{MJ}$, is given to tenths.
**Reasonableness:** The result is greater than either part. The wrong result $802.2$ would come from adding the numbers of different units. Beside the $10\,\text{MJ}$ of a day's food, $3.0\,\text{MJ}$ is a third, which suits a hiking snack.

## fy2-2-L2-02
**Given:** petrol $V = 40\,\textsf{l}$ and the energy of one litre $E_\text{litre} = 32\,\text{MJ}/\textsf{l}$
**Find:** energy of the tank $E$ in gigajoules
**Principle:** Every litre contains the same energy, so the total energy is the number of litres multiplied by the energy of one litre.
**Formula:** $E = V \cdot E_\text{litre}$
**Conversions:** Finally the megajoules are converted to gigajoules by writing the number using a thousand and replacing $1000\,\text{MJ}$ with a gigajoule.
**Substitution:** $E = 40\,\textsf{l} \cdot 32\,\text{MJ}/\textsf{l} = 1280\,\text{MJ} = 1.28 \cdot 1000\,\text{MJ} = 1.28\,\text{GJ}$
**Answer:** $1.3\,\text{GJ}$, two significant figures as in the data $40\,\textsf{l}$ and $32\,\text{MJ}/\textsf{l}$.
**Reasonableness:** The litres cancel, and the unit of energy remains. Estimate with round numbers: $40 \cdot 30\,\text{MJ} = 1200\,\text{MJ} \approx 1\,\text{GJ}$. The result is of the same order of magnitude as the $1.6\,\text{GJ}$ of a $50$-litre tank.

## fy2-2-L2-03
**Given:** electrical energy $E_\text{electric} = 6.0\,\text{kJ}$, energy turned into light $E_\text{light} = 1.5\,\text{kJ}$; the light is absorbed by the surfaces of the room
**Find:** energy that turned into thermal energy in the room $E_\text{heat}$ in kilojoules
**Principle:** Energy is conserved. The thermal energy of the lamp is $E_\text{electric} - E_\text{light}$, and when the light is absorbed, it also turns into thermal energy. No energy leaves the room as light.
**Formula:** $E_\text{heat} = (E_\text{electric} - E_\text{light}) + E_\text{light} = E_\text{electric}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: all energies are given in kilojoules.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{heat} = (6.0\,\text{kJ} - 1.5\,\text{kJ}) + 1.5\,\text{kJ} = 4.5\,\text{kJ} + 1.5\,\text{kJ} = 6.0\,\text{kJ}$
**Answer:** $6.0\,\text{kJ}$, two significant figures as in the given electrical energy.
**Reasonableness:** All the electrical energy ends up as thermal energy, as at the end of a chain whenever nothing is stored. The light is not stored anywhere but heats the surfaces.

## fy2-2-L2-04
**Given:** a tonne of TNT $E_\text{TNT} = 4.2\,\text{GJ}$ and a chocolate bar ($200\,\text{g}$) $E_\text{chocolate} = 4.4\,\text{MJ}$
**Find:** number of chocolate bars $N$
**Principle:** Every bar has the same energy, so the number is obtained by dividing the energy of a tonne of TNT by the energy of one bar, when both are in the same unit.
**Formula:** $N \cdot E_\text{chocolate} = E_\text{TNT}$, so $N = E_\text{TNT} : E_\text{chocolate}$
**Conversions:** Replace the prefix giga with its value: $4.2\,\text{GJ} = 4.2 \cdot 1000\,\text{MJ} = 4200\,\text{MJ}$
**Substitution:** $N = 4200\,\text{MJ} : 4.4\,\text{MJ} = 954.5$
**Answer:** About $950$ chocolate bars, two significant figures as in the data $4.2\,\text{GJ}$ and $4.4\,\text{MJ}$.
**Reasonableness:** A gigajoule is a thousand megajoules, and the energy of a bar is roughly the same number as the gigajoules of a tonne of TNT ($4.4$ and $4.2$), so the ratio is a little under a thousand. An explosive does not contain an especially large amount of energy, but it releases it very quickly.

## fy2-2-L3-01
**Given:** electricity consumption of Finland $E_\text{Finland} = 790\,\text{TJ}$ per day and the output of one unit $E_\text{unit} = 140\,\text{TJ}$ per day
**Find:** the smallest number of units $N$, as a whole number
**Principle:** Identical units together produce the number of units times the energy of one unit. The output must be at least as large as the consumption, so the result of the division is rounded up to the next whole number: less than a whole unit is not enough.
**Formula:** At the limit the output equals the consumption, $N \cdot E_\text{unit} = E_\text{Finland}$, so $N = E_\text{Finland} : E_\text{unit}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: both energies are given in terajoules, and the unit cancels.
**Substitution:** $N = 790\,\text{TJ} : 140\,\text{TJ} = 5.64$, so at least the next whole number of units is needed.
**Answer:** At least $6$ units. Five units would produce only $5 \cdot 140\,\text{TJ} = 700\,\text{TJ}$, which is less than the consumption; six produce $6 \cdot 140\,\text{TJ} = 840\,\text{TJ}$. The number is a whole number, so rounding to significant figures does not apply to it.
**Reasonableness:** Estimate: $800 : 150 \approx 5.3$, so a little over five are needed, that is, six units. One Olkiluoto 3 therefore covers about a sixth of Finland's electricity, which is a realistic share for one large power plant unit.

## fy2-2-L3-02
**Given:** energy released in the reactor $E_\text{reactor} = 370\,\text{TJ}$ and electricity $E_\text{electric} = 140\,\text{TJ}$ per day; heating a detached house for a year $E_\text{house} = 72\,\text{GJ}$ (an example)
**Find:** number of detached houses $N$
**Principle:** First conservation of energy: the energy of the reactor is divided into electricity and the heat that goes to the sea, so the energy going to the sea is their difference. Then the comparison: the number is obtained by dividing the energy going to the sea by the yearly heating energy of one house, in the same unit.
**Formula:** $E_\text{reactor} = E_\text{electric} + E_\text{sea}$, so $E_\text{sea} = E_\text{reactor} - E_\text{electric}$, and $N = E_\text{sea} : E_\text{house}$
**Conversions:** Replace the prefix tera with its value: $230\,\text{TJ} = 230 \cdot 1000\,\text{GJ} = 230\,000\,\text{GJ}$
**Substitution:** $E_\text{sea} = 370\,\text{TJ} - 140\,\text{TJ} = 230\,\text{TJ} = 230\,000\,\text{GJ}$, and $N = 230\,000\,\text{GJ} : 72\,\text{GJ} = 3194$
**Answer:** About $3200$ detached houses, two significant figures as in the data $72\,\text{GJ}$, $140\,\text{TJ}$ and $370\,\text{TJ}$.
**Reasonableness:** More energy goes to the sea than into electricity, as is usual in power plants that run on heat: $140\,\text{TJ} + 230\,\text{TJ} = 370\,\text{TJ}$. Estimate: $200\,000 : 70 \approx 3000$, so thousands of houses is the right order of magnitude. This amount of energy therefore corresponds to heating a small town for a year, but the heat going to the sea is too cool for district heating; in combined heat and power production the steam is condensed at a higher temperature, so the heat can be recovered and slightly less electricity is produced.

## fy2-2-L3-03
**Given:** energy of the reactor per day $E = 370\,\text{TJ}$, $4.5\,\text{kg}$ of uranium-235 and the energy of petrol per litre $E_\text{litre} = 32\,\text{MJ}/\textsf{l}$
**Find:** volume of petrol $V$ in litres and a comparison with the amount of uranium
**Principle:** Every litre of petrol gives the same energy, so the number of litres that gives the same energy is obtained by dividing the total energy by the energy of one litre. The energies must first be given in the same unit.
**Formula:** $E = V \cdot E_\text{litre}$, so $V = E : E_\text{litre}$
**Conversions:** Replace the prefixes with their values: $370\,\text{TJ} = 370 \cdot 1000\,\text{GJ} = 370 \cdot 1000 \cdot 1000\,\text{MJ} = 3.7 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{MJ}$
**Substitution:** $V = 3.7 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{MJ} : 32\,\text{MJ}/\textsf{l} = 1.16 \cdot 10^{7}\,\textsf{l}$
**Answer:** About $1.2 \cdot 10^{7}\,\textsf{l}$, that is, about $12$ million litres of petrol, two significant figures as in the data $370\,\text{TJ}$ and $32\,\text{MJ}/\textsf{l}$. Comparison: only about $4.5\,\text{kg}$ of uranium-235 gives the same energy in a day, which fits in the palm of a hand, whereas the petrol would fill hundreds of tanker lorries.
**Reasonableness:** Estimate: $400\,000\,000 : 40 = 10\,000\,000$ litres, so about ten million litres is the right order of magnitude. The litres remain in the result, because the megajoules cancel. The difference from uranium is huge, which fits the fact that much more nuclear energy is released from the same amount of matter than in burning.
