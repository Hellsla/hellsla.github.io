## fy2-5-L1-01
**Given:** petrol, mass $m = 2.0\,\text{kg}$ and heat of combustion $H = 43\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}}$
**Find:** the energy $E$ released in burning, in megajoules
**Principle:** The chemical energy of petrol is released when it burns. The heat of combustion gives the energy released per kilogram, so the energy is the product of the heat of combustion and the mass.
**Formula:** $E = Hm$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the mass is already in kilograms and the answer is wanted in megajoules, like the heat of combustion.
**Substitution:** $E = 43\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}} \cdot 2.0\,\text{kg} = 86\,\text{MJ}$
**Answer:** $86\,\text{MJ}$, two significant figures like the mass $2.0\,\text{kg}$.
**Reasonableness:** One kilogram gives $43\,\text{MJ}$, so two kilograms give twice as much, about $2 \cdot 40\,\text{MJ} = 80\,\text{MJ}$. The result is of the right order of magnitude and positive.

## fy2-5-L1-02
**Given:** natural gas, mass $m = 2.0\,\text{kg}$ and heat of combustion $H = 48\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}}$
**Find:** the energy $E$ released, in megajoules
**Principle:** When the gas burns, its chemical energy is transformed into heat. The energy released is proportional to the mass, so we use the definition of the heat of combustion.
**Formula:** $E = Hm$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the mass is in kilograms.
**Substitution:** $E = 48\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}} \cdot 2.0\,\text{kg} = 96\,\text{MJ}$
**Answer:** $96\,\text{MJ}$, two significant figures like the mass $2.0\,\text{kg}$.
**Reasonableness:** The heat of combustion of gas is about $50\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}}$, so two kilograms give about $100\,\text{MJ}$. The result is slightly smaller, because $48 < 50$.

## fy2-5-L2-01
**Given:** according to the label $1200\,\text{kJ}$ per hundred grams, mass of the slice $m = 40\,\text{g}$
**Find:** the energy $E$ of the slice, in kilojoules
**Principle:** The label gives the heat of combustion of the food in the same way as for a fuel: energy per unit of mass. It must first be converted to megajoules per kilogram, and then the law $E = Hm$ is used.
**Formula:** $E = Hm$, where the heat of combustion $H$ is the energy on the label divided by the mass on the label
**Conversions:** $1200\,\text{kJ} = 1200 \cdot (1 : 1000)\,\text{MJ} = 1.2\,\text{MJ}$, $100\,\text{g} = 100 \cdot (1 : 1000)\,\text{kg} = 0.100\,\text{kg}$ and $40\,\text{g} = 40 \cdot (1 : 1000)\,\text{kg} = 0.040\,\text{kg}$.
**Substitution:** $H = 1.2\,\text{MJ} : 0.100\,\text{kg} = 12\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}}$ and $E = 12\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}} \cdot 0.040\,\text{kg} = 0.48\,\text{MJ} = 480\,\text{kJ}$
**Answer:** $480\,\text{kJ}$, two significant figures like the mass $40\,\text{g}$.
**Reasonableness:** The slice is $40\,\%$ of a hundred grams, and $40\,\%$ of $1200\,\text{kJ}$ is $0.4 \cdot 1200\,\text{kJ} = 480\,\text{kJ}$. The result is smaller than $1200\,\text{kJ}$, as it should be.

## fy2-5-L2-02
**Given:** the energy released must be $E = 1.8\,\text{GJ}$, heat of combustion of diesel $H = 43\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}}$
**Find:** the mass $m$ of diesel to be burnt, in kilograms
**Principle:** By the law $E = Hm$ the energy is the product of the heat of combustion and the mass. Now the energy is known and the mass is wanted, so the law is solved for the mass.
**Formula:** $E = Hm$, so $m = \dfrac{E}{H}$
**Conversions:** $1.8\,\text{GJ} = 1.8 \cdot 1000\,\text{MJ} = 1800\,\text{MJ}$.
**Substitution:** $m = 1800\,\text{MJ} : 43\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}} = 41.86\,\text{kg}$
**Answer:** $42\,\text{kg}$, two significant figures like the data $1.8\,\text{GJ}$ and $43\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}}$.
**Reasonableness:** One kilogram gives about $43\,\text{MJ}$, so $42\,\text{kg}$ gives about $42 \cdot 43\,\text{MJ} \approx 1800\,\text{MJ}$, which is $1.8\,\text{GJ}$.

## fy2-5-L2-03
**Given:** wood pellets, mass $m = 40\,\text{kg}$ and heat of combustion $H = 17\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}}$; efficiency of the boiler $\eta = 85\,\% = 0.85$
**Find:** the useful energy $E_\text{useful}$ in megajoules
**Principle:** The pellets release the energy $E_\text{in} = Hm$. Efficiency is the share of the energy taken in that is obtained as useful energy (chapter *Efficiency*), so only part of the released energy ends up heating the house.
**Formula:** $E_\text{in} = Hm$ and $\eta = \dfrac{E_\text{useful}}{E_\text{in}}$, so $E_\text{useful} = \eta\, E_\text{in}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the mass is in kilograms and $85\,\% = 0.85$.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{in} = 17\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}} \cdot 40\,\text{kg} = 680\,\text{MJ}$ and $E_\text{useful} = 0.85 \cdot 680\,\text{MJ} = 578\,\text{MJ}$
**Answer:** $580\,\text{MJ}$, two significant figures like the data $40\,\text{kg}$ and $85\,\%$.
**Reasonableness:** The useful energy is smaller than the $680\,\text{MJ}$ released, because part of the heat leaves with the flue gases. Estimate: $0.85 \approx 0.9$ and $0.9 \cdot 680\,\text{MJ} \approx 610\,\text{MJ}$, so $578\,\text{MJ}$ is of the right order of magnitude.

## fy2-5-L2-04
**Given:** useful energy $E_\text{useful} = 48\,\text{MJ}$, efficiency $\eta = 25\,\% = 0.25$, heat of combustion of petrol $H = 43\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}}$
**Find:** the mass $m$ of petrol, in kilograms
**Principle:** The engine transforms only part of the energy of the petrol into motion (chapter *Efficiency*). First we use the efficiency to find the energy needed from the petrol, and then the law $E = Hm$ to find the mass.
**Formula:** $\eta = \dfrac{E_\text{useful}}{E_\text{in}}$, so $E_\text{in} = \dfrac{E_\text{useful}}{\eta}$; $E_\text{in} = Hm$, so $m = \dfrac{E_\text{in}}{H}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: $25\,\% = 0.25$ and the energies are in megajoules.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{in} = 48\,\text{MJ} : 0.25 = 192\,\text{MJ}$ and $m = 192\,\text{MJ} : 43\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}} = 4.465\,\text{kg}$
**Answer:** $4.5\,\text{kg}$, two significant figures like the data $48\,\text{MJ}$ and $25\,\%$.
**Reasonableness:** The density of petrol is about $0.74\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\textsf{l}}$, so $4.5\,\text{kg}$ is about $6\,\textsf{l}$, a typical consumption per hundred kilometres. The energy of the petrol, $192\,\text{MJ}$, is four times the useful energy, because the efficiency is one quarter.

## fy2-5-L3-01
**Given:** the energy needed $E = 120\,\text{MJ}$, air-dried firewood $H_\text{wood} = 15\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}}$ and heating oil $H_\text{oil} = 43\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}}$
**Find:** how many kilograms more wood than oil are needed, $\Delta m = m_\text{wood} - m_\text{oil}$
**Principle:** The same energy $E$ must be released from both fuels. By the law $E = Hm$ the mass is the energy divided by the heat of combustion, so a smaller heat of combustion needs a larger mass.
**Formula:** $E = Hm$, so $m = \dfrac{E}{H}$ for each fuel, and $\Delta m = m_\text{wood} - m_\text{oil}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the energy is in megajoules and the heats of combustion in megajoules per kilogram.
**Substitution:** $m_\text{wood} = 120\,\text{MJ} : 15\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}} = 8.0\,\text{kg}$ and $m_\text{oil} = 120\,\text{MJ} : 43\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}} = 2.79\,\text{kg}$. Difference: $\Delta m = 8.0\,\text{kg} - 2.79\,\text{kg} = 5.21\,\text{kg}$.
**Answer:** $5.2\,\text{kg}$ more wood is needed, two significant figures like the data $120\,\text{MJ}$ and $15\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}}$. More wood is needed because one kilogram of wood contains less chemical energy than one kilogram of oil.
**Reasonableness:** The mass of wood is about $2.9$ times the mass of oil, since $43 : 15 = 2.87$. So $2.87 \cdot 2.79\,\text{kg} \approx 8.0\,\text{kg}$, and the difference is $2.87 - 1 = 1.87$ times the mass of oil: $1.87 \cdot 2.79\,\text{kg} \approx 5.2\,\text{kg}$, as it should be.

## fy2-5-L3-02
**Given:** energy content of the food $H = 18\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}}$, mass $m = 2.4\,\text{kg}$, daily consumption $12\,\text{MJ}$
**Find:** the number of daily rations $N$ that the food provides (as a decimal number)
**Principle:** The total energy in the food is given by the law $E = Hm$. The number of rations is how many times the daily consumption fits into the total energy, so $N = E : \text{daily consumption}$.
**Formula:** $E = Hm$ and the number of days $N = \dfrac{E}{12\,\text{MJ}}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the mass is in kilograms and the energy in megajoules.
**Substitution:** $E = 18\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}} \cdot 2.4\,\text{kg} = 43.2\,\text{MJ}$ and $N = 43.2\,\text{MJ} : 12\,\text{MJ} = 3.6$
**Answer:** $3.6$ daily rations, two significant figures like the data $2.4\,\text{kg}$ and $18\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}}$.
**Reasonableness:** The need for three rations is $36\,\text{MJ}$ and for four $48\,\text{MJ}$. There is $43.2\,\text{MJ}$ of food, so the result lies between three and four: three full days and part of a fourth.

## fy2-5-L3-03
**Given:** peat $m = 36\,\text{t}$ per hour, $H = 10\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}}$, efficiency of electricity production $\eta = 35\,\% = 0.35$, time $t = 1\,\text{h}$
**Find:** the electric power $P$ in megawatts
**Principle:** In one hour the peat releases the energy $E_\text{in} = Hm$. By the efficiency, only the share $\eta$ of it becomes electricity (chapter *Efficiency*). Power is energy per unit of time, so power = energy : time (chapter *Power P = ΔE/Δt*).
**Formula:** $E_\text{in} = Hm$, $E_\text{useful} = \eta\, E_\text{in}$ and $P = \dfrac{E_\text{useful}}{t}$, together $P = \dfrac{\eta H m}{t}$
**Conversions:** $36\,\text{t} = 36 \cdot 1000\,\text{kg} = 36\,000\,\text{kg}$ and $1\,\text{h} = 3600\,\text{s}$. Since $1\,\text{MJ/s} = 1\,\text{MW}$, the unit of power comes out directly in megawatts.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{in} = 10\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}} \cdot 36\,000\,\text{kg} = 360\,000\,\text{MJ}$, $E_\text{useful} = 0.35 \cdot 360\,000\,\text{MJ} = 126\,000\,\text{MJ}$ and $P = 126\,000\,\text{MJ} : 3600\,\text{s} = 35\,\text{MJ/s} = 35\,\text{MW}$
**Answer:** $35\,\text{MW}$, two significant figures like the data $10\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}}$ and $35\,\%$.
**Reasonableness:** If all the energy of the peat became electricity, the power would be $360\,000\,\text{MJ} : 3600\,\text{s} = 100\,\text{MW}$. With an efficiency of $35\,\%$ we get $35\,\%$ of that, $35\,\text{MW}$, which is a typical power for a small power plant.
