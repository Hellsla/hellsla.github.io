## Lesson plan (75 minutes)

- **$0$–$5\,\text{min}$, Think about it.** Read the opening situation aloud. Pairs decide whose method gives the density and write a one-sentence justification. Do not give the answer yet: the last check task of the lesson comes back to the same question.
- **$5$–$15\,\text{min}$, Predict (starting-point test).** Six questions alone, without notes. Ask the students to note which questions they got wrong; the feedback on each says which chapter the topic belongs to.
- **$15$–$25\,\text{min}$, Investigate.** Pair work: pressure and temperature of a gas. One student draws the line in a notebook or a graphing program, the other reads the slope and the constant term and explains their meaning. Discuss together why extrapolating by three hundred degrees is only a prediction of the model.
- **$25$–$35\,\text{min}$, Theory summary.** Go through the chain of steps and the parts of the graph. Stop at linearisation: ask what should go on the axes if one quantity is inversely proportional to another.
- **$35$–$57\,\text{min}$, Apply.** Four multiple-choice tasks alone or in pairs, then four calculations alone. If time runs short, leave *A stack of coins* and *A ball in a channel* out of the lesson; they are done at home.
- **$57$–$70\,\text{min}$, Check your understanding.** Three checks alone and the exit ticket. Allow about five minutes for the explanation task.
- **$70$–$75\,\text{min}$, Exercises.** Go through the data of the problem *An unknown liquid* together: what goes on the axes, where the vertical axis should start and why the row $60\,\text{ml}$ is suspicious. Drawing the graph and the fit (parts a and b, about $15\,\text{min}$) are done at home on paper. The other problems are homework: *Hand timing on sports day* and *Thickness and density of copy paper* first, then *Checking a kitchen scale*, *Speed of a toy car from a video* and *Air in a syringe*.

## Using the starting-point test
- Starting points 1 and 2 review precision and the types of error, 3, 4 and 6 the graph and the interpretation of a line, and 5 linearisation. If more than a third of the group answers the same question wrongly, go through the topic on the board before the Apply stage.
- Encourage everyone to note the chapters of the questions they got wrong and to start the homework with the problems whose *Skills from the chapters* line lists those chapters.

## Pair work
- In the Investigate stage and in the graph of the problem *An unknown liquid*, one student draws and the other checks: the quantities and units of the axes, an evenly spaced scale, no broken line, a line among the points.
- In the explanation task of the Check your understanding stage, pairs can read each other's answers against the criteria before the model answer.

## Fast and slow
- **Fast** students do the problem *Air in a syringe* in class, which combines measuring with the ideal gas law, or draw the graph of the problem *An unknown liquid* with a spreadsheet program and compare the program's fit with the line drawn by hand.
- **Slow** students do at least the tasks *Find the error: the form of the result*, *The line of a spring* and *Read from the equation of the line* of the Apply stage and, if needed, review using the *Review if needed* links of the theory summary. Of the long problems they start with *Thickness and density of copy paper*, whose steps are short.

## Assessment
- The points of the long problems are the student's own estimate based on the model answer and the marking scheme. If you wish, collect one problem on paper and compare the student's points with your own assessment: the axes of the graph, the fit and the rounding of the result in particular are often marked too leniently.
- In the graphing problems, all justified lines drawn by eye are accepted; the model answers give the accepted ranges (*Also accepted*).

## Notes
- The data of the lesson are invented examples with small variation added; the source note of the problems says so too.
- The graph of the Investigate stage is familiar from an exercise of the chapter *Thermodynamic system, state variables and temperature*. In the review the emphasis is on interpreting the line and on the validity of the extrapolation.
- The ICT guides *Graph of measurement points*, *Fitting a straight line to measurement points*, *Interpreting the slope and the constant term* and *Uncertainty of the slope* (in Finnish) are a good support if the students draw the graphs with a program.
