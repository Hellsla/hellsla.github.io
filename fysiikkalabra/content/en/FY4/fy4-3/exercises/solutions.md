## fy4-3-L1-01
**Given:** weight of the lamp $G = 18\,\text{N}$, the lamp is at rest
**Find:** the tension of the cord $T$ in $\text{N}$
**Principle:** The lamp is at rest, so by Newton's first law the sum of the forces acting on it is zero. Only the Earth and the cord act on the lamp.
**Force diagram:** The lamp as a rectangle, positive direction up. The weight $\overline{G}$ down from the centre and the tension of the cord $\overline{T}$ up from the top surface, equally long.
```voimakuvio
G 18
T 18 ylös
a 0
```
**Formula:** $T - G = 0$, so $T = G$
**Conversions:** The weight is in newtons, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $T = G = 18\,\text{N}$
**Answer:** $18\,\text{N}$, two significant figures like the weight $18\,\text{N}$.
**Reasonableness:** If the cord pulled with less than $18\,\text{N}$, the weight would win and the lamp would start to move downwards; if with more, upwards. Staying at rest requires a force exactly as large as the weight. The cord acts on the lamp upwards, because a cord can only pull.

## fy4-3-L1-02
**Given:** force of team A on the rope $F_A = 850\,\text{N}$ to the left, the rope is at rest, the weight of the rope is ignored
**Find:** the force of team B on the rope $F_B$ in $\text{N}$
**Principle:** The rope is at rest, so by Newton's first law the sum of the forces acting on it is zero. In the horizontal direction only the pulls of the teams act on the rope.
**Force diagram:** The rope as a long rectangle, positive direction to the right. The pull of team A $\overline{F}_A$ from the left end of the rope to the left and the pull of team B $\overline{F}_B$ from the right end to the right.
```voimakuvio
F_A 850 vasen
F_B 850 oikea
a 0
```
**Formula:** $F_B - F_A = 0$, so $F_B = F_A$
**Conversions:** The force is in newtons, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $F_B = F_A = 850\,\text{N}$
**Answer:** $850\,\text{N}$, three significant figures like the force $850\,\text{N}$.
**Reasonableness:** If B pulled more weakly, the rope would start to move to the left. A tug of war is won by the team that gets a larger friction force from the ground on its feet, not by the team that pulls the rope harder: the rope pulls both teams with an equally large force.

## fy4-3-L1-03
**Given:** force of the skater on the board $F_{\text{SB}} = 90\,\text{N}$ towards the board
**Find:** the force of the board on the skater $F_{\text{BS}}$ in $\text{N}$
**Principle:** Newton's third law: the skater and the board interact, so the board acts on the skater with an equally large but oppositely directed force. The law holds regardless of whether either of them moves.
**Force diagram:** Choose the skater as the object and draw the skater as a rectangle; the positive direction is away from the board. The skater's weight acts down, the normal force of the ice up and the force of the board $\overline{F}_{\text{BS}}$ away from the board, ending at the skater's side. The skater's own force on the board $\overline{F}_{\text{SB}}$ acts on the board, so it does not belong in the skater's diagram but is the reaction force of the force asked for.
```voimakuvio
pinta 0
G 590
N 590
F_KL 90 oikea työntö
a oikea
```
**Formula:** $\overline{F}_{\text{BS}} = -\overline{F}_{\text{SB}}$, so in magnitudes $F_{\text{BS}} = F_{\text{SB}}$
**Conversions:** The force is in newtons, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $F_{\text{BS}} = F_{\text{SB}} = 90\,\text{N}$
**Answer:** $90\,\text{N}$ on the skater, directed away from the board, two significant figures like the force $90\,\text{N}$.
**Reasonableness:** The skater starts to glide away from the board, so a force away from the board has acted on the skater. Its cause is the board. The board does not move, because it is fixed to the ice and the structures of the rink, which act on it with their own forces.

## fy4-3-L1-04
**Given:** force of the road on the tyres forwards $F = 620\,\text{N}$, air resistance $F_\text{a} = 380\,\text{N}$, the velocity is constant and the road is straight
**Find:** the rolling resistance $F_\text{r}$ in $\text{N}$
**Principle:** The car moves in a straight line at constant velocity, so by Newton's first law the net force is zero also in the direction of motion: the forces acting forwards and backwards are equally large.
**Force diagram:** The car as a rectangle, positive direction forwards. In the horizontal direction the force of the road $\overline{F}$ forwards, and the air resistance $\overline{F}_\text{a}$ and rolling resistance $\overline{F}_\text{r}$ backwards. In the vertical direction the weight and the normal force of the road cancel each other.
```voimakuvio
pinta 0
G 900
N 900
F 620 oikea
F_i 380 vasen
F_r 240 vasen
a 0
```
**Formula:** $F - F_\text{a} - F_\text{r} = 0$, so $F_\text{r} = F - F_\text{a}$
**Conversions:** The forces are in newtons, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $F_\text{r} = 620\,\text{N} - 380\,\text{N} = 240\,\text{N}$
**Answer:** $240\,\text{N}$, to the nearest ten newtons like the given forces, so two significant figures.
**Reasonableness:** The sum of the resistances $380\,\text{N} + 240\,\text{N} = 620\,\text{N}$ is as large as the force driving forwards, so the velocity does not change. The rolling resistance is smaller than the air resistance, which fits motorway speeds, at which air resistance is the largest resistance on a car.

## fy4-3-L1-05
**Given:** mass of the book $m = 0.65\,\text{kg}$, the force of the Earth on the book is the weight of the book; $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the force of the book on the Earth $F$ in $\text{N}$
**Principle:** The Earth and the book pull each other. By Newton's third law the book pulls the Earth with an equally large force as the Earth pulls the book, but in the opposite direction, that is, upwards. The force of the Earth on the book is the weight $G = mg$.
**Force diagram:** Choose the book as the object and draw it as a rectangle; the positive direction is up. The weight $\overline{G}$ acts down from the centre and the normal force of the table $\overline{N}$ up from the bottom surface. The weight is the force of the Earth on the book, and its reaction force is the force $\overline{F}$ of the book on the Earth, which acts on the Earth and does not belong in the diagram of the book.
```voimakuvio
pinta 0
G 6,3765
N 6,3765
a 0
```
**Formula:** $F = G$ and $G = mg$, so $F = mg$
**Conversions:** The mass is in kilograms, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $F = 0.65\,\text{kg}\cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 = 6.3765\,\text{N}$
**Answer:** $6.4\,\text{N}$ upwards towards the book, two significant figures like the mass $0.65\,\text{kg}$.
**Reasonableness:** The weight of a kilogram is about $10\,\text{N}$, so the weight of a book of $0.65\,\text{kg}$ is about $6\,\text{N}$. The Earth does not noticeably move because of this force, since its mass is enormous. The normal force of the table is not the reaction force of the weight, but belongs to the interaction between the book and the table.

## fy4-3-L2-01
**Given:** weight of the lower book $G_1 = 8.0\,\text{N}$, weight of the upper book $G_2 = 5.0\,\text{N}$, the books are at rest
**Find:** the force of the lower book on the table $F$ in $\text{N}$
**Principle:** The upper book is at rest, so the lower book supports it with a force $G_2$ (first law), and by the third law the upper book presses the lower one down with an equally large force. The lower book is also at rest, so the normal force of the table on it is as large as the downward forces together. Finally, the lower book presses the table with a force as large as the normal force (third law).
**Force diagram:** The lower book as a rectangle, positive direction up. The weight $\overline{G}_1$ down from the centre, the force of the upper book $\overline{F}_2$ down from the top surface and the normal force of the table $\overline{N}$ up from the bottom surface.
```voimakuvio
pinta 0
G_1 8,0
F_2 5,0 alas työntö
N 13
a 0
```
**Formula:** $N - G_1 - F_2 = 0$ and $F_2 = G_2$, so $F = N = G_1 + G_2$
**Conversions:** The forces are in newtons, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $F = 8.0\,\text{N} + 5.0\,\text{N} = 13\,\text{N}$
**Answer:** $13\,\text{N}$ downwards. The sum is to the nearest tenth of a newton like the weights $8.0\,\text{N}$ and $5.0\,\text{N}$, so it has two significant figures.
**Reasonableness:** The table supports both books, so it must support their combined weight. The upper book does not touch the table, however: its weight is transmitted to the table through the lower book as a chain of force pairs.

## fy4-3-L2-02
**Given:** force of the swimmer on the water $F_{\text{SW}} = 65\,\text{N}$ backwards, weight of the swimmer $720\,\text{N}$ and an equally large buoyancy of the water, the swimmer swims straight in the horizontal direction at constant velocity
**Find:** the drag of the water on the swimmer $F_\text{d}$ in $\text{N}$
**Principle:** By the third law the water pushes the swimmer forwards with an equally large force $F_{\text{WS}} = 65\,\text{N}$; this force moves the swimmer forwards. The swimmer moves at constant velocity, so by the first law the sum of the horizontal forces is zero. The weight and the buoyancy are vertical and do not affect the horizontal equilibrium, so they are not needed: the drag of the water backwards is as large as the push of the water forwards.
**Force diagram:** The swimmer as a rectangle, positive direction forwards. The push of the water $\overline{F}_{\text{WS}}$ forwards and the drag of the water $\overline{F}_\text{d}$ backwards. In the vertical direction the buoyancy and the weight cancel each other.
```voimakuvio
G 720
F_n 720 ylös
F_VU 65 oikea
F_v 65 vasen
a 0
```
**Formula:** $F_{\text{WS}} - F_\text{d} = 0$ and $F_{\text{WS}} = F_{\text{SW}}$, so $F_\text{d} = F_{\text{SW}}$
**Conversions:** The force is in newtons, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $F_\text{d} = F_{\text{SW}} = 65\,\text{N}$
**Answer:** $65\,\text{N}$ backwards, two significant figures like the force $65\,\text{N}$.
**Reasonableness:** The swimmer moves forwards only because the water pushes the swimmer; without a reaction force, pulling with the hands would not move the swimmer anywhere. If the swimmer pushed the water harder, the forward force would increase and she would accelerate until the drag grew equally large.

## fy4-3-L2-03
**Given:** mass with equipment $m = 85\,\text{kg}$, the velocity is constant and straight down; $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the air resistance $F_\text{d}$ in $\text{N}$
**Principle:** The skydiver descends in a straight line at constant velocity, so by Newton's first law the net force is zero. The Earth (weight, down) and the air (resistance, up, against the motion) act on the skydiver.
**Force diagram:** The skydiver and the parachute as a rectangle, positive direction up. The weight $\overline{G}$ down from the centre and the air resistance $\overline{F}_\text{d}$ up, equally long. The velocity, directed down, is drawn separately and is not a force.
```voimakuvio
G 833,85
F_v 833,85 ylös
a 0
v alas
```
**Formula:** $F_\text{d} - G = 0$ and $G = mg$, so $F_\text{d} = mg$
**Conversions:** The mass is in kilograms, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $F_\text{d} = 85\,\text{kg}\cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 = 833.85\,\text{N}$
**Answer:** $830\,\text{N}$, or $8.3\cdot 10^{2}\,\text{N}$, upwards, two significant figures like the mass $85\,\text{kg}$.
**Reasonableness:** The weight of $85\,\text{kg}$ is about $850\,\text{N}$, and the air resistance is equally large. Motion downwards does not require a net force downwards: the skydiver moves down because of inertia, and the forces are in balance.

## fy4-3-L2-04
**Given:** weight of the car $G_\text{c} = 5.8\,\text{kN}$, mass of the passengers $m = 320\,\text{kg}$, the elevator rises at constant velocity; $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the tension of the cable $T$ in $\text{kN}$
**Principle:** The car and the passengers move together at constant velocity, so by the first law the sum of the forces acting on them is zero. When the car and the passengers are thought of as one object, the cable supports their combined weight.
**Force diagram:** The car with the passengers as a rectangle, positive direction up. The tension of the cable $\overline{T}$ up from the top surface, and the weight of the car $\overline{G}_\text{c}$ and the weight of the passengers $\overline{G}_\text{p}$ down.
```voimakuvio
T 8939,2 ylös
G_k 5800
G_m 3139,2
a 0
```
**Formula:** $T - G_\text{c} - mg = 0$, so $T = G_\text{c} + mg$
**Conversions:** $5.8\,\text{kN} = 5800\,\text{N}$; at the end the newtons are converted to kilonewtons by dividing by $1000$.
**Substitution:** $T = 5800\,\text{N} + 320\,\text{kg}\cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 = 5800\,\text{N} + 3139.2\,\text{N} = 8939.2\,\text{N}$
**Answer:** $8.9\,\text{kN}$, two significant figures like the weight of the car $5.8\,\text{kN}$.
**Reasonableness:** The weight of the passengers is about $3\,\text{kN}$, and $5.8\,\text{kN} + 3\,\text{kN} \approx 9\,\text{kN}$. In steady rising the tension is exactly the same as in an elevator standing still, because the velocity does not change.

## fy4-3-L3-01
**Given:** combined weight $G = 4.62\,\text{kN}$, buoyancy $F_\text{b} = 4.35\,\text{kN}$, the balloon descends straight down at constant velocity
**Find:** the air resistance $F_\text{d}$ in $\text{kN}$ and its direction
**Principle:** The balloon moves at constant velocity, so by the first law the net force is zero. Air resistance opposes the motion, and the balloon moves down, so the resistance points up. The buoyancy alone is not enough to cancel the weight, so the air resistance supports the rest.
**Force diagram:** The balloon with its basket as a rectangle, positive direction up. The weight $\overline{G}$ down from the centre, the buoyancy $\overline{F}_\text{b}$ up and the air resistance $\overline{F}_\text{d}$ up. The velocity down is drawn separately and is not a force.
```voimakuvio
G 4620
F_n 4350 ylös
F_v 270 ylös
a 0
v alas
```
**Formula:** $F_\text{b} + F_\text{d} - G = 0$, so $F_\text{d} = G - F_\text{b}$
**Conversions:** All forces are in kilonewtons and the answer is asked in kilonewtons, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $F_\text{d} = 4.62\,\text{kN} - 4.35\,\text{kN} = 0.27\,\text{kN}$
**Answer:** $0.27\,\text{kN}$ upwards. The difference has two significant figures, because the given forces are to the nearest hundredth of a kilonewton.
**Reasonableness:** If there were no air resistance, the difference of $0.27\,\text{kN}$ between the weight and the buoyancy, directed down, would change the motion of the balloon and it would descend faster and faster. Steady descent means that the air resistance has grown exactly as large as this difference. The air resistance points up, although the motion is down.

## fy4-3-L3-02
**Given:** weight of each magnet $G = 0.30\,\text{N}$, the upper magnet floats at rest, friction and the force of the stick are ignored
**Find:** the normal force of the table on the lower magnet $N$ in $\text{N}$
**Principle:** The upper magnet is at rest, so the repulsion of the lower magnet on it points up and is as large as its weight (first law). By the third law the upper magnet repels the lower one downwards with an equally large force, although the magnets do not touch each other. The lower magnet is at rest, so the normal force of the table cancels its weight and this force.
**Force diagram:** The lower magnet as a rectangle, positive direction up. The weight $\overline{G}$ down from the centre, the force of the upper magnet $\overline{F}_\text{m}$ down from the top surface and the normal force of the table $\overline{N}$ up from the bottom surface.
```voimakuvio
pinta 0
G 0,30
F_m 0,30 alas työntö
N 0,60
a 0
```
**Formula:** $N - G - F_\text{m} = 0$ and $F_\text{m} = G$, so $N = 2G$
**Conversions:** The forces are in newtons, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $N = 2\cdot 0.30\,\text{N} = 0.60\,\text{N}$
**Answer:** $0.60\,\text{N}$, two significant figures like the weight $0.30\,\text{N}$.
**Reasonableness:** The table carries the weight of both magnets, just as if the upper magnet were resting on the lower one. A floating magnet is not weightless: its weight is transmitted to the table through a magnetic pair of forces.

## fy4-3-L3-03
**Given:** weight of the apple $G_\text{a} = 1.5\,\text{N}$, weight of the lower scale $G_\text{s} = 0.40\,\text{N}$, everything is at rest
**Find:** the reading of the upper scale, that is, the force $F$ with which the lower scale pulls the hook of the upper one, in $\text{N}$
**Principle:** The apple is at rest, so the hook of the lower scale pulls it up with a force of $1.5\,\text{N}$ (first law), and by the third law the apple pulls the hook down with an equally large force. The lower scale is at rest, so the hook of the upper scale must pull it up by the force of the apple plus the weight of the scale itself. By the third law the lower scale pulls the upper one down with an equally large force, and this is what the upper scale shows.
**Force diagram:** The lower scale as a rectangle, positive direction up. The force of the hook of the upper scale $\overline{F}$ up from the top surface, the weight $\overline{G}_\text{s}$ down from the centre and the force of the apple $\overline{F}_\text{a}$ down from the bottom surface.
```voimakuvio
F 1,9 ylös
G_v 0,40
F_o 1,5 alas
a 0
```
**Formula:** $F - G_\text{s} - F_\text{a} = 0$ and $F_\text{a} = G_\text{a}$, so $F = G_\text{a} + G_\text{s}$
**Conversions:** The forces are in newtons, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $F = 1.5\,\text{N} + 0.40\,\text{N} = 1.9\,\text{N}$
**Answer:** $1.9\,\text{N}$, to the nearest tenth of a newton like the weight of the apple $1.5\,\text{N}$, so two significant figures.
**Reasonableness:** The lower scale shows the weight of the apple, $1.5\,\text{N}$, and the upper one also supports the lower scale, so its reading must be larger. The difference of the readings, $0.40\,\text{N}$, is exactly the weight of the lower scale.
