## Timing (75 minutes)

- **0–8 min, Think about it.** Read the hook aloud; the review question on $Q$; pairs discuss the opening question with the sentence starters and write their first ideas (no answer is given yet).
- **8–12 min, Predict.** Both predictions (the fuel of JET's record pulse, the main radioactive waste) answered individually and committed, each with a short reason. Feedback opens only when the student reaches the theory summary.
- **12–40 min, Investigate.** Read *The world's energy challenge* together; pairs study the 2025 electricity table and answer its question (5 min); pair discussion on firm and variable power with the word bank (8 min). Read *Results, plans and claims* and *Uncertainties*; the sorting task in pairs (8 min), then each student justifies two choices in writing.
- **40–52 min, Theory summary.** Key idea, fuel and tritium breeding (with the fuel-cycle diagram), fusion and fission compared; the vocabulary match; back to the predictions.
- **52–64 min, Apply.** The worked example step by step on the board; the tritium task individually (hints available). The error-spotting task about NIF is optional.
- **64–75 min, Check your understanding.** Three checks, the summary and the French corner in pairs; start the headline evaluation (check 4, with its word bank and sentence starters) in class and usually finish it as homework.

**Fast pairs.** Pairs that finish the sorting early do the optional NIF error-spotting task, or look up one more fusion headline on a news site, date it, and sort its main statement. In Apply they can compute how long the 25 kg stock would last a 1 GW plant (about five months), and what the decay of about $5\ \%$ a year means for storing tritium.

**Running out of time.** Shorten the pair discussion on firm power to an oral round (no writing) and give the headline evaluation (check 4) and the French corner as homework. Do not cut the sorting task: it is the core of the lesson and prepares the debate.

## Organisation and preparation
- Pairs share one device; both students commit their own predictions. The sorting task works best when pairs disagree and must justify: ask them to argue before pressing *Check*.
- Bring one or two current fusion headlines (a national newspaper, a company press release) to sort at the end, and check their dates. Statements and figures in the unit were checked on 1 November 2026: ITER's schedule, company plans and the Fusion Industry Association figures change. Check them again before teaching (sources at the end of the page).
- The unit prepares the debate (*Debate: should governments invest more in fusion?*): keep the students' sorting and their headline evaluation.

## Assessment
- Written answers are self-assessed against the criteria shown after the student has written. For a grade: the sorting justification names a signal for a result (past, measured, checked) and for a plan or claim (future tense, *aims*, *expects*, a company as source) and explains why dates matter; the headline evaluation names a dated result, recognises the headline year as a claim, names two unsolved problems and corrects *clean* with a fact about activation or tritium.
- Physics: the tritium task solved with the complete steps (energy, number of reactions, mass) and compared with the world stock.
- Language: at least three key words used correctly (dispatchable, variable, breeding blanket, neutron activation, half-life) and a sentence starter used to argue from evidence.

## Common difficulties
- *Scientific gain is electricity.* Return to the review question: ITER's $Q = 10$ compares fusion power with heating power, and ITER makes no electricity; NIF's lasers drew about $300\ \text{MJ}$ of electricity for $2.05\ \text{MJ}$ of light.
- *Fusion makes no radioactive waste* and the opposite, *fusion waste is like fission waste*. Both are wrong: activation gives low- and intermediate-level waste that fades mostly within about 100 years; tritium must be contained.
- *A survey is a result.* The Fusion Industry Association survey is real, but what it reports are companies' expectations: a claim.
- Units in the calculations: MeV to joules, u to kilograms, GW times seconds.

## Extension ideas
- Compare the land use, construction time and firm output of a wind farm, a fission plant and a planned fusion plant; which questions cannot be answered yet for fusion?
- Read ITER's *Tritium breeding* page and explain why a breeding ratio slightly above 1 is needed (decay, losses, start-up inventory for new plants).
- Find out what Finland's electricity mix was last year (Fingrid or Energiateollisuus) and discuss where fusion could fit.
