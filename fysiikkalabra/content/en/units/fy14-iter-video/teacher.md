## Before the lesson: check these items against the video

The video's spoken content could not be played from the course's development environment. The questions are built on the narration that The B1M publishes as an article on its own page (theb1m.com, checked 1 November 2026), on the YouTube title and channel, and on facts verified at iter.org. Watch the video once (about 10 minutes) and check:

- **Prediction 1** (the date): the video gives 2025 for "first power-on" / first plasma.
- **Prediction 2** (the focus): most of the time goes to size, parts, transport and the many nations; physics is short.
- **Frames 1–4** (model notes): the goal of "net energy" for a short time; carbon-free power independent of the weather; fission splits uranium and plutonium and leaves waste, fusion joins atoms with hydrogen isotopes from water; "ten times hotter than the Sun's core" and "twice the force needed to launch a space shuttle"; 23,000 tonnes and about one million components; 35 nations, different languages and cultures; 104 km of adapted roads with bridges and police convoys; cranes in the assembly hall and the construction-kit comparison; grid connection decades away; private companies. Delete any model point you do not hear.
- **Check 1** (23,000 tonnes and about one million components) and **check 3** ("net energy").
- **The sponsor** segment (Thinkproject, contract management) and where it appears.

An automatically generated transcript on a third-party site also mentions points that the channel's article does not contain: the 1985 Reagan–Gorbachev proposal, a plasma temperature of 150 million °C, energy comparisons with fission and coal, an ITER staff member on logistics, schedule changes because of the pandemic and other events, and many languages at the head office. These were **not** used for questions. If you hear them, you can add them to the note frames.

## Timing (75 minutes)

- **0–8 min, Think about it.** Hook, the review question (plan or result?).
- **8–12 min, Predict.** Two predictions, committed with a reason.
- **12–15 min, Before watching.** The vocabulary match.
- **15–45 min, While watching.** First viewing without stopping (about 10 min); second viewing with pauses, each student fills all four note frames; a pair can share the listening (one listens closely for frames 1–2, the other for 3–4) and then they complete each other's notes. Then sort the claims and answer the sponsor question *before* the table of checked claims, which checks the sort (both predictions open).
- **45–53 min, Theory summary.** Key idea on evaluating a video; pair discussion with the word bank; back to the predictions.
- **53–65 min, Apply.** The 80–100-word written explanation. The Kurzgesagt comparison is optional.
- **65–75 min, Check your understanding.** Three checks, the recommendation paragraph, the summary and the French corner.

## Organisation and preparation
- Show the video on a shared screen for the first viewing (subtitles on); pairs use their own devices with headphones for the second viewing. YouTube must be reachable from the school network; the player loads only when a student presses the button (youtube-nocookie).
- Print or share the four frames if students prefer paper notes.

## Assessment
- The note frames: teacher check of key words and numbers (criteria under each frame).
- The written explanation (the short written task): says what ITER will and will not do, corrects one claim with a dated fact, 80–100 words, word-bank terms.
- The recommendation paragraph: a position, a strength and a weakness with reasons, practical advice.
- Language: sentence starters used to qualify claims (*however*, *I partly agree*, *as of November 2026*).

## Common difficulties
- Fast speech: allow the second viewing with pauses and English subtitles.
- Students call outdated claims "lies". Discuss the difference between *false when said* and *true then, outdated now*.
- "35 nations" against "seven Members": the EU is one Member made of many states. 35 was ITER's long-used count (EU-28, Switzerland and six others); since the UK left it is 34.
- "Ten times hotter than the Sun" is about the core temperature only.

## Fast pairs and running out of time
- **Fast pairs:** the optional Kurzgesagt video (2016) and the comparison task; or find on iter.org one milestone after April 2022 that the video could not know.
- **Short of time:** watch once only and fill frames 1 and 4; set the written explanation as homework; keep checks 2 and 3.
