Ahjo Makerspace

Ahjo is Kiviruukin lukio's shared space for making: a cross-curricular STEAM learning environment where ideas become models, prototypes and solutions.

Kiviruukin lukio opens in autumn 2027. Ahjo's equipment, guides and safety instructions will be ready before that, and this page grows as they are completed.

Ahjo 1Technology basics

Being planned

Basics of 3D modelling, 3D printing, electronics and robotics.

Contents

  • 3D modelling
  • 3D printing
  • Electronics and Arduino
  • Basics of programming and robotics
  • Getting to know the equipment and working safely

Draft: the goals and the lesson outline await the teacher's approval.

Goals

In this course, you will learn to

  • work safely in Ahjo and use its equipment as you were introduced to it
  • model objects to measure and make them with a 3D printer
  • build simple circuits and measure them with a multimeter
  • program a microcontroller and use sensors and actuators
  • build and program a simple robot
  • document your work and assess how well it succeeded
16 lessons
  1. Welcome to Ahjo The space, the equipment and how we work; the rules and the induction.Example: An Ahjo tour: guess what each machine does and think of what you would like to build.
  2. Working safely Hand and power tools, protective equipment and an orderly workstation.Example: Make Ahjo's safety poster together, with rules the students justify themselves.
  3. Basics of 3D modelling Basic shapes, joining and cutting in a modelling program.Example: Model your own key ring or name tag and print it at the end of the lesson.
  4. A model to measure Dimensions, tolerances and fitting parts together.Example: A phone stand or cable holder that fits your own phone to the millimetre.
  5. From model to print Slicing, print settings and using the printer.Example: How fast and light can you print a bridge that carries a drink can?
  6. Judging a print and 3D scanning Print defects and how to fix them; scanning an object into a model.Example: Scan a stone, a fruit or your own face and print a miniature of it.
  7. Circuits and components Power source, resistor, LED and breadboard.Example: Light an LED with a lemon battery and find out how many lemons it takes.
  8. Measuring with a multimeter Measuring voltage, current and resistance connected the right way.Example: Find a broken lead or a flat battery by measuring: Ahjo's electrical detective.
  9. A first Arduino program The programming environment, digital outputs and a blinking LED.Example: Traffic lights or a Morse message a classmate tries to decode.
  10. Sensors and inputs Button, light and distance sensors; conditions and loops.Example: A night light that turns on in the dark, or a parking sensor that beeps faster and faster.
  11. Motors and actuators Servo and DC motor; power supply and control.Example: A waving cat, a pointer, or a small fan that starts when it gets warm.
  12. Soldering Using a soldering station and soldering a small circuit.Example: Solder your own blinking badge or a small sound circuit to take home.
  13. Building a robot The structure of a robotics kit, drive train and sensors.Example: Build a robot that carries a ping-pong ball from one end of the table to the other.
  14. Programming a robot Movement, sensor control and solving a task with a program.Example: A line-following robot and a class race on a track.
  15. Mini project A small device of your own with the methods learned: design and build.Example: Solve a small everyday problem at school: a doorbell, a plant-watering reminder or a classroom noise meter.
  16. Presentation and self-assessment Presenting and documenting the mini project and assessing your own learning.Example: A fair: everyone presents their device in a minute and the others try it.

Ahjo 2Design and build

Being planned

Design, build and test your own prototype.

Contents

  • Ideation and defining the problem
  • Design and prototyping
  • Building
  • Testing and improving
  • Documentation and assessment

Draft: the goals and the lesson outline await the teacher's approval.

Goals

In this course, you will learn to

  • define a problem and the user's needs
  • come up with possible solutions and choose between them with reasons
  • plan and schedule a project in a group
  • build a prototype with Ahjo's equipment and methods
  • test a solution and improve it on the basis of tests and feedback
  • document and present the project and assess your own and the group's work
16 lessons
  1. Starting the project How Ahjo 2 runs, teams and finding a project topic.Example: Look at young inventors' solutions and collect problems from your own school life.
  2. Defining the problem Whose problem, what need, and how success is recognised.Example: Interview a user: what makes the PE lesson, canteen or bike park awkward?
  3. Ideation Ideation methods and producing several possible solutions.Example: A six-minute sketch round: six ideas for the same problem.
  4. Judging and choosing ideas Criteria, comparison and a justified choice.Example: Score the ideas: impact, feasibility and how exciting they are.
  5. Project plan Goals, roles, schedule, materials and risks.Example: Make a one-page plan and timeline of the project and update it every lesson.
  6. Design Sketches, dimensioned drawings and digital models.Example: Cardboard first: build the idea from cardboard and tape before the computer model.
  7. Materials and methods Choosing suitable materials, equipment and ways of making.Example: Compare: what does the same part need when 3D printed, cut from vinyl or sewn?
  8. A first prototype A quick, simple model to try out the idea.Example: A one-lesson prototype: what is the smallest version that lets you try the idea?
  9. Testing the prototype A test plan, measuring and recording observations.Example: A stress test: drop it, load it, or let someone use it without instructions.
  10. Feedback and improvement Feedback from users and the group; planning changes.Example: A feedback café: groups rotate and leave each other "keep, change, try" notes.
  11. Building 1 Making the parts of the solution according to the plan.Example: Split the building into parts so that everyone learns a new machine.
  12. Building 2 Assembling the parts; electronics, programming or finishing as needed.Example: Add a sensor, light, sound or printed fabric part that makes it clearer for the user.
  13. Final testing Does the solution work: tests, measurements and comparison with the goals.Example: Measure before and after: how much does the solution really help?
  14. Finishing Last fixes, appearance and usability.Example: A finish that makes it look like a product: a sticker, logo or case with the vinyl cutter and 3D printer.
  15. Documentation The project's stages, decisions and results into a portfolio.Example: Shoot a short video or make an illustrated portfolio from which someone else could build it.
  16. Presentation and assessment Presenting the project and assessing your own and the group's work.Example: A project exhibition in the school lobby or for visitors.
