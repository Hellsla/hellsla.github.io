## Lesson plan (75 min)

- **0–5 min, Think about it.** The spark from a door handle on a frosty day: thousands of volts, but no danger. In pairs the students estimate in words what the length of the spark tells and why the shock does no harm. The answer is not given yet: it is at the end of the Key idea part of the theory summary (a $3\,\text{mm}$ spark about $9\,\text{kV}$, the capacitance of the body about $100\,\text{pF}$, the charge about $0.9\,\mu\text{C}$, the energy about $4\,\text{mJ}$).
- **5–15 min, Predict (diagnostic test).** Six questions alone, without notes. Ask the students to write the numbers of the questions they got wrong in their notebooks.
- **15–25 min, Investigate.** Potential between the plates in pairs: the pair reads the potential at $3.0\,\text{cm}$ ($150\,\text{V}$) from the graph and calculates the speed of the electron (about $7.3 \cdot 10^{3}\,\text{km/s}$). Debrief together: the slope is the field strength $5000\,\text{V/m}$, the electron moves towards the higher potential, and its potential energy decreases.
- **25–35 min, Theory summary.** Go through the concept map: every branch starts from the fact that a charge creates a field and the field exerts a force on a charge. Use the figure *levyt* to show that the field and the force are different arrows and that the force on the electron points against the field. Return to Think about it with the figure *kipina*.
- **35–55 min, Apply.** Four multiple-choice questions in pairs (about $6\,\text{min}$), then the calculations alone. The whole group will not manage eight tasks in 20 minutes, so in class the calculations *A dust particle in an electrostatic precipitator* and *The capacitor of a defibrillator* are done (about $5\,\text{min}$ each), and the calculations *Three charges on a rail* and *The charging time of an electric car* are left for home. Fast students do them in class without hints; slow students use the hints one at a time.
- **55–65 min, Check your understanding and exit ticket.** Alone: the concept and calculation tasks and the exit ticket. The explanation task (why a balloon sticks to a neutral wall) is homework, and it is self-assessed with the criteria.
- **65–75 min, Tasks.** In ten minutes there is time for the start of one long task in exam conditions, without the model answer: recommended *A demonstration capacitor and a glass plate* a–b (calculation task) or *The ion thruster of a satellite* a–b (combining courses). The rest of the task and the other five tasks are homework, two or three at a time before the next lesson.

## Using the diagnostic test
The Predict questions are a diagnostic test, not a grade. Each question corresponds to one chapter:

- Question 1: how Coulomb's law depends on charge and distance (chapter *Electric charge and Coulomb's law*).
- Question 2: the direction of the force on an electron in a field (chapter *Uniform electric field*).
- Question 3: a proton and an electron through the same potential difference (chapter *Potential energy and potential in an electric field*).
- Question 4: a disconnected capacitor when the plates are separated (chapter *The capacitor and its energy*).
- Question 5: an LED in the reverse direction (chapter *Semiconductors, the diode, the LED and the solar cell*).
- Question 6: how the residual current device works (chapter *Electrical safety at home and at work*).

The chapter on the home electrical system (*Applied chapter: the electric car and the home electrical system*) comes up in the Apply calculation *The charging time of an electric car*. If more than a third of the group answers question 2 or 3 wrongly, draw the plates and two charges on the board before the Investigate task: the field in green, the forces in red. A field line shows the direction of the force on a positive charge, and each charge starts from rest in the direction in which its potential energy decreases. The mistake in question 4 ("the voltage stays the same") is revisited in the long task *A demonstration capacitor and a glass plate*, where the source is disconnected before the dielectric is added.

## Pair work
- Investigate: one reads the potential and the slope from the graph, the other calculates the speed with the energy principle; then the pair checks together that the force on the electron points towards the positive plate.
- Apply multiple-choice questions: each first chooses on their own and only then justifies the choice to their partner. The pair answers only when they agree.
- In the long task *The efficiency of a solar panel* (assessing a report) pairs can write the experimental plan together and assess it with another pair using the marking scheme.

## Fast and slow
- **Fast students** do the Apply calculations without hints and move straight on to the long task *Plate separation and capacitance* (graph, linearisation and interpreting the constant term, $20$ points), which has the most steps. Extra question: at what plate separation would the capacitance of the capacitor of the task be $500\,\text{pF}$ without the leads? (The condition $\tfrac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d} = 500\,\text{pF}$ gives $d \approx 0.71\,\text{mm}$.)
- **Slow students** do the calculations *A dust particle in an electrostatic precipitator* and *The capacitor of a defibrillator* of the Apply part with the hints and leave the calculations *Three charges on a rail* and *The charging time of an electric car* for home. The *Review if needed* links of the theory summary lead straight to the original chapters, which have basic-level exercises.

## Assessment
The long tasks are self-assessed with the marking scheme. The points follow the division of the matriculation exam: the graph and fitting task *Plate separation and capacitance* is of the Part III type ($20$ points), the other five of the Part II type ($15$ points each), $95$ points in total. If the teacher assesses them, the same criteria work: the free-body diagram, choosing and justifying the principle, the directions of the field and the force, the symbolic solution, unit conversions by substitution, precision and reasonableness; in the written part, assessing the claim with numbers. All the data are invented examples, not real measurements. In the electrical safety tasks emphasise that nothing is ever tried near high-voltage lines.
