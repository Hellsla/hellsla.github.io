More about the courseGoals, the licence and what comes next.

How a lesson is built

  1. Start right away 5–10 minutes. A first success with a ready-made model, circuit or program.
  2. Try The basic work for everyone, which reaches the lesson's goal.
  3. Modify You change the solution according to your own ideas.
  4. Challenge yourself An optional follow-up task that is harder technically or scientifically.
  5. What did you make? The basic-level output and, if you did the challenge, its output.

The basic level is Start right away, Try and Modify. Challenge yourself and the small challenges in the materials bank are optional. You do not have to do everything in the same lesson.

Goals

Draft: the goals and the lesson outline await the teacher's approval.

In this course, you will learn to

  • work safely in Ahjo and use its equipment as you were introduced to it
  • model objects to measure and make them with a 3D printer
  • build simple circuits and measure them with a multimeter
  • program a microcontroller and use sensors and actuators
  • program and test a simple robot
  • document your work and assess how well it succeeded

Assessment

The assessment is based on the lessons' outputs, the project journal and the teacher's observations. It takes into account:

  • safe and responsible work
  • command of basic technical skills
  • measuring and interpreting results
  • the basics of programming and digital fabrication
  • trying out and developing solutions
  • documenting the work and assessing your own learning

Technical flair or a working device alone does not decide the assessment. A failed attempt can also show excellent skills when the design, the testing and the decisions are justified. The assessment is a draft, and the teacher decides on the weighting.

Makerspace licence

Ahjo 1 is also Ahjo's Makerspace licence. It shows that you master the general makerspace skills, and you need it to work on independent projects in Ahjo. The licence does not, however, let you use every device on your own.

  1. Makerspace licence (Ahjo 1). General skills: working safely, designing to measure, the basics of electronics and programming, and documenting your work.
  2. Safety induction to the space. For everyone before working in Ahjo: the rules of the space, protective equipment, first aid and what to do in a dangerous situation.
  3. Device induction and permission to use. Separately for each device. Only after it may you use the device, and the permission can be limited.

Licence skills checklist

  • I follow Ahjo's rules, keep my workstation in order and know where the fire extinguisher and first aid kit are and what to do in an emergency. (lessons 1–2)
  • I handle coin cells and circuits safely: I do not short-circuit a battery, I return coin cells and I disconnect the power before I change the wiring. (lessons 1, 7–11)
  • I measure voltage by connecting the multimeter in parallel and current by connecting it in series, on the right range. (lesson 8)
  • I know the 3D printer's hot parts and moving parts and use the printer only as my induction and permit allow. (lessons 2, 5–6)
  • I use hand tools and robots safely: safety glasses when needed, and a new program is tested first with the wheels in the air. (lessons 2, 12–13)
  • I model an accurately sized object and check its dimensions by measuring. (lessons 3–6)
  • I program an Arduino and read a sensor with it. (lessons 9–11)
  • I test and present my device safely: it has no hot parts, sharp edges or loose wires. (lessons 15–16)
  • I document my work in the project journal. (whole course)

The list says what the licence requires. The licence does not replace device induction or a device permit. The list is a draft until the school approves Ahjo's safety practices.

Ahjo's equipment and approved safety practices are still being prepared.

Optional deepening

Optional extra work for those who get further or want to go deeper. It is not part of the outline everyone follows.

  • 3D scanning Scan an object, repair the model and print a scale copy. How accurate is the copy, and where do the differences come from?
  • Soldering Solder your measuring device onto stripboard once you have had the soldering station induction. Test the joints with a multimeter.
  • Solar panel power Measure with two multimeters how much more power a light-following panel gives than a fixed one.
  • P control Improve line following so that the robot's turning depends on how far it is from the line. Compare lap times.
