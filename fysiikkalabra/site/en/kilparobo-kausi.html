Competitive robotics

Competition season and development diary

The game and rules of VEX V5 competitions change every season. This model describes how the team moves through a season whatever the game. The phases partly overlap, and the team keeps returning to them.

How a competition season goes

  1. 1Getting to know the competition rules

    Read the current season's game manual and follow its updates. Everyone reads at least the game's description and the robot rules.

    Ask: What may the robot be, and what may it not be? How are points scored?

    Record: A short summary of the most important rules in your own words.

  2. 2Analysing the competition challenge

    Break the game into tasks: what the robot has to be able to do and how many points each task gives.

    Ask: Which tasks give the most points for their difficulty? What can the robot do autonomously?

    Record: The robot's tasks in order of importance.

  3. 3Planning the strategy

    Decide what the team focuses on. A good strategy fits the team's time, skills and parts.

    Ask: What do we do first in a match? How do we work with our alliance partner?

    Record: The strategy and the reasons for it.

  4. 4Designing and building the robot

    Sketch alternatives, choose one with reasons and build it. The first version may be simple.

    Ask: Which alternatives did we consider? Why did we choose this one?

    Record: Sketches, the reasons for the choice and a parts list.

  5. 5Programming

    Program the driver control and the autonomous actions. Save every working version so that you can go back to it.

    Ask: Which part of the program does what? Which version worked best, and why?

    Record: The program's versions and a short note on how they differ.

  6. 6Testing and practice matches

    First test the tasks one by one, then the whole robot in practice matches. The drivers practise regularly.

    Ask: What fails most often? What do we fix first?

    Record: Test results and a list of fixes.

  7. 7Taking part in a competition

    The teacher handles registration and travel with the team. On competition day you check the robot before every match and record your observations.

    Ask: What worked in the match? What did other teams do well?

    Record: Match notes.

  8. 8Evaluating and improving after the competition

    Go through what worked and what did not, and decide what to improve first. At the end of the season, record what next year's team should know.

    Ask: What did we learn? What would we do differently?

    Record: A season summary for the next team.

The season's schedule and competitions are agreed with the teacher. This page has no dates because they change every year.

The team's development diary

The development diary is the team's memory. A short entry for each experiment is enough. When you record what was tried and what was learnt, next year's team can carry on from there.

  1. What were we trying to solve?
  2. What did we change?
  3. How did we test it?
  4. What did we observe?
  5. What do we do next?

You can use the template in the school's own tools, for example a shared text document, a note-taking app or a paper notebook. This page stores nothing. The diary needs no personal data: first names or initials are enough for the authors.

At VEX competitions the judges may evaluate a team's Engineering Notebook. According to RECF's guides the notebook can be on paper or digital, and each event decides which it accepts. The five questions of this template suit recording design, testing and improvements. If the team wants its notebook judged, check the current season's criteria in RECF's guides.
