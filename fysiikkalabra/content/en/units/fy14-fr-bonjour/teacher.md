## Aim and level

The first lesson of the French chapter, for complete beginners. The goal is practical, confident speech in a first
meeting, not grammar: students should leave able to greet, introduce themselves, ask the same questions back and
say goodbye, with the right choice of *<span lang="fr">tu</span>* or *<span lang="fr">vous</span>*. Instructions are in English; every French phrase can be heard
with the browser's French voice, which is a model voice, not a native speaker. Your own pronunciation is the main
model.

The ITER visit is a **scenario** for practice. If a real visit is ever planned: the ITER site admits visitors aged
16 or over (16- and 17-year-olds with a parent or legal guardian), groups book at least four weeks ahead and every
visitor needs a passport or national ID card (iter.org, *Visiting ITER*, checked 2026-11-01).

## Timing (75 minutes)

- **0–8 min, Think about it.** Read the hook together. Pairs discuss greetings in Finnish and collect the French they already know; collect a class list on the board.
- **8–13 min, Predict.** Both predictions individually; prediction 2 stays closed until the theory section is read.
- **13–25 min, Investigate: listen.** Go through the phrase table together: you say each phrase, the class repeats in chorus, then pairs listen and repeat. Pick out the silent letters.
- **25–37 min, Investigate: dialogue.** Pairs read the ITER dialogue (step 1), practise it (step 2), swap roles and perform from the cues (step 3).
- **37–42 min, Investigate: order.** Pairs order the evening conversation and answer *What did you notice?*
- **42–50 min, Theory summary.** Read *Tu or vous?* and the sound tips; drill the four sounds in chorus; look at the summary table.
- **50–57 min, Apply.** Gap fill and matching, individually; partners compare.
- **57–70 min, Role-play.** Two rounds in pairs (see below), two or three pairs perform for the class.
- **70–75 min, Check.** The three checks and the short written introduction (may be finished as homework).

Fast pairs: the optional extension (introducing a classmate with *<span lang="fr">il</span>* / *<span lang="fr">elle</span>*), then introduce their partner to
another pair.

## Pairs and organisation

Work in pairs throughout; mix confident and hesitant speakers. Students with some French from earlier studies can be
the "researcher" in round 1 and add more questions. Headphones help if many devices play the voice at once; otherwise
let one device per pair play.

## Running the role-play

1. Round 1 (polite): A is the Finnish student, B a French-speaking researcher at the visitor centre in the morning.
   B decides where they come from (France, Italy, Japan …), which makes the nationality line real.
2. Round 2 (informal): swap roles; two young visitors at the hotel in the evening, with *<span lang="fr">Salut</span>* or *<span lang="fr">Bonsoir</span>* and *<span lang="fr">tu</span>*.
3. Encourage speaking from memory: the word bank is a safety net, not a script. A pair that gets stuck uses
   *<span lang="fr">Pardon ?</span>* or *<span lang="fr">Encore une fois, s'il vous plaît.</span>*
4. While pairs speak, walk around and listen for the three criteria below; note one strength and one sound to work on
   for each pair. Correct only what blocks understanding during the activity, and give the rest as feedback afterwards.

## Assessment of speaking (formative)

Use the same three criteria the students see:

- **Communicates the message:** Greets, says name, origin, home town and languages, asks at least one question back, says goodbye.
- **Pronunciation understandable:** A French listener would understand: final letters mostly silent, liaison in *<span lang="fr">vous‿êtes</span>* / *<span lang="fr">vous‿appelez</span>*, an attempt at nasal vowels and the French *r*. A Finnish accent is fine.
- **Polite form chosen correctly:** *<span lang="fr">Bonjour, madame / monsieur</span>* and *<span lang="fr">vous</span>* with the adult researcher; *<span lang="fr">Salut</span>* or *<span lang="fr">Bonsoir</span>* and *<span lang="fr">tu</span>* with the young visitor.

Peer assessment with the same criteria works well when a pair performs for another pair.

## Common difficulties

- Pronouncing every letter (*<span lang="fr">vus</span>*, *<span lang="fr">finlandais</span>* with a clear *s*); the *u* of *<span lang="fr">salut</span>* said as a Finnish *u*.
- *<span lang="fr">Salut</span>* to adults, or *<span lang="fr">tu</span>* with everyone because it is shorter. Remind students: when in doubt, *<span lang="fr">vous</span>*.
- *<span lang="fr">Je suis Finlande</span>* instead of *<span lang="fr">Je viens de Finlande</span>* / *<span lang="fr">Je suis finlandais(e)</span>*; the feminine nationality forgotten.
- *<span lang="fr">Et tu ?</span>* instead of *<span lang="fr">Et toi ?</span>*

## Follow-up

centre in Provence. The written introduction from check 4 can be kept and improved during the chapter.
