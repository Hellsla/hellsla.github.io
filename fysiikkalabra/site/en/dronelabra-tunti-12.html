Lesson 12 · FPV, measuring and data

Report on the control test

Drone control engineering and comparing the flight tests

Feedback: the sensor measures, the program compares and the motors correct. The results of the flight tests are brought together.

In this lesson you learn: You describe the stabilisation of the drone as feedback and compare test results.

Needs the teacher's safety approval

Start right away (5–10 min)

Hold the drone in your hand without propellers and tilt it slowly. Use the motor test or the display to see in which direction the program tries to correct.

The Air:bit 2 radio controller and control program →

Try · Modify · Challenge yourself

  1. Try for everyone

    Draw the drone's control loop as a block diagram: target attitude, sensors, feedback controller, motors and drone. Attach the results of your group's stability test to the diagram.

  2. Modify your own ideas

    Compare the stability results of at least two groups and explain what the differences could be due to: the drone, the battery, the pilot or the way of measuring.

  3. Challenge yourself optional

    Do a second control test with a different value from the one in lesson 8 and plot the results in the same graph as the earlier ones. With which value was the hover steadiest?

If you have to wait

If the drone is in use, finish the block diagram and write the summary of the results.

What did you make?

Basic level: A block diagram of the drone's control loop and a summary of the results of the stability test.

Challenge yourself: A graph comparing at least two tuning values.

Write in your project diary

  • the block diagram
  • the summary of the results and the graph
  • an idea for your own project

Together

Explain your block diagram to your partner by following one loop with your finger: what happens when the wind tilts the drone?

You need

  • Air:bit 2 and a controller
  • a computer
  • earlier measurement results
  • charged batteries and safety glasses, if you fly

Safety

The hand-held test is always done without propellers. In a flight test the same limits apply as in lesson 8.

  • Drones: propellers and motors
  • Drones: charging and storing batteries
  • Drones: flight area and roles
  • Drones: emergency stop and abnormal situations

Ahjo's safety instructions are still being prepared. Follow your teacher's induction.

For the teacher: preparation and timing

Timing: Hand-held test 10 min, block diagram 25 min, comparing the results or a second control test 30 min, summary 10 min.

Preparation: Collect the groups' stability results in one table. Decide whether there is flying this time. At the end, introduce the project bank for next time.
