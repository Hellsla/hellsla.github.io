## fy4-14-L1-01
**Given:** $m = 75.0\,\text{kg}$, the skydiver falls at terminal velocity; $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the air resistance $F_\text{v}$ in $\text{N}$
**Principle:** At terminal velocity the speed does not change, so the acceleration is zero. By Newton's second law the net force is then zero, even though the skydiver is moving: air resistance cancels the weight.
**Free-body diagram:** The skydiver as a rectangle, positive direction down. The weight $\overline{G}$ from the centre down and the air resistance $\overline{F}_\text{v}$ from the bottom surface up, equally long. The velocity is not drawn as a force.
```voimakuvio
G 735,8
F_v 735,8 ylös työntö @ala
a 0
```
**Formula:** $mg - F_\text{v} = 0$, so $F_\text{v} = mg$
**Conversions:** The mass is in kilograms, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $F_\text{v} = 75.0\,\text{kg}\cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 = 735.75\,\text{N}$
**Answer:** $736\,\text{N}$ upward, three significant figures like the mass $75.0\,\text{kg}$.
**Reasonableness:** The weight of one kilogram is about $10\,\text{N}$, so the weight of a $75\,\text{kg}$ skydiver is about $750\,\text{N}$, and at terminal velocity the air resistance is equal to it. Air resistance is not greater than the weight, because then the skydiver would slow down.

## fy4-14-L1-02
**Given:** $m = 2.00\,\text{kg}$, air resistance $F_\text{v} = 5.00\,\text{N}$, the stone is falling down; $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the net force $\Sigma F$ in $\text{N}$
**Principle:** The Earth (weight) and the air (air resistance) act on the stone. The air resistance of a falling stone points against the velocity, that is up, so the net force is the difference of the weight and the air resistance.
**Free-body diagram:** The stone as a rectangle, positive direction down. The weight $\overline{G}$ from the centre down and the air resistance $\overline{F}_\text{v}$ from the bottom surface up, shorter than the weight.
```voimakuvio
G 19,62
F_v 5 ylös työntö @ala
a alas
```
**Formula:** $\Sigma F = G - F_\text{v}$, and $G = mg$, so $\Sigma F = mg - F_\text{v}$
**Conversions:** The mass is in kilograms and the force in newtons, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $\Sigma F = 2.00\,\text{kg}\cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 - 5.00\,\text{N} = 19.62\,\text{N} - 5.00\,\text{N} = 14.62\,\text{N}$
**Answer:** $14.6\,\text{N}$ downward, three significant figures like the mass $2.00\,\text{kg}$ and the air resistance $5.00\,\text{N}$.
**Reasonableness:** The net force is smaller than the weight $19.6\,\text{N}$ but positive, so the stone is still accelerating downward, now more slowly than without air. The net force decreases as the speed grows, because air resistance grows.

## fy4-14-L1-03
**Given:** $m = 0.600\,\text{kg}$, air resistance $F_\text{v} = 8.00\,\text{N}$, the ball has been thrown down and is moving down; $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the acceleration $a$ of the ball in $\text{m/s}^2$ and its direction
**Principle:** By Newton's second law the acceleration is determined by the net force. The ball moves down, so the air resistance points up, against the velocity. Air resistance is greater than the weight, so the net force points up even though the ball is moving down.
**Free-body diagram:** The ball as a rectangle, positive direction down. The weight $\overline{G}$ from the centre down and the air resistance $\overline{F}_\text{v}$ from the bottom surface up, longer than the weight.
```voimakuvio
G 5,886
F_v 8 ylös työntö @ala
a ylös
```
**Formula:** $mg - F_\text{v} = ma$, so $a = \dfrac{mg - F_\text{v}}{m}$
**Conversions:** The quantities are in SI units, and $\tfrac{\text{N}}{\text{kg}} = \tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2}$, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $a = \dfrac{0.600\,\text{kg}\cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 - 8.00\,\text{N}}{0.600\,\text{kg}} = \dfrac{5.886\,\text{N} - 8.00\,\text{N}}{0.600\,\text{kg}} = -3.523\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Answer:** $3.52\,\text{m/s}^2$ upward, three significant figures like the mass $0.600\,\text{kg}$ and the air resistance $8.00\,\text{N}$. The minus sign tells that the acceleration points up.
**Reasonableness:** Air resistance is about $1.4$ times the weight $5.9\,\text{N}$, so about $2\,\text{N}$ remains upward and the acceleration is about $2\,\text{N} : 0.6\,\text{kg} \approx 3.5\,\text{m/s}^2$. The ball slows down until the air resistance has decreased to the size of the weight.

## fy4-14-L1-04
**Given:** $m = 0.450\,\text{kg}$, air resistance $F_\text{v} = 1.20\,\text{N}$, the ball is rising; $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the magnitude of the net force $\Sigma F$ in $\text{N}$
**Principle:** After the foot has left the ball, only the Earth (weight) and the air (air resistance) act on the ball. The rising ball moves up, so the air resistance points down, in the same direction as the weight. There is no force carrying the ball upward.
**Free-body diagram:** The ball as a rectangle, positive direction up. The weight $\overline{G}$ from the centre down and the air resistance $\overline{F}_\text{v}$ from the top surface down. The velocity up is marked separately next to the diagram.
```voimakuvio
G 4,415
F_v 1,2 alas työntö @ylä
a alas
v ylös
```
**Formula:** $\Sigma F = -G - F_\text{v} = -mg - F_\text{v}$, so the magnitude of the net force is $|\Sigma F| = mg + F_\text{v}$
**Conversions:** The mass is in kilograms and the force in newtons, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $|\Sigma F| = 0.450\,\text{kg}\cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 + 1.20\,\text{N} = 4.4145\,\text{N} + 1.20\,\text{N} = 5.6145\,\text{N}$
**Answer:** $5.61\,\text{N}$ downward, three significant figures like the mass $0.450\,\text{kg}$ and the air resistance $1.20\,\text{N}$.
**Reasonableness:** The net force is greater than the weight $4.41\,\text{N}$, because the air resistance pulls in the same direction. That is why a rising ball slows down faster than without air. The net force points down even though the ball is moving up.

## fy4-14-L1-05
**Given:** the speed is constant, air resistance $F_\text{v} = 18\,\text{N}$, rolling resistance (the resistance caused by the tyres and the road) $F_\text{r} = 6.0\,\text{N}$
**Find:** the friction force of the road $F$ that pushes the driving wheel forward, in $\text{N}$
**Principle:** In uniform motion the acceleration is zero, so by Newton's second law the net force is zero. In the horizontal direction, the friction force of the road, with which the road pushes the driving wheel forward, must cancel both resistive forces; the rolling resistance is a separate resistive force here. In the vertical direction the weight and the normal force of the road cancel each other.
**Free-body diagram:** The cyclist and the bicycle as one rectangle, positive direction forward. The weight $\overline{G}$ from the centre down and the normal force of the road $\overline{N}$ from the bottom surface up, equally long. The friction force of the road $\overline{F}$ from the bottom surface of the driving wheel forward, the air resistance $\overline{F}_\text{v}$ and the rolling resistance $\overline{F}_\text{r}$ backward.
```voimakuvio
pinta 0
G 784,8
N 784,8
F 24 oikea kitka
F_v 18 vasen
F_r 6,0 vasen
a 0
```
**Formula:** $F - F_\text{v} - F_\text{r} = 0$, so $F = F_\text{v} + F_\text{r}$
**Conversions:** All forces are in newtons, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $F = 18\,\text{N} + 6.0\,\text{N} = 24\,\text{N}$
**Answer:** $24\,\text{N}$ forward, two significant figures like the air resistance $18\,\text{N}$.
**Reasonableness:** The force forward is equal to the resistive forces together, not greater, because the speed does not grow. A force of a few tens of newtons fits ordinary cycling on a level road; most of it goes to air resistance.

## fy4-14-L2-01
**Given:** $m = 5.10\,\text{g}$, the shuttlecock falls at terminal velocity; $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the air resistance $F_\text{v}$ in $\text{N}$
**Principle:** At terminal velocity the speed does not change, so by Newton's second law the net force is zero: the air resistance is equal to the weight and points up.
**Free-body diagram:** The shuttlecock as a rectangle, positive direction down. The weight $\overline{G}$ from the centre down and the air resistance $\overline{F}_\text{v}$ from the bottom surface up, equally long.
```voimakuvio
G 0,05003
F_v 0,05003 ylös työntö @ala
a 0
```
**Formula:** $mg - F_\text{v} = 0$, so $F_\text{v} = mg$
**Conversions:** $5.10\,\text{g} = 5.10 : 1000\,\text{kg} = 5.10\cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{kg}$
**Substitution:** $F_\text{v} = 5.10\cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{kg}\cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 = 0.050031\,\text{N}$
**Answer:** $0.0500\,\text{N}$ or $50.0\,\text{mN}$ upward, three significant figures like the mass $5.10\,\text{g}$.
**Reasonableness:** The weight of five grams is about $0.05\,\text{N}$, so the result is of the right order of magnitude. If the grams were not converted, the result would be a thousand times greater, about $50\,\text{N}$, which is the weight of five kilograms and far too much for a shuttlecock.

## fy4-14-L2-02
**Given:** $m = 70.0\,\text{kg}$, acceleration $a = 6.00\,\text{m/s}^2$ downward; $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the air resistance $F_\text{v}$ in $\text{N}$
**Principle:** The acceleration of the skydiver is smaller than $g$, so some force opposes the weight: air resistance, up. By Newton's second law the net force $G - F_\text{v}$ is the mass times the acceleration, and the air resistance is solved from it.
**Free-body diagram:** The skydiver as a rectangle, positive direction down. The weight $\overline{G}$ from the centre down and the air resistance $\overline{F}_\text{v}$ from the bottom surface up, shorter than the weight. The acceleration down is marked separately next to the diagram.
```voimakuvio
G 686,7
F_v 266,7 ylös työntö @ala
a alas
```
**Formula:** $mg - F_\text{v} = ma$, so $F_\text{v} = mg - ma = m(g - a)$
**Conversions:** The quantities are in SI units, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $F_\text{v} = 70.0\,\text{kg}\cdot(9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 - 6.00\,\text{m/s}^2) = 70.0\,\text{kg}\cdot 3.81\,\text{m/s}^2 = 266.7\,\text{N}$
**Answer:** $267\,\text{N}$ upward, three significant figures like the mass $70.0\,\text{kg}$ and the acceleration $6.00\,\text{m/s}^2$.
**Reasonableness:** The weight of the skydiver is about $690\,\text{N}$. The acceleration is about $60\,\%$ of the free-fall acceleration, so air resistance cancels about $40\,\%$ of the weight, about $270\,\text{N}$. The skydiver is not yet at terminal velocity, because the air resistance is smaller than the weight.

## fy4-14-L2-03
**Given:** $m = 57.0\,\text{g}$, air resistance $F_\text{v} = 0.250\,\text{N}$, the ball is rising; $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the magnitude of the acceleration $a$ in $\text{m/s}^2$
**Principle:** After the racket has hit the ball, only the weight and the air resistance act on it. The air resistance of a rising ball points against the velocity, that is down, in the same direction as the weight, so both slow down the rise.
**Free-body diagram:** The ball as a rectangle, positive direction up. The weight $\overline{G}$ from the centre down and the air resistance $\overline{F}_\text{v}$ from the top surface down. The velocity up and the acceleration down are marked separately.
```voimakuvio
G 0,5592
F_v 0,25 alas työntö @ylä
a alas
v ylös
```
**Formula:** $-mg - F_\text{v} = ma$, so $a = -\dfrac{mg + F_\text{v}}{m} = -\left(g + \dfrac{F_\text{v}}{m}\right)$
**Conversions:** $57.0\,\text{g} = 57.0 : 1000\,\text{kg} = 0.0570\,\text{kg}$
**Substitution:** $a = -\left(9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 + \dfrac{0.250\,\text{N}}{0.0570\,\text{kg}}\right) = -(9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 + 4.386\,\text{m/s}^2) = -14.196\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Answer:** $14.2\,\text{m/s}^2$ downward, three significant figures like the mass $57.0\,\text{g}$ and the air resistance $0.250\,\text{N}$. The minus sign tells that the acceleration points down.
**Reasonableness:** The magnitude of the acceleration is greater than $g$, because air resistance and weight pull in the same direction. For a light ball even a small air resistance is significant: $0.25\,\text{N}$ is almost half of the weight of the ball, $0.56\,\text{N}$.

## fy4-14-L2-04
**Given:** $m = 1200\,\text{kg}$, air resistance $F_\text{v} = 450\,\text{N}$, rolling resistance $F_\text{r} = 180\,\text{N}$, the engine is not driving; speed $100\,\text{km/h}$ (not needed)
**Find:** the deceleration, that is the magnitude of the acceleration $a$, in $\text{m/s}^2$
**Principle:** When the engine no longer drives, there is no force forward. In the horizontal direction only the resistive forces backward remain, and by Newton's second law they give the car an acceleration backward. Right after the clutch is pressed the air resistance is the same as before, because the speed has not yet had time to change.
**Free-body diagram:** The car as a rectangle, positive direction in the direction of motion. The weight $\overline{G}$ from the centre down and the normal force of the road $\overline{N}$ from the bottom surface up, equally long. The air resistance $\overline{F}_\text{v}$ and the rolling resistance $\overline{F}_\text{r}$ backward. There is no force forward.
```voimakuvio
pinta 0
G 11772
N 11772
F_v 450 vasen
F_r 180 vasen
a vasen
```
**Formula:** $-F_\text{v} - F_\text{r} = ma$, so $a = -\dfrac{F_\text{v} + F_\text{r}}{m}$
**Conversions:** The speed is not needed in the calculation, so it is not converted. The forces are in newtons and the mass in kilograms.
**Substitution:** $a = -\dfrac{450\,\text{N} + 180\,\text{N}}{1200\,\text{kg}} = -\dfrac{630\,\text{N}}{1200\,\text{kg}} = -0.525\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Answer:** $0.525\,\text{m/s}^2$, three significant figures, when the numbers of the task $450\,\text{N}$, $180\,\text{N}$ and $1200\,\text{kg}$ are read as three-digit values.
**Reasonableness:** The resistive forces are together only about $5\,\%$ of the weight of the car, $11.8\,\text{kN}$, so the deceleration is about $5\,\%$ of the free-fall acceleration, $0.5\,\text{m/s}^2$. A car rolling in neutral slows down slowly, which agrees with experience. As the speed decreases, the air resistance decreases, so the deceleration also becomes smaller later.

## fy4-14-L3-01
**Given:** $m = 72.0\,\text{kg}$, at first terminal velocity, after the change of position the air resistance is half of the earlier value at the same speed; $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the acceleration $a$ right after the change of position in $\text{m/s}^2$
**Principle:** At terminal velocity the air resistance is equal to the weight, because the net force is zero. Right after the change of position the speed is still the same, but the air resistance has decreased to half of the weight. The net force is no longer zero, and Newton's second law gives the acceleration.
**Free-body diagram:** The skydiver as a rectangle, positive direction down. Before the change, the weight $\overline{G}$ and the air resistance $\overline{F}_\text{v}$ are equally long. Right after the change, the weight from the centre down and the air resistance from the bottom surface up, half the length of the weight.
```voimakuvio
G 706,3
F_v 706,3 ylös työntö @ala
a 0
---
G 706,3
F_v 353,2 ylös työntö @ala
a alas
```
**Formula:** Before the change $F_\text{v} = mg$, so after the change $F_\text{v}' = \tfrac{1}{2}mg$. The equation of motion $mg - F_\text{v}' = ma$ gives $a = \dfrac{mg - \tfrac{1}{2}mg}{m} = \dfrac{g}{2}$
**Conversions:** The mass cancels out, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $a = \dfrac{9.81\,\text{m/s}^2}{2} = 4.905\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Answer:** $4.91\,\text{m/s}^2$ downward, three significant figures like the free-fall acceleration $9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$. The mass was not needed.
**Reasonableness:** Half of the weight is left uncancelled, so the acceleration is half of the free-fall acceleration. The skydiver starts to accelerate again until the air resistance has grown to the size of the weight in the new position, now at a greater terminal velocity.

## fy4-14-L3-02
**Given:** $m = 0.200\,\text{kg}$, air resistance $F_\text{v} = 0.600\,\text{N}$ both on the way up and on the way down; $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the difference of the magnitudes of the accelerations $\Delta a$ in $\text{m/s}^2$
**Principle:** Air resistance always points against the velocity. On the way up it points down together with the weight, on the way down up against the weight. Newton's second law gives the acceleration at both moments, and they are subtracted from each other.
**Free-body diagram:** Two diagrams, the ball as a rectangle. On the way up, the weight $\overline{G}$ from the centre down and the air resistance $\overline{F}_\text{v}$ from the top surface down. On the way down, the weight down and the air resistance from the bottom surface up. In both, the positive direction is down.
```voimakuvio
G 1,962
F_v 0,6 alas työntö @ylä
a alas
---
G 1,962
F_v 0,6 ylös työntö @ala
a alas
```
**Formula:** On the way up $mg + F_\text{v} = ma_1$, so $a_1 = g + \dfrac{F_\text{v}}{m}$. On the way down $mg - F_\text{v} = ma_2$, so $a_2 = g - \dfrac{F_\text{v}}{m}$. The difference is $\Delta a = a_1 - a_2 = \dfrac{2F_\text{v}}{m}$
**Conversions:** The quantities are in SI units, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $\Delta a = \dfrac{2\cdot 0.600\,\text{N}}{0.200\,\text{kg}} = 6.00\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Answer:** $6.00\,\text{m/s}^2$, three significant figures like the mass $0.200\,\text{kg}$ and the air resistance $0.600\,\text{N}$.
**Reasonableness:** Calculated separately, $a_1 = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 + 3.00\,\text{m/s}^2 = 12.81\,\text{m/s}^2$ and $a_2 = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 - 3.00\,\text{m/s}^2 = 6.81\,\text{m/s}^2$, and their difference is $6.00\,\text{m/s}^2$. Both accelerations point down, but on the way up it is greater than $g$ and on the way down smaller.

## fy4-14-L3-03
**Given:** $m = 0.150\,\text{kg}$, a graph of the speed of the ball as a function of time and a tangent at the point $P$; $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the air resistance $F_\text{v}$ at the moment corresponding to the point $P$, in $\text{N}$
**Principle:** The acceleration is the slope of the $(t,v)$ graph. The slope of the curve at a given moment is the slope of its tangent. When the acceleration is known, Newton's second law gives the air resistance, because only the weight and the air resistance act on the ball.
**Free-body diagram:** The ball as a rectangle, positive direction down. The weight $\overline{G}$ from the centre down and the air resistance $\overline{F}_\text{v}$ from the bottom surface up, shorter than the weight.
```voimakuvio
G 1,472
F_v 0,4965 ylös työntö @ala
a alas
```
**Reading the graph:** The tangent starts on the vertical axis at $(0;\, 2.0\,\text{m/s})$ and passes through the grid crossing $(2.0\,\text{s};\, 15.0\,\text{m/s})$. These points are far apart and on the grid lines, so they are read accurately. The slope is read from the tangent, not from points of the curve.
**Formula:** $a = \dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$ and $mg - F_\text{v} = ma$, so $F_\text{v} = m(g - a)$
**Conversions:** The speeds are in $\text{m/s}$, the times in seconds and the mass in kilograms, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $a = \dfrac{15.0\,\text{m/s} - 2.0\,\text{m/s}}{2.0\,\text{s} - 0\,\text{s}} = 6.5\,\text{m/s}^2$ and $F_\text{v} = 0.150\,\text{kg}\cdot(9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 - 6.5\,\text{m/s}^2) = 0.150\,\text{kg}\cdot 3.31\,\text{m/s}^2 = 0.4965\,\text{N}$
**Answer:** $0.50\,\text{N}$ upward, two significant figures, because the acceleration $6.5\,\text{m/s}^2$ read from the graph is given to two digits.
**Reasonableness:** The weight of the ball is $0.150\,\text{kg}\cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \approx 1.5\,\text{N}$. The acceleration is about two thirds of the free-fall acceleration, so the air resistance cancels about a third of the weight, about $0.5\,\text{N}$. Later the curve flattens, the air resistance grows and approaches the weight.
