Lesson 5 · Design and 3D print

A beam that holds

From model to print

Slicing in PrusaSlicer, print settings and using the printer.

In this lesson you learn: You slice a model and find out how infill and print orientation affect the beam's strength.

Start right away (5–10 min)

Open a ready-made beam in PrusaSlicer and change the infill. How do the print time and the mass change?

Getting started with 3D printing →

Try · Modify · Challenge yourself

  1. Try for everyone

    Print the beam with the teacher's settings. Rest its ends on two piles of books 10 cm apart, hang a bag in the middle and add water or coins until the beam bends clearly or breaks. If your own beam is not ready yet, test one the teacher printed in advance.

  2. Modify your own ideas

    Choose the infill and print orientation yourself and give your reasons. Before the test, guess whether your beam holds more or less than the teacher's beam.

  3. Challenge yourself optional

    Weigh the load the beam held and compare two beams: which carries more per its own mass? Show the comparison as a table.

If you have to wait

If the printer is busy, do the load test with a beam the teacher printed in advance. Meanwhile record from PrusaSlicer your own beam's print time and mass, and a guess whether it holds more or less. Your own beam is tested when it is ready.

What did you make?

Basic level: A printed beam, reasoned infill and orientation, and the load test result: how much the beam carried.

Challenge yourself: A table comparing two beams: load per own mass.

You need

  • computer and PrusaSlicer
  • 3D printer with the teacher's permission
  • beam model (starter file)
  • a bag and water or coins
  • kitchen scale and safety glasses

Step-by-step guide

  • Getting started with 3D printing

Starter files

  • Beam for the load test (STL, 120 × 12 × 6 mm) untested draft: the teacher prints or tries it before use
  • Load test table (CSV) spreadsheet template

Safety

Do not touch the printer's nozzle or bed during printing or right after it: they are hot. In the load test the beam can snap without warning. Wear safety glasses, keep the load close to the floor and your feet away from under it.
