## fy5-1-L1-01
**Given:** force $F = 150\,\text{N}$ perpendicular to the handle, distance of the point of application from the centre of the nut $d = 0.25\,\text{m}$
**Find:** moment $M$ of the force about the axis of the nut in the unit $\text{Nm}$
**Principle:** The nut rotates about its axis, so the turning effect of the force is given by its moment $M = Fr$. The force is perpendicular to the handle, so the lever arm is the whole distance to the point of application, $r = d$.
**Formula:** $M = Fr$ and $r = d$, so $M = Fd$
**Conversions:** The force is in newtons and the distance in metres, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $M = 150\,\text{N}\cdot 0.25\,\text{m} = 37.5\,\text{Nm}$
**Answer:** $37.5\,\text{Nm} \approx 38\,\text{Nm}$, two significant figures like the distance $0.25\,\text{m}$.
**Reasonableness:** With a quarter-metre handle the moment is a quarter of the numerical value of the force: $150 : 4 \approx 38$. The tightening moment of an ordinary nut is of the order of tens of newton metres, so the result is believable.

## fy5-1-L1-02
**Given:** push $F = 12\,\text{N}$ perpendicular to the door, distance of the handle from the hinges $d = 0.80\,\text{m}$
**Find:** moment $M$ of the force about the hinges in the unit $\text{Nm}$
**Principle:** The door rotates about the axis of rotation through the hinges, so the turning effect of the push is given by its moment. The push is perpendicular to the door, so the lever arm is the distance of the handle from the hinges.
**Formula:** $M = Fr$ and $r = d$, so $M = Fd$
**Conversions:** The force is in newtons and the distance in metres, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $M = 12\,\text{N}\cdot 0.80\,\text{m} = 9.6\,\text{Nm}$
**Answer:** $9.6\,\text{Nm}$, two significant figures like the force $12\,\text{N}$ and the distance $0.80\,\text{m}$.
**Reasonableness:** The lever arm is a little less than a metre, so the numerical value of the moment is a little smaller than the numerical value of the force, $12$. If the same force acted $0.10\,\text{m}$ from the hinges, the moment would be only $1.2\,\text{Nm}$, which explains why the handle is at the edge of the door.

## fy5-1-L1-03
**Given:** required moment $M = 45\,\text{Nm}$, distance of the point of application $d = 0.30\,\text{m}$, force perpendicular to the handle
**Find:** required force $F$ in the unit $\text{N}$
**Principle:** The tightness of the nut is decided by the moment $M = Fr$. The force is perpendicular to the handle, so the lever arm is $r = d$, and the force is solved from the formula for the moment.
**Formula:** $M = Fd$, so $F = \dfrac{M}{d}$
**Conversions:** The moment is in newton metres and the distance in metres, so the force comes directly in newtons.
**Substitution:** $F = \dfrac{45\,\text{Nm}}{0.30\,\text{m}} = 150\,\text{N}$
**Answer:** $150\,\text{N} = 1.5 \cdot 10^{2}\,\text{N}$, two significant figures like the moment $45\,\text{Nm}$ and the distance $0.30\,\text{m}$.
**Reasonableness:** Check: $150\,\text{N}\cdot 0.30\,\text{m} = 45\,\text{Nm}$. The force corresponds to the weight of a body of about $15\,\text{kg}$, which an adult can pull by hand. With a spanner half as long, twice the force would be needed.

## fy5-1-L1-04
**Given:** horizontal force $F = 40\,\text{N}$ at the point $P$; one square of the grid is $0.10\,\text{m}$
**Find:** moment $M$ of the force about the axis $O$ in the unit $\text{Nm}$
**Principle:** The rod rotates about the axis $O$, so the turning effect of the force is given by its moment $M = Fr$. The lever arm is the perpendicular distance of the axis from the line of action of the force, not the length $OP$ of the rod.
**Reading the figure:** The force is horizontal, so its line of action is the horizontal dashed line through the point $P$. The axis $O$ is three squares below this line, so the lever arm is three squares. The rod itself is slanted in the figure, four squares to the right and three up, so its length is five squares, but that is not the lever arm.
**Formula:** $M = Fr$
**Conversions:** The squares are converted to metres: $r = 3\cdot 0.10\,\text{m} = 0.30\,\text{m}$.
**Substitution:** $M = 40\,\text{N}\cdot 0.30\,\text{m} = 12\,\text{Nm}$
**Answer:** $12\,\text{Nm}$, two significant figures like the force $40\,\text{N}$; the lever arm is read from the grid exactly, in whole squares.
**Reasonableness:** If the length of the rod, $0.50\,\text{m}$, were taken as the lever arm, the moment would be $20\,\text{Nm}$, but that would hold only for a perpendicular force. The horizontal force pulls the rod partly along its own direction, so the moment is smaller. The same result is obtained with the formula $r = d\sin\alpha$: $0.50\,\text{m}\cdot\tfrac{3}{5} = 0.30\,\text{m}$.

## fy5-1-L1-05
**Given:** force $F = 60\,\text{N}$, moment $M = 18\,\text{Nm}$
**Find:** lever arm $r$ of the force in the unit $\text{m}$
**Principle:** The moment is the product of the force and the lever arm, $M = Fr$. When the moment and the force are known, the lever arm is solved from the same formula.
**Formula:** $M = Fr$, so $r = \dfrac{M}{F}$
**Conversions:** The moment is in newton metres and the force in newtons, so the lever arm comes directly in metres: $\tfrac{\text{Nm}}{\text{N}} = \text{m}$.
**Substitution:** $r = \dfrac{18\,\text{Nm}}{60\,\text{N}} = 0.30\,\text{m}$
**Answer:** $0.30\,\text{m}$, two significant figures like the moment $18\,\text{Nm}$ and the force $60\,\text{N}$.
**Reasonableness:** Check: $60\,\text{N}\cdot 0.30\,\text{m} = 18\,\text{Nm}$. The numerical value of the moment is smaller than that of the force, so the lever arm must be less than a metre.

## fy5-1-L2-01
**Given:** tension of the string $F = 40\,\text{N}$, distance of the handle from the hinges $d = 0.75\,\text{m}$, angle of the string to the plane of the door $\alpha = 35^\circ$
**Find:** moment $M$ of the string's force about the hinges in the unit $\text{Nm}$
**Principle:** The door rotates about the hinges, so the turning effect of the string is given by its moment $M = Fr$. The string is oblique to the door, so the lever arm is shorter than $d$. The plane of the door runs from the hinges to the handle, so the angle $35^\circ$ is the angle between the force and the extension of the arm, and $r = d\sin\alpha$.
**Formula:** $M = Fr$ and $r = d\sin\alpha$, so $M = Fd\sin\alpha$
**Conversions:** The force is in newtons and the distance in metres, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $M = 40\,\text{N}\cdot 0.75\,\text{m}\cdot\sin 35^\circ = 30\,\text{Nm}\cdot 0.5736 = 17.21\,\text{Nm}$
**Answer:** $17\,\text{Nm}$, two significant figures like the force $40\,\text{N}$ and the distance $0.75\,\text{m}$.
**Reasonableness:** A perpendicular pull would give $40\,\text{N}\cdot 0.75\,\text{m} = 30\,\text{Nm}$. An oblique string gives less, because $\sin 35^\circ \approx 0.57$, a little more than half. The part of the string's force along the door pulls the door towards the hinges and does not rotate it.

## fy5-1-L2-02
**Given:** force $F = 85\,\text{N}$ perpendicular to the handle, distance of the point of application $d = 35\,\text{cm}$
**Find:** moment $M$ of the force about the axis of the nut in the unit $\text{Nm}$
**Principle:** The nut rotates about its axis, so the turning effect of the force is given by its moment $M = Fr$. The force is perpendicular to the handle, so the lever arm is the whole distance to the point of application.
**Formula:** $M = Fr$ and $r = d$, so $M = Fd$
**Conversions:** The distance is converted to metres so that the moment comes in newton metres: $d = 35\,\text{cm} = 35\cdot 0.01\,\text{m} = 0.35\,\text{m}$.
**Substitution:** $M = 85\,\text{N}\cdot 0.35\,\text{m} = 29.75\,\text{Nm}$
**Answer:** $30\,\text{Nm}$, two significant figures like the force $85\,\text{N}$ and the distance $35\,\text{cm}$.
**Reasonableness:** Without the conversion the result would be $2975$, which would be a hundred times too large: centimetres must always be converted to metres. With a handle of about a third of a metre, the moment is about a third of the numerical value of the force, $85 : 3 \approx 28$, which is close to the result.

## fy5-1-L2-03
**Given:** force $F_1 = 30\,\text{N}$ upwards at the distance $r_1 = 0.50\,\text{m}$ from the axis, force $F_2 = 40\,\text{N}$ downwards at the distance $r_2 = 0.20\,\text{m}$; the board is horizontal and the axis is at its left end
**Find:** net moment $\Sigma M$ about the axis in the unit $\text{Nm}$, anticlockwise positive
**Principle:** The board rotates about the axis, and its rotation is decided by the net moment, which is the sum of the moments of the forces with their signs. Both forces are vertical and the board is horizontal, so the lever arms are the distances from the axis. On the right-hand side of the axis, an upward force rotates the board anticlockwise and a downward force clockwise.
**Formula:** $M_1 = F_1 r_1$ and $M_2 = -F_2 r_2$, so $\Sigma M = F_1 r_1 - F_2 r_2$
**Conversions:** The forces are in newtons and the distances in metres, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $\Sigma M = 30\,\text{N}\cdot 0.50\,\text{m} - 40\,\text{N}\cdot 0.20\,\text{m} = 15\,\text{Nm} - 8.0\,\text{Nm} = 7.0\,\text{Nm}$
**Answer:** $7.0\,\text{Nm}$ anticlockwise, two significant figures like the data.
**Reasonableness:** The moment of the weight of the board itself is ignored: the board is assumed to be light compared with the forces. The larger force $40\,\text{N}$ does not decide the direction of rotation, because its lever arm is short. The moment of the smaller force, $15\,\text{Nm}$, is greater than $8.0\,\text{Nm}$, so the board starts to rotate anticlockwise. The net moment is positive, as it should be.

## fy5-1-L2-04
**Given:** required moment $M = 50\,\text{Nm}$, length of the lever, that is the distance of the point of application from the axis, $d = 0.25\,\text{m}$, angle between the force and the extension of the lever $\alpha = 60^\circ$
**Find:** required force $F$ in the unit $\text{N}$
**Principle:** Turning the valve is decided by the moment $M = Fr$. The force is oblique to the lever, so its lever arm is $r = d\sin\alpha$, shorter than the length of the lever. The force is solved from the formula for the moment.
**Formula:** $M = Fd\sin\alpha$, so $F = \dfrac{M}{d\sin\alpha}$
**Conversions:** The moment is in newton metres and the distance in metres, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $F = \dfrac{50\,\text{Nm}}{0.25\,\text{m}\cdot\sin 60^\circ} = \dfrac{50\,\text{Nm}}{0.2165\,\text{m}} = 230.9\,\text{N}$
**Answer:** $231\,\text{N} \approx 2.3 \cdot 10^{2}\,\text{N}$, two significant figures like the moment $50\,\text{Nm}$ and the length $0.25\,\text{m}$.
**Reasonableness:** With a perpendicular pull, $50\,\text{Nm} : 0.25\,\text{m} = 200\,\text{N}$ would be enough. An oblique pull needs more, because the lever arm is only $\sin 60^\circ \approx 0.87$ times the length of the lever: $200\,\text{N} : 0.87 \approx 230\,\text{N}$, the same as $231\,\text{N}$ to the accuracy of the roundings.

## fy5-1-L3-01
**Given:** force of the foot $F = 500\,\text{N}$ straight down, distance of the pedal from the axis $d = 0.175\,\text{m}$, crank $45^\circ$ below the horizontal
**Find:** moment $M$ of the force about the axis of the crank in the unit $\text{Nm}$, and the positions in which the moment is greatest and zero
**Principle:** The crank rotates about its axis, so the turning effect of the foot's force is given by its moment $M = Fr$. The force is vertical, so its line of action is the vertical line through the pedal, and the lever arm is the horizontal distance of the pedal from the axis. It is greatest when the crank is horizontal ($r = d$) and zero when the crank is vertical, because then the line of action passes through the axis. In the position $45^\circ$ the horizontal distance is the side adjacent to the angle.
**Formula:** $M = Fr$ and $r = d\cos 45^\circ$, so $M = Fd\cos 45^\circ$
**Conversions:** The force is in newtons and the distance in metres, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $M = 500\,\text{N}\cdot 0.175\,\text{m}\cdot\cos 45^\circ = 87.5\,\text{Nm}\cdot 0.7071 = 61.87\,\text{Nm}$
**Answer:** $61.9\,\text{Nm}$, three significant figures like the distance $0.175\,\text{m}$. The moment is greatest, $87.5\,\text{Nm}$, when the crank is horizontal pointing forwards, and zero when the crank points straight down or up.
**Reasonableness:** The result lies between the moment of a horizontal crank, $87.5\,\text{Nm}$, and zero, as it should. The formula $r = d\sin\alpha$ gives the same: the angle between the force and the extension of the crank is $45^\circ$, and $\sin 45^\circ = \cos 45^\circ$. This is why a cyclist presses the pedal hardest when the crank is horizontal at the front, that is, pointing in the direction of travel.

## fy5-1-L3-02
**Given:** adult's force $F_1 = 50\,\text{N}$ and lever arm $r_1 = 0.40\,\text{m}$, child's force $F_2 = 20\,\text{N}$; both push perpendicular to the gate
**Find:** distance $r_2$ of the child's push from the hinges in the unit $\text{m}$
**Principle:** The turning effects are equal when the moments about the hinges are equal. The lever arm of a perpendicular push is the distance of the pushing point from the hinges, so a smaller force needs a longer lever arm.
**Formula:** $F_2 r_2 = F_1 r_1$, so $r_2 = \dfrac{F_1 r_1}{F_2}$
**Conversions:** The forces are in newtons and the lever arm in metres, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $r_2 = \dfrac{50\,\text{N}\cdot 0.40\,\text{m}}{20\,\text{N}} = \dfrac{20\,\text{Nm}}{20\,\text{N}} = 1.0\,\text{m}$
**Answer:** $1.0\,\text{m}$, two significant figures like the forces and the distance $0.40\,\text{m}$.
**Reasonableness:** The child's force is $\tfrac{2}{5}$ of the adult's, so the lever arm must be $\tfrac{5}{2}$ times as long: $2.5\cdot 0.40\,\text{m} = 1.0\,\text{m}$. An ordinary gate is about a metre wide, so the child has to push right at the edge of the gate.

## fy5-1-L3-03
**Given:** force of each hand $F = 120\,\text{N}$, the left one downwards and the right one upwards, distance of each point of application from the centre of the nut $d = 0.20\,\text{m}$; the arms are horizontal
**Find:** sum of the hands' forces and net moment $\Sigma M$ about the axis of the nut in the unit $\text{Nm}$
**Principle:** The forces are equal and opposite, so their sum is zero and the wheel brace does not move sideways. The rotation, however, is decided by the net moment. Both forces are vertical and the arms horizontal, so the lever arm of each is $d$. On the left of the axis a downward force and on the right of the axis an upward force both rotate anticlockwise, so the moments have the same sign and add up.
**Formula:** $\Sigma F = F - F$ and $\Sigma M = Fd + Fd$, so $\Sigma M = 2Fd$
**Conversions:** The forces are in newtons and the distances in metres, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $\Sigma F = 120\,\text{N} - 120\,\text{N} = 0$ and $\Sigma M = 2\cdot 120\,\text{N}\cdot 0.20\,\text{m} = 48\,\text{Nm}$
**Answer:** The sum of the forces is zero, and the net moment is $48\,\text{Nm}$ anticlockwise, two significant figures like the distance $0.20\,\text{m}$.
**Reasonableness:** One hand would produce $24\,\text{Nm}$, so two hands rotating in the same direction give twice the moment. Because the sum of the forces is zero, the wheel brace only rotates and does not push the bolt sideways, which is the advantage of a wheel brace over turning with one hand.
