## fy4-2-L1-01
**Given:** initial velocity $v_0 = 0$ (from rest), final velocity $v = 25\,\text{m/s}$, time $\Delta t = 8.0\,\text{s}$; the acceleration is uniform
**Find:** the car's acceleration $a$ in $\text{m/s}^2$
**Principle:** Acceleration is the change in velocity divided by the time it took. The acceleration is uniform, so the velocity grows by the same amount every second and the average over the whole interval equals the acceleration at every moment.
**Formula:** $a = \dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$, where $\Delta v = v - v_0$. So $a = \dfrac{v - v_0}{\Delta t}$
**Conversions:** The velocity is in $\text{m/s}$ and the time in seconds, so the result comes out directly in $\text{m/s}^2$. No conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $a = \dfrac{25\,\text{m/s} - 0\,\text{m/s}}{8.0\,\text{s}} = 3.125\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Answer:** $3.1\,\text{m/s}^2$, two significant figures like the velocity $25\,\text{m/s}$ and the time $8.0\,\text{s}$.
**Reasonableness:** The velocity grows by about $3\,\text{m/s}$ every second, and $8 \cdot 3\,\text{m/s} = 24\,\text{m/s}$, which is close to the given $25\,\text{m/s}$. Reaching $25\,\text{m/s} = 90\,\text{km/h}$ in eight seconds is a brisk acceleration for an ordinary passenger car. The acceleration is positive because the velocity grows.

## fy4-2-L1-02
**Given:** initial velocity $v_0 = 6.0\,\text{m/s}$, acceleration $a = -1.5\,\text{m/s}^2$, time $t = 3.0\,\text{s}$; the direction of motion is positive
**Find:** the velocity $v$ at $t = 3.0\,\text{s}$ in $\text{m/s}$
**Principle:** The acceleration of braking is constant and opposite to the velocity, so the velocity decreases by $1.5\,\text{m/s}$ every second. In uniformly accelerated motion the velocity is the initial velocity plus the product of the acceleration and the time.
**Formula:** $v = v_0 + a\,t$. The formula is already solved for the velocity.
**Conversions:** All quantities are in SI units, and $\text{m/s}^2 \cdot \text{s} = \text{m/s}$. No conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $v = 6.0\,\text{m/s} + (-1.5\,\text{m/s}^2) \cdot 3.0\,\text{s} = 6.0\,\text{m/s} - 4.5\,\text{m/s} = 1.5\,\text{m/s}$
**Answer:** $1.5\,\text{m/s}$, two significant figures like the given values. The difference in the calculation is known to the nearest tenth.
**Reasonableness:** The cyclist would stop in the time $6.0\,\text{m/s} : 1.5\,\text{m/s}^2 = 4.0\,\text{s}$, so at $3.0\,\text{s}$ she is still moving slowly forward. The velocity is positive and smaller than at the start, as it should be in braking.

## fy4-2-L1-03
**Given:** initial velocity $v_0 = 0$, time $t = 1.50\,\text{s}$, acceleration of free fall from the table $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$; air resistance is small
**Find:** the distance fallen $s$ in metres
**Principle:** Air resistance is small, so the stone falls with the constant acceleration $g$. Take downwards as the positive direction. The stone starts from rest, so the displacement is the area of the triangle in the $(t,v)$ graph, $\tfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2$.
**Formula:** $x = x_0 + v_0\,t + \tfrac{1}{2}\,a\,t^2$, where $x - x_0 = s$, $v_0 = 0$ and $a = g$. So $s = \dfrac{g\,t^2}{2}$
**Conversions:** All quantities are in SI units, and $\text{m/s}^2 \cdot \text{s}^2 = \text{m}$. No conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $s = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot (1.50\,\text{s})^2 = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 2.25\,\text{s}^2 \approx 11.04\,\text{m}$
**Answer:** $11.0\,\text{m}$, three significant figures like the time $1.50\,\text{s}$.
**Reasonableness:** The final velocity is $9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 1.50\,\text{s} \approx 14.7\,\text{m/s}$, so the average velocity is about $7.4\,\text{m/s}$ and $7.4\,\text{m/s} \cdot 1.50\,\text{s} \approx 11\,\text{m}$. In the first second the stone falls about $5\,\text{m}$, so a good $10\,\text{m}$ in one and a half seconds is plausible.

## fy4-2-L1-04
**Given:** from the graph, the velocity $v_1 = 2.0\,\text{m/s}$ at $t_1 = 0$ and $v_2 = 7.0\,\text{m/s}$ at $t_2 = 5.0\,\text{s}$; the graph is a straight line
**Find:** the scooter's acceleration $a$ in $\text{m/s}^2$
**Principle:** The $(t,v)$ graph is a straight line, so the acceleration is constant and equals the slope of the line, that is, the change in velocity divided by the change in time.
**Reading the graph:** The end points of the line are at crossings of grid lines: the starting point $(0;\, 2.0\,\text{m/s})$ and the end point $(5.0\,\text{s};\, 7.0\,\text{m/s})$. Points far apart give the most accurate slope.
**Formula:** $a = \dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{v_2 - v_1}{t_2 - t_1}$
**Conversions:** The velocities are in $\text{m/s}$ and the times in seconds, so the result comes out in $\text{m/s}^2$. No conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $a = \dfrac{7.0\,\text{m/s} - 2.0\,\text{m/s}}{5.0\,\text{s} - 0\,\text{s}} = 1.0\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Answer:** $1.0\,\text{m/s}^2$, two significant figures like the values read from the graph.
**Reasonableness:** In the graph the velocity grows by one $\text{m/s}$ square for every one-second square, so the acceleration $1\,\text{m/s}^2$ can be seen straight from the grid. The acceleration is positive because the line rises.

## fy4-2-L1-05
**Given:** initial velocity $v_0 = 0$, acceleration $a = 0.40\,\text{m/s}^2$, time $t = 30\,\text{s}$
**Find:** the distance travelled $\Delta x$ in metres
**Principle:** The train accelerates uniformly from rest and does not turn round, so the distance is the displacement $\Delta x = v_0\,t + \tfrac{1}{2}\,a\,t^2$, in which the term of the initial velocity is zero.
**Formula:** $x = x_0 + v_0\,t + \tfrac{1}{2}\,a\,t^2$, so $v_0 = 0$ gives $\Delta x = x - x_0 = \tfrac{1}{2}\,a\,t^2$
**Conversions:** All quantities are in SI units, and $\text{m/s}^2 \cdot \text{s}^2 = \text{m}$. No conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $\Delta x = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 0.40\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot (30\,\text{s})^2 = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 0.40\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 900\,\text{s}^2 = 180\,\text{m}$
**Answer:** $180\,\text{m}$ or $1.8 \cdot 10^2\,\text{m}$, two significant figures like the acceleration $0.40\,\text{m/s}^2$ and the time $30\,\text{s}$.
**Reasonableness:** The final velocity is $0.40\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 30\,\text{s} = 12\,\text{m/s}$, so the average velocity is $6.0\,\text{m/s}$ and $6.0\,\text{m/s} \cdot 30\,\text{s} = 180\,\text{m}$. The final velocity $12\,\text{m/s} = 12 \cdot 3.6\,\text{km/h} \approx 43\,\text{km/h}$ is an ordinary speed for a train leaving a station, and $180\,\text{m}$ is about two lengths of a sprint track ($100\,\text{m}$), roughly the length of a station platform.

## fy4-2-L2-01
**Given:** initial velocity $v_0 = 72\,\text{km/h}$, final velocity $v = 0$, braking time $t = 4.0\,\text{s}$; the braking is uniform
**Find:** the braking distance $\Delta x$ in metres
**Principle:** The braking is uniform, so the acceleration is constant. The acceleration comes from the change in velocity and the time, and then the distance comes from the equation of position. The car does not turn round, so the distance equals the displacement.
**Formula:** $a = \dfrac{v - v_0}{t}$ and $\Delta x = v_0\,t + \tfrac{1}{2}\,a\,t^2$
**Conversions:** The velocity is converted by substituting units: $72\,\text{km/h} = 72 \cdot \tfrac{1000\,\text{m}}{3600\,\text{s}} = 20\,\text{m/s}$.
**Substitution:** $a = \dfrac{0 - 20\,\text{m/s}}{4.0\,\text{s}} = -5.0\,\text{m/s}^2$ and $\Delta x = 20\,\text{m/s} \cdot 4.0\,\text{s} + \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot (-5.0\,\text{m/s}^2) \cdot (4.0\,\text{s})^2 = 80\,\text{m} - 40\,\text{m} = 40\,\text{m}$
**Answer:** $40\,\text{m}$, two significant figures like the velocity $72\,\text{km/h}$ and the time $4.0\,\text{s}$.
**Reasonableness:** The velocity decreases steadily from $20\,\text{m/s}$ to zero, so the average velocity is $10\,\text{m/s}$ and $10\,\text{m/s} \cdot 4.0\,\text{s} = 40\,\text{m}$, the same result. The magnitude of the acceleration, $5\,\text{m/s}^2$, is about half of $g$, which is an ordinary hard braking on a dry road.

## fy4-2-L2-02
**Given:** from the graph, the points of the tangent $(t_1;\, x_1) = (2.0\,\text{s};\, 0\,\text{m})$ and $(t_2;\, x_2) = (6.0\,\text{s};\, 16\,\text{m})$; the tangent is drawn at $t = 4.0\,\text{s}$
**Find:** the instantaneous velocity $v$ at $4.0\,\text{s}$ in $\text{m/s}$
**Principle:** The $(t,x)$ graph is a curve because the velocity changes. The velocity at a given moment is the slope of the tangent of the curve at that point. The slope is calculated from the tangent, not from points of the curve.
**Reading the graph:** The tangent crosses the time axis at $t = 2.0\,\text{s}$, where $x = 0$, and passes through the grid crossing $(6.0\,\text{s};\, 16\,\text{m})$. These points are far apart and are read exactly.
**Formula:** $v = \dfrac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{x_2 - x_1}{t_2 - t_1}$
**Conversions:** The positions are in metres and the times in seconds, so the result comes out in $\text{m/s}$. No conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $v = \dfrac{16\,\text{m} - 0\,\text{m}}{6.0\,\text{s} - 2.0\,\text{s}} = 4.0\,\text{m/s}$
**Answer:** $4.0\,\text{m/s}$, two significant figures like the values read from the graph.
**Reasonableness:** In the graph the cart is at the position $8.0\,\text{m}$ at $4.0\,\text{s}$, so its average velocity from the start is $2.0\,\text{m/s}$. For an object accelerating uniformly from rest the final velocity is twice the average velocity, that is, $4.0\,\text{m/s}$, the same as from the tangent.

## fy4-2-L2-03
**Given:** initial velocity $v_0 = 15.0\,\text{m/s}$ upwards, acceleration of free fall from the table $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$ downwards; air resistance is small
**Find:** the height of the rise $h$ from the point of release in metres
**Principle:** Take upwards as the positive direction, so the acceleration is $-g$ during the whole flight. At the highest point the velocity is zero for a moment. First solve the time of the rise from the condition $v = 0$, then the height from the equation of position.
**Formula:** $v = v_0 - g\,t = 0$, so $t = \dfrac{v_0}{g}$, and the height is $h = v_0\,t - \dfrac{g\,t^2}{2}$
**Conversions:** All quantities are in SI units. No conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $t = \dfrac{15.0\,\text{m/s}}{9.81\,\text{m/s}^2} \approx 1.529\,\text{s}$ and $h = 15.0\,\text{m/s} \cdot 1.529\,\text{s} - \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot (1.529\,\text{s})^2 \approx 22.94\,\text{m} - 11.47\,\text{m} \approx 11.47\,\text{m}$
**Answer:** $11.5\,\text{m}$, three significant figures like the initial velocity $15.0\,\text{m/s}$.
**Reasonableness:** During the rise the velocity decreases steadily from $15.0\,\text{m/s}$ to zero, so the average velocity is $7.5\,\text{m/s}$ and $7.5\,\text{m/s} \cdot 1.529\,\text{s} \approx 11.5\,\text{m}$. A height of about three storeys is plausible for a hard throw.

## fy4-2-L2-04
**Given:** drop height $h = 1.60\,\text{m}$, fall time $t = 1.41\,\text{s}$, initial velocity $v_0 = 0$; there is no air resistance on the Moon
**Find:** the Moon's acceleration of free fall $g_\text{M}$ in $\text{m/s}^2$
**Principle:** There is no air resistance, so the hammer falls with constant acceleration. It starts from rest, so the drop height is $\tfrac{1}{2}\,g_\text{M}\,t^2$. Solve the acceleration from this.
**Formula:** $h = \tfrac{1}{2}\,g_\text{M}\,t^2$, so $g_\text{M} = \dfrac{2h}{t^2}$
**Conversions:** The height is in metres and the time in seconds, so the result comes out in $\text{m/s}^2$. No conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $g_\text{M} = \dfrac{2 \cdot 1.60\,\text{m}}{(1.41\,\text{s})^2} = \dfrac{3.20\,\text{m}}{1.9881\,\text{s}^2} \approx 1.610\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Answer:** $1.61\,\text{m/s}^2$, three significant figures like the height $1.60\,\text{m}$ and the time $1.41\,\text{s}$.
**Reasonableness:** The table value for the Moon's acceleration of free fall is $1.62\,\text{m/s}^2$, about one sixth of the Earth's value, so the result is close to it. On Earth the same drop would take only $\sqrt{2 \cdot 1.60\,\text{m} : 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2} \approx 0.57\,\text{s}$; the slower fall on the Moon corresponds to the smaller acceleration.

## fy4-2-L3-01
**Given:** initial velocity $v_0 = 8.00\,\text{m/s}$ upwards, point of release $6.00\,\text{m}$ above the ground, acceleration of free fall from the table $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$; air resistance is small
**Find:** the time $t$ from the throw to hitting the ground, in seconds
**Principle:** The ball's acceleration is $g$ downwards during the whole flight, also at the highest point, so the whole flight is one uniformly accelerated motion. Place the origin at the point of release and take upwards as positive. The ground is then at the position $y = -6.00\,\text{m}$, and the time comes from a quadratic equation.
**Formula:** $y = v_0\,t - \tfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2$. When $y = -h$: $\tfrac{1}{2}\,g\,t^2 - v_0\,t - h = 0$, whose positive solution is $t = \dfrac{v_0 + \sqrt{v_0^2 + 2gh}}{g}$
**Conversions:** All quantities are in SI units. No conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** Under the root $v_0^2 + 2gh = (8.00\,\text{m/s})^2 + 2 \cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 6.00\,\text{m} = 181.72\,\text{m}^2/\text{s}^2$, and $\sqrt{181.72\,\text{m}^2/\text{s}^2} \approx 13.48\,\text{m/s}$. So $t = \dfrac{8.00\,\text{m/s} + 13.48\,\text{m/s}}{9.81\,\text{m/s}^2} \approx 2.19\,\text{s}$
**Answer:** $2.19\,\text{s}$, three significant figures like the initial velocity and the height. The other solution, $t \approx -0.56\,\text{s}$, is rejected because it is before the throw.
**Reasonableness:** The rise takes $8.00\,\text{m/s} : 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \approx 0.82\,\text{s}$ and lifts the ball $3.26\,\text{m}$, so from the highest point to the ground it is $9.26\,\text{m}$. A fall from rest takes $\sqrt{2 \cdot 9.26\,\text{m} : 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2} \approx 1.37\,\text{s}$, and $0.82\,\text{s} + 1.37\,\text{s} = 2.19\,\text{s}$, the same result.

## fy4-2-L3-02
**Given:** acceleration $0 \to 15.0\,\text{m/s}$ in $t_1 = 20.0\,\text{s}$, constant speed $v = 15.0\,\text{m/s}$ for $t_2 = 60.0\,\text{s}$, braking to zero in $t_3 = 15.0\,\text{s}$
**Find:** the distance between the stations $s$ in metres
**Principle:** The train always moves in the same direction, so the distance is the area under the $(t,v)$ graph. The graph is a triangle (accelerating), a rectangle (constant speed) and a triangle (braking). The accelerations do not need to be calculated, because the areas come directly from the velocity and the times.
**Formula:** $s = \tfrac{1}{2}\,v\,t_1 + v\,t_2 + \tfrac{1}{2}\,v\,t_3$
**Conversions:** The velocity is in $\text{m/s}$ and the times in seconds, so the result comes out in metres. No conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $s = \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 15.0\,\text{m/s} \cdot 20.0\,\text{s} + 15.0\,\text{m/s} \cdot 60.0\,\text{s} + \tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 15.0\,\text{m/s} \cdot 15.0\,\text{s} = 150\,\text{m} + 900\,\text{m} + 112.5\,\text{m} = 1162.5\,\text{m}$
**Answer:** $1160\,\text{m}$ or $1.16\,\text{km}$, three significant figures like the velocity and the times. The result $1162.5\,\text{m}$ is rounded to three significant figures, so the zero at the end of the number is not significant; in kilometres this is clearer.
**Reasonableness:** If the train travelled the whole $95\,\text{s}$ at $15.0\,\text{m/s}$, the distance would be $1425\,\text{m}$; the acceleration and the braking take away half of their own shares, $150\,\text{m} + 112.5\,\text{m}$, so $1425\,\text{m} - 262.5\,\text{m} = 1162.5\,\text{m}$. A distance of a little over a kilometre between stations is usual for a metro.

## fy4-2-L3-03
**Given:** the fitted polynomial $y = -4.86\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2} \cdot t^2 + 3.10\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}} \cdot t + 1.20\,\text{m}$, upwards positive; the moment $t = 0.500\,\text{s}$
**Find:** the ball's velocity $v$ with its sign in $\text{m/s}$ at $0.500\,\text{s}$
**Principle:** The polynomial is compared with the equation of position of uniformly accelerated motion, $y = y_0 + v_0\,t + \tfrac{1}{2}\,a\,t^2$. From the coefficients we read the initial velocity $v_0 = 3.10\,\text{m/s}$ and $\tfrac{1}{2}\,a = -4.86\,\text{m/s}^2$, so $a = -9.72\,\text{m/s}^2$. The velocity then comes from the equation $v = v_0 + a\,t$.
**Formula:** From the coefficients of the polynomial $y = A\,t^2 + B\,t + C$ we get $v_0 = B$ and $a = 2A$, so $v = B + 2A\,t$
**Conversions:** The coefficients are in SI units and the time in seconds. No conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $v = 3.10\,\text{m/s} + 2 \cdot (-4.86\,\text{m/s}^2) \cdot 0.500\,\text{s} = 3.10\,\text{m/s} - 4.86\,\text{m/s} = -1.76\,\text{m/s}$
**Answer:** $-1.76\,\text{m/s}$, three significant figures, because the coefficients of the polynomial and the moment $0.500\,\text{s}$ are given to three significant figures. The negative sign means that the ball is already moving downwards.
**Reasonableness:** The acceleration obtained from the measurement, $-9.72\,\text{m/s}^2$, is close to $-g$, as it should be. The ball is at its highest point at the time $3.10\,\text{m/s} : 9.72\,\text{m/s}^2 \approx 0.32\,\text{s}$, so at $0.500\,\text{s}$ it is already descending and the velocity must be negative. The descent has lasted $0.18\,\text{s}$, and $9.72\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 0.18\,\text{s} \approx 1.8\,\text{m/s}$.
