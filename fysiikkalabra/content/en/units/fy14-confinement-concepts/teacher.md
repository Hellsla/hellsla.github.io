## Timing (75 minutes)

- **0–6 min, Think about it.** Read the hook; one minute of pair talk on the question. The review question recalls open ends from the magnetic confinement unit.
- **6–11 min, Predict.** Both predictions individually, each with a reason. No answers yet: the feedback opens with the theory sections.
- **11–30 min, Investigate.** Pairs: NIF's shot table and the target gain of the record shot; the sorting of statements into results, plans and claims; then the pair discussion with the word bank and sentence starters.
- **30–47 min, Theory summary.** Read the four sections in two halves: pinches and inertial confinement together on the board; then pairs split *Spherical tokamaks and stronger magnets* and *Private concepts* and tell each other the main points. The vocabulary match; back to the predictions.
- **47–62 min, Apply.** The worked example (NIF energy accounting) on the board; the needed-gain task individually; the matching of concepts and problems; the misconception text.
- **62–75 min, Check your understanding.** Three checks individually, the funding argument with its sentence starters, the summary and the French corner.

## Organisation and preparation
- One device per student or per pair. The sorting task and the funding argument work well with mixed pairs: one reads the statement aloud, the other decides and gives a reason.
- Optional: show a short official video or press release of the NIF result of December 2022 and ask students to find the sentence that mentions the electricity the lasers use (if it is there at all).
- The dates of companies change quickly. Before the lesson, check the companies' latest statements (the unit's sources give the state on 1 November 2026) and tell students what has changed: this is a useful lesson in itself.

## Plan for fast pairs and for running out of time
- **Fast pairs:** the optional error-spotting task; then find one more fusion company on the web, write one of its claims, and decide what evidence would turn it into a result.
- **Running out of time:** set the French corner and check 3 as homework; in Theory, read only the key idea and the summary of *Private concepts* aloud.

## Assessment
- Written answers are self-assessed against the criteria shown after the student has written something. For a grade: the discussion separates a measured result from a claim and says what evidence is missing; the funding argument names a concept, one correct advantage and one correct limitation, and separates a result from a plan with a date or a number.
- Calculation: the target gain as a ratio; the needed gain with the electricity, not the laser energy, in the numerator.
- Language: argument structure (I would …, One advantage …, On the other hand …) and the words result, plan, claim, evidence used correctly.

## Common difficulties
- Target gain versus the energy balance of the whole facility; scientific gain $Q$ (magnetic machines) and target gain $G$ (lasers) are both *not* electricity.
- "Z" and "theta" are directions: along the axis $z$ or around it (angle $\theta$).
- Treating a company's date as a fact. Ask: "Who says so, and has it happened yet?"
- Spherical tokamaks are not spheres: the plasma is still a ring, with a small hole in the middle.

## Extension ideas
- Read LLNL's list of ignition shots and plot the fusion energy against the date. Has the laser energy changed much?
- France's Laser Mégajoule near Bordeaux: what is it for, and how does it compare with NIF?
- Find out what "magnetized target fusion" means, and why it lies between magnetic and inertial confinement.
