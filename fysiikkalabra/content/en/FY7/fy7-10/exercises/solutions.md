## fy7-10-L1-01
**Given:** speed of light in ethanol $v = 2.20 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$, speed of light in a vacuum $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$
**Find:** the refractive index $n$ of ethanol (no unit)
**Principle:** The refractive index of a material tells how many times more slowly light travels in the material than in a vacuum. When the speed of light in the material is known, the refractive index follows directly from its definition.
**Formula:** $n = \dfrac{c}{v}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: both speeds are in $\text{m/s}$, so the units cancel.
**Substitution:** $n = \dfrac{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}}{2.20 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}} = 1.3636$
**Answer:** $n \approx 1.36$, three significant figures like the speeds.
**Reasonableness:** The refractive index is greater than $1$, as it should be in a material, and slightly greater than that of water, $1.33$. According to the table, the refractive index of ethanol is $1.36$.

## fy7-10-L1-02
**Given:** refractive index of diamond $n = 2.42$, speed of light in a vacuum $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$
**Find:** the speed of light in diamond $v$ in the form $\ldots \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$
**Principle:** The refractive index is the ratio of the speed of light in a vacuum to that in the material, $n = c : v$. When the refractive index is known, the speed in the material is found by solving $v$ from the equation.
**Formula:** $n = \dfrac{c}{v}$, so $v = \dfrac{c}{n}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the refractive index has no unit, so $v$ comes out in the same unit as $c$.
**Substitution:** $v = \dfrac{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}}{2.42} = 1.2397 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$
**Answer:** $v \approx 1.24 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$, so the answer is $1.24$; three significant figures like the refractive index.
**Reasonableness:** In diamond light travels at less than half the speed in a vacuum, because the refractive index is over $2$, so $3.00 : 2 = 1.5$, and the result is a little smaller than this.

## fy7-10-L1-03
**Given:** angle between the incident and the reflected ray $76^\circ$, plane mirror
**Find:** the angle of incidence $\alpha_1$ in degrees
**Principle:** According to the law of reflection, the angle of reflection is equal to the angle of incidence, $\beta = \alpha_1$, when both are measured from the normal. The normal is halfway between the incident and the reflected ray, so the angle between the rays is $\alpha_1 + \beta = 2\alpha_1$.
**Formula:** $\alpha_1 + \beta = 76^\circ$ and $\beta = \alpha_1$, so $2\alpha_1 = 76^\circ$ and $\alpha_1 = \dfrac{76^\circ}{2}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the angles are in degrees.
**Substitution:** $\alpha_1 = \dfrac{76^\circ}{2} = 38^\circ$
**Answer:** $\alpha_1 = 38^\circ$, two significant figures like the angle $76^\circ$.
**Reasonableness:** The angle of reflection is also $38^\circ$, and $38^\circ + 38^\circ = 76^\circ$. If the angle of incidence were measured from the surface of the mirror, it would be $90^\circ - 38^\circ = 52^\circ$; the angle measured from the normal is the right one, because the law of reflection concerns angles measured from the normal.

## fy7-10-L1-04
**Given:** angle of incidence $\alpha_1 = 60.0^\circ$, refractive index of air $n_1 = 1.00$, refractive index of glass $n_2 = 1.52$
**Find:** the angle of refraction $\alpha_2$ in degrees
**Principle:** Light passes from air into glass, so it is refracted at the boundary according to the law of refraction. Glass is optically denser, so the ray is refracted towards the normal.
**Formula:** $n_1 \sin\alpha_1 = n_2 \sin\alpha_2$, so $\sin\alpha_2 = \dfrac{n_1 \sin\alpha_1}{n_2}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the refractive indices have no unit, and the angle is in degrees.
**Substitution:** $\sin\alpha_2 = \dfrac{1.00 \cdot \sin 60.0^\circ}{1.52} = \dfrac{0.8660}{1.52} = 0.5698$, so $\alpha_2 = 34.73^\circ$
**Answer:** $\alpha_2 \approx 34.7^\circ$, three significant figures like the angle of incidence $60.0^\circ$ and the refractive indices.
**Reasonableness:** The angle of refraction is smaller than the angle of incidence, so the ray was refracted towards the normal, as it should be into an optically denser material. Check: $1.52 \cdot \sin 34.7^\circ \approx 0.866 = \sin 60.0^\circ$.

## fy7-10-L1-05
**Given:** angle of incidence in water $\alpha_1 = 40.0^\circ$, refractive index of water $n_1 = 1.33$, refractive index of air $n_2 = 1.00$
**Find:** the angle of refraction in air $\alpha_2$ in degrees
**Principle:** The ray passes from water into air, so it is refracted according to the law of refraction. Now the material on the incident side is water, so $n_1 = 1.33$. Air is optically less dense, so the ray is refracted away from the normal.
**Formula:** $n_1 \sin\alpha_1 = n_2 \sin\alpha_2$, so $\sin\alpha_2 = \dfrac{n_1 \sin\alpha_1}{n_2}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the refractive indices have no unit, and the angle is in degrees.
**Substitution:** $\sin\alpha_2 = \dfrac{1.33 \cdot \sin 40.0^\circ}{1.00} = 1.33 \cdot 0.6428 = 0.8549$, so $\alpha_2 = 58.749^\circ$
**Answer:** $\alpha_2 \approx 58.7^\circ$, three significant figures like the angle of incidence $40.0^\circ$ and the refractive index $1.33$.
**Reasonableness:** The angle of refraction is greater than the angle of incidence, so the ray was refracted away from the normal, as it should be from water into air. $\sin\alpha_2$ is less than $1$, so the refracted ray exists.

## fy7-10-L2-01
**Given:** angle between the ray and the surface of the glass $25.0^\circ$, refractive index of air $n_1 = 1.00$, refractive index of glass $n_2 = 1.52$
**Find:** the angle of refraction $\alpha_2$ measured from the normal, in degrees
**Principle:** The law of refraction concerns angles measured from the normal. The normal is perpendicular to the surface, so the angle of incidence is $90^\circ$ minus the angle between the ray and the surface. Glass is optically denser, so the ray is refracted towards the normal.
**Formula:** $\alpha_1 = 90^\circ - 25.0^\circ$ and $n_1 \sin\alpha_1 = n_2 \sin\alpha_2$, so $\sin\alpha_2 = \dfrac{n_1 \sin\alpha_1}{n_2}$
**Conversions:** The angle from the surface is converted into the angle of incidence from the normal: $\alpha_1 = 90^\circ - 25.0^\circ = 65.0^\circ$.
**Substitution:** $\sin\alpha_2 = \dfrac{1.00 \cdot \sin 65.0^\circ}{1.52} = \dfrac{0.9063}{1.52} = 0.5963$, so $\alpha_2 = 36.60^\circ$
**Answer:** $\alpha_2 \approx 36.6^\circ$, three significant figures like the angle $25.0^\circ$ and the refractive indices.
**Reasonableness:** The angle of refraction $36.6^\circ$ is smaller than the angle of incidence $65.0^\circ$, so the ray was refracted towards the normal. If the angle $25.0^\circ$ were substituted directly, the result would be $16.1^\circ$, and the error would show in the angles being measured from the wrong line.

## fy7-10-L2-02
**Given:** angle of incidence in air $\alpha_1 = 40.0^\circ$, angle of refraction in the plastic $\alpha_2 = 25.4^\circ$, refractive index of air $n_1 = 1.00$, $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$
**Find:** the speed of light in the plastic $v$ in the form $\ldots \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$
**Principle:** The refractive index of the plastic follows from the law of refraction, because both angles and the refractive index of air are known. The refractive index tells how many times more slowly light travels in the plastic than in a vacuum, $n = c : v$.
**Formula:** $n_1 \sin\alpha_1 = n_2 \sin\alpha_2$, so $n_2 = \dfrac{n_1 \sin\alpha_1}{\sin\alpha_2}$, and $v = \dfrac{c}{n_2}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the angles are in degrees, the refractive indices have no unit, and the speed comes out in the same unit as $c$.
**Substitution:** $n_2 = \dfrac{1.00 \cdot \sin 40.0^\circ}{\sin 25.4^\circ} = \dfrac{0.6428}{0.4289} = 1.4986$ and $v = \dfrac{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}}{1.4986} = 2.002 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$
**Answer:** $v \approx 2.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$, so the answer is $2.00$; three significant figures like the angles.
**Reasonableness:** The refractive index $1.50$ is about the same as that of ordinary plastic such as acrylic, $1.49$. In the plastic light travels at about two thirds of the speed in a vacuum, which fits the refractive index $1.5$.

## fy7-10-L2-03
**Given:** thickness of the glass $d = 2.0\,\text{cm}$, refractive index of the glass $n = 1.52$, speed of light in air $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$
**Find:** the difference in travel times $\Delta t$ in picoseconds
**Principle:** In the glass light travels at the speed $v = c : n$, so travelling the same distance takes longer in the glass than in the air. The travel time is the distance divided by the speed. The light arrives perpendicular to the glass, so it is not refracted and travels the distance $d$ in the glass.
**Formula:** $\Delta t = \dfrac{d}{v} - \dfrac{d}{c}$, where $v = \dfrac{c}{n}$, so $\Delta t = \dfrac{n d}{c} - \dfrac{d}{c} = \dfrac{(n - 1)\, d}{c}$
**Conversions:** $d = 2.0\,\text{cm} = 2.0 \cdot 0.01\,\text{m} = 0.020\,\text{m}$. Finally $1\,\text{ps} = 10^{-12}\,\text{s}$.
**Substitution:** $\Delta t = \dfrac{(1.52 - 1) \cdot 0.020\,\text{m}}{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}} = 3.467 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\text{s} = 34.67\,\text{ps}$
**Answer:** $\Delta t \approx 35\,\text{ps}$, two significant figures like the thickness $2.0\,\text{cm}$.
**Reasonableness:** In air the distance takes $0.020\,\text{m} : 3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s} \approx 67\,\text{ps}$, and in glass about $1.5$ times longer, that is, about $100\,\text{ps}$. The difference is about half of the travel time in air, as follows from the refractive index $1.52$.

## fy7-10-L3-01
**Given:** mirrors perpendicular to each other, angle of incidence at the first mirror $30.0^\circ$, the ray in the plane of the normals of the mirrors
**Find:** the angle of incidence at the second mirror in degrees
**Principle:** At the first mirror, according to the law of reflection, the angle of reflection is $30.0^\circ$, so the reflected ray makes the angle $90^\circ - 30.0^\circ = 60.0^\circ$ with the surface of the first mirror. The second mirror is perpendicular to the first, so its normal is parallel to the surface of the first mirror. Therefore the angle between the ray and the normal of the second mirror is the same as the angle between the ray and the surface of the first mirror.
**Formula:** $\beta_1 = \alpha_1$ and $\alpha_\text{second} = 90^\circ - \beta_1$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the angles are in degrees.
**Substitution:** $\alpha_\text{second} = 90^\circ - 30.0^\circ = 60.0^\circ$
**Answer:** $60.0^\circ$, three significant figures like the angle of incidence $30.0^\circ$.
**Reasonableness:** The angles of incidence $30.0^\circ$ and $60.0^\circ$ add up to $90^\circ$, as follows from the angle between the normals of perpendicular mirrors. After two reflections the ray returns in the direction opposite to its original direction, and bicycle reflectors, for example, are based on the same principle.

## fy7-10-L3-02
**Given:** depth of the lamp $h = 2.0\,\text{m}$, horizontal distance $x = 1.5\,\text{m}$, refractive index of water $n_1 = 1.33$, refractive index of air $n_2 = 1.00$
**Find:** the angle between the ray and the normal in air, $\alpha_2$, in degrees
**Principle:** In the water the ray travels straight from the lamp to the surface, so the angle of incidence is found from the right-angled triangle whose legs are the depth and the horizontal distance. The normal is vertical, so $\tan\alpha_1 = x : h$. At the surface the ray is refracted according to the law of refraction, away from the normal, because air is optically less dense.
**Formula:** $\tan\alpha_1 = \dfrac{x}{h}$ and $n_1 \sin\alpha_1 = n_2 \sin\alpha_2$, so $\sin\alpha_2 = \dfrac{n_1 \sin\alpha_1}{n_2}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the distances are in metres, and in their ratio the units cancel.
**Substitution:** $\tan\alpha_1 = \dfrac{1.5\,\text{m}}{2.0\,\text{m}} = 0.75$, so $\alpha_1 = 36.87^\circ$; $\sin\alpha_2 = \dfrac{1.33 \cdot \sin 36.87^\circ}{1.00} = 1.33 \cdot 0.600 = 0.798$, so $\alpha_2 = 52.94^\circ$
**Answer:** $\alpha_2 \approx 53^\circ$, two significant figures like the depth $2.0\,\text{m}$ and the distance $1.5\,\text{m}$.
**Reasonableness:** The angle in air is greater than the angle in water, $36.9^\circ$, so the ray was refracted away from the normal. $\sin\alpha_2 = 0.798$ is less than $1$, so the ray gets out into the air.

## fy7-10-L3-03
**Given:** speed of light in material A $v_1 = 2.25 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$, in material B $v_2 = 1.97 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$, angle of incidence $\alpha_1 = 40.0^\circ$
**Find:** the angle of refraction $\alpha_2$ in degrees
**Principle:** The law of refraction $n_1 \sin\alpha_1 = n_2 \sin\alpha_2$ and the definition of the refractive index $n = c : v$ together give $\dfrac{\sin\alpha_1}{v_1} = \dfrac{\sin\alpha_2}{v_2}$: the ratio of the sines of the angles is the same as the ratio of the speeds. Light slows down when it passes into material B, so it is refracted towards the normal.
**Formula:** $\dfrac{c}{v_1}\sin\alpha_1 = \dfrac{c}{v_2}\sin\alpha_2$, so $\sin\alpha_2 = \dfrac{v_2 \sin\alpha_1}{v_1}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the speeds are in the same unit, so they cancel.
**Substitution:** $\sin\alpha_2 = \dfrac{1.97 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s} \cdot \sin 40.0^\circ}{2.25 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}} = 0.8756 \cdot 0.6428 = 0.5628$, so $\alpha_2 = 34.249^\circ$
**Answer:** $\alpha_2 \approx 34.2^\circ$, three significant figures like the speeds and the angle of incidence.
**Reasonableness:** The angle of refraction is smaller than the angle of incidence, as it should be when light passes into a material where it travels more slowly. The refractive indices are $3.00 : 2.25 = 1.33$ (water) and $3.00 : 1.97 = 1.52$ (glass), and with them $\sin\alpha_2 = 1.33 \cdot 0.6428 : 1.52 = 0.563$, the same result.
