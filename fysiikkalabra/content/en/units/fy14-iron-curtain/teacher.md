## Timing (75 minutes)

- **0–7 min, Think about it.** Read the hook aloud. Pairs share one surprising claim from the news and decide what made it believable.
- **7–12 min, Predict.** Both predictions individually, committed, with a short reason.
- **12–42 min, Investigate.** The introduction together (3 min). Pairs split the sources: A reads Sources A and C, B reads Sources B and D, both read E (10 min); they explain their sources to each other (5 min). The question on Sources A and B, the evidence-or-circumstance sorting and the source question on the invitation (8 min). Then *How Thomson scattering works* with the figure (4 min).
- **42–50 min, Theory summary.** The key idea and the misconception; back to the predictions.
- **50–65 min, Apply.** The keV-to-kelvin calculation (4 min), the Culham role-play in groups of three or four (8 min), the find-the-error task (1 min). The magazine report is started in class and finished at home if needed.
- **65–75 min, Check your understanding.** Three checks individually, the transfer task, the summary and the French corner.

**Fast pairs** read the extension below (the Doppler width estimate) or open the course timeline at *Surprise results at Novosibirsk* and *The Culham Five confirm T-3* and compare the timeline's wording with the sources. **If time runs short**, shorten the role-play to the director's decision after two speakers, make the magazine report homework, and leave the French corner for the next lesson.

## Organisation and preparation
- The sources are short texts written for this course from the documents in *Sources and verification* (ITER Newsline, UKAEA, the 1969 *Nature* paper, histories of the tokamak). Tell the students so: it is part of source criticism to ask what a summary is based on.
- Prepare role cards for the role-play (director, laser scientist, sceptical physicist, security officer). In groups of three, the director also speaks for security.
- A map of Europe in 1968 with the Iron Curtain, Culham (near Oxford), Moscow and Novosibirsk helps; so does a projected photograph of the T-3 tokamak if one is available (the photo pass will add one where licensed).
- Physics prerequisite: the Doppler effect in one sentence ("a source moving towards you gives a shorter wavelength").

## Assessment
- Self-assessment with the criteria shown after writing. For a grade: the source question gives a reason for each side with its source; the magazine report states the method, the result and why independent verification matters in 80–120 words; the transfer task describes real verification (another team, method, peer review).
- Evidence and circumstance: the student can classify statements and explain the difference in their own words (the sorting task and check 3).
- Language: source vocabulary (*according to*, *based on*, *reliable*) and the role-play phrases for agreeing and disagreeing.

## Common difficulties
- Students treat a famous person's statement as evidence. Ask: "Could someone else repeat this and get the same result?"
- *Repeating* versus *verifying*: repeating an indirect measurement does not remove a systematic error. Check 1 and the misconception target this.
- The Doppler width: some students expect the whole spectrum to shift. A plasma at rest has electrons moving both ways, so the light spreads on both sides.
- keV and kelvin: $1\ \text{eV} \approx 11\ 600\ \text{K}$; the task asks for millions of kelvins to avoid long numbers.

## Extension ideas
- Estimate the width: an electron with $k_\text{B} T = 1\ \text{keV}$ has a typical speed of about $\sqrt{2 k_\text{B} T / m_\text{e}} \approx 1.9 \cdot 10^{7}\ \text{m/s}$, about $6\ \%$ of the speed of light. What relative change of wavelength does the Doppler effect give at this speed?
- Compare with ZETA (unit *Secret beginnings*): what did Rose's neutron-direction measurement and the Culham laser measurement have in common?
- Discuss: is science more international today? Look at ITER's seven members and the effect of the 2022 war in Ukraine on cooperation with Russia (teacher-led, as of November 2026).
