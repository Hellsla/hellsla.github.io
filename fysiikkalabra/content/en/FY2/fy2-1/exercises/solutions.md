## fy2-1-L1-01
**Given:** potential energy of the stone on the cliff $E_\text{p} = 50\,\text{J}$, kinetic energy on the cliff $0\,\text{J}$, potential energy on the ground $0\,\text{J}$
**Find:** kinetic energy of the stone $E_\text{k}$ just before it hits the ground in $\text{J}$
**Principle:** Air resistance is ignored, so no energy enters or leaves the system formed by the stone and the Earth. The system is isolated, so energy is conserved: $E_\text{initial} = E_\text{final}$. Without friction no thermal energy is produced.
**Formula:** At the start there is only potential energy and at the end only kinetic energy: $E_\text{p} = E_\text{k}$, so $E_\text{k} = E_\text{p}$
**Conversions:** The energy is given in joules and the answer is asked in joules, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{k} = E_\text{p} = 50\,\text{J}$
**Answer:** $50\,\text{J}$, two significant figures as in the potential energy $50\,\text{J}$.
**Reasonableness:** In the fall the potential energy changes into kinetic energy, and because nothing changes into thermal energy, the amount of energy is the same $50\,\text{J}$ at the start and at the end. The kinetic energy cannot be greater than the initial energy, because energy is not created.

## fy2-1-L1-02
**Given:** radiant energy absorbed by the leaf $E_\text{rad} = 245\,\text{J}$, chemical energy produced $E_\text{chem} = 6.0\,\text{J}$
**Find:** energy changed into thermal energy $E_\text{thermal}$ in $\text{J}$
**Principle:** The leaf changes the absorbed radiant energy into chemical energy and thermal energy, and energy is neither created nor destroyed. By the law of conservation of energy $E_\text{initial} = E_\text{final}$.
**Formula:** $E_\text{rad} = E_\text{chem} + E_\text{thermal}$, so $E_\text{thermal} = E_\text{rad} - E_\text{chem}$
**Conversions:** All energies are in joules, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{thermal} = 245\,\text{J} - 6.0\,\text{J} = 239\,\text{J}$
**Answer:** $239\,\text{J}$, in whole joules, because the radiant energy $245\,\text{J}$ is given to the nearest joule and this limits the precision of the difference.
**Reasonableness:** The chemical energy and thermal energy together are $6.0\,\text{J} + 239\,\text{J} = 245\,\text{J}$, as at the start. Only $6.0\,\text{J} : 245\,\text{J} \approx 0.02$, about two per cent, changes into chemical energy, and plants are known to capture only a small part of the energy of sunlight; the leaf warms up in the sun.

## fy2-1-L1-03
**Given:** energies read from the figure at points A and C: $E_\text{p,A} = 700\,\text{J}$, $E_\text{k,A} = 0\,\text{J}$, $E_\text{p,C} = 0\,\text{J}$ and $E_\text{k,C} = 500\,\text{J}$
**Find:** energy changed into thermal energy $E_\text{thermal}$ between points A and C in $\text{J}$
**Principle:** The skater, the half-pipe and the air form an isolated system, so energy is conserved: $E_\text{initial} = E_\text{final}$. Friction changes part of the mechanical energy into thermal energy, so at the end the energy is mechanical energy and thermal energy.
**Reading the graph:** At point A there is only the blue potential-energy bar, and its top is at the gridline $700\,\text{J}$; there is no kinetic energy because the skater is at rest. At point C there is only the orange kinetic-energy bar, and its top is at the line $500\,\text{J}$; there is no potential energy because the bottom is the zero level. The bars of point B are not needed.
**Formula:** $E_\text{p,A} + E_\text{k,A} = E_\text{p,C} + E_\text{k,C} + E_\text{thermal}$, so $E_\text{thermal} = (E_\text{p,A} + E_\text{k,A}) - (E_\text{p,C} + E_\text{k,C})$
**Conversions:** All energies are in joules, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{thermal} = (700\,\text{J} + 0\,\text{J}) - (0\,\text{J} + 500\,\text{J}) = 200\,\text{J}$
**Answer:** $200\,\text{J}$, to the nearest hundred joules, because the lengths of the bars are read from the $100\,\text{J}$ gridlines.
**Reasonableness:** The mechanical energy decreases from $700\,\text{J}$ to $500\,\text{J}$, as it must on a track with friction. At point B the mechanical energy is $300\,\text{J} + 300\,\text{J} = 600\,\text{J}$, so by then only $100\,\text{J}$ has changed into thermal energy, and the thermal energy grows along the whole way. Check: $500\,\text{J} + 200\,\text{J} = 700\,\text{J}$.

## fy2-1-L1-04
**Find:** in each situation, the form of energy at the start and the useful form of energy into which the energy changes
**Principle:** The form of energy tells you where the energy is stored or how it shows itself. In fuels the energy is in chemical bonds, sunlight is radiation, and water high up has potential energy because of its position. Energy does not come from nothing, so the useful energy always comes from the form of energy at the start, and in every change part of it also changes into thermal energy.
**Model answer:** a) The chemical energy of the wood changes in combustion into thermal energy (and a little light), which warms the room. b) The radiant energy of the Sun changes in the panel into electrical energy; however, most of it changes into thermal energy in the panel. c) The potential energy of the water in the reservoir changes into the kinetic energy of the flowing water, the turbine and generator change it into electrical energy, and a little changes into thermal energy. So the forms of energy at the start are a) chemical energy, b) radiant energy and c) potential energy, and the useful forms of energy at the end are a) thermal energy, b) electrical energy and c) electrical energy.

## fy2-1-L1-05
**Given:** potential energy of the sledge at the top $E_\text{p} = 600\,\text{J}$, energy changed into thermal energy by friction $E_\text{thermal} = 90\,\text{J}$, potential energy at the bottom of the hill $0\,\text{J}$
**Find:** kinetic energy $E_\text{k}$ at the bottom of the hill in $\text{J}$
**Principle:** The sledge, the hill and the surroundings form an isolated system, so energy is conserved: $E_\text{initial} = E_\text{final}$. At the start all the energy is potential energy, and at the end it is kinetic energy and thermal energy, into which friction has changed part of it.
**Formula:** $E_\text{p} = E_\text{k} + E_\text{thermal}$, so $E_\text{k} = E_\text{p} - E_\text{thermal}$
**Conversions:** All energies are in joules, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{k} = 600\,\text{J} - 90\,\text{J} = 510\,\text{J}$
**Answer:** $510\,\text{J}$, three significant figures as in the potential energy $600\,\text{J}$.
**Reasonableness:** The kinetic energy is smaller than the initial energy $600\,\text{J}$, because part of it changed into thermal energy, as it should with friction. Checking backwards: $510\,\text{J} + 90\,\text{J} = 600\,\text{J}$.

## fy2-1-L2-01
**Given:** chemical energy released from the battery $E_\text{chem} = 3.00\,\text{kJ}$, kinetic energy of the scooter and rider $E_\text{k} = 2150\,\text{J}$
**Find:** energy changed into thermal energy $E_\text{thermal}$ in $\text{J}$
**Principle:** The chemical energy of the battery changes through electrical energy into kinetic energy and thermal energy, and energy is neither created nor destroyed. By the law of conservation of energy $E_\text{initial} = E_\text{final}$.
**Formula:** $E_\text{chem} = E_\text{k} + E_\text{thermal}$, so $E_\text{thermal} = E_\text{chem} - E_\text{k}$
**Conversions:** The chemical energy is in kilojoules and the kinetic energy in joules. The prefix kilo means a thousand, so we substitute $3.00\,\text{kJ} = 3.00 \cdot 1000\,\text{J} = 3000\,\text{J}$.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{thermal} = 3000\,\text{J} - 2150\,\text{J} = 850\,\text{J}$
**Answer:** $850\,\text{J}$, to the nearest ten joules, because the chemical energy $3.00\,\text{kJ}$ is given to the nearest ten joules.
**Reasonableness:** $2150\,\text{J} : 3000\,\text{J} \approx 0.72$, a little under three quarters, ends up as kinetic energy and the rest is thermal energy. This suits an electric motor, which is much more efficient than a combustion engine. Checking backwards: $2150\,\text{J} + 850\,\text{J} = 3000\,\text{J}$.

## fy2-1-L2-02
**Given:** kinetic energy at the bottom $E_\text{k} = 2400\,\text{J}$, energy changed into thermal energy by friction $E_\text{thermal} = 0.60\,\text{kJ}$, potential energy at the bottom $0\,\text{J}$, the child starts from rest
**Find:** potential energy $E_\text{p}$ at the top in $\text{kJ}$
**Principle:** The child, the slide and the water form an isolated system, so energy is conserved: $E_\text{initial} = E_\text{final}$. At the top the child is at rest, so all the energy is potential energy.
**Formula:** $E_\text{p} = E_\text{k} + E_\text{thermal}$. The formula is already solved for the quantity asked.
**Conversions:** The answer is asked in kilojoules, and the kinetic energy is in joules. The joule is a thousandth of a kilojoule, so we substitute $2400\,\text{J} = 2400 \cdot 0.001\,\text{kJ} = 2.4\,\text{kJ}$.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{p} = 2.4\,\text{kJ} + 0.60\,\text{kJ} = 3.0\,\text{kJ}$
**Answer:** $3.0\,\text{kJ}$, two significant figures as in the kinetic energy $2400\,\text{J}$, that is $2.4\,\text{kJ}$.
**Reasonableness:** The initial energy must be greater than the kinetic energy at the bottom, $2.4\,\text{kJ}$, because part changed into thermal energy, and $3.0\,\text{kJ} > 2.4\,\text{kJ}$. Energy changed into thermal energy: $0.60\,\text{kJ} : 3.0\,\text{kJ} = 0.20$, that is a fifth.

## fy2-1-L2-03
**Given:** potential energy at the highest point $E_\text{p} = 0.36\,\text{kJ}$, the swing starts from rest and finally stops at the lowest point, sound is ignored
**Find:** total energy changed into thermal energy $E_\text{thermal}$ in $\text{J}$
**Principle:** The child, the swing, its fixings and the surrounding air form an isolated system, so energy is conserved. At the end the swing is at rest at the lowest point, so it has neither kinetic energy nor potential energy. All the initial energy has changed into thermal energy through air resistance and friction in the fixings.
**Formula:** $E_\text{p} = E_\text{thermal}$, so $E_\text{thermal} = E_\text{p}$
**Conversions:** The prefix kilo means a thousand, so we substitute $0.36\,\text{kJ} = 0.36 \cdot 1000\,\text{J} = 360\,\text{J}$.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{thermal} = E_\text{p} = 360\,\text{J}$
**Answer:** $360\,\text{J}$ or $3.6 \cdot 10^{2}\,\text{J}$, two significant figures as in the given $0.36\,\text{kJ}$.
**Reasonableness:** Each swing is lower than the one before, because part of the mechanical energy changes into thermal energy, and finally all $360\,\text{J}$ is thermal energy. It spreads into the air and the fixings so widely that the warming cannot be noticed, but no energy has been lost.

## fy2-1-L2-04
**Given:** electrical energy going into the kettle $E_\text{elec} = 360\,\text{kJ}$, energy transferred to the water as heat $E_\text{water} = 331\,\text{kJ}$
**Find:** energy ending up in the kettle and the surroundings $E_\text{other}$ in $\text{J}$
**Principle:** The electrical energy changes into thermal energy, and part of it ends up in the water and the rest in the kettle and the surroundings. Energy is neither created nor destroyed, so $E_\text{initial} = E_\text{final}$.
**Formula:** $E_\text{elec} = E_\text{water} + E_\text{other}$, so $E_\text{other} = E_\text{elec} - E_\text{water}$
**Conversions:** The prefix kilo means a thousand, so we substitute $360\,\text{kJ} = 360 \cdot 1000\,\text{J} = 360\,000\,\text{J}$ and $331\,\text{kJ} = 331 \cdot 1000\,\text{J} = 331\,000\,\text{J}$.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{other} = 360\,000\,\text{J} - 331\,000\,\text{J} = 29\,000\,\text{J}$
**Answer:** $29\,000\,\text{J}$ or $2.9 \cdot 10^{4}\,\text{J}$, two significant figures, because the data are given to the nearest kilojoule and the difference is $29\,\text{kJ}$.
**Reasonableness:** $331\,\text{kJ} : 360\,\text{kJ} \approx 0.92$, over $90\,\%$, goes into the water, and the rest, $29\,\text{kJ}$, is a small part, as it should be with a well-insulated kettle. Checking backwards: $331\,000\,\text{J} + 29\,000\,\text{J} = 360\,000\,\text{J}$.

## fy2-1-L3-01
**Given:** at point A $E_\text{p} = 900\,\text{J}$ and $E_\text{k} = 0\,\text{J}$; at point B $E_\text{p} = 400\,\text{J}$ and $E_\text{k} = 480\,\text{J}$; at point C $E_\text{p} = 0\,\text{J}$ and $E_\text{k} = 750\,\text{J}$
**Find:** energy changed into thermal energy between points B and C, $\Delta E_\text{thermal}$, in $\text{J}$
**Principle:** The total energy of the system is conserved, so at every point $E_\text{p} + E_\text{k} + E_\text{thermal}$ is equal to its value at point A, which is $900\,\text{J}$. The thermal energy is what the mechanical energy is missing.
**Formula:** $E_\text{thermal} = E_\text{A} - E_\text{p} - E_\text{k}$ at each point, and between points B and C $\Delta E_\text{thermal} = E_\text{thermal,C} - E_\text{thermal,B}$
**Conversions:** All energies are in joules, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{thermal,B} = 900\,\text{J} - 400\,\text{J} - 480\,\text{J} = 20\,\text{J}$ and $E_\text{thermal,C} = 900\,\text{J} - 0\,\text{J} - 750\,\text{J} = 150\,\text{J}$, so $\Delta E_\text{thermal} = 150\,\text{J} - 20\,\text{J} = 130\,\text{J}$
**Answer:** $130\,\text{J}$, two significant figures, because the initial data are given to the nearest ten joules.
**Reasonableness:** The thermal energy grows from point to point ($0$, $20\,\text{J}$, $150\,\text{J}$), as it should when friction changes mechanical energy into thermal energy, and it never decreases. The mechanical energy is $880\,\text{J}$ at B and $750\,\text{J}$ at C, and the difference $880\,\text{J} - 750\,\text{J} = 130\,\text{J}$ confirms the result.

## fy2-1-L3-02
**Given:** mechanical energy of the ball when dropped $E_0 = 5.00\,\text{J}$, after the first bounce $E_1 = 4.00\,\text{J}$ and after the second bounce $E_2 = 3.20\,\text{J}$
**Find:** how much more energy is changed into thermal energy in the first bounce than in the second, $\Delta E$, in $\text{J}$
**Principle:** The system formed by the ball, the floor and the air is isolated, so the total energy is conserved. In each bounce the mechanical energy the ball is missing has changed into thermal energy. We calculate the energy balance for each bounce separately and compare.
**Formula:** In the first bounce $E_0 = E_1 + E_\text{thermal,1}$ and in the second $E_1 = E_2 + E_\text{thermal,2}$, so $E_\text{thermal,1} = E_0 - E_1$, $E_\text{thermal,2} = E_1 - E_2$ and $\Delta E = E_\text{thermal,1} - E_\text{thermal,2}$
**Conversions:** All energies are in joules, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{thermal,1} = 5.00\,\text{J} - 4.00\,\text{J} = 1.00\,\text{J}$ and $E_\text{thermal,2} = 4.00\,\text{J} - 3.20\,\text{J} = 0.80\,\text{J}$, so $\Delta E = 1.00\,\text{J} - 0.80\,\text{J} = 0.20\,\text{J}$
**Answer:** $0.20\,\text{J}$, to two decimal places like the energies in the table.
**Reasonableness:** In each bounce a fifth of the ball's energy at that moment changes into thermal energy ($1.00\,\text{J}$ is a fifth of $5.00\,\text{J}$ and $0.80\,\text{J}$ is a fifth of $4.00\,\text{J}$). In the second bounce the ball has less energy, so less energy changes into thermal energy, and so the bounces get lower and lower but the decrease slows down. Checking backwards: $3.20\,\text{J} + 0.80\,\text{J} + 1.00\,\text{J} = 5.00\,\text{J}$.

## fy2-1-L3-03
**Find:** a) the forms of energy at each stage of the nuclear power plant chain; b) where about two thirds of the nuclear energy goes and why the energy has not been lost
**Principle:** Energy changes form and moves, but is neither created nor destroyed: in the system formed by the power plant, the cooling water and the surroundings $E_\text{initial} = E_\text{final}$. In every change part of the energy changes into thermal energy, which spreads into the surroundings and is hard to use.
**Model answer:** a) The nuclear energy stored in the atomic nuclei of uranium is released in the reactor and changes into the thermal energy of water and steam. The steam flows into the turbine and turns it, so the energy is kinetic energy. The generator changes the kinetic energy of the turning turbine into electrical energy. b) Only about a third becomes electricity, because the steam leaving the turbine is still warm: energy is transferred from it as heat to the cooling water, which carries it to the sea. In addition, in the turbine, the generator and the pipes part of the energy changes into thermal energy, which is transferred as heat into the surroundings. The energy has not been lost: the electrical energy and the thermal energy transferred to the sea and the surroundings together equal the nuclear energy released. So about two thirds ends up as thermal energy that has spread widely and can no longer easily be used.
