## fy7-11-L1-01
**Given:** angle of incidence $\alpha_1 = 50.0^\circ$, refractive index of water for red light $n = 1.332$, the light arrives from air
**Find:** angle of refraction $\alpha_2$ in degrees
**Principle:** Light is refracted at the boundary according to the law of refraction. The light arrives from air, whose refractive index is rounded to $1$, so $\sin\alpha_1 = n \sin\alpha_2$. For red light we use the refractive index of red light, because the refractive index depends on wavelength.
**Formula:** $\sin\alpha_1 = n \sin\alpha_2$, so $\sin\alpha_2 = \dfrac{\sin\alpha_1}{n}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the refractive index has no unit, and the angles are in degrees.
**Substitution:** $\sin\alpha_2 = \dfrac{\sin 50.0^\circ}{1.332} = 0.57511$, so $\alpha_2 = 35.11^\circ$
**Answer:** $35.1^\circ$, three significant figures like the angle of incidence $50.0^\circ$.
**Reasonableness:** The angle of refraction is smaller than the angle of incidence, because light slows down in water and bends towards the normal. The ratio $\sin 50.0^\circ : \sin 35.1^\circ = 0.766 : 0.575 \approx 1.33$ is the refractive index of water.

## fy7-11-L1-02
**Given:** refractive index of crown glass for violet light $n = 1.530$, speed of light in vacuum $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^8\,\text{m/s}$
**Find:** speed $v$ of violet light in the glass in kilometres per second
**Principle:** The refractive index tells how many times slower light travels in a material than in vacuum. For violet light we use the refractive index of violet light.
**Formula:** $n = \dfrac{c}{v}$, so $v = \dfrac{c}{n}$
**Conversions:** $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^8\,\text{m/s} = 3.00 \cdot 10^5\,\text{km/s}$, because $1\,\text{km} = 1000\,\text{m}$.
**Substitution:** $v = \dfrac{3.00 \cdot 10^5\,\text{km/s}}{1.530} = 196\,078\,\text{km/s}$
**Answer:** $1.96 \cdot 10^5\,\text{km/s}$, that is, about $196\,000\,\text{km/s}$, three significant figures like the speed of light $3.00 \cdot 10^8\,\text{m/s}$.
**Reasonableness:** The speed is about two thirds of the speed of light in vacuum, as it should be in glass, because $1 : 1.53 \approx 0.65$. The speed of red light in glass is slightly greater, because its refractive index is smaller.

## fy7-11-L1-03
**Given:** angle of incidence $\alpha_1 = 45.0^\circ$, angle of refraction $\alpha_2 = 27.8^\circ$, the light arrives from air
**Find:** refractive index $n$ of the glass for green light (no unit)
**Principle:** Light is refracted at the boundary according to the law of refraction, and the refractive index of air is rounded to $1$. When both angles are known, the refractive index is obtained from the ratio of the sines of the angles. The result applies only to green light, because the refractive index depends on wavelength.
**Formula:** $\sin\alpha_1 = n \sin\alpha_2$, so $n = \dfrac{\sin\alpha_1}{\sin\alpha_2}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the angles are in degrees, and the refractive index has no unit.
**Substitution:** $n = \dfrac{\sin 45.0^\circ}{\sin 27.8^\circ} = \dfrac{0.70711}{0.46639} = 1.516$
**Answer:** $1.52$, three significant figures like the measured angles.
**Reasonableness:** The refractive index of ordinary glass is about $1.5$, and the result lies between the table values for crown glass for red ($1.5143$) and blue ($1.5224$), which is where green light belongs. The refractive index is greater than $1$, because the light bent towards the normal.

## fy7-11-L1-04
**Given:** apex angle of the prism $A = 60.0^\circ$, angle of incidence $\alpha_1 = 45.0^\circ$, angle of refraction of the emerging ray $\alpha_4 = 52.0^\circ$
**Find:** angle of deviation $\delta$ in degrees
**Principle:** In a prism the ray turns by $\alpha_1 - \alpha_2$ at the first surface and by $\alpha_4 - \alpha_3$ at the second. Because the geometry of the prism gives $\alpha_2 + \alpha_3 = A$, the total turning depends only on the outer angles and the apex angle.
**Formula:** $\delta = \alpha_1 + \alpha_4 - A$
**Conversions:** Not needed: all the angles are in degrees.
**Substitution:** $\delta = 45.0^\circ + 52.0^\circ - 60.0^\circ = 37.0^\circ$
**Answer:** $37.0^\circ$, to one decimal like the angles.
**Reasonableness:** The angle of deviation is positive, that is, the ray turns towards the base of the prism, as it should in a prism. It is of the same order of magnitude as the angles of deviation of the crown-glass prism in the simulation of the chapter, about $38\text{–}40^\circ$ when the apex angle is $60^\circ$.

## fy7-11-L1-05
**Given:** angle of incidence at the surface of the droplet $\alpha = 55.0^\circ$, angle of refraction $\beta = 38.0^\circ$, one reflection from the back surface of the droplet
**Find:** angle $\theta$ from the direction of the antisolar point in degrees
**Principle:** The ray turns by $\alpha - \beta$ at the point of entry, by $180^\circ - 2\beta$ at the reflection and by $\alpha - \beta$ at the point of exit. The total turning is $180^\circ + 2\alpha - 4\beta$, so the light leaves at $180^\circ$ minus the turning, measured from the direction the sunlight came from.
**Formula:** $\theta = 4\beta - 2\alpha$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the angles are in degrees.
**Substitution:** $\theta = 4 \cdot 38.0^\circ - 2 \cdot 55.0^\circ = 152.0^\circ - 110.0^\circ = 42.0^\circ$
**Answer:** $42.0^\circ$, to one decimal like the angles.
**Reasonableness:** The result is close to the angle of a rainbow, $42^\circ$. From the angles the refractive index is $\sin 55.0^\circ : \sin 38.0^\circ = 0.819 : 0.616 \approx 1.33$, which is the refractive index of water, so the situation is possible.

## fy7-11-L2-01
**Given:** length of the fibre $L = 5.00\,\text{km}$, refractive index of red $n_\text{r} = 1.5143$, of violet $n_\text{v} = 1.5302$, speed of light in vacuum $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^8\,\text{m/s}$
**Find:** difference $\Delta t$ in arrival times in nanoseconds
**Principle:** According to the assumption of the problem, each colour travels in the glass at speed $v = c : n$; a real short flash travels slightly more slowly at the group velocity, but that is ignored here. The refractive index depends on wavelength. The refractive index of violet is greater, so violet travels more slowly and arrives later. Each colour travels the same distance at a steady speed, so the travel time is $t = L : v$.
**Formula:** $v = \dfrac{c}{n}$ and $t = \dfrac{L}{v} = \dfrac{L n}{c}$, so $\Delta t = t_\text{v} - t_\text{r} = \dfrac{L n_\text{v}}{c} - \dfrac{L n_\text{r}}{c}$
**Conversions:** $L = 5.00\,\text{km} = 5.00 \cdot 1000\,\text{m} = 5000\,\text{m}$. Finally $1\,\text{s} = 10^9\,\text{ns}$.
**Substitution:** $t_\text{v} = \dfrac{5000\,\text{m} \cdot 1.5302}{3.00 \cdot 10^8\,\text{m/s}} = 2.55033 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\text{s}$ and $t_\text{r} = \dfrac{5000\,\text{m} \cdot 1.5143}{3.00 \cdot 10^8\,\text{m/s}} = 2.52383 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\text{s}$, so $\Delta t = 2.55033 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\text{s} - 2.52383 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\text{s} = 2.650 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\text{s} = 265\,\text{ns}$
**Answer:** $265\,\text{ns}$, three significant figures like the difference of the refractive indices $0.0159$ and the length $5.00\,\text{km}$.
**Reasonableness:** Each travel time is about $25\,\mu\text{s}$, and the difference is about $1\,\%$ of it, as the ratio of the refractive indices $1.5302 : 1.5143 \approx 1.01$ leads us to expect. The intermediate results must be kept accurate: the difference of times rounded to three figures would come out wrong.

## fy7-11-L2-02
**Given:** apex angle $A = 60.0^\circ$, refractive index of the glass for yellow light $n = 1.52$, angle of incidence $\alpha_1 = 40.0^\circ$, the light arrives from air
**Find:** angle of incidence $\alpha_3$ at the second surface of the prism in degrees
**Principle:** At the first surface the ray is refracted according to the law of refraction, and the refractive index of air is rounded to $1$. Inside the glass the normals of the surfaces of the prism and the ray form a triangle, and the sum of its angles gives $\alpha_2 + \alpha_3 = A$.
**Formula:** $\sin\alpha_1 = n \sin\alpha_2$, so $\sin\alpha_2 = \dfrac{\sin\alpha_1}{n}$, and $\alpha_2 + \alpha_3 = A$, so $\alpha_3 = A - \alpha_2$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the angles are in degrees, and the refractive index has no unit.
**Substitution:** $\sin\alpha_2 = \dfrac{\sin 40.0^\circ}{1.52} = 0.42289$, so $\alpha_2 = 25.02^\circ$, and $\alpha_3 = 60.0^\circ - 25.02^\circ = 34.98^\circ$
**Answer:** $35.0^\circ$, three significant figures like the angle of incidence $40.0^\circ$.
**Reasonableness:** The sum of the angles $\alpha_2$ and $\alpha_3$ is the apex angle $60.0^\circ$. At the second surface $n \sin\alpha_3 = 1.52 \cdot \sin 35.0^\circ = 0.87 < 1$, so the ray can leave, at an angle of refraction of about $61^\circ$.

## fy7-11-L2-03
**Given:** graph of the refractive index of flint glass as a function of wavelength, wavelength $\lambda = 450\,\text{nm}$, $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^8\,\text{m/s}$
**Find:** speed $v$ of blue light in the glass in kilometres per second
**Principle:** The refractive index depends on wavelength, so it is read from the graph at exactly the wavelength $450\,\text{nm}$. The speed of light in a material is $v = c : n$.
**Reading the graph:** At $450\,\text{nm}$ on the horizontal axis there is a measured point on the curve. From the point, reading horizontally to the vertical axis gives $n \approx 1.639$: the point lies between the grid lines $1.638$ and $1.640$, close to halfway between them.
**Formula:** $n = \dfrac{c}{v}$, so $v = \dfrac{c}{n}$
**Conversions:** $c = 3.00 \cdot 10^8\,\text{m/s} = 3.00 \cdot 10^5\,\text{km/s}$, because $1\,\text{km} = 1000\,\text{m}$.
**Substitution:** $v = \dfrac{3.00 \cdot 10^5\,\text{km/s}}{1.639} = 183\,038\,\text{km/s}$
**Answer:** $1.83 \cdot 10^5\,\text{km/s}$, three significant figures like the speed of light $3.00 \cdot 10^8\,\text{m/s}$; a reading uncertainty of $\pm 0.001$ in the refractive index changes the speed by only about $0.1\,\%$.
**Reasonableness:** The refractive index of flint glass is greater than that of crown glass, so light travels more slowly in it than in crown glass (about $196\,000\text{–}198\,000\,\text{km/s}$). The speed is about $61\,\%$ of the speed of light in vacuum, because $1 : 1.64 \approx 0.61$.

## fy7-11-L2-04
**Given:** height of the sun $h = 25.0^\circ$, angle of incidence of the rays at the violet edge $\alpha = 58.8^\circ$, refractive index of water for violet light $n = 1.343$, one reflection from the back surface of the droplet
**Find:** height $h_\text{v}$ of the top of the violet arc above the horizon in degrees
**Principle:** At the surface of the droplet light is refracted according to the law of refraction, and the refractive index of air is rounded to $1$. The ray turns in two refractions and one reflection, so violet light is seen at the angle $\theta = 4\beta - 2\alpha$ from the direction of the antisolar point. The antisolar point is as far below the horizon as the sun is above it, so the top of the arc is at the height $\theta - h$.
**Formula:** $\sin\alpha = n \sin\beta$, so $\sin\beta = \dfrac{\sin\alpha}{n}$, $\theta = 4\beta - 2\alpha$ and $h_\text{v} = \theta - h$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the angles are in degrees.
**Substitution:** $\sin\beta = \dfrac{\sin 58.8^\circ}{1.343} = 0.63691$, so $\beta = 39.56^\circ$; $\theta = 4 \cdot 39.56^\circ - 2 \cdot 58.8^\circ = 158.25^\circ - 117.60^\circ = 40.65^\circ$ and $h_\text{v} = 40.65^\circ - 25.0^\circ = 15.65^\circ$
**Answer:** $15.6^\circ$, to one decimal like the height of the sun $25.0^\circ$.
**Reasonableness:** The angle of violet, $40.6^\circ$, is smaller than that of red, $42^\circ$, so the violet edge is lower than the red one, whose top is at a height of about $17^\circ$. If the sun were higher than $40.6^\circ$, the top of the violet arc would be below the horizon.

## fy7-11-L3-01
**Given:** apex angle $A = 50.0^\circ$, angle of incidence $\alpha_1 = 45.0^\circ$, refractive index of red $n_\text{r} = 1.5143$, of violet $n_\text{v} = 1.5302$, prism in air
**Find:** angle between the emerging rays $\Delta\delta = \delta_\text{v} - \delta_\text{r}$ in degrees
**Principle:** Each colour is refracted at both surfaces with its own refractive index, because the refractive index depends on wavelength. Both colours arrive from the same direction, so the angle between the emerging rays is the difference of the angles of deviation. From the geometry of the prism $\alpha_2 + \alpha_3 = A$ and $\delta = \alpha_1 + \alpha_4 - A$.
**Formula:** $\sin\alpha_2 = \dfrac{\sin\alpha_1}{n}$, $\alpha_3 = A - \alpha_2$, $\sin\alpha_4 = n \sin\alpha_3$ and $\delta = \alpha_1 + \alpha_4 - A$, so $\Delta\delta = \alpha_{4,\text{v}} - \alpha_{4,\text{r}}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the angles are in degrees, and the refractive indices have no unit.
**Substitution:** Red: $\sin\alpha_2 = \dfrac{\sin 45.0^\circ}{1.5143} = 0.46695$, $\alpha_2 = 27.84^\circ$, $\alpha_3 = 50.0^\circ - 27.84^\circ = 22.16^\circ$, $\sin\alpha_4 = 1.5143 \cdot \sin 22.16^\circ = 0.57127$, $\alpha_4 = 34.84^\circ$. Violet: $\sin\alpha_2 = \dfrac{\sin 45.0^\circ}{1.5302} = 0.46210$, $\alpha_2 = 27.52^\circ$, $\alpha_3 = 22.48^\circ$, $\sin\alpha_4 = 1.5302 \cdot \sin 22.48^\circ = 0.58502$, $\alpha_4 = 35.80^\circ$. $\Delta\delta = 35.80^\circ - 34.84^\circ = 0.96^\circ$
**Answer:** $0.96^\circ$ (with accurate intermediate values $0.966^\circ$), two significant figures, because the result is the difference of two almost equal angles.
**Reasonableness:** The angles of deviation are $\delta_\text{r} = 29.84^\circ$ and $\delta_\text{v} = 30.80^\circ$, and violet is deviated more, as it should be. The difference is greater than at one surface of glass of uniform thickness (about $0.3^\circ$), because the second surface of the prism increases it.

## fy7-11-L3-02
**Given:** angle of incidence $\alpha = 80.0^\circ$, refractive index of water for red light $n = 1.332$, one reflection from the back surface of the droplet
**Find:** angle $\theta$ from the direction of the antisolar point in degrees, and its comparison with the angle of the red edge, $42.3^\circ$
**Principle:** At the surface of the droplet light is refracted according to the law of refraction, and the refractive index of air is rounded to $1$. The ray turns in two refractions and one reflection, so the angle of the emerging light from the direction of the antisolar point is $\theta = 4\beta - 2\alpha$. The red edge of a rainbow is at an angle that no red ray exceeds.
**Formula:** $\sin\alpha = n \sin\beta$, so $\sin\beta = \dfrac{\sin\alpha}{n}$, and $\theta = 4\beta - 2\alpha$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the angles are in degrees.
**Substitution:** $\sin\beta = \dfrac{\sin 80.0^\circ}{1.332} = 0.73935$, so $\beta = 47.676^\circ$, and $\theta = 4 \cdot 47.676^\circ - 2 \cdot 80.0^\circ = 190.70^\circ - 160.0^\circ = 30.70^\circ$
**Answer:** $30.7^\circ$, three significant figures like the angle of incidence $80.0^\circ$. The angle is smaller than the angle of the red edge, $42.3^\circ$, so the light is seen inside the arc.
**Reasonableness:** The angle of red light cannot exceed $42.3^\circ$, and the result is smaller than that. Rays that hit near the edge like this light the sky inside the arc, which is therefore brighter than the sky outside the arc.

## fy7-11-L3-03
**Given:** red edge: angle of incidence $\alpha_\text{r} = 59.5^\circ$, refractive index $n_\text{r} = 1.3315$; violet edge: $\alpha_\text{v} = 58.8^\circ$, $n_\text{v} = 1.3431$; one reflection from the back surface of the droplet
**Find:** width of the arc $\Delta\theta = \theta_\text{r} - \theta_\text{v}$ in degrees
**Principle:** The angle of the edge of each colour from the direction of the antisolar point follows from the geometry of the droplet, $\theta = 4\beta - 2\alpha$, when the angle of refraction $\beta$ is calculated from the law of refraction with its own refractive index. The width of the arc is the difference between the angles of the red and violet edges, because red is on the outer edge.
**Formula:** $\sin\beta = \dfrac{\sin\alpha}{n}$, $\theta = 4\beta - 2\alpha$ and $\Delta\theta = \theta_\text{r} - \theta_\text{v}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the angles are in degrees.
**Substitution:** Red: $\sin\beta_\text{r} = \dfrac{\sin 59.5^\circ}{1.3315} = 0.64711$, $\beta_\text{r} = 40.324^\circ$, $\theta_\text{r} = 4 \cdot 40.324^\circ - 2 \cdot 59.5^\circ = 42.296^\circ$. Violet: $\sin\beta_\text{v} = \dfrac{\sin 58.8^\circ}{1.3431} = 0.63686$, $\beta_\text{v} = 39.558^\circ$, $\theta_\text{v} = 4 \cdot 39.558^\circ - 2 \cdot 58.8^\circ = 40.632^\circ$. $\Delta\theta = 42.296^\circ - 40.632^\circ = 1.664^\circ$
**Answer:** about $1.66^\circ$, that is, rounded $1.7^\circ$: the result is the difference of two almost equal angles, so significant figures are lost.
**Reasonableness:** The width is about three times the apparent diameter of the sun, $0.5^\circ$, which fits experience: a rainbow is a narrow band. The angle of red is greater, so red is on the outer edge.
