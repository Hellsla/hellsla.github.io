## Organising the exam

The practice exam is full length: $6\,\text{h}$, $11$ problems, of which the student answers seven (Part I compulsory, $20$ p; four of the seven problems of Part II, $15$ p each; two of the three of Part III, $20$ p each; $120$ p in total). The structure was confirmed by the teacher on 4 November 2026. Before the course, check on the website of the Matriculation Examination Board whether the structure, the marking or the allowed programs have changed.

### On one six-hour day (recommended)
- Book a hall or classroom for the whole day, for example from 9 to 15, and computers with calculator, spreadsheet, graphing and formula editor programs. If the school uses a practice environment for the exam, the problems can be copied there; otherwise the students answer in a word processor and attach the graphs as screenshots.
- The model answers on the page are locked until the student marks the problem as done, but they can be opened. During the exam only the problems are read from the page: ask the students to close the page once they have copied or printed the problems, or hand out the problems on paper or in your own exam environment.
- The multiple-choice items and short calculations of Part I can be done on the page if you make sure that the other parts are not opened. Otherwise hand out Part I on paper.
- Explain the time plan (unit.md): $15\,\text{min}$ for reading and choosing, Part I about $50\,\text{min}$, the problems of Part II about $40\,\text{min}$ and those of Part III about $55\,\text{min}$ each, and checking at the end. Give a reminder halfway through.
- Food and drinks may be brought in, as in the real exam.

### In parts, if six hours are not available
- **Three $2\,\text{h}$ sessions**: (1) reading and choosing, Part I and one problem of Part II; (2) three problems of Part II; (3) two problems of Part III. The student chooses the problems in the first session and writes the choice down, so that the choice does not change after the problems have been seen.
- **$75\,\text{min}$ lessons** (five sessions): (1) reading and choosing and Part I; (2) and (3) two problems of Part II at a time; (4) and (5) one problem of Part III at a time. The student writes down the choice in the first session. The model answers are opened only after all the sessions.
- Done at home, the exam is good practice, but then the use of time and the aids are not comparable.

## Assessment with the marking scheme

- **Part I** ($20$ p): the page checks the multiple-choice items and the results of the short calculations. Instructions for the student: a multiple-choice item and a statement give $2$ p if the first choice is correct, otherwise $0$ p; a calculation gives $2$ p for a correct result with a complete solution and $1$ p for a correct method if the result is wrong or the unit is missing. If you assess it yourself, look at the solution in the notebook.
- **Parts II and III**: each problem has a marking scheme whose criteria are whole points ($1$–$4$ p), $15$ or $20$ p in total. Give the points of a criterion only when the answer shows what the criterion requires; *Also accepted* gives the other acceptable solutions and *Common mistakes* the most typical errors that cost points.
- **Self-assessment and peer assessment**: the student first marks their own answers, and you or a partner check a sample, for example one problem of Part II and one of Part III. A difference of more than $3$ p per problem shows that it is worth going through the criteria together.
- **Grades**: if you want to convert the points into a course grade or an estimate of a matriculation grade, the limits are up to your own judgement. The grade limits of the matriculation exam are decided separately for each exam on the basis of the distribution of points, so no point limit of this practice exam is official or predicts the grade reliably. Tell the students that any limits are only indicative.

## After the exam
- Collect which problems were chosen and which gave the fewest points. The problems left unchosen show which topics the students avoid; go through one of them together.
- Every problem has a list of the chapters whose skills it uses. Guide the student to review the chapters in whose problems they got less than half the points, and to do one problem from them from the course's exam task mixer.
- Ask the students to compare their use of time with the plan. Many spend too long on Part I; for them a drill with five minutes for each item of Part I is suitable.

## Differentiation
- **Fast students**: if time is left, the student solves one of the unchosen problems without the model answer and marks it separately. It is not counted in the points of the exam.
- **Students who need support**: a shorter version with Part I, two problems of Part II and one problem of Part III ($70$ p, about $3.5\,\text{h}$). Special arrangements, such as extra time, are made in the same way as in the real exam.
- The page does not save the answers or the points anywhere; only the student sees the results.
