## fy4-14-L1-01
**Given:** $m = 75.0\,\text{kg}$, the skydiver falls at terminal velocity; $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the air resistance $F_\text{v}$ in $\text{N}$
**Principle:** At terminal velocity the speed does not change, so the acceleration is zero. By Newton's second law the net force is then zero, even though the skydiver is moving: air resistance cancels the weight.
**Free-body diagram:** The skydiver as a rectangle, positive direction down. The weight $\overline{G}$ from the centre down and the air resistance $\overline{F}_\text{v}$ from the bottom surface up, equally long. The velocity is not drawn as a force.
```voimakuvio
G 735,8
F_v 735,8 ylös työntö @ala
a 0
```
**Formula:** $mg - F_\text{v} = 0$, so $F_\text{v} = mg$
**Conversions:** The mass is in kilograms, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $F_\text{v} = 75.0\,\text{kg}\cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 = 735.75\,\text{N}$
**Answer:** $736\,\text{N}$ upward, three significant figures like the mass $75.0\,\text{kg}$.
**Reasonableness:** The weight of one kilogram is about $10\,\text{N}$, so the weight of a $75\,\text{kg}$ skydiver is about $750\,\text{N}$, and at terminal velocity the air resistance is equal to it. Air resistance is not greater than the weight, because then the skydiver would slow down.

## fy4-14-L1-02
**Given:** $m = 2.00\,\text{kg}$, air resistance $F_\text{v} = 5.00\,\text{N}$, the stone is falling down; $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the net force $\Sigma F$ in $\text{N}$
**Principle:** The Earth (weight) and the air (air resistance) act on the stone. The air resistance of a falling stone points against the velocity, that is up, so the net force is the difference of the weight and the air resistance.
**Free-body diagram:** The stone as a rectangle, positive direction down. The weight $\overline{G}$ from the centre down and the air resistance $\overline{F}_\text{v}$ from the bottom surface up, shorter than the weight.
```voimakuvio
G 19,62
F_v 5 ylös työntö @ala
a alas
```
**Formula:** $\Sigma F = G - F_\text{v}$, and $G = mg$, so $\Sigma F = mg - F_\text{v}$
**Conversions:** The mass is in kilograms and the force in newtons, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $\Sigma F = 2.00\,\text{kg}\cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 - 5.00\,\text{N} = 19.62\,\text{N} - 5.00\,\text{N} = 14.62\,\text{N}$
**Answer:** $14.6\,\text{N}$ downward, three significant figures like the mass $2.00\,\text{kg}$ and the air resistance $5.00\,\text{N}$.
**Reasonableness:** The net force is smaller than the weight $19.6\,\text{N}$ but positive, so the stone is still accelerating downward, now more slowly than without air. The net force decreases as the speed grows, because air resistance grows.

## fy4-14-L1-03
**Given:** $m = 0.600\,\text{kg}$, air resistance $F_\text{v} = 8.00\,\text{N}$, the ball has been thrown down and is moving down; $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the acceleration $a$ of the ball in $\text{m/s}^2$ and its direction
**Principle:** By Newton's second law the acceleration is determined by the net force. The ball moves down, so the air resistance points up, against the velocity. Air resistance is greater than the weight, so the net force points up even though the ball is moving down.
**Free-body diagram:** The ball as a rectangle, positive direction down. The weight $\overline{G}$ from the centre down and the air resistance $\overline{F}_\text{v}$ from the bottom surface up, longer than the weight.
```voimakuvio
G 5,886
F_v 8 ylös työntö @ala
a ylös
```
**Formula:** $mg - F_\text{v} = ma$, so $a = \dfrac{mg - F_\text{v}}{m}$
**Conversions:** The quantities are in SI units, and $\tfrac{\text{N}}{\text{kg}} = \tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2}$, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $a = \dfrac{0.600\,\text{kg}\cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 - 8.00\,\text{N}}{0.600\,\text{kg}} = \dfrac{5.886\,\text{N} - 8.00\,\text{N}}{0.600\,\text{kg}} = -3.523\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Answer:** $3.52\,\text{m/s}^2$ upward, three significant figures like the mass $0.600\,\text{kg}$ and the air resistance $8.00\,\text{N}$. The minus sign tells that the acceleration points up.
**Reasonableness:** Air resistance is about $1.4$ times the weight $5.9\,\text{N}$, so about $2\,\text{N}$ remains upward and the acceleration is about $2\,\text{N} : 0.6\,\text{kg} \approx 3.5\,\text{m/s}^2$. The ball slows down until the air resistance has decreased to the size of the weight.

## fy4-14-L1-05
**Given:** the speed is constant, air resistance $F_\text{v} = 18\,\text{N}$, rolling resistance (the resistance caused by the tyres and the road) $F_\text{r} = 6.0\,\text{N}$
**Find:** the friction force of the road $F$ that pushes the driving wheel forward, in $\text{N}$
**Principle:** In uniform motion the acceleration is zero, so by Newton's second law the net force is zero. In the horizontal direction, the friction force of the road, with which the road pushes the driving wheel forward, must cancel both resistive forces; the rolling resistance is a separate resistive force here. In the vertical direction the weight and the normal force of the road cancel each other.
**Free-body diagram:** The cyclist and the bicycle as one rectangle, positive direction forward. The weight $\overline{G}$ from the centre down and the normal force of the road $\overline{N}$ from the bottom surface up, equally long. The friction force of the road $\overline{F}$ from the bottom surface of the driving wheel forward, the air resistance $\overline{F}_\text{v}$ and the rolling resistance $\overline{F}_\text{r}$ backward.
```voimakuvio
pinta 0
G 784,8
N 784,8
F 24 oikea kitka
F_v 18 vasen
F_r 6,0 vasen
a 0
```
**Formula:** $F - F_\text{v} - F_\text{r} = 0$, so $F = F_\text{v} + F_\text{r}$
**Conversions:** All forces are in newtons, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $F = 18\,\text{N} + 6.0\,\text{N} = 24\,\text{N}$
**Answer:** $24\,\text{N}$ forward, two significant figures like the air resistance $18\,\text{N}$.
**Reasonableness:** The force forward is equal to the resistive forces together, not greater, because the speed does not grow. A force of a few tens of newtons fits ordinary cycling on a level road; most of it goes to air resistance.

## fy4-14-L3-02
**Given:** $m = 0.200\,\text{kg}$, air resistance $F_\text{v} = 0.600\,\text{N}$ both on the way up and on the way down; $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the difference of the magnitudes of the accelerations $\Delta a$ in $\text{m/s}^2$
**Principle:** Air resistance always points against the velocity. On the way up it points down together with the weight, on the way down up against the weight. Newton's second law gives the acceleration at both moments, and they are subtracted from each other.
**Free-body diagram:** Two diagrams, the ball as a rectangle. On the way up, the weight $\overline{G}$ from the centre down and the air resistance $\overline{F}_\text{v}$ from the top surface down. On the way down, the weight down and the air resistance from the bottom surface up. In both, the positive direction is down.
```voimakuvio
G 1,962
F_v 0,6 alas työntö @ylä
a alas
---
G 1,962
F_v 0,6 ylös työntö @ala
a alas
```
**Formula:** On the way up $mg + F_\text{v} = ma_1$, so $a_1 = g + \dfrac{F_\text{v}}{m}$. On the way down $mg - F_\text{v} = ma_2$, so $a_2 = g - \dfrac{F_\text{v}}{m}$. The difference is $\Delta a = a_1 - a_2 = \dfrac{2F_\text{v}}{m}$
**Conversions:** The quantities are in SI units, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $\Delta a = \dfrac{2\cdot 0.600\,\text{N}}{0.200\,\text{kg}} = 6.00\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Answer:** $6.00\,\text{m/s}^2$, three significant figures like the mass $0.200\,\text{kg}$ and the air resistance $0.600\,\text{N}$.
**Reasonableness:** Calculated separately, $a_1 = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 + 3.00\,\text{m/s}^2 = 12.81\,\text{m/s}^2$ and $a_2 = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 - 3.00\,\text{m/s}^2 = 6.81\,\text{m/s}^2$, and their difference is $6.00\,\text{m/s}^2$. Both accelerations point down, but on the way up it is greater than $g$ and on the way down smaller.

Later the curve flattens, the air resistance grows and approaches the weight.

## fy4-14-L1-06
**Find:** which statement correctly describes a falling object that has reached its terminal velocity.
**Principle:** At terminal velocity the speed of the object does not change, so the acceleration is zero. By Newton's second law the net force is then zero, even though the object is moving. The weight down and the air resistance up still act on the object.
**Model answer:** The correct statement is that the air resistance and the weight are equal and the net force is zero. If the air resistance were greater than the weight, the net force would point up and the object would slow down. If the weight were greater, the net force would point down and the speed would grow. The claim that no forces act at all is wrong, because the speed stays constant only since two forces cancel each other. The direction of motion does not tell the magnitudes of the forces.

## fy4-14-L1-07
**Find:** which of the balls of the same size and shape, the one filled with sand or the empty one, reaches the greater terminal velocity.
**Principle:** At terminal velocity the air resistance is equal to the weight. About the same air resistance acts on balls of the same size and shape at the same speed, and the air resistance grows as the speed grows.
**Model answer:** The sand ball reaches the greater terminal velocity. Its weight is greater, so the air resistance must grow greater before it cancels the weight. Air resistance grows only as the speed grows, so the heavier ball has to accelerate to a greater speed. The empty ball levels off at a smaller speed, because its weight is smaller. The claim that all objects fall in the same way holds only without air resistance. In air the difference in weight shows in the terminal velocity.
