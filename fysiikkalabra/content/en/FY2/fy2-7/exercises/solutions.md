## fy2-7-L1-01
**Given:** energy fed into the network $E_\text{sent} = 500\,\text{MWh}$, loss $4.0\,\%$ of the sent energy
**Find:** the energy that arrives $E_\text{arrived}$ in megawatt-hours
**Principle:** The loss percentage is taken of the sent energy. When $4.0\,\%$ is lost, the remaining $96.0\,\%$ arrives, so the efficiency of the transfer is $\eta = 0.960$.
**Formula:** $\eta = \dfrac{E_\text{arrived}}{E_\text{sent}}$, so $E_\text{arrived} = \eta\, E_\text{sent}$
**Conversions:** The efficiency is obtained from the percentage: $\eta = 100\,\% - 4.0\,\% = 96.0\,\% = 96.0 : 100 = 0.960$. The unit of energy stays the same.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{arrived} = 0.960 \cdot 500\,\text{MWh} = 480\,\text{MWh}$
**Answer:** $480\,\text{MWh}$, three significant figures as in the given data.
**Reasonableness:** The loss is $4.0\,\% \cdot 500\,\text{MWh} = 20\,\text{MWh}$, and $480\,\text{MWh} + 20\,\text{MWh} = 500\,\text{MWh}$, so the energy is conserved. The arriving energy is smaller than the sent energy, as it should be.

**Principle:** Energy does not disappear. The part of the sent energy that does not arrive turns into heat in the surroundings on the way, that is, it is transmission loss. The share of the loss is taken of the sent energy, so the loss is the share times the sent energy.
**Reading the graph:** The lower energy flow in the figure is the district heating network. So the share of the loss is $9\,\% = 0.09$. The upper flow is the electricity grid and is not needed.
**Formula:** $E_\text{loss} = 0.09 \cdot E_\text{sent}$
**Conversions:** Percentage to decimal number: $9\,\% = 9 \cdot 0.01 = 0.09$. The energy stays in megawatt-hours.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{loss} = 0.09 \cdot 360\,\text{MWh} = 32.4\,\text{MWh}$
**Answer:** about $32\,\text{MWh}$, two significant figures, because the percentage in the figure is rounded to whole percent.
**Reasonableness:** One tenth of $360\,\text{MWh}$ is $36\,\text{MWh}$, and $9\,\%$ is a little less than a tenth, so about $32\,\text{MWh}$ is the right order of magnitude. The energy that arrives is $360\,\text{MWh} - 32.4\,\text{MWh} \approx 328\,\text{MWh}$, or $91\,\%$, as in the figure.

## fy2-7-L2-01
**Given:** arrived energy $E_\text{arrived} = 600\,\text{MWh}$, loss $20\,\%$ of the sent energy
**Find:** the energy lost $E_\text{loss}$ in megawatt-hours
**Principle:** The loss percentage belongs to the sent energy, not to the energy that arrived. So first calculate the sent energy using the efficiency, and then the loss as a difference.
**Formula:** $\eta = \dfrac{E_\text{arrived}}{E_\text{sent}}$, so $E_\text{sent} = \dfrac{E_\text{arrived}}{\eta}$, and $E_\text{loss} = E_\text{sent} - E_\text{arrived}$
**Conversions:** $\eta = 100\,\% - 20\,\% = 80\,\% = 80 : 100 = 0.80$. The unit of energy stays megawatt-hours.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{sent} = \dfrac{600\,\text{MWh}}{0.80} = 750\,\text{MWh}$, and $E_\text{loss} = 750\,\text{MWh} - 600\,\text{MWh} = 150\,\text{MWh}$
**Answer:** $150\,\text{MWh}$, two significant figures as in the loss percentage $20\,\%$.
**Reasonableness:** The loss is $20\,\% \cdot 750\,\text{MWh} = 150\,\text{MWh}$, which matches. If the percentage were taken of the energy that arrived, we would get $0.20 \cdot 600\,\text{MWh} = 120\,\text{MWh}$, which is too little: the sent energy would then be $720\,\text{MWh}$, and $120\,\text{MWh}$ of that is only about $17\,\%$, not $20\,\%$.

## fy2-7-L1-06
**Find:** why electrical energy is transmitted at a high voltage in the national grid.
**Principle:** In the transmission of electricity, the loss occurs when current heats the conductors. The power of electricity depends on the voltage and the current, so the higher the voltage, the smaller the current that is enough for the same power. A smaller current heats the conductors less.
**Model answer:** At a high voltage, a smaller current is enough for the same power, so the conductors heat up less and the loss becomes smaller. Voltage does not add energy, and the loss still does not disappear completely: about $4\,\%$ of the sent energy is still lost in the grid. The cause of the loss is not the speed of electricity but the heating of the conductor as current flows.

The share for the whole day is obtained from the sums, not from the average of the percentages of the periods, because different amounts of energy are fed in during the periods.
**Formula:** $E_\text{sent} = P_\text{sent}\, t$, $E_\text{loss} = p_i\, E_\text{sent}$ for period $i$, and for the whole day $p = \dfrac{E_\text{loss,total}}{E_\text{sent,total}}$
**Conversions:** The loss shares are converted into decimal numbers, for example $1.5\,\% = 1.5 : 100 = 0.015$. A megawatt times an hour is directly a megawatt-hour.
**Substitution:** The energies fed in during the periods (hours · megawatts = megawatt-hours) and the losses (share · energy, in megawatt-hours) are
```latex
\begin{aligned}
E_1 &= 7 \cdot 18 = 126\,\text{MWh} \\
H_1 &= 0.015 \cdot 126 = 1.89\,\text{MWh} \\
E_2 &= 5 \cdot 42 = 210\,\text{MWh} \\
H_2 &= 0.035 \cdot 210 = 7.35\,\text{MWh} \\
E_3 &= 7 \cdot 50 = 350\,\text{MWh} \\
H_3 &= 0.042 \cdot 350 = 14.70\,\text{MWh} \\
E_4 &= 5 \cdot 66 = 330\,\text{MWh} \\
H_4 &= 0.055 \cdot 330 = 18.15\,\text{MWh}
\end{aligned}
```
The sum of the energies fed in is $1016\,\text{MWh}$ and the sum of the losses $42.09\,\text{MWh}$. So $p = \dfrac{42.09\,\text{MWh}}{1016\,\text{MWh}} = 0.0414 = 4.1\,\%$
**Answer:** $4.1\,\%$, two significant figures as in the loss shares (example data set). The result is close to the loss of about $4\,\%$ in the Finnish electricity grid (Finnish Energy, 2024), that is, of the same order of magnitude. The lost energy is greatest in the period 19–24, when $18.15\,\text{MWh}$ is lost, about $43\,\%$ of the loss of the whole day. A greater power requires a greater current, and the loss grows faster than the current (a doubled current gives about a fourfold loss), so the loss grows faster than the power fed in, and its share increases.
**Reasonableness:** The ordinary average of the loss shares of the periods would be $3.7\,\%$, which is too small, because a lot of energy is fed in during the evening and the share is then the greatest. The result $4.1\,\%$ lies between the shares $1.5\,\%$ and $5.5\,\%$, and $0.041 \cdot 1016\,\text{MWh} \approx 42\,\text{MWh}$ matches the sum $42.09\,\text{MWh}$.
