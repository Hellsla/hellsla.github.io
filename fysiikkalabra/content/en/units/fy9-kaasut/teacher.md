## Lesson plan (75 min)

- **0–5 min, Think about it.** The tyre in a warm garage and after a night of frost. Pairs first estimate in words whether the gauge reading rises or falls and by how much. The answer is not given yet: it is at the end of the Key idea part of the Theory summary (total pressure $331\,\text{kPa} \cdot 253\,\text{K} : 293\,\text{K} \approx 286\,\text{kPa}$, gauge about $185\,\text{kPa}$). Ask who calculated with degrees Celsius or with the gauge reading alone: these are the two most important mistakes of the lesson.
- **5–15 min, Predict (diagnostic test).** Six questions alone, without notes. The students write the numbers of the questions they got wrong in their notebooks.
- **15–25 min, Investigate.** The $(T, p)$ graph of the two containers in pairs: one reads a point and says it aloud, the other does the unit conversions. Discuss together: why the line of gas A is steeper and why the extensions pass through the origin.
- **25–35 min, Theory summary.** Go through the concept map and ask for one example for each branch. Return to Think about it with the tyre chart: the total pressure decreases by $14\,\%$, the gauge reading by $20\,\%$.
- **35–55 min, Apply.** Four multiple-choice questions in pairs (about $6\,\text{min}$), then four calculations alone (about $14\,\text{min}$, that is, $3$–$4\,\text{min}$ per calculation; unfinished ones are done at home). Fast students continue without hints, slower ones use the hints one at a time.
- **55–65 min, Check your understanding and exit ticket.** Alone. The explanation task (the suction pump of a well) is self-assessed with the criteria.
- **65–75 min, Problems.** In ten minutes there is time for the beginning of one long problem in exam conditions, without the model answer: recommended *Compressed-air lift* a–b (calculation problem) or *Vacuum lifter and glass pane* a (cross-module problem). The rest of the problem and the other five problems are homework, two or three at a time before the next lesson.

## Using the diagnostic test
The Predict questions are a diagnostic test, not a grade. Each question corresponds to one chapter:

- Questions 1 and 2: pressure and pressure at depth (chapter *Pressure and hydrostatic pressure*).
- Questions 3 and 4: Boyle's law and the ratio of kelvin temperatures (chapter *Changes of state of a gas and Boyle's law*).
- Question 5: the amount of substance does not depend on the kind of gas (chapter *Ideal gas law $pV = nRT$*).
- Question 6: the limit of a heat engine (chapter *Work done by a gas and the heat engine*).

If more than a third of the group answers question 4 wrong, work through one example on the board with degrees Celsius and kelvins side by side before the Apply part. The mistakes in question 2 are usually corrected in the Apply task *The service hatch of a diving pool*.

## Pair work
- Investigate: one reads from the graph, the other converts the units; swap at gas B.
- Apply multiple-choice questions: each first chooses alone and only then justifies the choice to the partner. They answer only when the pair agrees.
- In the long problem *A ship powered by the heat of seawater* (written answer) pairs can write part a together and assess it with another pair using the marking scheme.

## Fast and slow
- **Fast students** do the Apply calculations without hints and go straight to the long problem *Air in a glass syringe* (graph, $20$ points), which has the most steps. Extra question: what would the volume of the air in the syringe be in a frost of $-25\,{}^\circ\text{C}$? (From the line about $34\,\text{ml}$.)
- **Slower students** do the Apply calculations *The service hatch of a diving pool* and *The gauge of a bicycle pump* with the hints and leave *Hot-air balloon* for home. The *Review if needed* links of the Theory summary lead straight to the original chapters, which have basic-level exercises.

## Assessment
The long problems are self-assessed with the marking scheme. The calculation problems, the written-answer, experimental and cross-module problems are $15$-point problems and the graph problem a $20$-point problem, as in parts II and III of the exam. If the teacher assesses them, the same criteria work: in a calculation part the justification of the principle, the total pressure and the kelvin temperature, the symbolic solution, the unit conversions on substitution, the precision and the reasonableness; in a written part the assessment of the claim with a law and the numbers of the data. All the data are invented examples, not measurements or statistics.
