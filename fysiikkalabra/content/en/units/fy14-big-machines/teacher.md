## Timing (75 minutes)

- **0–7 min, Think about it.** Read the hook; the review question on the three conditions of a fusion plasma (the Lawson criterion).
- **7–12 min, Predict.** Both predictions individually, committed, with a reason. A quick show of hands for the budget estimate makes the reveal more memorable.
- **12–40 min, Investigate.** Pairs as a "newspaper desk": *After the stampede* and *Heating beyond the current* together (8 min), *Stronger magnets* and *Oil, money and big machines* (8 min), the data question on the machines (2 min), *Geneva 1985* (5 min), the source question on the joint statement (4 min), the order task (1 min).
- **40–48 min, Theory summary.** The key idea and the misconception; back to the predictions.
- **48–63 min, Apply.** The beam-speed calculation (5 min), the find-the-error task (1 min), the group debate on public money in 1976 and today (6 min), the paragraph started (3 min; finished at home if needed).
- **63–75 min, Check your understanding.** Three checks, the "two Genevas" comparison, the summary and the French corner.

**Fast pairs** look up in the course timeline (unit *Fusion history: an interactive timeline*) what happened to TFTR, JET and JT-60 after 1985 (deuterium–tritium records in 1993–1997), or calculate the speed of a $40\ \text{keV}$ hydrogen atom and compare it with the deuterium atom. **If time runs short**, read *Stronger magnets* aloud in one minute, skip the order task, and make the debate paragraph and the French corner homework.

## Organisation and preparation
- Pairs for the reading; groups of four for the debate (two for, two against). Assign sides.
- Recall $P = RI^2$ and $E_\text{k} = \frac{1}{2} m v^2$ in one minute before the predictions if the class needs it.
- If possible, show a photograph of the JET torus hall or a short official video clip of JET (EUROfusion, UKAEA) to make the scale real; the photo pass will add a licensed photograph where it fits.
- The debate prepares the chapter question *Should governments invest more public money in fusion research?* in chapter 6; keep the students' paragraphs.

## Assessment
- Self-assessment with the shown criteria. For a grade: the source question gives two different reasons why the rivals could agree on fusion and refers to the statement; the debate paragraph gives a 1976 argument linked to the oil crisis, a different argument for today, a fact from the unit and a clear conclusion without presenting a date for fusion power as fact; the "two Genevas" answer explains both steps and the reason for each.
- Physics: ohmic heating weakens because the resistance falls; neutral beams must be neutral to cross the magnetic field; superconductors remove resistive losses; the beam-speed calculation with units.

## Common difficulties
- "Less resistance means more current, so more heating." In a tokamak the current is set by the transformer and stays about the same; with $P = RI^2$ the heating falls. In a kettle connected to a fixed voltage it would be different ($P = U^2/R$); discuss this with strong students.
- The temperature record is read as "fusion solved"; the misconception and the find-the-error task address it (the Lawson criterion).
- Budget figures are in dollars of the time, not adjusted for inflation; the trend matters, not the exact value.
- ITER dates are institutional plans (2024 baseline, as of November 2026), not results; check iter.org before teaching if the schedule has changed.

## Extension ideas
- Compare the costs: about 332 million dollars a year for the whole US fusion programme in 1978 and ITER's estimated construction cost of tens of billions of euros. Why did the cost of a single machine grow so much?
- Read the whole joint statement of 21 November 1985 (Reagan Presidential Library) and find the other areas of cooperation it names.
- Radio-wave heating at the ion cyclotron frequency: calculate $f = qB/(2\pi m)$ for deuterium ions in a field of $3\ \text{T}$ (about $23\ \text{MHz}$, a radio frequency).
