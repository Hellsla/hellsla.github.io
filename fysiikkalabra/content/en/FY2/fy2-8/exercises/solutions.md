## fy2-8-L1-01
**Given:** energy $E = 3000\,\text{kWh}$; emission factor of hydropower $e = 24\,\text{g/kWh}$
**Find:** emission $m$ in kilograms
**Principle:** The emission is directly proportional to the energy produced, and the emission factor gives the emission per kilowatt-hour. So the emission is obtained by multiplying the energy by the emission factor.
**Formula:** $m = e \cdot E$
**Conversions:** The kilowatt-hours cancel, so the result comes out in grams. Grams are converted into kilograms: $1\,\text{g} = 0.001\,\text{kg}$.
**Substitution:** $m = 24\,\text{g/kWh} \cdot 3000\,\text{kWh} = 72\,000\,\text{g} = 72\,000 \cdot 0.001\,\text{kg} = 72\,\text{kg}$
**Answer:** $72\,\text{kg}$, two significant figures like the emission factor $24\,\text{g/kWh}$.
**Reasonableness:** With rounded numbers $25\,\text{g/kWh} \cdot 3000\,\text{kWh} = 75\,000\,\text{g} = 75\,000 \cdot 0.001\,\text{kg} = 75\,\text{kg}$, close to the result. The same electricity from coal would give $820\,\text{g/kWh} \cdot 3000\,\text{kWh} = 2\,460\,000\,\text{g} = 2\,460\,000 \cdot 0.001\,\text{kg} = 2460\,\text{kg}$, so with hydropower the emission is only a few per cent of that.

## fy2-8-L1-02
**Given:** energy $E = 3.0\,\text{kWh}$; emission factor of coal $e = 820\,\text{g/kWh}$
**Find:** emission $m$ in kilograms
**Principle:** Every kilowatt-hour produced causes the same emission, so the total emission is the emission factor times the energy.
**Formula:** $m = e \cdot E$
**Conversions:** The kilowatt-hours cancel, and the result comes out in grams. Finally $1\,\text{g} = 0.001\,\text{kg}$.
**Substitution:** $m = 820\,\text{g/kWh} \cdot 3.0\,\text{kWh} = 2460\,\text{g} = 2460 \cdot 0.001\,\text{kg} = 2.46\,\text{kg}$
**Answer:** $2.5\,\text{kg}$, two significant figures like the energy $3.0\,\text{kWh}$.
**Reasonableness:** With rounded numbers $800\,\text{g/kWh} \cdot 3\,\text{kWh} = 2400\,\text{g} = 2400 \cdot 0.001\,\text{kg} = 2.4\,\text{kg}$, close to the result. With coal, one kilowatt-hour causes less than a kilogram, so three kilowatt-hours cause less than three kilograms.

## fy2-8-L1-04
**Given:** emission factor of rooftop panels $e_1 = 41\,\text{g/kWh}$; emission factor of natural gas electricity $e_2 = 490\,\text{g/kWh}$; the same energy $E$
**Find:** how many times as large the emission $m_2$ of natural gas electricity is compared with the emission $m_1$ of rooftop panels
**Principle:** Each emission is the factor times the energy, $m = e \cdot E$. When the energy is the same, it cancels in the comparison, so the ratio of the emissions is the ratio of the emission factors. We compare by division.
**Formula:** $m = e \cdot E$; when the energy $E$ is the same, the emission of natural gas electricity compared with that of rooftop panels is as many times as large as the factor, so we compare the factors: ratio $= e_2 : e_1$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the factors are in the same unit $\text{g/kWh}$, so the unit cancels.
**Substitution:** $e_2 : e_1 = 490\,\text{g/kWh} : 41\,\text{g/kWh} = 11.95$
**Answer:** about $12$ times, two significant figures like the factor $41\,\text{g/kWh}$.
**Reasonableness:** With rounded numbers $500 : 40 = 12.5$, close to the result. Check by multiplication: one kilowatt-hour from rooftop panels causes $41\,\text{g}$, and $12 \cdot 41\,\text{g} = 492\,\text{g}$, which is almost the $490\,\text{g}$ of natural gas.

**Reading the graph:** The second bar from the top in the figure is natural gas, dark grey like the fossil fuels, and the number $490$ is at its end. The bar ends between the grid lines $400$ and $600$, a little before the middle ($500$). The unit of the axis is $\text{g}\ \text{CO}_2\text{-eq.}/\text{kWh}$, so $e = 490\,\text{g/kWh}$.
**Formula:** $m = e \cdot E$
**Conversions:** The kilowatt-hours cancel, and the result comes out in grams. Finally $1\,\text{g} = 0.001\,\text{kg}$.
**Substitution:** $m = 490\,\text{g/kWh} \cdot 2.4\,\text{kWh} = 1176\,\text{g} = 1176 \cdot 0.001\,\text{kg} = 1.176\,\text{kg}$

**Reasonableness:** With rounded numbers $500\,\text{g/kWh} \cdot 2\,\text{kWh} = 1000\,\text{g} = 1000 \cdot 0.001\,\text{kg} = 1\,\text{kg}$, close to the result. With gas, one kilowatt-hour causes less than a kilogram, so the emission of two kilowatt-hours is about a kilogram.

## fy2-8-L2-01
**Given:** energy $E = 2.0\,\text{GWh}$; emission factor of biomass $e = 230\,\text{g/kWh}$
**Find:** emission $m$ in tonnes
**Principle:** The emission is the emission factor times the energy. The factor is given per kilowatt-hour, so the energy must first be converted into kilowatt-hours.
**Formula:** $m = e \cdot E$
**Conversions:** $1\,\text{GWh} = 10^{6}\,\text{kWh}$, so $E = 2.0 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{kWh}$. Finally $1\,\text{g} = 10^{-6}\,\text{t}$.
**Substitution:** $m = 230\,\text{g/kWh} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{kWh} = 4.6 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{g} = 4.6 \cdot 10^{8} \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{t} = 460\,\text{t}$
**Answer:** $460\,\text{t}$, two significant figures like the energy $2.0\,\text{GWh}$ and the factor $230\,\text{g/kWh}$.
**Reasonableness:** With rounded numbers $200\,\text{g/kWh} \cdot 2 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{kWh} = 4 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{g} = 4 \cdot 10^{8} \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{t} = 400\,\text{t}$, close to the result. With biomass, one kilowatt-hour causes $0.23\,\text{kg}$, and two million kilowatt-hours give $0.23\,\text{kg} \cdot 2 \cdot 10^{6} = 460\,000\,\text{kg} = 460\,000 \cdot 0.001\,\text{t} = 460\,\text{t}$.

## fy2-8-L2-02
**Given:** energy $E = 3600\,\text{kWh}$; for coal $e_1 = 820\,\text{g/kWh}$ and for wind power $e_2 = 12\,\text{g/kWh}$
**Find:** decrease in the emission $m_1 - m_2$ in tonnes
**Principle:** The amount of energy stays the same, only the way of producing it changes. The decrease in the emission is the difference between the old and the new emission, and each emission is the factor times the energy.
**Formula:** $m_1 - m_2 = e_1 \cdot E - e_2 \cdot E = (e_1 - e_2) \cdot E$
**Conversions:** The kilowatt-hours cancel, so the result comes out in grams. Finally $1\,\text{g} = 10^{-6}\,\text{t}$.
**Substitution:** $m_1 - m_2 = (820\,\text{g/kWh} - 12\,\text{g/kWh}) \cdot 3600\,\text{kWh} = 808\,\text{g/kWh} \cdot 3600\,\text{kWh} = 2\,908\,800\,\text{g} = 2\,908\,800 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{t} = 2.9088\,\text{t}$
**Answer:** $2.9\,\text{t}$, two significant figures like the factors $820\,\text{g/kWh}$ and $12\,\text{g/kWh}$ (rounded medians; the zeros of the energy $3600\,\text{kWh}$ do not show precision).
**Reasonableness:** With coal the emission is $820 \cdot 3600\,\text{g} = 2\,952\,000\,\text{g} = 2\,952\,000 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{t} \approx 2.95\,\text{t}$ and with wind $12 \cdot 3600\,\text{g} = 43\,200\,\text{g} = 43\,200 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{t} \approx 0.043\,\text{t}$. With wind the emission is almost zero compared with coal, so the saving is almost the whole emission of coal.

## fy2-8-L2-03
**Given:** emission $m = 12\,\text{t}$; emission factor of natural gas $e = 490\,\text{g/kWh}$
**Find:** energy produced $E$ in kilowatt-hours
**Principle:** The emission is the emission factor times the energy, so the energy is obtained by dividing the emission by the emission factor.
**Formula:** $m = e \cdot E$, so $E = \dfrac{m}{e}$
**Conversions:** Substitute $1\,\text{t} = 10^{6}\,\text{g}$: $m = 12\,\text{t} = 12 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{g}$.
**Substitution:** $E = 12 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{g} : 490\,\text{g/kWh} = 24\,490\,\text{kWh}$
**Answer:** $24\,000\,\text{kWh}$, two significant figures like the emission $12\,\text{t}$.
**Reasonableness:** With rounded numbers $500\,\text{g/kWh} \cdot 24\,000\,\text{kWh} = 12 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{g} = 12 \cdot 10^{6} \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{t} = 12\,\text{t}$, so the result matches. The unit $\text{kWh}$ appears when grams are divided by the unit $\text{g/kWh}$.

## fy2-8-L2-04
**Given:** emission factor of petrol $e = 69\,\text{g/MJ}$; $1\,\text{kWh} = 3.6\,\text{MJ}$
**Find:** emission factor in the unit $\text{g/kWh}$
**Principle:** The emission factor gives the emission per unit of energy. One kilowatt-hour of fuel energy is $3.6\,\text{MJ}$, so its emission is $3.6$ times the emission per megajoule.
**Formula:** $e_\text{kWh} = e_\text{MJ} \cdot 3.6\,\text{MJ/kWh}$
**Conversions:** The unit $\text{MJ}$ cancels: $\text{g/MJ} \cdot \text{MJ/kWh} = \text{g/kWh}$.
**Substitution:** $e_\text{kWh} = 69\,\text{g/MJ} \cdot 3.6\,\text{MJ/kWh} = 248.4\,\text{g/kWh}$
**Answer:** $250\,\text{g/kWh}$, two significant figures like the factor $69\,\text{g/MJ}$.
**Reasonableness:** With rounded numbers $70 \cdot 3.6 = 252$, close to the result. The number is the emission per energy of the fuel, not the whole life cycle, so it is not the same thing as the emission factor of electricity in the table: the $\text{g/kWh}$ in the table means electricity produced, this one means fuel burnt. Per unit of electricity the emission would be larger, because in a power plant only part of the fuel energy becomes electricity and the rest is waste heat.

## fy2-8-L3-01
**Given:** petrol car $V = 6.0\,\textsf{l}$ per hundred kilometres, $32\,\text{MJ/}\textsf{l}$, $69\,\text{g/MJ}$; electric car $E = 18\,\text{kWh}$ per hundred kilometres
**Find:** emission factor of electricity $e$ in the unit $\text{g/kWh}$ at which the emissions are equal
**Principle:** Calculate the emission of the petrol car per hundred kilometres as the factor times the energy. The emission of the electric car is $e \cdot E$, and it is set equal to the emission of the petrol car.
**Formula:** $m_\text{petrol} = e_\text{p} \cdot V \cdot h$ and $m_\text{petrol} = e \cdot E$, so $e = \dfrac{m_\text{petrol}}{E}$, where $e_\text{p}$ is the emission factor of petrol and $h$ is its energy content per litre
**Conversions:** Not needed: the litres and megajoules cancel, and the emission comes out in grams.
**Substitution:** $m_\text{petrol} = 69\,\text{g/MJ} \cdot 6.0\,\textsf{l} \cdot 32\,\text{MJ/}\textsf{l} = 13\,248\,\text{g}$, and $e = 13\,248\,\text{g} : 18\,\text{kWh} = 736\,\text{g/kWh}$
**Answer:** $740\,\text{g/kWh}$, two significant figures like the consumption $6.0\,\textsf{l}$ and $18\,\text{kWh}$.
**Reasonableness:** The limit is close to the $820\,\text{g/kWh}$ of coal but below it. With natural gas electricity ($490\,\text{g/kWh}$) and wind electricity ($12\,\text{g/kWh}$) the emission of the electric car stays clearly below the limit. With rounded numbers $13\,000\,\text{g} : 18\,\text{kWh} \approx 720\,\text{g/kWh}$.
**Conclusion:** The factor of coal electricity $820\,\text{g/kWh}$ is only about $10\,\%$ larger than the limit $740\,\text{g/kWh}$. The difference is smaller than the uncertainty of the comparison, because for petrol only burning has been counted and not, for example, the emissions from producing, refining and transporting the oil. With coal electricity the emission reduction of an electric car is therefore small or nonexistent, within the accuracy of the comparison. With low-emission electricity, such as wind or hydropower, the electric car is clearly better.

## fy2-8-L3-02
**Given:** energy $E = 100\,\text{GWh}$ per year; yield of the solar park $q_\text{a} = 0.50\,\text{GWh/ha}$ and yield of the energy field $q_\text{b} = 0.010\,\text{GWh/ha}$ per year; $1\,\text{ha} = 0.01\,\text{km}^2$
**Find:** ratio of the field area $A_\text{b}$ needed by biomass electricity to the area of the solar park $A_\text{a}$ (areas calculated in square kilometres)
**Principle:** Every hectare produces the same energy per year, so the energy of a year is the yield per hectare times the area, in the same way as the emission is the factor times the energy. The area is obtained by dividing the energy needed by the yield per hectare.
**Formula:** $E = q \cdot A$, so $A = \dfrac{E}{q}$, and the ratio is $A_\text{b} : A_\text{a}$
**Conversions:** The gigawatt-hours cancel, so the area comes out in hectares. Hectares are converted into square kilometres by substituting $1\,\text{ha} = 0.01\,\text{km}^2$.
**Substitution:** $A_\text{b} = 100\,\text{GWh} : 0.010\,\text{GWh/ha} = 10\,000\,\text{ha} = 10\,000 \cdot 0.01\,\text{km}^2 = 100\,\text{km}^2$, and $A_\text{a} = 100\,\text{GWh} : 0.50\,\text{GWh/ha} = 200\,\text{ha} = 200 \cdot 0.01\,\text{km}^2 = 2.0\,\text{km}^2$, so $A_\text{b} : A_\text{a} = 100\,\text{km}^2 : 2.0\,\text{km}^2 = 50$
**Answer:** the field area needed is $50$ times the area of the solar park ($100\,\text{km}^2$ and $2.0\,\text{km}^2$), two significant figures like the yields $0.50\,\text{GWh/ha}$ and $0.010\,\text{GWh/ha}$.
**Reasonableness:** $100\,\text{km}^2$ is a square with a side of $10\,\text{km}$. The ratio of the areas $100 : 2.0 = 50$ is the same as the ratio of the yields $0.50 : 0.010 = 50$, as it should be, because the energy is the same.
**Conclusion:** Biomass electricity needs about $50$ times the area of a solar park, a square of field with a side of ten kilometres, which could otherwise grow food or forest. The emission factor tells only the greenhouse gases per kilowatt-hour; it does not tell how much land a way of producing energy takes from food production, nature and the landscape. That is why land use must be weighed separately.
## fy2-8-L3-03
**Given:** bus $30\,\textsf{l}$ per hundred kilometres, $36\,\text{MJ/}\textsf{l}$, $74\,\text{g/MJ}$, $25$ passengers; car $6.0\,\textsf{l}$ per hundred kilometres, $32\,\text{MJ/}\textsf{l}$, $69\,\text{g/MJ}$, one passenger
**Find:** ratio of the emission of the car to the emission of the bus per passenger
**Principle:** Calculate the emission of each vehicle per hundred kilometres as the factor times the energy and divide the emission of the bus among the passengers. Then compare the emissions of one person by division.
**Formula:** $m_\text{bus} = e_\text{b} \cdot V_\text{b} \cdot h_\text{b} : N$, $m_\text{car} = e_\text{c} \cdot V_\text{c} \cdot h_\text{c}$ and the ratio is $m_\text{car} : m_\text{bus}$, where $N$ is the number of passengers on the bus, $e$ the emission factor, $V$ the amount of fuel and $h$ the energy content per litre
**Conversions:** Not needed: the litres and megajoules cancel, and the emissions come out in grams.
**Substitution:** $m_\text{bus} = 74\,\text{g/MJ} \cdot 30\,\textsf{l} \cdot 36\,\text{MJ/}\textsf{l} : 25 = 79\,920\,\text{g} : 25 = 3196.8\,\text{g}$, $m_\text{car} = 69\,\text{g/MJ} \cdot 6.0\,\textsf{l} \cdot 32\,\text{MJ/}\textsf{l} = 13\,248\,\text{g}$, and $13\,248\,\text{g} : 3196.8\,\text{g} = 4.14$
**Answer:** $4.1$ times, two significant figures like the consumptions $6.0\,\textsf{l}$ and $30\,\textsf{l}$.
**Reasonableness:** The bus uses five times as much fuel as the car, but its load is shared among $25$ passengers, so the emission per passenger is smaller. With rounded numbers $13\,000 : 3200 \approx 4$.
