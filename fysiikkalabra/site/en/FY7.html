Induction and electrical energy

  1. Chapter 5Electromagnetic induction

    Why does a bicycle dynamo light the lamp only while the wheel is turning, even though the dynamo contains no battery or accumulator?

  2. Chapter 6Lenz's law and eddy currents

    Why does a strong magnet take several seconds to fall through a copper tube, although copper is not magnetic and an ordinary object falls the same distance in less than a second?

  3. Chapter 7The generator and alternating current

    Why does the rotating rotor of a wind turbine produce in the electricity grid a voltage that reverses its direction a hundred times a second?

  4. Chapter 8The transformer and the transmission of electrical energy

    Why is electricity carried from a power plant to a city at a voltage of $400\,\text{kV}$, when the voltage at a socket at home is only $230\,\text{V}$?

Radiation and the propagation of light

  1. Chapter 9Electromagnetic radiation and the black-body spectrum

    Why does an iron bar heated in a forge glow first red and then more and more yellow, and why can a thermal camera see a person even in pitch darkness?

  2. Chapter 10Reflection and refraction of light

    Why does a straw in a glass of water seem to break exactly at the surface of the water?

  3. Chapter 11Dispersion: the prism and the rainbow

    Why is the red of a rainbow always on the outer edge and the violet on the inner edge, and why can you see a rainbow only when the sun is behind you?

  4. Chapter 12Total internal reflection and optical fibre

    Why can light travel in a glass fibre along the bed of the Baltic Sea from Helsinki to Germany without escaping on the way, even though glass is transparent?

Module goals

Show all 4 goals

The student

  • understands the central importance of induction in electromagnetism
  • understands the physical basis of producing and transmitting electrical energy and its importance for society
  • recognises sources and effects of electromagnetic radiation
  • understands light as an electromagnetic phenomenon
