## fy8-10-L1-01
**Given:** mass of the carbon-12 atom $m_\text{atom} = 12.000000\,\text{u}$, $m_\text{H} = 1.007825\,\text{u}$, $m_\text{n} = 1.008665\,\text{u}$; the nucleus ${}^{12}_{6}\text{C}$ has $Z = 6$ protons and $N = 12 - 6 = 6$ neutrons
**Asked:** the mass defect $\Delta m$ in atomic mass units
**Principle:** A nucleus is lighter than its separate nucleons. When the masses of hydrogen atoms are used, they contain six electrons just like the carbon atom, so the masses of the electrons cancel and the difference is the mass defect of the nucleus.
**Formula:** $\Delta m = Z\,m_\text{H} + N\,m_\text{n} - m_\text{atom}$
**Unit conversions:** None needed: all masses are in atomic mass units.
**Substitution:** $\Delta m = 6 \cdot 1.007825\,\text{u} + 6 \cdot 1.008665\,\text{u} - 12.000000\,\text{u} = 6.046950\,\text{u} + 6.051990\,\text{u} - 12.000000\,\text{u} = 0.098940\,\text{u}$
**Answer:** $0.098940\,\text{u}$, six decimals like the given masses.
**Reasonableness:** The mass of the separate nucleons is about $12.10\,\text{u}$, so the mass defect is about $0.8\,\%$ of it, as it usually is for nuclei. The result is positive, because a bound nucleus is lighter than its parts.

## fy8-10-L1-02
**Given:** the mass defect of the oxygen-16 nucleus $\Delta m = 0.137005\,\text{u}$, $1\,\text{u} = 931.5\,\tfrac{\text{MeV}}{c^2}$
**Asked:** the binding energy $E_\text{B}$ in megaelectronvolts
**Principle:** By the equivalence of mass and energy, the mass defect corresponds to the energy $\Delta m\,c^2$. This is exactly the energy needed to break the nucleus into separate nucleons.
**Formula:** $E_\text{B} = \Delta m\,c^2$
**Unit conversions:** The mass is not converted to kilograms: $931.5\,\tfrac{\text{MeV}}{c^2}$ is put in place of the unit $\text{u}$, so $c^2$ cancels.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{B} = 0.137005\,\text{u} \cdot c^2 = 0.137005 \cdot 931.5\,\tfrac{\text{MeV}}{c^2} \cdot c^2 = 127.620\,\text{MeV}$
**Answer:** $127.6\,\text{MeV}$, four significant figures like the conversion factor $931.5\,\tfrac{\text{MeV}}{c^2}$.
**Reasonableness:** By estimation $0.14 \cdot 900\,\text{MeV} \approx 126\,\text{MeV}$. Per nucleon this is $127.6\,\text{MeV} : 16 \approx 8.0\,\text{MeV}$, which fits the binding energy graph at oxygen-16.

## fy8-10-L1-03
**Given:** the binding energy of the uranium-238 nucleus $E_\text{B} = 1801.7\,\text{MeV}$, mass number $A = 238$
**Asked:** the binding energy per nucleon $\tfrac{E_\text{B}}{A}$ in megaelectronvolts
**Principle:** The binding energy per nucleon tells how much energy is needed on average to remove one nucleon. It is obtained by dividing the whole binding energy by the number of nucleons, that is, the mass number.
**Formula:** $E_\text{B} = A \cdot \dfrac{E_\text{B}}{A}$, so the asked quantity is $\dfrac{E_\text{B}}{A}$, the binding energy divided by the mass number
**Unit conversions:** None needed: the energy is already in megaelectronvolts, and the mass number is a pure number.
**Substitution:** $\dfrac{E_\text{B}}{A} = \dfrac{1801.7\,\text{MeV}}{238} = 7.5702\,\text{MeV}$
**Answer:** $7.570\,\text{MeV}$, four significant figures like the conversion factor $931.5\,\tfrac{\text{MeV}}{c^2}$ with which the binding energy $1801.7\,\text{MeV}$ was calculated.
**Reasonableness:** In the binding energy graph the value for the heaviest nuclei is about $7.6\,\text{MeV}$, less than the about $8.8\,\text{MeV}$ of iron. The estimate $1800 : 240 = 7.5$ agrees.

## fy8-10-L1-04
**Given:** mass $m = 1.00\,\text{g}$, speed of light $c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$
**Asked:** the energy $E$ corresponding to the mass, in the unit $10^{13}\,\text{J}$
**Principle:** Mass and energy are equivalent: a mass $m$ corresponds to the energy $mc^2$. We calculate in SI units so that the energy is obtained in joules.
**Formula:** $E = mc^2$
**Unit conversions:** $1.00\,\text{g} = 1.00 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{kg}$, and $1\,\text{kg} \cdot \tfrac{\text{m}^2}{\text{s}^2} = 1\,\text{J}$.
**Substitution:** $E = 1.00 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{kg} \cdot (2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s})^2 = 1.00 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{kg} \cdot 8.988 \cdot 10^{16}\,\tfrac{\text{m}^2}{\text{s}^2} = 8.988 \cdot 10^{13}\,\text{J}$
**Answer:** $8.99 \cdot 10^{13}\,\text{J}$, three significant figures like the mass $1.00\,\text{g}$.
**Reasonableness:** By estimation $10^{-3} \cdot (3 \cdot 10^{8})^2 = 9 \cdot 10^{13}$. The energy is enormous: since $1\,\text{GWh} = 10^{9}\,\text{W} \cdot 3600\,\text{s} = 3.6 \cdot 10^{12}\,\text{J}$, it is about $25\,\text{GWh}$, so a nuclear power plant unit with an electric power of $1600\,\text{MW}$ produces it as electricity in about $16\,\text{h}$.

## fy8-10-L1-05
**Given:** the binding energy of the deuterium nucleus $E_\text{B} = 2.224\,\text{MeV}$, $1\,\text{u} = 931.5\,\tfrac{\text{MeV}}{c^2}$
**Asked:** the mass defect $\Delta m$ in the unit $10^{-3}\,\text{u}$
**Principle:** The binding energy is the energy corresponding to the mass defect. When the energy is known, the mass defect is obtained by dividing it by $c^2$.
**Formula:** $E_\text{B} = \Delta m\,c^2$, from which $\Delta m = \dfrac{E_\text{B}}{c^2}$
**Unit conversions:** $1\,\text{u} = 931.5\,\tfrac{\text{MeV}}{c^2}$, that is, $1\,\tfrac{\text{MeV}}{c^2} = \tfrac{1}{931.5}\,\text{u}$; finally $1\,\text{u} = 1000 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{u}$.
**Substitution:** $\Delta m = \dfrac{2.224\,\text{MeV}}{c^2} = 2.224 \cdot \tfrac{1}{931.5}\,\text{u} = 0.0023876\,\text{u} = 2.3876 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{u}$
**Answer:** $2.388 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{u}$, four significant figures like the binding energy and the conversion factor.
**Reasonableness:** Deuterium has only two nucleons, so the mass defect is small: about $0.12\,\%$ of the atomic mass $2.014\,\text{u}$, much less than for heavier nuclei. Check: $0.002388 \cdot 931.5\,\text{MeV} = 2.224\,\text{MeV}$.

## fy8-10-L2-01
**Given:** mass of the iron-56 atom $m_\text{atom} = 55.934936\,\text{u}$, $Z = 26$, $A = 56$, so $N = 30$; $m_\text{H} = 1.007825\,\text{u}$, $m_\text{n} = 1.008665\,\text{u}$, $1\,\text{u} = 931.5\,\tfrac{\text{MeV}}{c^2}$
**Asked:** the binding energy per nucleon $\tfrac{E_\text{B}}{A}$ in megaelectronvolts
**Principle:** The mass defect of the nucleus corresponds to the binding energy $E_\text{B} = \Delta m\,c^2$. The masses of hydrogen atoms are used so that the masses of $26$ electrons cancel with the electrons of the iron atom. Finally we divide by the number of nucleons.
**Formula:** $\Delta m = Z\,m_\text{H} + N\,m_\text{n} - m_\text{atom}$ and $E_\text{B} = \Delta m\,c^2$, so $\dfrac{E_\text{B}}{A} = \dfrac{(Z\,m_\text{H} + N\,m_\text{n} - m_\text{atom})\,c^2}{A}$
**Unit conversions:** $931.5\,\tfrac{\text{MeV}}{c^2}$ is put in place of the unit $\text{u}$, so $c^2$ cancels.
**Substitution:** $\Delta m = 26 \cdot 1.007825\,\text{u} + 30 \cdot 1.008665\,\text{u} - 55.934936\,\text{u} = 26.203450\,\text{u} + 30.259950\,\text{u} - 55.934936\,\text{u} = 0.528464\,\text{u}$, so $\dfrac{E_\text{B}}{A} = \dfrac{0.528464 \cdot 931.5\,\text{MeV}}{56} = \dfrac{492.264\,\text{MeV}}{56} = 8.7904\,\text{MeV}$
**Answer:** $8.790\,\text{MeV}$, four significant figures like the conversion factor $931.5\,\tfrac{\text{MeV}}{c^2}$.
**Reasonableness:** Iron-56 is at the peak of the binding energy graph, about $8.8\,\text{MeV}$, which agrees. Estimate: $\Delta m \approx 0.53\,\text{u}$ and $0.53 \cdot 930 : 56 \approx 8.8$.

## fy8-10-L2-02
**Given:** $m({}^{226}\text{Ra}) = 226.025410\,\text{u}$, $m({}^{222}\text{Rn}) = 222.017578\,\text{u}$, $m({}^{4}\text{He}) = 4.002603\,\text{u}$, $1\,\text{u} = 931.5\,\tfrac{\text{MeV}}{c^2}$
**Asked:** the energy released in the decay, that is, the reaction energy $Q$ in megaelectronvolts
**Principle:** In the reaction the mass number and the charge number are conserved, but the mass decreases, and the energy corresponding to the decrease of mass is released as the kinetic energy of the particles. Atomic masses can be used, because in alpha decay there are $88$ electrons on both sides: $86$ in the radon atom and $2$ in the helium atom.
**Formula:** $Q = (m_\text{initial} - m_\text{final})\,c^2$, where $m_\text{initial} = m({}^{226}\text{Ra})$ and $m_\text{final} = m({}^{222}\text{Rn}) + m({}^{4}\text{He})$
**Unit conversions:** $931.5\,\tfrac{\text{MeV}}{c^2}$ is put in place of the unit $\text{u}$, so $c^2$ cancels.
**Substitution:** $m_\text{initial} - m_\text{final} = 226.025410\,\text{u} - 222.017578\,\text{u} - 4.002603\,\text{u} = 0.005229\,\text{u}$, so $Q = 0.005229 \cdot 931.5\,\text{MeV} = 4.8708\,\text{MeV}$
**Answer:** $4.871\,\text{MeV}$, four significant figures like the mass difference $0.005229\,\text{u}$.
**Reasonableness:** $Q$ is positive, as it must be in a spontaneous decay. The energies of alpha particles are typically $4$–$9\,\text{MeV}$, and the result is of the same order of magnitude as the $4.27\,\text{MeV}$ of the alpha decay of uranium-238.

## fy8-10-L2-03
**Given:** mass defect $\Delta m = 0.137005\,\text{u}$, $1\,\text{u} = 1.66054 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\text{kg}$, $c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$
**Asked:** the binding energy $E_\text{B}$ in the unit $10^{-11}\,\text{J}$
**Principle:** The binding energy is the energy corresponding to the mass defect. In SI units the mass is in kilograms and the speed in metres per second, so the energy is obtained in joules.
**Formula:** $E_\text{B} = \Delta m\,c^2$
**Unit conversions:** $\Delta m = 0.137005\,\text{u} = 0.137005 \cdot 1.66054 \cdot 10^{-27}\,\text{kg} = 2.27502 \cdot 10^{-28}\,\text{kg}$
**Substitution:** $E_\text{B} = 2.27502 \cdot 10^{-28}\,\text{kg} \cdot (2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s})^2 = 2.27502 \cdot 10^{-28}\,\text{kg} \cdot 8.98800 \cdot 10^{16}\,\tfrac{\text{m}^2}{\text{s}^2} = 2.04479 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\text{J}$
**Answer:** $2.045 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\text{J}$, four significant figures like the speed of light $2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$.
**Reasonableness:** Let us convert to electronvolts by putting in $1\,\text{J} = \tfrac{1}{1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}}\,\text{eV}$: $2.045 \cdot 10^{-11} : 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{eV} \approx 1.277 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{eV} = 127.7\,\text{MeV}$, almost the same as the $127.6\,\text{MeV}$ calculated with the factor $931.5\,\tfrac{\text{MeV}}{c^2}$; the difference comes from the rounding of the constants.

## fy8-10-L2-04
**Given:** the radiated power of the Sun $P = 3.83 \cdot 10^{26}\,\text{W}$, time $t = 1\,\text{d}$, $c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$
**Asked:** the decrease $\Delta m$ of the mass of the Sun in one day, in the unit $10^{14}\,\text{kg}$
**Principle:** The radiation takes energy $E = Pt$ away from the Sun. By the equivalence of mass and energy, a decrease of energy decreases the mass by $\tfrac{E}{c^2}$. We assume the power constant and ignore the matter carried away by the solar wind.
**Formula:** $E = Pt$ and $E = \Delta m\,c^2$, so $\Delta m = \dfrac{Pt}{c^2}$
**Unit conversions:** $t = 1\,\text{d} = 24 \cdot 3600\,\text{s} = 86\,400\,\text{s}$, and $1\,\text{W} = 1\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{s}}$.
**Substitution:** $\Delta m = \dfrac{3.83 \cdot 10^{26}\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{s}} \cdot 86\,400\,\text{s}}{(2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s})^2} = \dfrac{3.3091 \cdot 10^{31}\,\text{J}}{8.988 \cdot 10^{16}\,\tfrac{\text{m}^2}{\text{s}^2}} = 3.6817 \cdot 10^{14}\,\text{kg}$
**Answer:** $3.68 \cdot 10^{14}\,\text{kg}$, three significant figures like the radiated power $3.83 \cdot 10^{26}\,\text{W}$.
**Reasonableness:** In a second the Sun loses about $4.3 \cdot 10^{9}\,\text{kg}$, and in $86\,400$ seconds about $4 \cdot 10^{9}\,\text{kg} \cdot 9 \cdot 10^{4} \approx 4 \cdot 10^{14}\,\text{kg}$. Compared with the mass of the Sun, about $2 \cdot 10^{30}\,\text{kg}$, the amount is negligible.

## fy8-10-L3-01
**Given:** $m({}^{4}\text{He}) = 4.002603\,\text{u}$, $m({}^{3}\text{He}) = 3.016029\,\text{u}$, $m_\text{n} = 1.008665\,\text{u}$, $1\,\text{u} = 931.5\,\tfrac{\text{MeV}}{c^2}$, reference value $\tfrac{E_\text{B}}{A} = 7.07\,\text{MeV}$
**Asked:** the energy $E$ needed to remove a neutron, in megaelectronvolts
**Principle:** The particles of the final state, helium-3 and a free neutron, are together heavier than helium-4. So the reaction does not release energy; an amount of energy corresponding to the difference of masses must be supplied, that is, the reaction energy is negative. Atomic masses can be used, because both helium atoms have two electrons.
**Formula:** $Q = (m_\text{initial} - m_\text{final})\,c^2$ and the energy needed is $E = -Q$, so $E = (m_\text{final} - m_\text{initial})\,c^2$, where $m_\text{final} = m({}^{3}\text{He}) + m_\text{n}$ and $m_\text{initial} = m({}^{4}\text{He})$
**Unit conversions:** $931.5\,\tfrac{\text{MeV}}{c^2}$ is put in place of the unit $\text{u}$, so $c^2$ cancels.
**Substitution:** $m_\text{final} - m_\text{initial} = 3.016029\,\text{u} + 1.008665\,\text{u} - 4.002603\,\text{u} = 0.022091\,\text{u}$, so $E = 0.022091 \cdot 931.5\,\text{MeV} = 20.578\,\text{MeV}$
**Answer:** $20.58\,\text{MeV}$, four significant figures like the conversion factor. This is almost three times the binding energy per nucleon of helium-4, $7.07\,\text{MeV}$.
**Reasonableness:** The energy must be positive, because a tightly bound nucleus is being broken up. It is greater than the average $7.07\,\text{MeV}$, because the helium-3 that is left is much more loosely bound: its binding energy is only about $7.7\,\text{MeV}$, that is, $2.6\,\text{MeV}$ per nucleon, so removing the neutron takes most of the binding energy $28.3\,\text{MeV}$ of helium-4. This is why helium-4 is exceptionally stable.

## fy8-10-L3-02
**Given:** for deuterium $\tfrac{E_\text{B}}{A} = 1.112\,\text{MeV}$ ($A = 2$), for helium-4 $\tfrac{E_\text{B}}{A} = 7.074\,\text{MeV}$ ($A = 4$)
**Asked:** the reaction energy $Q$ in megaelectronvolts
**Principle:** Let us imagine the reaction in two steps: the deuterium nuclei are first broken into nucleons, which requires their binding energy, and the nucleons are then assembled into a helium-4 nucleus, which releases its binding energy. The number of nucleons is conserved, so the energy released is the increase of the binding energy. By the conservation of energy, the intermediate steps do not matter.
**Formula:** $Q = E_\text{B}({}^{4}\text{He}) - 2\,E_\text{B}({}^{2}\text{H})$, where $E_\text{B} = A \cdot \tfrac{E_\text{B}}{A}$, so $Q = 4 \cdot \tfrac{E_\text{B}}{A}({}^{4}\text{He}) - 2 \cdot 2 \cdot \tfrac{E_\text{B}}{A}({}^{2}\text{H})$
**Unit conversions:** None needed: all energies are in megaelectronvolts.
**Substitution:** $Q = 4 \cdot 7.074\,\text{MeV} - 2 \cdot 2 \cdot 1.112\,\text{MeV} = 28.296\,\text{MeV} - 4.448\,\text{MeV} = 23.848\,\text{MeV}$
**Answer:** $23.85\,\text{MeV}$, four significant figures like the given binding energies $1.112\,\text{MeV}$ and $7.074\,\text{MeV}$.
**Reasonableness:** From atomic masses the same result: $(2 \cdot 2.014102\,\text{u} - 4.002603\,\text{u})\,c^2 = 0.025601 \cdot 931.5\,\text{MeV} \approx 23.85\,\text{MeV}$. Energy is released because helium-4 is much higher in the graph than deuterium: this is the source of the energy of fusion.

## fy8-10-L3-03
**Given:** mass of the water $m = 1.00\,\text{kg}$, initial temperature $80.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, final temperature $20.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, specific heat capacity $c_\text{v} = 4.19\,\tfrac{\text{kJ}}{\text{kg} \cdot \text{K}}$, speed of light $c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$
**Asked:** the decrease $\Delta m$ of the mass of the water, in the unit $10^{-12}\,\text{kg}$
**Principle:** As it cools, the water gives off heat $Q = c_\text{v} m \Delta T$, and its internal energy decreases by the same amount, because the state of matter does not change. The equivalence of mass and energy applies to every change of energy, so the mass decreases by $\tfrac{Q}{c^2}$.
**Formula:** $Q = c_\text{v} m \Delta T$ and $Q = \Delta m\,c^2$, so $\Delta m = \dfrac{c_\text{v} m \Delta T}{c^2}$
**Unit conversions:** $c_\text{v} = 4.19\,\tfrac{\text{kJ}}{\text{kg} \cdot \text{K}} = 4190\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{kg} \cdot \text{K}}$; the change of temperature is the same in degrees Celsius and in kelvins: $\Delta T = 80.0\,{}^\circ\text{C} - 20.0\,{}^\circ\text{C} = 60.0\,\text{K}$.
**Substitution:** $\Delta m = \dfrac{4190\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{kg} \cdot \text{K}} \cdot 1.00\,\text{kg} \cdot 60.0\,\text{K}}{(2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s})^2} = \dfrac{2.514 \cdot 10^{5}\,\text{J}}{8.988 \cdot 10^{16}\,\tfrac{\text{m}^2}{\text{s}^2}} = 2.797 \cdot 10^{-12}\,\text{kg}$
**Answer:** $2.80 \cdot 10^{-12}\,\text{kg}$, three significant figures like the mass, the specific heat capacity and the change of temperature.
**Reasonableness:** The change of mass is about three trillionths of the mass of the water, far below the precision of any balance. This is why the equivalence of mass and energy shows in practice only in nuclear reactions, where the mass defect is almost one per cent.
