After a long morning at ITER, you sit down at a café terrace in Provence with a friend. The waiter comes to your
table. In this lesson you practise everything you need there: ordering a drink and something to eat, asking what
there is and what it costs, asking for the bill, paying and thanking. The café, its menu and its prices are invented
for practice.

## At a French café: what happens when

A visit to a café follows a simple order, and each step has its own phrase.

<figure class="unit-photo"><img src="units/fy14/pics/paris-cafe.jpg" alt="Colour photograph of people sitting at tables on a café terrace on a Paris street." width="1600" height="1093" loading="lazy"><figcaption>A café terrace on the Rue de Buci in Paris. Ordering at a café is a classic first conversation in French. <span class="photo-credit">Photo: zoetnet, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons</span></figcaption></figure>

1. **Greet.** When the waiter comes, say <span lang="fr">Bonjour, madame</span> or <span lang="fr">Bonjour, monsieur</span> before anything else.
   Call a waiter with <span lang="fr">S'il vous plaît !</span> or <span lang="fr"><span class="nobr">Excusez-moi</span> !</span>, not with *<span lang="fr">Garçon !</span>*: it is old-fashioned, and many waiters find it rude.
2. **Order.** The waiter asks <span lang="fr">Vous avez choisi ?</span> or <span lang="fr">Vous désirez ?</span>, and you answer with
   **<span lang="fr">Je voudrais …</span>** (I would like) or **<span lang="fr">Je vais prendre …</span>** (I'll have), and <span lang="fr">s'il vous plaît</span>.
   <span lang="fr">Et avec ça ?</span> means *anything else?*
3. **Ask.** <span lang="fr"><span class="nobr">Qu'est-ce</span> que vous avez comme sandwichs ?</span> (What kind of sandwiches do you have?), <span lang="fr"><span class="nobr">Qu'est-ce</span> qu'il y a dans la quiche ?</span>
   (What is in the quiche?), <span lang="fr">C'est combien, le <span class="nobr">croque-monsieur</span> ?</span>
4. **Ask for the bill.** In France the waiter usually does not hurry you, and the bill often comes only when you
   ask for it: **<span lang="fr">L'addition, s'il vous plaît.</span>**
5. **Pay and thank.** <span lang="fr">Je peux payer par carte ?</span> or pay in cash (<span lang="fr">en espèces</span>). The service charge is already
   included in French prices by law (<span lang="fr">service compris</span>), so a tip is not required; many people simply round up
   a small bill: <span lang="fr">Gardez la monnaie.</span> (Keep the change.) Then <span lang="fr">Merci, bonne journée !</span> and <span lang="fr">Au revoir !</span>

**Coffee words.** *<span lang="fr">Un café</span>* is a small cup of strong black coffee. *<span lang="fr">Un café crème</span>* has milk or cream,
*<span lang="fr">un café noisette</span>* a little milk foam, and *<span lang="fr">un café allongé</span>* is a longer, weaker coffee.

## Polite ordering

The waiter is an adult you do not know, so you use **<span lang="fr">vous</span>**, even if you are only two teenagers at the table.
With your friend you say **<span lang="fr">tu</span>**: <span lang="fr">Tu prends quoi ?</span> (What are you having?).

The most useful polite form is **<span lang="fr">je voudrais</span>** (I would like). <span lang="fr">Je veux</span> (I want) is grammatically correct, but to
a waiter it sounds like a demand, as *I want* does in English. Short orders are fine when they end politely:
<span lang="fr">Un café, s'il vous plaît.</span>
