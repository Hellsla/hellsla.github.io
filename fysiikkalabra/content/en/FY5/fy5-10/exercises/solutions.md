## fy5-10-L1-01
**Given:** frequency $f = 4.0\,\text{Hz}$, wavelength $\lambda = 0.75\,\text{m}$
**Find:** the speed of the wave $v$
**Principle:** During one period the wave travels one wavelength, so the speed is the wavelength times the frequency. The fundamental equation holds for all periodic waves, also for a transverse wave travelling in a spring.
**Formula:** $v = f\lambda$
**Conversions:** Not needed: $1\,\text{Hz} = 1\,\text{s}^{-1}$, so $\text{Hz} \cdot \text{m} = \text{m/s}$.
**Substitution:** $v = 4.0\,\text{s}^{-1} \cdot 0.75\,\text{m} = 3.0\,\text{m/s}$
**Answer:** $3.0\,\text{m/s}$, two significant figures like the frequency $4.0\,\text{Hz}$ and the wavelength $0.75\,\text{m}$.
**Reasonableness:** The period is $T = \dfrac{1}{4.0\,\text{Hz}} = 0.25\,\text{s}$, and $0.75\,\text{m} : 0.25\,\text{s} = 3.0\,\text{m/s}$ gives the same. A few metres per second is a typical speed in a stretched spring: the wave can be seen travelling at a pace the eye can follow.

## fy5-10-L1-02
**Given:** frequency $f = 440\,\text{Hz}$, speed of sound in air $v = 343\,\text{m/s}$
**Find:** the wavelength $\lambda$
**Principle:** Sound is a longitudinal wave travelling in air, and the fundamental equation of wave motion holds for it as well: during one period the sound travels one wavelength. The frequency is the frequency of the vibration of the loudspeaker cone.
**Formula:** $v = f\lambda$, so $\lambda = \dfrac{v}{f}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: $\tfrac{\text{m/s}}{\text{Hz}} = \tfrac{\text{m/s}}{1/\text{s}} = \text{m}$.
**Substitution:** $\lambda = \dfrac{343\,\text{m/s}}{440\,\text{s}^{-1}} = 0.7795\,\text{m}$
**Answer:** $0.780\,\text{m}$, three significant figures like the speed $343\,\text{m/s}$ and the frequency $440\,\text{Hz}$.
**Reasonableness:** An estimate with round numbers: $\tfrac{350\,\text{m/s}}{450\,\text{Hz}} \approx 0.8\,\text{m}$. The wavelengths of audible sound in air range from about two centimetres to over ten metres, so a wavelength of a little under a metre suits a note in the middle of the range.

## fy5-10-L1-03
**Given:** speed of the wave $v = 6.0\,\text{m/s}$, distance between crests, that is, the wavelength $\lambda = 1.5\,\text{m}$
**Find:** the frequency $f$
**Principle:** The distance between two successive crests is the wavelength. Every point of the rope vibrates with the same frequency as the hand, so the frequency of the wave is the frequency of the shaking, and it follows from the fundamental equation.
**Formula:** $v = f\lambda$, so $f = \dfrac{v}{\lambda}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: $\tfrac{\text{m/s}}{\text{m}} = \text{s}^{-1} = \text{Hz}$.
**Substitution:** $f = \dfrac{6.0\,\text{m/s}}{1.5\,\text{m}} = 4.0\,\text{s}^{-1} = 4.0\,\text{Hz}$
**Answer:** $4.0\,\text{Hz}$, two significant figures like the initial values $6.0\,\text{m/s}$ and $1.5\,\text{m}$.
**Reasonableness:** Four shakes per second is fast but possible by hand. A check: in a second the wave travels $6.0\,\text{m}$, and $6.0\,\text{m} : 1.5\,\text{m} = 4$ wavelengths fit in that distance, that is, four crests formed in a second.

## fy5-10-L1-04
**Given:** period $T = 2.5\,\text{s}$, wavelength $\lambda = 4.0\,\text{m}$
**Find:** the speed of the waves $v$
**Principle:** During one period the wave travels one wavelength. So the speed is the wavelength divided by the period; this form of the fundamental equation fits because the problem gives the period and not the frequency.
**Formula:** $v = \dfrac{\lambda}{T}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the wavelength is in metres and the period in seconds.
**Substitution:** $v = \dfrac{4.0\,\text{m}}{2.5\,\text{s}} = 1.6\,\text{m/s}$
**Answer:** $1.6\,\text{m/s}$, two significant figures like the period $2.5\,\text{s}$ and the wavelength $4.0\,\text{m}$.
**Reasonableness:** The same follows through the frequency: $f = \tfrac{1}{2.5\,\text{s}} = 0.40\,\text{Hz}$ and $v = 0.40\,\text{Hz} \cdot 4.0\,\text{m} = 1.6\,\text{m/s}$. A pace a little faster than walking suits the gentle waves of a wave pool.

## fy5-10-L1-05
**Given:** speed of the wave $v = 4.8\,\text{m/s}$, wavelength $\lambda = 1.2\,\text{m}$
**Find:** the period $T$
**Principle:** The period is the time in which the wave travels one wavelength, so it follows from the form $v = \tfrac{\lambda}{T}$ of the fundamental equation. The period is also the time of one full vibration of every point of the rope.
**Formula:** $v = \dfrac{\lambda}{T}$, so $T = \dfrac{\lambda}{v}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: $\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{m/s}} = \text{s}$.
**Substitution:** $T = \dfrac{1.2\,\text{m}}{4.8\,\text{m/s}} = 0.25\,\text{s}$
**Answer:** $0.25\,\text{s}$, two significant figures like the speed $4.8\,\text{m/s}$ and the wavelength $1.2\,\text{m}$.
**Reasonableness:** The frequency is $f = \dfrac{1}{0.25\,\text{s}} = 4.0\,\text{Hz}$, and $4.0\,\text{Hz} \cdot 1.2\,\text{m} = 4.8\,\text{m/s}$, as given. The wavelength is a quarter of the distance the wave travels in a second, so the period must be a quarter of a second.

## fy5-10-L2-01
**Given:** the $(t, y)$ graph of one point of the rope (figure), distance between crests in the snapshot, that is, the wavelength $\lambda = 1.0\,\text{m}$
**Find:** the speed of the wave $v$
**Principle:** On the horizontal axis of the graph of one point there is time, so the distance between two similar points is the period $T$. The wavelength comes from the snapshot. During one period the wave travels one wavelength.
**Reading the graph:** The curve crosses the time axis going upwards at the moments $0$, $0.4\,\text{s}$, $0.8\,\text{s}$, … , $2.0\,\text{s}$. So in the interval $2.0\,\text{s}$ there are $5$ full vibrations, and $T = 2.0\,\text{s} : 5 = 0.40\,\text{s}$.
**Formula:** $v = \dfrac{\lambda}{T}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the wavelength is in metres and the period in seconds. The displacement in centimetres is not needed, because the amplitude does not affect the speed.
**Substitution:** $v = \dfrac{1.0\,\text{m}}{0.40\,\text{s}} = 2.5\,\text{m/s}$
**Answer:** $2.5\,\text{m/s}$, two significant figures like the wavelength $1.0\,\text{m}$ and the period $0.40\,\text{s}$ read from the graph.
**Reasonableness:** The frequency is $f = \tfrac{1}{0.40\,\text{s}} = 2.5\,\text{Hz}$, and $f\lambda = 2.5\,\text{Hz} \cdot 1.0\,\text{m} = 2.5\,\text{m/s}$ gives the same. The distance between peaks, $0.40\,\text{s}$, is not the wavelength, because it is a time.

## fy5-10-L2-02
**Given:** $N = 18$ full swings in the time $t = 12.0\,\text{s}$, distance between crests, that is, the wavelength $\lambda = 1.40\,\text{m}$
**Find:** the speed of the wave $v$
**Principle:** Every point of the rope vibrates with the frequency of the hand, so the frequency of the wave is the number of swings of the hand per second. The speed then follows from the fundamental equation, because during one period the wave travels one wavelength.
**Formula:** $f = \dfrac{N}{t}$ and $v = f\lambda$, so $v = \dfrac{N\lambda}{t}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: $\text{s}^{-1} = \text{Hz}$ and $\text{Hz} \cdot \text{m} = \text{m/s}$.
**Substitution:** $f = \dfrac{18}{12.0\,\text{s}} = 1.5\,\text{Hz}$ and $v = 1.5\,\text{s}^{-1} \cdot 1.40\,\text{m} = 2.10\,\text{m/s}$
**Answer:** $2.10\,\text{m/s}$, three significant figures like the time $12.0\,\text{s}$ and the wavelength $1.40\,\text{m}$; the number of swings $18$ is a counted number, so it does not limit the significant figures.
**Reasonableness:** The period is $T = \tfrac{12.0\,\text{s}}{18} = 0.667\,\text{s}$, and $\tfrac{1.40\,\text{m}}{0.667\,\text{s}} = 2.10\,\text{m/s}$. The unit also agrees: $\text{s}^{-1} \cdot \text{m} = \text{m/s}$, and a few metres per second is a typical speed for a skipping rope.

## fy5-10-L2-03
**Given:** in air $v_\text{a} = 343\,\text{m/s}$ and $\lambda_\text{a} = 0.750\,\text{m}$, in water $v_\text{w} = 1480\,\text{m/s}$
**Find:** the wavelength in water $\lambda_\text{w}$
**Principle:** When a wave passes from one medium to another, the frequency stays the same, because the water is set moving by the vibration of the air at the boundary in step with it. The speed changes with the medium, so the wavelength changes. In both media $v = f\lambda$ holds.
**Formula:** $f = \dfrac{v_\text{a}}{\lambda_\text{a}}$ and $\lambda_\text{w} = \dfrac{v_\text{w}}{f}$, so $\lambda_\text{w} = \dfrac{v_\text{w}\lambda_\text{a}}{v_\text{a}}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the speeds are in the same unit, so their ratio has no unit, and the wavelength is in metres.
**Substitution:** $f = \dfrac{343\,\text{m/s}}{0.750\,\text{m}} = 457.3\,\text{Hz}$ and $\lambda_\text{w} = \dfrac{1480\,\text{m/s}}{457.3\,\text{s}^{-1}} = 3.236\,\text{m}$
**Answer:** $3.24\,\text{m}$, three significant figures like the initial values $343\,\text{m/s}$, $0.750\,\text{m}$ and $1480\,\text{m/s}$.
**Reasonableness:** Sound travels in water about $\tfrac{1480}{343} \approx 4.3$ times faster than in air, so at the same frequency the wavelength is about $4.3$ times as long: $4.3 \cdot 0.750\,\text{m} \approx 3.2\,\text{m}$.

## fy5-10-L2-04
**Given:** frequency $f = 0.50\,\text{Hz}$, wavelength $\lambda = 12\,\text{km}$
**Find:** the speed of the wave $v$ in metres per second
**Principle:** The longitudinal wave of an earthquake is a mechanical wave in which the rock vibrates parallel to the direction of travel. The fundamental equation of wave motion holds for it as well, because during one period the wave travels one wavelength.
**Formula:** $v = f\lambda$
**Conversions:** $\lambda = 12\,\text{km} = 12 \cdot 1000\,\text{m} = 12\,000\,\text{m}$
**Substitution:** $v = 0.50\,\text{s}^{-1} \cdot 12\,000\,\text{m} = 6000\,\text{m/s}$
**Answer:** $6.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\text{m/s}$, that is, $6000\,\text{m/s}$, two significant figures like the frequency $0.50\,\text{Hz}$ and the wavelength $12\,\text{km}$.
**Reasonableness:** The period is $T = \tfrac{1}{0.50\,\text{Hz}} = 2.0\,\text{s}$, and $12\,\text{km}$ in two seconds is $6\,\text{km/s}$. Sound travels in solid rock much faster than in air ($343\,\text{m/s}$), so kilometres per second is a believable speed.

## fy5-10-L3-01
**Given:** a crest at $x_1 = 0.60\,\text{m}$ and $\Delta t = 0.15\,\text{s}$ later at $x_2 = 1.05\,\text{m}$; the crest has not travelled one wavelength; distance between crests $\lambda = 1.20\,\text{m}$
**Find:** the frequency of the shaking $f$
**Principle:** A crest is a part of the wave, so it travels at the speed of the wave, $v = \tfrac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$. Since the crest has travelled less than a wavelength, the photographs show the same crest and not already the next one. The displacement of the crest is not a displacement of the matter of the rope: the points of the rope only move up and down, and the crest is always at the point that is at its highest position at that moment. The frequency follows from the fundamental equation.
**Formula:** $v = \dfrac{x_2 - x_1}{\Delta t}$ and $v = f\lambda$, so $f = \dfrac{x_2 - x_1}{\lambda\,\Delta t}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: all lengths are in metres and the time in seconds.
**Substitution:** $v = \dfrac{1.05\,\text{m} - 0.60\,\text{m}}{0.15\,\text{s}} = \dfrac{0.45\,\text{m}}{0.15\,\text{s}} = 3.0\,\text{m/s}$ and $f = \dfrac{3.0\,\text{m/s}}{1.20\,\text{m}} = 2.5\,\text{Hz}$
**Answer:** $2.5\,\text{Hz}$, two significant figures, because the time $0.15\,\text{s}$ and the displacement $0.45\,\text{m}$ are known to two figures.
**Reasonableness:** The period is $T = \tfrac{1}{2.5\,\text{Hz}} = 0.40\,\text{s}$, so the interval between the photographs, $0.15\,\text{s}$, is less than a period, and the crest travelled $0.45\,\text{m}$, less than the wavelength $1.20\,\text{m}$, as assumed.

## fy5-10-L3-02
**Given:** time difference $\Delta t = 0.50\,\text{s}$, speed of sound in steel $v_\text{s} = 5100\,\text{m/s}$ and in air $v_\text{a} = 340\,\text{m/s}$
**Find:** the length of the rail $d$
**Principle:** The blow of the hammer produces sound both in the rail and in the air. The speed of a wave depends on the medium, so the two sounds travel the same distance $d$ at different speeds and arrive at different times: the sound that travelled in the steel first. Each travels at a steady speed, so the travel time is $\tfrac{d}{v}$, and the time difference is the difference of the travel times.
**Formula:** $\Delta t = \dfrac{d}{v_\text{a}} - \dfrac{d}{v_\text{s}}$, so $d = \dfrac{\Delta t}{\dfrac{1}{v_\text{a}} - \dfrac{1}{v_\text{s}}}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the speeds are in metres per second and the time in seconds.
**Substitution:** $d = \dfrac{0.50\,\text{s}}{\dfrac{1}{340\,\text{m/s}} - \dfrac{1}{5100\,\text{m/s}}} = \dfrac{0.50\,\text{s}}{0.002\,745\,\text{s/m}} = 182.1\,\text{m}$
**Answer:** $1.8 \cdot 10^{2}\,\text{m}$, that is, about $180\,\text{m}$, two significant figures, because the time difference $0.50\,\text{s}$ is known to two figures.
**Justification:** The same blow produces sound both in the rail and in the air, and sound travels much faster in steel than in air. Both sounds travel the same distance, but the one that travelled in the steel arrives first, so the listener hears two bangs.
**Reasonableness:** In air the distance takes $\tfrac{182\,\text{m}}{340\,\text{m/s}} = 0.536\,\text{s}$ and in steel $\tfrac{182\,\text{m}}{5100\,\text{m/s}} = 0.036\,\text{s}$; the difference is $0.50\,\text{s}$. The sound in the steel is so fast that the length is only slightly more than the sound in air would travel in half a second ($170\,\text{m}$).

## fy5-10-L3-03
**Given:** delay relative to the neighbour $\Delta t = 0.050\,\text{s}$, distance between seats $\Delta x = 0.55\,\text{m}$, length of the row $s = 440\,\text{m}$
**Find:** the time $t$ in which the wave goes once round the stand
**Principle:** The wave in a stadium stand is a model of wave motion: every spectator repeats the movement of the neighbour after a small delay, so the standing up, the disturbance, travels along the row although the spectators stay in their places and only stand up and sit down. The disturbance travels one seat spacing in the time $\Delta t$, so its speed is constant and the time of one round is the distance divided by the speed.
**Formula:** $v = \dfrac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$ and $s = vt$, so $t = \dfrac{s}{v} = \dfrac{s\,\Delta t}{\Delta x}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the lengths are in metres and the time in seconds.
**Substitution:** $v = \dfrac{0.55\,\text{m}}{0.050\,\text{s}} = 11\,\text{m/s}$ and $t = \dfrac{440\,\text{m}}{11\,\text{m/s}} = 40\,\text{s}$
**Answer:** $40\,\text{s}$, two significant figures like the delay $0.050\,\text{s}$ and the seat spacing $0.55\,\text{m}$.
**Reasonableness:** The row has $440\,\text{m} : 0.55\,\text{m} = 800$ spectators, and each adds a delay of $0.050\,\text{s}$: $800 \cdot 0.050\,\text{s} = 40\,\text{s}$. About $11\,\text{m/s}$ is close to the speed of the wave observed in stadiums, and an individual spectator moves only about a metre up and down.
