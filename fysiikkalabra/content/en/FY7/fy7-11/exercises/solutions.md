## fy7-11-L1-01
**Given:** angle of incidence $\alpha_1 = 50.0^\circ$, refractive index of water for red light $n = 1.332$, the light arrives from air
**Find:** angle of refraction $\alpha_2$ in degrees
**Principle:** Light is refracted at the boundary according to the law of refraction. The light arrives from air, whose refractive index is rounded to $1$, so $\sin\alpha_1 = n \sin\alpha_2$. For red light we use the refractive index of red light, because the refractive index depends on wavelength.
**Formula:** $\sin\alpha_1 = n \sin\alpha_2$, so $\sin\alpha_2 = \dfrac{\sin\alpha_1}{n}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the refractive index has no unit, and the angles are in degrees.
**Substitution:** $\sin\alpha_2 = \dfrac{\sin 50.0^\circ}{1.332} = 0.57511$, so $\alpha_2 = 35.11^\circ$
**Answer:** $35.1^\circ$, three significant figures like the angle of incidence $50.0^\circ$.
**Reasonableness:** The angle of refraction is smaller than the angle of incidence, because light slows down in water and bends towards the normal. The ratio $\sin 50.0^\circ : \sin 35.1^\circ = 0.766 : 0.575 \approx 1.33$ is the refractive index of water.

**Reasonableness:** Each travel time is about $25\,\mu\text{s}$, and the difference is about $1\,\%$ of it, as the ratio of the refractive indices $1.5302 : 1.5143 \approx 1.01$ leads us to expect. **Substitution:** $v = \dfrac{3.00 \cdot 10^5\,\text{km/s}}{1.516} = 197\,889\,\text{km/s}$ and $\lambda = \dfrac{600\,\text{nm}}{1.516} = 395.8\,\text{nm}$
**Answer:** $1.98 \cdot 10^5\,\text{km/s}$, three significant figures like the speed of light $3.00 \cdot 10^8\,\text{m/s}$. The wavelength in the glass is about $396\,\text{nm}$.
**Reasonableness:** The speed is about $66\,\%$ of the speed of light in vacuum, because $1 : 1.516 \approx 0.66$. It lies between the speeds of red light ($n = 1.5143$) and yellow light ($n = 1.5167$), close to them, as the speed of orange light should. A reading accuracy of $\pm 0.002$ changes the speed by only about $0.15\,\%$.

## fy7-11-L3-05
**Given:** $n_\text{r} = 1.6150$, $n_\text{v} = 1.6498$, angle of incidence $\alpha_1 = 50.0^\circ$, prism in air, apex angle $A = 64.0^\circ$ (part b)

**Principle:** Each colour is refracted at both surfaces with its own refractive index. From the geometry of the prism $\alpha_2 + \alpha_3 = A$ and $\delta = \alpha_1 + \alpha_4 - A$. The ray can leave only if $\sin\alpha_4 = n\sin\alpha_3 \le 1$; in the limiting case $\alpha_4 = 90^\circ$, so $\sin\alpha_3 = 1 : n$.
**Formula:** $\sin\alpha_2 = \dfrac{\sin\alpha_1}{n}$, $\alpha_3 = A - \alpha_2$, $\sin\alpha_4 = n\sin\alpha_3$ and $\delta = \alpha_1 + \alpha_4 - A$; in the limiting case $A_\text{max} = \alpha_2 + \alpha_{3,\text{max}}$, where $\sin\alpha_{3,\text{max}} = \dfrac{1}{n}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the angles are in degrees, and the refractive indices have no unit.
**Substitution:** a) Violet: $\sin\alpha_2 = \dfrac{\sin 50.0^\circ}{1.6498} = 0.46433$, $\alpha_2 = 27.667^\circ$, $\sin\alpha_{3,\text{max}} = \dfrac{1}{1.6498} = 0.60613$, $\alpha_{3,\text{max}} = 37.310^\circ$ and $A_\text{max} = 27.667^\circ + 37.310^\circ = 64.98^\circ$. ## fy7-11-L1-09
**Find:** which of the six statements about the dispersion of a prism and about the rainbow are true and which are false.
**Principle:** The refractive index depends on wavelength (dispersion): in glass the refractive index of violet is greater than that of red, so violet slows down more and is refracted more. The speed of light in a material is $v = c : n$. At a boundary the frequency of light, and so the colour, is preserved, but the speed and the wavelength change. In a rainbow each colour is seen at its own angle from the direction of the antisolar point, and if the sun is at altitude $h$, the top of the red edge is at altitude $42^\circ - h$.
**Model answer:** Statement 1 is false. The refractive index of glass is $1.530$ for violet and $1.514$ for red, so violet is refracted more at both surfaces of the prism and deviates the most. Red deviates the least, although its wavelength is the longest.

Statement 2 is true. The speeds of light in the glass are

$v_\text{v} = \dfrac{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}}{1.530}$

$v_\text{v} = 1.961 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$

$v_\text{r} = \dfrac{3.00 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}}{1.514}$

$v_\text{r} = 1.982 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$

So violet travels more slowly.

Statement 3 is false. The frequency is determined by the light source, and it does not change at the boundary. When the light slows down, its wavelength becomes shorter according to the equation $v = f\lambda$, but the colour, which is determined by the frequency, stays the same.

Statement 4 is true. The colours are already present in white light, and the prism only separates them. The separated red light is refracted in the second prism, but it does not split, because it contains no other colours.

Statement 5 is true. The droplets seen at the same angle from the direction of the antisolar point form a circle. The angle of violet, $40.6^\circ$, is smaller than that of red, $42^\circ$, so the circle of violet is smaller and violet is at the inner edge of the arc.

Statement 6 is false. When the sun is at an altitude of $45^\circ$, the antisolar point is $45^\circ$ below the horizon. The top of the red edge is at the altitude

$42^\circ - 45^\circ = -3^\circ$

that is, below the horizon, so the arc cannot be seen on level ground.

Answer: statements 2, 4 and 5 are true, and statements 1, 3 and 6 are false.
