## fy5-5-L1-01
**Given:** masses $m_1 = 70\,\text{kg}$ and $m_2 = 55\,\text{kg}$, distance between the centres of mass $r = 0.80\,\text{m}$, gravitational constant $\gamma = 6.674 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\tfrac{\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2}{\text{kg}^2}$
**Find:** the gravitational force $F$ between the students in micronewtons
**Principle:** All bodies attract each other according to Newton's law of gravitation. When the students are treated as point masses, the distance is measured between their centres of mass, and the same force acts on each of them.
**Formula:** $F = \gamma \dfrac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}$
**Conversions:** The masses and the distance are in SI units. Finally $1\,\mu\text{N} = 10^{-6}\,\text{N}$.
**Substitution:** $F = 6.674 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\tfrac{\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2}{\text{kg}^2} \cdot \dfrac{70\,\text{kg} \cdot 55\,\text{kg}}{(0.80\,\text{m})^2} = 4.015 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\text{N} = 0.4015\,\mu\text{N}$
**Answer:** $0.40\,\mu\text{N}$, two significant figures as in the masses and in the distance $0.80\,\text{m}$.
**Reasonableness:** The force corresponds to the weight of a particle of mass about $0.04\,\text{mg}$, so it is not noticed in everyday life. The order of magnitude is the same as in the example of the chapter, where the force between two people one metre apart was about $0.28\,\mu\text{N}$; here the distance is smaller, so the force is slightly greater.

## fy5-5-L1-02
**Given:** masses of the ships $m_1 = 5.0 \cdot 10^{7}\,\text{kg}$ and $m_2 = 3.0 \cdot 10^{7}\,\text{kg}$, distance between the centres of mass $r = 200\,\text{m}$, $\gamma = 6.674 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\tfrac{\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2}{\text{kg}^2}$
**Find:** the gravitational force $F$ between the ships in newtons
**Principle:** The ships attract each other according to Newton's law of gravitation. In the task the ships are treated as point masses. In reality the ships are as long as the distance between them, so the result is only an estimate of the order of magnitude.
**Formula:** $F = \gamma \dfrac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}$
**Conversions:** None needed: the masses are in kilograms and the distance in metres.
**Substitution:** $F = 6.674 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\tfrac{\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2}{\text{kg}^2} \cdot \dfrac{5.0 \cdot 10^{7}\,\text{kg} \cdot 3.0 \cdot 10^{7}\,\text{kg}}{(200\,\text{m})^2} = 2.503\,\text{N}$
**Answer:** $2.5\,\text{N}$, two significant figures as in the masses.
**Reasonableness:** With rounded numbers $7 \cdot 10^{-11} \cdot \tfrac{1.5 \cdot 10^{15}}{4 \cdot 10^{4}}\,\text{N} \approx 2.6\,\text{N}$. Even between enormous ships the pull is only of the order of the weight of a body of $250\,\text{g}$, and compared with the forces of ropes and water it is negligible.

## fy5-5-L1-03
**Given:** mass of Mercury $M = 3.30 \cdot 10^{23}\,\text{kg}$ and radius $R = 2.44 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{m}$, $\gamma = 6.674 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\tfrac{\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2}{\text{kg}^2}$
**Find:** the gravitational field strength $g$ on the surface of Mercury
**Principle:** The planet is approximately spherically symmetric, so it attracts as if its mass were at its centre. On the surface the distance from the centre is the radius of the planet.
**Formula:** $g = \gamma \dfrac{M}{R^2}$
**Conversions:** None needed: the mass is in kilograms and the radius in metres, so the result is in $\text{m/s}^2$.
**Substitution:** $g = 6.674 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\tfrac{\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2}{\text{kg}^2} \cdot \dfrac{3.30 \cdot 10^{23}\,\text{kg}}{(2.44 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{m})^2} = 3.699\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Answer:** $3.70\,\text{m/s}^2$, three significant figures as in the mass and the radius.
**Reasonableness:** Mercury is smaller and lighter than the Earth, so the field must be weaker than $9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$. The result is almost the same as on Mars ($3.71\,\text{m/s}^2$), which is larger than Mercury but also more massive.

## fy5-5-L1-04
**Given:** mass of the rover $m = 1025\,\text{kg}$, mass of Mars $M = 6.42 \cdot 10^{23}\,\text{kg}$ and radius $R = 3.40 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{m}$, $\gamma = 6.674 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\tfrac{\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2}{\text{kg}^2}$
**Find:** the gravitational force $F$ exerted by Mars on the rover in kilonewtons
**Principle:** Mars is approximately spherically symmetric, so the force between it and the rover is obtained from the law of gravitation when the distance is the radius of Mars.
**Formula:** $F = \gamma \dfrac{M m}{R^2}$
**Conversions:** The quantities are in SI units. Finally $1\,\text{kN} = 1000\,\text{N}$.
**Substitution:** $F = 6.674 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\tfrac{\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2}{\text{kg}^2} \cdot \dfrac{6.42 \cdot 10^{23}\,\text{kg} \cdot 1025\,\text{kg}}{(3.40 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{m})^2} = 3799\,\text{N} = 3.799\,\text{kN}$
**Answer:** $3.80\,\text{kN}$, three significant figures as in the mass and radius of Mars.
**Reasonableness:** Another route: the weight of the rover on Mars is $mg = 1025\,\text{kg} \cdot 3.71\,\text{m/s}^2 \approx 3800\,\text{N}$, which is only about two fifths of its weight on the Earth ($10.1\,\text{kN}$).

## fy5-5-L1-05
**Given:** original force $F = 36\,\mu\text{N}$; the distance grows from $r$ to $3r$, the masses stay the same
**Find:** the new gravitational force $F'$ in micronewtons
**Principle:** By Newton's law of gravitation the force is inversely proportional to the square of the distance. When the masses do not change, the ratio of the forces depends only on the ratio of the distances.
**Formula:** $\dfrac{F'}{F} = \dfrac{r^2}{(3r)^2} = \dfrac{1}{9}$, so $F' = \dfrac{F}{9}$
**Conversions:** None needed: the force stays in micronewtons.
**Substitution:** $F' = \dfrac{36\,\mu\text{N}}{9} = 4.0\,\mu\text{N}$
**Answer:** $4.0\,\mu\text{N}$, two significant figures as in the force $36\,\mu\text{N}$.
**Reasonableness:** The force decreases as the spacecraft move apart, to less than a third ($12\,\mu\text{N}$), because the distance is raised to the second power in the formula.

## fy5-5-L2-01
**Given:** distance between the centres of the spheres $r = 0.40\,\text{m}$, force $F = 0.60\,\mu\text{N}$, equal masses $m_1 = m_2 = m$, $\gamma = 6.674 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\tfrac{\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2}{\text{kg}^2}$
**Find:** the mass $m$ of each sphere
**Principle:** The lead spheres are uniform spheres, so the law of gravitation holds when $r$ is the distance between the centres. Because the masses are equal, the law contains the product $m \cdot m = m^2$, from which the mass is solved.
**Formula:** $F = \gamma \dfrac{m^2}{r^2}$, so $m = r\sqrt{\dfrac{F}{\gamma}}$
**Conversions:** $F = 0.60\,\mu\text{N} = 0.60 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{N}$
**Substitution:** $m = 0.40\,\text{m} \cdot \sqrt{\dfrac{0.60 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{N}}{6.674 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\tfrac{\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2}{\text{kg}^2}}} = 0.40\,\text{m} \cdot 94.82\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}} = 37.93\,\text{kg}$
**Answer:** $38\,\text{kg}$, two significant figures as in the force and the distance.
**Reasonableness:** Check by substituting back: $6.674 \cdot 10^{-11} \cdot \tfrac{38^2}{0.40^2}\,\text{N} \approx 6.0 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\text{N}$. The density of lead is about $11\,300\,\text{kg/m}^3$, so the radius of a sphere of $38\,\text{kg}$ is about $9\,\text{cm}$: the spheres fit easily at a distance of $0.40\,\text{m}$ from each other.

## fy5-5-L2-02
**Given:** on the surface of Venus $g = 8.87\,\text{m/s}^2$, radius $R = 6052\,\text{km}$, mass of the Earth $M_\text{E} = 5.97 \cdot 10^{24}\,\text{kg}$, $\gamma = 6.674 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\tfrac{\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2}{\text{kg}^2}$
**Find:** the ratio of the mass of Venus to the mass of the Earth, $\tfrac{M}{M_\text{E}}$, without a unit
**Principle:** The gravitational field strength on the surface of a spherically symmetric planet is $g = \gamma \tfrac{M}{R^2}$, so the mass of the planet can be solved from the measured $g$ and the radius. The mass is then compared with the mass of the Earth by dividing.
**Formula:** $g = \gamma \dfrac{M}{R^2}$, so $M = \dfrac{g R^2}{\gamma}$ and the ratio is $\dfrac{M}{M_\text{E}} = \dfrac{g R^2}{\gamma M_\text{E}}$
**Conversions:** $R = 6052\,\text{km} = 6052 \cdot 10^{3}\,\text{m} = 6.052 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{m}$
**Substitution:** $R^2 = (6.052 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{m})^2 = 3.6627 \cdot 10^{13}\,\text{m}^2$, $g R^2 = 8.87\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 3.6627 \cdot 10^{13}\,\text{m}^2 = 3.2488 \cdot 10^{14}\,\text{m}^3/\text{s}^2$ and $M = \dfrac{g R^2}{\gamma} = \dfrac{3.2488 \cdot 10^{14}\,\text{m}^3/\text{s}^2}{6.674 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\tfrac{\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2}{\text{kg}^2}} = 4.868 \cdot 10^{24}\,\text{kg}$ and $\dfrac{M}{M_\text{E}} = \dfrac{4.868 \cdot 10^{24}\,\text{kg}}{5.97 \cdot 10^{24}\,\text{kg}} = 0.8154$
**Answer:** $0.815$, three significant figures as in the field strength $8.87\,\text{m/s}^2$.
**Reasonableness:** Venus is almost the size of the Earth and its field is a little weaker than the Earth's, so its mass must be a little smaller than the Earth's. According to NASA's table the mass of Venus is $4.87 \cdot 10^{24}\,\text{kg}$, which agrees with the result.

## fy5-5-L2-03
**Given:** mass of the probe $m = 1200\,\text{kg}$, force $F = 1.00\,\text{kN}$, mass of the Earth $M = 5.97 \cdot 10^{24}\,\text{kg}$ and radius $R = 6378\,\text{km}$, $\gamma = 6.674 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\tfrac{\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2}{\text{kg}^2}$
**Find:** the height $h$ above the Earth's surface in kilometres
**Principle:** The Earth attracts the probe as if its mass were at its centre, so the law of gravitation first gives the distance $r$ from the Earth's centre. The height above the surface is this distance minus the radius of the Earth.
**Formula:** $F = \gamma \dfrac{M m}{r^2}$, so $r = \sqrt{\dfrac{\gamma M m}{F}}$ and $h = r - R = \sqrt{\dfrac{\gamma M m}{F}} - R$
**Conversions:** $F = 1.00\,\text{kN} = 1000\,\text{N}$; $R = 6378\,\text{km} = 6.378 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{m}$; finally $1\,\text{km} = 1000\,\text{m}$
**Substitution:** The numerator is $\gamma M m = 6.674 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\tfrac{\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2}{\text{kg}^2} \cdot 5.97 \cdot 10^{24}\,\text{kg} \cdot 1200\,\text{kg} = 4.7812 \cdot 10^{17}\,\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2$, so $r = \sqrt{\dfrac{4.7812 \cdot 10^{17}\,\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2}{1000\,\text{N}}} = 2.1866 \cdot 10^{7}\,\text{m}$ and $h = 2.1866 \cdot 10^{7}\,\text{m} - 6.378 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{m} = 1.5488 \cdot 10^{7}\,\text{m} = 15\,488\,\text{km}$
**Answer:** $15\,500\,\text{km}$, three significant figures as in the force $1.00\,\text{kN}$.
**Reasonableness:** On the Earth's surface the weight of the probe is $1200\,\text{kg} \cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \approx 11.8\,\text{kN}$. The force $1.00\,\text{kN}$ is about $\tfrac{1}{11.8}$ of that, so the distance from the centre must be $\sqrt{11.8} \approx 3.4$ Earth radii: $3.4 \cdot 6378\,\text{km} \approx 21\,900\,\text{km}$, which agrees with the value of $r$.

## fy5-5-L2-04
**Given:** flight altitude $h = 11.0\,\text{km}$, radius of the Earth $R = 6378\,\text{km}$
**Find:** by what percentage the field strength $g_h$ is smaller than the surface value $g_0$
**Principle:** The Earth's gravitational field strength is inversely proportional to the square of the distance from the Earth's centre. When the ratio $\tfrac{g_h}{g_0}$ is calculated, $\gamma$ and the mass of the Earth cancel, so they are not needed.
**Formula:** $\dfrac{g_h}{g_0} = \dfrac{\gamma \tfrac{M}{(R + h)^2}}{\gamma \tfrac{M}{R^2}} = \left(\dfrac{R}{R + h}\right)^2$, so the relative change is $1 - \left(\dfrac{R}{R + h}\right)^2$
**Conversions:** None needed: the radius and the altitude are both in kilometres, and the unit cancels. Finally the ratio is converted to a percentage by multiplying by $100\,\%$.
**Substitution:** $1 - \left(\dfrac{6378\,\text{km}}{6378\,\text{km} + 11.0\,\text{km}}\right)^2 = 1 - 0.996560 = 0.003440 = 0.3440\,\%$
**Answer:** $0.344\,\%$, three significant figures as in the altitude $11.0\,\text{km}$.
**Reasonableness:** At small heights the change is about $\tfrac{2h}{R} = \tfrac{22.0}{6378} \approx 0.345\,\%$. At cruising altitude the field strength is still about $9.78\,\text{m/s}^2$, so gravity does not disappear even at flight altitude.

## fy5-5-L3-01
**Given:** mass of the Earth $M = 5.97 \cdot 10^{24}\,\text{kg}$, mass of the Moon $m_\text{M} = 7.35 \cdot 10^{22}\,\text{kg}$, distance between the centres $d = 3.84 \cdot 10^{5}\,\text{km}$
**Find:** the distance $x$ from the Earth's centre at which the forces exerted on the probe by the Earth and the Moon are equal
**Principle:** When the probe is between the Earth and the Moon, its distance from the Earth's centre is $x$ and from the Moon's centre $d - x$. Each force is obtained from the law of gravitation, and they are set equal; the mass of the probe and $\gamma$ cancel.
**Formula:** $\gamma \dfrac{M m}{x^2} = \gamma \dfrac{m_\text{M} m}{(d - x)^2}$, so $\dfrac{d - x}{x} = \sqrt{\dfrac{m_\text{M}}{M}}$ and $x = \dfrac{d}{1 + \sqrt{\tfrac{m_\text{M}}{M}}}$
**Conversions:** None needed: the distance can be kept in kilometres, because the answer is asked in kilometres, and in the ratio of the masses the unit cancels.
**Substitution:** $\sqrt{\dfrac{7.35 \cdot 10^{22}\,\text{kg}}{5.97 \cdot 10^{24}\,\text{kg}}} = 0.11096$ and $x = \dfrac{3.84 \cdot 10^{5}\,\text{km}}{1.11096} = 3.456 \cdot 10^{5}\,\text{km}$
**Answer:** $346\,000\,\text{km}$, that is, $3.46 \cdot 10^{5}\,\text{km}$, three significant figures as in the masses and the distance.
**Reasonableness:** The point is about $90\,\%$ of the way to the Moon, which makes sense because the mass of the Earth is about $81$ times the mass of the Moon: the forces are equal when the distance to the Earth is $\sqrt{81} = 9$ times the distance to the Moon. Here $\tfrac{345\,600}{38\,400} \approx 9.0$.

## fy5-5-L3-02
**Given:** radius of the planet $R_\text{p} = 2R$, the same mean density $\rho$ as the Earth, on the Earth's surface $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the gravitational field strength $g_\text{p}$ on the surface of the planet
**Principle:** The field on the surface of a spherically symmetric planet is $g = \gamma \tfrac{M}{R^2}$. When the density is the same, the mass is the density times the volume of the sphere, $M = \rho \cdot \tfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3$, so the mass grows with the cube of the radius while the denominator grows only with the square. Then $g$ is directly proportional to the radius.
**Formula:** $g = \gamma \dfrac{\rho \cdot \tfrac{4}{3}\pi R^3}{R^2} = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi\gamma\rho R$, so $\dfrac{g_\text{p}}{g} = \dfrac{R_\text{p}}{R}$ and $g_\text{p} = \dfrac{R_\text{p}}{R}\, g$
**Conversions:** None needed: the ratio of the radii has no unit.
**Substitution:** $g_\text{p} = \dfrac{2R}{R} \cdot 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 = 19.62\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Answer:** $19.6\,\text{m/s}^2$, three significant figures as in the value for the Earth, $9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$.
**Reasonableness:** By another route: twice the radius with the same density gives a mass $2^3 = 8$ times as great, and the square of the distance grows by a factor $2^2 = 4$, so the field is $\tfrac{8}{4} = 2$ times as strong. The result lies between the field of Jupiter ($24.8\,\text{m/s}^2$) and the field of the Earth.

## fy5-5-L3-03
**Given:** mass of the Sun $M_\text{S} = 1.99 \cdot 10^{30}\,\text{kg}$ and distance $r_\text{S} = 1.50 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{km}$; mass of the Moon $M_\text{M} = 7.35 \cdot 10^{22}\,\text{kg}$ and distance $r_\text{M} = 3.84 \cdot 10^{5}\,\text{km}$
**Find:** the ratio $\tfrac{F_\text{S}}{F_\text{M}}$ of the forces exerted on the Earth by the Sun and the Moon
**Principle:** Each force is obtained from Newton's law of gravitation when the distances are measured between the centres. In the ratio $\gamma$ and the mass of the Earth cancel, so only the masses and distances are needed.
**Formula:** $\dfrac{F_\text{S}}{F_\text{M}} = \dfrac{\gamma \tfrac{M_\text{S} m}{r_\text{S}^2}}{\gamma \tfrac{M_\text{M} m}{r_\text{M}^2}} = \dfrac{M_\text{S}}{M_\text{M}} \cdot \left(\dfrac{r_\text{M}}{r_\text{S}}\right)^2$
**Conversions:** None needed: the distances are both in kilometres, and the units cancel.
**Substitution:** $\dfrac{F_\text{S}}{F_\text{M}} = \dfrac{1.99 \cdot 10^{30}\,\text{kg}}{7.35 \cdot 10^{22}\,\text{kg}} \cdot \left(\dfrac{3.84 \cdot 10^{5}\,\text{km}}{1.50 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{km}}\right)^2 = 2.707 \cdot 10^{7} \cdot 6.554 \cdot 10^{-6} = 177.4$
**Answer:** The Sun attracts the Earth about $177$ times as strongly as the Moon does; three significant figures as in the data.
**Reasonableness:** The mass of the Sun is about $27$ million times as great, but it is about $390$ times farther away, which reduces the force by a factor $390^2 \approx 150\,000$: $\tfrac{2.7 \cdot 10^{7}}{1.5 \cdot 10^{5}} \approx 180$. The Sun holds the Earth in its orbit, although the Moon is much closer.
