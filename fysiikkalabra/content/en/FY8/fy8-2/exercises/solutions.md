## fy8-2-L1-01
**Given:** wavelength of the helium line $\lambda = 502\,\text{nm}$, $h = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s}$, $c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$, $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$
**Find:** the energy difference $\Delta E = E_\text{u} - E_\text{l}$ in electronvolts
**Principle:** An atom emits a photon when it moves from a higher energy state to a lower one. Energy is conserved, so the energy $hf$ of the photon is the difference between the energies of the states, and the frequency is obtained from the wavelength with the equation $c = f\lambda$.
**Formula:** $\Delta E = hf$ and $f = \tfrac{c}{\lambda}$, so $\Delta E = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$
**Conversions:** $502\,\text{nm} = 502 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}$. The result in joules is converted to electronvolts by dividing by $1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{eV}}$.
**Substitution:** $hc = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s} \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s} = 1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}$ and $\Delta E = \dfrac{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}}{502 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}} = 3.9571 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J} = \dfrac{3.9571 \cdot 10^{-19}}{1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}}\,\text{eV} = 2.4701\,\text{eV}$
**Answer:** $2.47\,\text{eV}$, three significant figures like the wavelength $502\,\text{nm}$.
**Reasonableness:** With the estimate $hc \approx 1240\,\text{eV}\,\text{nm}$ we get $\tfrac{1240}{502}\,\text{eV} \approx 2.5\,\text{eV}$. The energy of a photon of green light is between those of red ($1.9\,\text{eV}$) and violet ($3.0\,\text{eV}$), as it should be.

## fy8-2-L1-02

**Find:** the wavelength $\lambda$ of the emitted photon in nanometres
**Principle:** The energy of a photon produced in a transition from an upper state to a lower one is the difference between the states, because energy is conserved. The energy and wavelength of a photon are connected by $E = \tfrac{hc}{\lambda}$.
**Formula:** $\Delta E = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$, so $\lambda = \dfrac{hc}{\Delta E}$
**Conversions:** $\Delta E = 2.86\,\text{eV} = 2.86 \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J} = 4.5817 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$. Finally $1\,\text{m} = 10^{9}\,\text{nm}$.
**Substitution:** $hc = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s} \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s} = 1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}$ and $\lambda = \dfrac{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}}{4.5817 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}} = 4.3357 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\text{m} = 433.57\,\text{nm}$
**Answer:** $434\,\text{nm}$, three significant figures like the energy difference $2.86\,\text{eV}$.
**Reasonableness:** With the estimate, $\tfrac{1240\,\text{eV}\,\text{nm}}{2.86\,\text{eV}} \approx 430\,\text{nm}$. The wavelength is at the violet end of visible light, and it is the same as the blue-violet line of hydrogen.

**Find:** the frequency $f$ of the light in the unit $10^{14}\,\text{Hz}$
**Principle:** Light is electromagnetic wave motion that travels at the speed $c$, so the wavelength and the frequency are connected by the basic equation of wave motion.
**Formula:** $c = f\lambda$, so $f = \dfrac{c}{\lambda}$

**Substitution:** $f = \dfrac{2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}}{656 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}} = 4.5701 \cdot 10^{14}\,\text{Hz}$
**Answer:** $4.57 \cdot 10^{14}\,\text{Hz}$, three significant figures like the wavelength $656\,\text{nm}$.
**Reasonableness:** The frequencies of visible light are about $4 \cdot 10^{14}$–$8 \cdot 10^{14}\,\text{Hz}$, and red light has the smallest frequency, so $4.57 \cdot 10^{14}\,\text{Hz}$ fits the red line.

## fy8-2-L1-04
**Given:** the energies of the states of the hydrogen atom $E_3 = -1.51\,\text{eV}$ and $E_2 = -3.40\,\text{eV}$
**Find:** the energy $E_\text{ph}$ of the photon emitted in the transition $3 \to 2$ in electronvolts
**Principle:** When an atom moves from a higher state to a lower one, its energy decreases. Energy is conserved, so the decrease in energy leaves as a photon, and the energy of the photon is the difference between the states.
**Formula:** $E_\text{ph} = E_\text{u} - E_\text{l} = E_3 - E_2$
**Conversions:** None are needed: both energies are in electronvolts, and the answer is asked in electronvolts.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{ph} = -1.51\,\text{eV} - (-3.40\,\text{eV}) = 1.89\,\text{eV}$
**Answer:** $1.89\,\text{eV}$, to the nearest hundredth like the energies of the states, that is, three significant figures.
**Reasonableness:** The energy is positive, because the energy of the upper state, $-1.51\,\text{eV}$, is greater than that of the lower one. $1.89\,\text{eV}$ is in the range of visible light, $1.7$–$3.3\,\text{eV}$; it is the photon of the red line $656\,\text{nm}$ of hydrogen.

**Substitution:** $hc = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s} \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s} = 1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}$ and $\lambda = \dfrac{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}}{4.0851 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}} = 4.8627 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\text{m} = 486.27\,\text{nm}$
**Answer:** $486\,\text{nm}$, three significant figures like the energy difference $2.55\,\text{eV}$.
**Reasonableness:** With the estimate $hc \approx 1240\,\text{eV}\,\text{nm}$ we get $\tfrac{1240\,\text{eV}\,\text{nm}}{2.55\,\text{eV}} \approx 490\,\text{nm}$, and the line looks blue-green, that is, cyan. The transition from level $4$ is larger than from level $3$ (red, $656\,\text{nm}$), so the wavelength is shorter.

## fy8-2-L2-02
**Given:** the energy states of the model atom $E_1 = 0$, $E_2 = 1.85\,\text{eV}$ and $E_3 = 4.00\,\text{eV}$, $h = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s}$, $c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$, $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$
**Find:** the wavelength $\lambda_\text{max}$ of the longest line in nanometres
**Principle:** Every pair of states gives one line, whose photon energy is the difference between the states. The wavelength is inversely proportional to the energy, so the longest wavelength comes from the smallest difference. The differences are $E_2 - E_1 = 1.85\,\text{eV}$, $E_3 - E_2 = 2.15\,\text{eV}$ and $E_3 - E_1 = 4.00\,\text{eV}$, so the smallest is $1.85\,\text{eV}$.
**Formula:** $\Delta E_\text{min} = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda_\text{max}}$, so $\lambda_\text{max} = \dfrac{hc}{\Delta E_\text{min}}$
**Conversions:** $\Delta E_\text{min} = 1.85\,\text{eV} = 1.85 \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J} = 2.9637 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$. Finally $1\,\text{m} = 10^{9}\,\text{nm}$.
**Substitution:** $hc = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s} \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s} = 1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}$ and $\lambda_\text{max} = \dfrac{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}}{2.9637 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}} = 6.7027 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\text{m} = 670.27\,\text{nm}$
**Answer:** $670\,\text{nm}$, three significant figures like the energy $1.85\,\text{eV}$.
**Reasonableness:** The other lines are $\tfrac{1240}{2.15}\,\text{nm} \approx 577\,\text{nm}$ and $\tfrac{1240}{4.00}\,\text{nm} \approx 310\,\text{nm}$, both shorter. The longest line is red, as is right for the photon with the smallest energy.

## fy8-2-L2-03
**Given:** the energy of the ground state of the hydrogen atom $E_1 = -13.6\,\text{eV}$ and of the next state $E_2 = -3.40\,\text{eV}$, $h = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s}$, $c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$, $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$
**Find:** the wavelength $\lambda$ of the absorbed light in nanometres
**Principle:** An atom absorbs a photon only if the energy of the photon is exactly the difference between two states. A transition from the ground state to state $2$ therefore requires a photon whose energy is $E_2 - E_1$.
**Formula:** $E_2 - E_1 = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$, so $\lambda = \dfrac{hc}{E_2 - E_1}$
**Conversions:** $E_2 - E_1 = -3.40\,\text{eV} - (-13.6\,\text{eV}) = 10.2\,\text{eV} = 10.2 \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J} = 1.6340 \cdot 10^{-18}\,\text{J}$. Finally $1\,\text{m} = 10^{9}\,\text{nm}$.
**Substitution:** $hc = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s} \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s} = 1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}$ and $\lambda = \dfrac{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}}{1.6340 \cdot 10^{-18}\,\text{J}} = 1.2157 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\text{m} = 121.57\,\text{nm}$
**Answer:** $122\,\text{nm}$, three significant figures like the energy of the ground state $-13.6\,\text{eV}$.
**Reasonableness:** $10.2\,\text{eV}$ is more than three times the energy of a photon of visible light, so the wavelength is less than a third of the wavelengths of visible light: ultraviolet. This is why cold hydrogen gas is transparent to visible light.

## fy8-2-L3-01

**Find:** the shortest wavelength $\lambda_\text{min}$ of the series in nanometres
**Principle:** The energy of the photon of a line is the difference $E_\text{u} - E_2$ between the states. The higher the level, the greater the energy, but the upper energy cannot exceed $0$, where the electron is freed from the atom. The greatest possible difference is therefore $0 - E_2$, and it gives the shortest wavelength, towards which the lines crowd together.
**Formula:** $0 - E_2 = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda_\text{min}}$, so $\lambda_\text{min} = \dfrac{hc}{0 - E_2}$
**Conversions:** $0 - E_2 = 3.40\,\text{eV} = 3.40 \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J} = 5.4468 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$. Finally $1\,\text{m} = 10^{9}\,\text{nm}$.
**Substitution:** $hc = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s} \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s} = 1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}$ and $\lambda_\text{min} = \dfrac{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}}{5.4468 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}} = 3.6470 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\text{m} = 364.70\,\text{nm}$
**Answer:** $365\,\text{nm}$, three significant figures like the energy $-3.40\,\text{eV}$.
**Reasonableness:** The limit is shorter than the shortest visible line of the series, $410\,\text{nm}$, as it should be, and just below the visible range ($380\,\text{nm}$): the last lines of the series are ultraviolet.

## fy8-2-L3-02
**Given:** wavelength of the radiation absorbed by carbon dioxide $\lambda = 15.0\,\mu\text{m}$, $h = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s}$, $c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$, $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$
**Find:** the energy difference $\Delta E$ of the vibrational states in millielectronvolts
**Principle:** The energy of the vibration of a molecule is quantised like the energy of the electron shell of an atom. A molecule absorbs a photon only if the energy of the photon is exactly the difference between two states, so the difference is obtained from the wavelength of the absorbed radiation.
**Formula:** $\Delta E = hf = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$
**Conversions:** $15.0\,\mu\text{m} = 15.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m}$. Joules are converted to electronvolts by dividing by $1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{eV}}$ and electronvolts to millielectronvolts by substituting $1\,\text{eV} = 1000\,\text{meV}$.
**Substitution:** $hc = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s} \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s} = 1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}$ and $\Delta E = \dfrac{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}}{15.0 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m}} = 1.3243 \cdot 10^{-20}\,\text{J} = \dfrac{1.3243 \cdot 10^{-20}}{1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}}\,\text{eV} = 0.082666\,\text{eV} = 82.666\,\text{meV}$
**Answer:** $82.7\,\text{meV}$, three significant figures like the wavelength $15.0\,\mu\text{m}$.
**Reasonableness:** The wavelength is $30$ times the $500\,\text{nm}$ of visible light, so the energy is about one thirtieth of the energy $2.5\,\text{eV}$ of its photon: $2.5\,\text{eV} : 30 \approx 0.083\,\text{eV}$. An energy difference this small corresponds to infrared radiation, not to visible light: the molecule cannot absorb visible light in this transition, but it does absorb the infrared radiation of the ground.

## fy8-2-L3-03
**Given:** wavelength of the absorbed UV photon $\lambda_\text{UV} = 365\,\text{nm}$, energy of the first emitted photon $E_1 = 1.20\,\text{eV}$, $h = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s}$, $c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$, $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$
**Find:** the wavelength $\lambda_2$ of the second emitted photon in nanometres
**Principle:** The molecule returns to the same ground state and no energy is transferred to heat, so by conservation of energy the sum of the energies of the two emitted photons is the energy of the absorbed photon.
**Formula:** $E_\text{UV} = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda_\text{UV}} = E_1 + \dfrac{hc}{\lambda_2}$, so $\lambda_2 = \dfrac{hc}{\tfrac{hc}{\lambda_\text{UV}} - E_1}$
**Conversions:** $365\,\text{nm} = 365 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}$; electronvolts and joules are converted by substituting $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$.
**Substitution:** $hc = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s} \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s} = 1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}$ and $E_\text{UV} = \dfrac{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}}{365 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}} = 5.4424 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J} = 3.3973\,\text{eV}$, $E_2 = 3.3973\,\text{eV} - 1.20\,\text{eV} = 2.1973\,\text{eV} = 3.5200 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$ and $\lambda_2 = \dfrac{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}}{3.5200 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}} = 5.6434 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\text{m} = 564.34\,\text{nm}$
**Answer:** $564\,\text{nm}$, three significant figures like the wavelength $365\,\text{nm}$ and the energy $1.20\,\text{eV}$.
**Reasonableness:** The energy $2.2\,\text{eV}$ of the second photon is smaller than that of the UV photon, so the wavelength is longer: yellow-green light. Estimate: $\tfrac{1240}{1.20}\,\text{nm} \approx 1030\,\text{nm}$ is infrared, as the task says, and $1.2\,\text{eV} + \tfrac{1240}{564}\,\text{eV} \approx 3.4\,\text{eV}$ is the energy of the absorbed photon.
