## fy8-8-L1-01
**Given:** the nucleus of uranium-235 ${}^{235}_{92}\text{U}$: mass number $A = 235$, atomic number $Z = 92$
**Find:** the number of neutrons $N$
**Principle:** The mass number is the number of all nucleons of the nucleus and the atomic number is the number of protons. The remaining nucleons are neutrons.
**Formula:** $A = Z + N$, from which $N = A - Z$
**Conversions:** None are needed, because the numbers are counts.
**Substitution:** $N = 235 - 92 = 143$
**Answer:** $143$. The result is the exact number of neutrons, because nucleons are counted as pieces.
**Reasonableness:** There are more neutrons than protons, as in heavy nuclei: $143 : 92 \approx 1.6$. Uranium-238 has three more neutrons, $146$, because isotopes have the same $Z$ but a different $N$.

## fy8-8-L1-02
**Given:** the parent nucleus ${}^{222}_{86}\text{Rn}$, alpha decay; the alpha particle is ${}^{4}_{2}\text{He}$
**Find:** the mass number $A'$ of the daughter nucleus
**Principle:** In a decay equation the mass number is conserved: the nucleons of the parent nucleus are shared between the daughter nucleus and the alpha particle, which has four nucleons.
**Formula:** $A = A' + 4$, from which $A' = A - 4$
**Conversions:** None are needed, because the mass numbers are counts.
**Substitution:** $A' = 222 - 4 = 218$
**Answer:** $218$. The result is an exact integer. The daughter nucleus is polonium-218 ${}^{218}_{84}\text{Po}$, because at the same time $Z$ decreases by two.
**Reasonableness:** The equation ${}^{222}_{86}\text{Rn} \to {}^{218}_{84}\text{Po} + {}^{4}_{2}\text{He}$ balances: $222 = 218 + 4$ and $86 = 84 + 2$. The same step can be seen in the series of uranium-238, from radon to polonium.

## fy8-8-L1-03
**Given:** the parent nucleus ${}^{14}_{6}\text{C}$, beta minus decay; the electron is ${}^{0}_{-1}\text{e}$
**Find:** the atomic number $Z'$ of the daughter nucleus
**Principle:** In beta minus decay a neutron changes into a proton and the electron that is created leaves. The charge number is conserved when the charge number $-1$ of the electron is counted; the antineutrino is neutral.
**Formula:** $Z = Z' + (-1)$, from which $Z' = Z + 1$
**Conversions:** None are needed, because the atomic numbers are counts.
**Substitution:** $Z' = 6 + 1 = 7$
**Answer:** $7$. The result is an exact integer. The atomic number $7$ is nitrogen, so the daughter nucleus is ${}^{14}_{7}\text{N}$.
**Reasonableness:** The mass number stays at $14$ and the charge number balances: $6 = 7 - 1$. Carbon-14 has eight neutrons and six protons, that is, more neutrons than suits a light stable nucleus, so beta minus decay moves it towards the band of stable nuclei.

**Conversions:** $E_\gamma = 59.54\,\text{keV} = 59.54 \cdot 1000 \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J} = 9.5383 \cdot 10^{-15}\,\text{J}$; finally $1\,\text{m} = 10^{12}\,\text{pm}$.

**Answer:** $20.8\,\text{pm}$, three significant figures; the energy is given with four figures, and $20.83\,\text{pm}$ is just as correct.
**Reasonableness:** The caesium photon of the example, $661.7\,\text{keV}$, has about $11$ times more energy, and its wavelength of $1.874\,\text{pm}$ is about $11$ times shorter: $20.8 : 1.874 \approx 11$. The wavelength is smaller than the diameter of an atom, about $100\,\text{pm}$.

## fy8-8-L1-05

**Find:** the neutron number $N'$ of the daughter nucleus
**Principle:** In beta plus decay a proton changes into a neutron, and a positron and a neutrino leave the nucleus. The mass number is conserved, and the charge number is conserved when the charge number $+1$ of the positron is counted.
**Formula:** $A' = A$ and $Z = Z' + 1$, so $Z' = Z - 1$ and $N' = A' - Z' = A - (Z - 1)$

## fy8-8-L1-06
**Find:** which decay increases the atomic number $Z$ by one and leaves the mass number $A$ unchanged
**Principle:** In a decay equation the mass number and the charge number are conserved. The atomic number increases by one only when a neutron of the nucleus changes into a proton, and the number of nucleons stays the same. Let us go through the decay modes: alpha decay changes $Z$ and $A$ ($Z - 2$, $A - 4$), beta plus decay decreases $Z$, and gamma decay changes neither.
**Model answer:** The correct option is beta minus decay, in which a neutron of the nucleus changes into a proton and an electron leaves. Then $Z' = Z + 1$ and $A' = A$, and the charge number is conserved when the charge number $-1$ of the electron is counted: for example ${}^{14}_{6}\text{C} \to {}^{14}_{7}\text{N} + {}^{0}_{-1}\text{e} + \bar{\nu}$ balances, because $14 = 14 + 0$ and $6 = 7 - 1$. In gamma decay only energy leaves the nucleus, so neither number changes.

## fy8-8-L1-08

**Find:** the number of protons $Z$ in the nucleus after the gamma photon has left, and a justification of why the gamma photon does not change the number of nucleons
**Principle:** In beta minus decay a neutron changes into a proton, so $Z$ increases by one and $A$ stays the same. In gamma decay the nucleus moves from a higher energy level to a lower one and emits a photon, which carries away only energy.
**Formula:** beta minus: $Z' = Z + 1$ and $A' = A$; gamma decay: ${}^{A}_{Z}\text{X}^{\ast} \to {}^{A}_{Z}\text{X} + \gamma$, so $Z'' = Z'$
**Conversions:** None are needed, because the numbers are counts.
**Substitution:** $Z' = 27 + 1 = 28$ and after the gamma decay $Z'' = 28$; the number of neutrons is $N'' = 60 - 28 = 32$
**Answer:** $28$. The result is the exact number of protons. The nucleus is nickel-60 ${}^{60}_{28}\text{Ni}$. Justification: the gamma photon has no mass number and no charge, so $A$ and $Z$ are the same on both sides of the equation. The nucleus only moves to a lower energy level, and no proton or neutron leaves.
**Reasonableness:** The decay equations balance: ${}^{60}_{27}\text{Co} \to {}^{60}_{28}\text{Ni}^{\ast} + {}^{0}_{-1}\text{e} + \bar{\nu}$ ($60 = 60 + 0$ and $27 = 28 - 1$) and ${}^{60}_{28}\text{Ni}^{\ast} \to {}^{60}_{28}\text{Ni} + \gamma$. The element changes only in the beta decay, where a cobalt atom becomes nickel, but the emission of a gamma photon does not change it.

## fy8-8-L3-05
**Given:** the measured lines $351.2$, $468.9$, $681.3$, $820.5$ and $1501.4\,\text{keV}$; three excited levels; the nucleus is at first on the top level; the lowest excited level $351.2\,\text{keV}$; $h = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s}$, $c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$, $1\,\text{keV} = 1000\,\text{eV}$, $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$
**Find:** the energy $E_\text{m}$ and the wavelength $\lambda$ in picometres of the missing gamma line
**Principle:** The energy of a gamma photon is the difference between two levels. With three excited levels and the ground state there are six possible transitions, of which five appear in the table. The greatest energy is the transition from the top level to the ground state, and every line has to be a difference between two levels. With the help of the lowest level $351.2\,\text{keV}$ the remaining levels can be solved uniquely. The missing transition is the one that is not in the table.
**Formula:** $E_\gamma = E_\text{u} - E_\text{l}$ and $\lambda = \dfrac{hc}{E_\gamma}$
**Conversions:** $E_\text{m} = 1150.2\,\text{keV} = 1150.2 \cdot 1000 \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J} = 1.8427 \cdot 10^{-13}\,\text{J}$; finally $1\,\text{m} = 10^{12}\,\text{pm}$.
**Substitution:** a) By the greatest line the top level is $E_3 = 1501.4\,\text{keV}$ and the lowest excited level is $E_1 = 351.2\,\text{keV}$, which is also the smallest line of the table (a transition to the ground state). The third level is obtained from the line $820.5\,\text{keV}$, if it is a transition to the ground state: $E_2 = 820.5\,\text{keV}$. Check with the differences of the levels: $E_2 - E_1 = 820.5 - 351.2 = 469.3$, which is the line $468.9\,\text{keV}$, and $E_3 - E_2 = 1501.4 - 820.5 = 680.9$, which is the line $681.3\,\text{keV}$. All five lines are explained when the measurement uncertainty is less than $0.5\,\text{keV}$. No other order works: if the second level were $681.3\,\text{keV}$, the transition $E_2 - E_1 = 330.1\,\text{keV}$ would be missing from the table and the line $468.9\,\text{keV}$ would remain unexplained.
b) The missing transition is from the top level to the lowest excited level: $E_\text{m} = E_3 - E_1 = 1501.4 - 351.2 = 1150.2\,\text{keV}$.
c) $hc = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s} \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s} = 1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}$ and $\lambda = \dfrac{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}}{1.8427 \cdot 10^{-13}\,\text{J}} = 1.0780 \cdot 10^{-12}\,\text{m} = 1.0780\,\text{pm}$
**Answer:** $1.08\,\text{pm}$, three significant figures. The levels are $351.2$, $820.5$ and $1501.4\,\text{keV}$ above the ground state, and the energy of the missing line is $1150.2\,\text{keV}$.
**Reasonableness:** The missing energy $1150.2\,\text{keV}$ is smaller than the greatest line $1501.4\,\text{keV}$ and greater than the line $820.5\,\text{keV}$. So the wavelength is longer than the $0.83\,\text{pm}$ of the greatest line and shorter than the wavelength $1.51\,\text{pm}$ of the line $820.5\,\text{keV}$. The result $1.08\,\text{pm}$ is between these.
