## fy2-6-L1-01
**Given:** average power $P = 2.5\,\text{MW}$, time $t = 168\,\text{h}$
**Find:** the energy $E$ produced in a week, in megawatt-hours
**Principle:** The average power is the power at which the plant would produce the same energy steadily all the time, so the energy is the average power multiplied by time.
**Formula:** $E = P t$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the power is in megawatts and the time in hours, so the product is directly in megawatt-hours.
**Substitution:** $E = 2.5\,\text{MW} \cdot 168\,\text{h} = 420\,\text{MWh}$
**Answer:** $420\,\text{MWh}$, two significant figures like the power $2.5\,\text{MW}$.
**Reasonableness:** Estimate: $2.5\,\text{MW} \cdot 170\,\text{h} \approx 425\,\text{MWh}$. A week is about $1 : 52$ of a year, so at the same average power the plant would produce in a year about $52 \cdot 420\,\text{MWh} \approx 22\,000\,\text{MWh}$, that is, tens of gigawatt-hours, which fits the size of one wind turbine.

## fy2-6-L1-06
**Find:** which of the listed energy sources is renewable
**Principle:** A renewable energy source does not run out when it is used, because the Sun and the water cycle keep replenishing it. A non-renewable source formed over a very long time, and no more of it forms at the rate at which it is used.
**Model answer:** Wind. The Sun keeps the atmosphere moving all the time, so the energy of the wind does not run out when it is used. Uranium, coal and natural gas are non-renewable: coal and natural gas are fossil fuels that formed over millions of years, and there is a limited amount of uranium. Wind is renewable but weather-dependent, so renewable does not always mean available.

## fy2-6-L1-07
**Find:** what is meant by regulating power
**Principle:** In an electricity grid, production must at every moment be equal to consumption. Regulating power is production whose power can be raised and lowered as needed. Base load power produces steadily, and the power of weather-dependent production varies without anyone being able to decide it.
**Model answer:** Regulating power is a power plant whose power can be changed quickly to follow consumption, such as a hydropower plant. A nuclear power plant that produces steadily all year is base load power, not regulating power. The power of a wind turbine varies with the weather, but it cannot be regulated as needed, so it is weather-dependent production. That is why regulating power is needed alongside wind power.

## fy2-6-L1-08
**Given:** rated power $P_\text{rated} = 4.0\,\text{MW}$, annual energy $E = 11\,\text{GWh}$
**Find:** the time $t$ in hours for which the turbine would have to run at its rated power to produce the same energy
**Principle:** Running at its rated power, the turbine produces the energy $E = P_\text{rated}\, t$. When this equals the actual annual energy, the time is obtained by dividing the energy by the rated power.
**Formula:** $E = P_\text{rated}\, t$, so $t = \dfrac{E}{P_\text{rated}}$
**Conversions:** $11\,\text{GWh} = 11 \cdot 1000\,\text{MWh} = 11\,000\,\text{MWh}$.
**Substitution:** $t = 11\,000\,\text{MWh} : 4.0\,\text{MW} = 2750\,\text{h}$
**Answer:** $2800\,\text{h}$, that is, $2.8 \cdot 10^{3}\,\text{h}$ (the calculated value $2750\,\text{h}$ rounded), two significant figures like the power $4.0\,\text{MW}$.
**Reasonableness:** At its rated power for the whole year the turbine would produce $4.0\,\text{MW} \cdot 8760\,\text{h} = 35\,040\,\text{MWh} \approx 35\,\text{GWh}$, and $11 : 35 \approx 0.31$. The time $2750\,\text{h}$ is about $31\,\%$ of the $8760$ hours of a year, which fits a wind turbine that does not produce at its rated power all the time.

## fy2-6-L2-05

**Principle:** The share is the energy of the source divided by the total energy, so the total production is calculated first by addition. The change in the share is the share of the second year minus the share of the first year.
**Formula:** $o = \dfrac{E_\text{wind}}{E_\text{tot}}$ and $\Delta o = o_2 - o_1$
**Conversions:** Not needed: all the energies are in terawatt-hours. The share is changed into a percentage by multiplying by one hundred.

The shares of wind power are $o_1 = 1.5 : 68.0 = 0.0221 = 2.2\,\%$ and $o_2 = 20.0 : 80.0 = 0.250 = 25\,\%$. The change is $\Delta o = 25.0\,\% - 2.2\,\% = 22.8$ percentage points.
**Answer:** The share of wind power grew by $23$ percentage points (calculated value $22.8$; from $2.2\,\%$ to $25\,\%$), two significant figures like the energy of wind power $1.5\,\text{TWh}$. The energy of thermal power using fuels decreased the most: $28.5\,\text{TWh} - 14.0\,\text{TWh} = 14.5\,\text{TWh}$. The production structure shifted from fuels to wind and nuclear power, so the share of production that causes emissions became smaller. At the same time the share of weather-dependent production grew, so the need for regulating power grew.

## fy2-6-L2-06
**Given:** farms A $30\,\text{MW}$, $84\,\text{GWh}$; B $60\,\text{MW}$, $150\,\text{GWh}$; C $12\,\text{MW}$, $35\,\text{GWh}$; year $8760\,\text{h}$
**Find:** the share of the average power of the rated power for each farm, and whether the student's claim is correct
**Principle:** The average power is the annual energy divided by the number of hours in a year, $P_\text{avg} = E : t$. Its share of the rated power tells how well the rated power is used. The annual energy also depends on the size of the rated power, so the greatest annual energy does not tell the share.
**Formula:** $P_\text{avg} = \dfrac{E}{t}$ and $o = \dfrac{P_\text{avg}}{P_\text{rated}}$
**Conversions:** $E_\text{A} = 84\,\text{GWh} = 84\,000\,\text{MWh}$, $E_\text{B} = 150\,000\,\text{MWh}$ and $E_\text{C} = 35\,000\,\text{MWh}$.
**Substitution:** $P_\text{avg} = E : t$ and $o = P_\text{avg} : P_\text{rated}$
```latex
\begin{aligned}
P_\text{A} &= 84\,000\,\text{MWh} : 8760\,\text{h} \\
&= 9.6\,\text{MW} \\
o_\text{A} &= 9.6\,\text{MW} : 30\,\text{MW} = 0.32 \\
P_\text{B} &= 150\,000\,\text{MWh} : 8760\,\text{h} \\
&= 17.1\,\text{MW} \\
o_\text{B} &= 17.1\,\text{MW} : 60\,\text{MW} = 0.29 \\
P_\text{C} &= 35\,000\,\text{MWh} : 8760\,\text{h} \\
&= 4.0\,\text{MW} \\
o_\text{C} &= 4.0\,\text{MW} : 12\,\text{MW} = 0.33
\end{aligned}
```
**Answer:** The student is not right. Farm B produces the most energy because its rated power is the greatest, but it makes the poorest use of its rated power ($29\,\%$). The error is to mix up the annual energy and the use of the rated power: the energy grows with the rated power, so the share must be calculated by dividing the average power by the rated power. The best is farm C, whose average power is $33\,\%$ of the rated power, two significant figures like the rated powers.
**Reasonableness:** If farm B ran at its rated power the whole year, it would produce $60\,\text{MW} \cdot 8760\,\text{h} = 525\,600\,\text{MWh} \approx 526\,\text{GWh}$, and $150 : 526 = 0.29$. All the shares are below one and of the order of a third, as is typical for wind power, because the wind varies.

## fy2-6-L2-07
**Given:** emission factors coal $820\,\text{g/kWh}$ and wind power $12\,\text{g/kWh}$, the city's electricity $E = 2.0\,\text{TWh}$ per year
**Find:** the emission saved, in carbon dioxide equivalent, from the life-cycle emissions that belong to one year's electricity production, in millions of tonnes, when coal is replaced by wind power; the best choice
**Principle:** The emission is the emission factor multiplied by the energy produced. The saving is the difference between the emissions of coal and wind power, because the energy is the same in both.
**Formula:** $\Delta m = (k_\text{coal} - k_\text{wind})\, E$
**Conversions:** $2.0\,\text{TWh} = 2.0 \cdot 10^{9}\,\text{kWh}$. Finally grams into tonnes: $1.616 \cdot 10^{12}\,\text{g} = 1.616 \cdot 10^{12} : 10^{6}\,\text{t} = 1.616 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{t}$.
**Substitution:** $\Delta m = (820\,\text{g/kWh} - 12\,\text{g/kWh}) \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{9}\,\text{kWh} = 808\,\text{g/kWh} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{9}\,\text{kWh} = 1.616 \cdot 10^{12}\,\text{g} = 1.616 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{t}$
**Answer:** About $1.6$ million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent per year, two significant figures like the energy $2.0\,\text{TWh}$. On the basis of emissions and lifetime the best choice is nuclear power: its emission factor $12\,\text{g/kWh}$ is the smallest (as small as that of wind power) and its lifetime $60\,\text{a}$ is the longest. The table does not tell the controllability of power, the costs, the weather dependence, the land use or the waste, all of which affect the choice. For example, wind power and solar power are weather-dependent and need regulating power.
**Reasonableness:** The emissions of coal are $820 : 12 \approx 68$ times the emissions of wind power, so almost the whole emission of coal, $820\,\text{g/kWh} \cdot 2.0 \cdot 10^{9}\,\text{kWh} = 1.64 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{t}$, is saved, and $1.616 : 1.64 = 0.99$.

**Reasonableness:** In weak wind, wind power covers only about $0.36 : 11 \approx 3\,\%$ of consumption, so most of the missing power must be produced by controllable means: hydropower, fuels or imports. This is why weather-dependent production needs controllable production alongside it.

## fy2-6-L3-05
**Given:** wind farm $P_\text{rated} = 100\,\text{MW}$, average power $33\,\%$, $K = 120$ million euros, lifetime $25\,\text{a}$, $12\,\text{g/kWh}$; nuclear power plant $1200\,\text{MW}$, $90\,\%$, $K = 6000$ million euros, $60\,\text{a}$, $12\,\text{g/kWh}$; year $8760\,\text{h}$
**Find:** a) the annual energies; b) the construction cost per megawatt-hour produced, the wind farm in the box; c) the emission in carbon dioxide equivalent from the life-cycle emissions that belongs to one year's production; d) a justified comparison
**Principle:** The annual energy is the average power multiplied by the length of a year, and the average power is a share of the rated power. The energy over the lifetime is the annual energy multiplied by the lifetime. The cost per energy is the construction cost divided by the energy over the lifetime. The emission is the emission factor multiplied by the energy.
**Formula:** $E_\text{y} = o\, P_\text{rated}\, t$, $E_\text{tot} = E_\text{y} \cdot a$ and $k = \dfrac{K}{E_\text{tot}}$
**Conversions:** $120\,\text{million euros} = 120 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{euros}$ and $6000\,\text{million euros} = 6000 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{euros}$. For emissions $1\,\text{MWh} = 1000\,\text{kWh}$ and $1\,\text{t} = 10^{6}\,\text{g}$.
**Substitution:** a) Wind farm: $E_\text{y} = 0.33 \cdot 100\,\text{MW} \cdot 8760\,\text{h} = 289\,080\,\text{MWh}$. Nuclear power plant: $E_\text{y} = 0.90 \cdot 1200\,\text{MW} \cdot 8760\,\text{h} = 9\,460\,800\,\text{MWh}$.
b) Wind farm: $E_\text{tot} = 289\,080\,\text{MWh} \cdot 25 = 7\,227\,000\,\text{MWh}$ and $k = 120 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{euros} : 7\,227\,000\,\text{MWh} = 16.6\,\text{euros/MWh}$. Nuclear power plant: $E_\text{tot} = 9\,460\,800\,\text{MWh} \cdot 60 = 567\,648\,000\,\text{MWh}$ and $k = 6000 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{euros} : 567\,648\,000\,\text{MWh} = 10.6\,\text{euros/MWh}$.
c) Wind farm: $289\,080\,000\,\text{kWh} \cdot 12\,\text{g/kWh} = 3.469 \cdot 10^{9}\,\text{g} = 3469\,\text{t}$. Nuclear power plant: $9\,460\,800\,000\,\text{kWh} \cdot 12\,\text{g/kWh} = 1.135 \cdot 10^{11}\,\text{g} = 113\,500\,\text{t}$.
**Answer:** The construction cost of the wind farm is $17\,\text{euros/MWh}$ (calculated value $16.6$; two significant figures like the share $33\,\%$) and that of the nuclear power plant $11\,\text{euros/MWh}$. The annual energies are $2.9 \cdot 10^{5}\,\text{MWh}$ and $9.5 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{MWh}$, and the life-cycle emissions that belong to one year's production are about $3500\,\text{t}$ and $114\,000\,\text{t}$. The emissions per energy are the same ($12\,\text{g/kWh}$), so the large annual emissions of the nuclear power plant are due only to its large production. In this data set the construction cost per energy is smaller for nuclear power, because it produces at a greater share of its rated power and for longer. Nuclear power is steady base load power, but it cannot be regulated quickly, and it involves nuclear waste. Wind power is weather-dependent, so it needs regulating power or storage alongside it. A justified recommendation is a combination: on the basis of emissions either is suitable, and on the basis of cost nuclear power is cheaper, but weather-dependent wind power suits complementing it when there is enough regulating power. The recommendation, however, rests only on this example data set and on the construction cost alone.
**Reasonableness:** The nuclear power plant produces $9\,460\,800 : 289\,080 \approx 33$ times as much per year as the wind farm, and the emissions are $113\,500 : 3469 \approx 33$ times as large, which matches the ratio of production, because the emission factor is the same $12\,\text{g/kWh}$ for both. At full power for the whole year the wind farm would produce $100\,\text{MW} \cdot 8760\,\text{h} = 876\,000\,\text{MWh}$, and $289\,080 : 876\,000 = 0.33$.

## fy2-6-L1-10
**Given:** power $P = 2.0\,\text{MW}$, time $t = 1\,\text{h}$; heat of combustion of milled peat from the MAFY table (Polttoaineiden LHV-lämpöarvoja, jyrsinturve) $H = 10\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}}$
**Find:** the mass of peat burnt, $m$, in kilograms
**Principle:** The energy the plant produces in an hour is $E = Pt$. The same energy is released when the fuel burns, and the energy released in burning is $E = Hm$, so the mass is found by solving for $m$.
**Formula:** $E = Pt$ and $E = Hm \Rightarrow m = \dfrac{Pt}{H}$
**Conversions:** $2.0\,\text{MW} = 2.0\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{s}}$ and $1\,\text{h} = 3600\,\text{s}$, so $E = 2.0\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{s}} \cdot 3600\,\text{s} = 7200\,\text{MJ}$.
**Substitution:** $m = \dfrac{7200\,\text{MJ}}{10\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}}} = 720\,\text{kg}$
**Answer:** $m \approx 720\,\text{kg}$, two significant figures like the power $2.0\,\text{MW}$. The heat of combustion $10\,\tfrac{\text{MJ}}{\text{kg}}$ is from the MAFY table (Polttoaineiden LHV-lämpöarvoja); different tables differ slightly, and the answer is accepted within about $\pm 4\,\%$.
**Reasonableness:** The heat of combustion of peat is small, so a lot burns: about $0.7$ tonnes per hour, or about $12\,\text{kg}$ per minute. This fits a small $2\,\text{MW}$ plant.
