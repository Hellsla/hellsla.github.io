## Lesson plan (75 min)

- **0–8 min, Think about it.** Read Aino's one-line answer aloud. In two minutes pairs list what is missing from it, and the list is written on the board. The list is the backbone of the lesson: you return to it at the six questions of the theory summary.
- **8–18 min, Predict (starting-point test).** Six questions alone, without discussion. When everyone has answered, ask with a show of hands which question felt hardest. Typically repetition and systematic error (questions 1 and 4) and the wording of the conclusion (6) divide the group.
- **18–33 min, Investigate.** The group's spring measurement in pairs: first each partner looks for the errors alone, then the pair writes a joint assessment. Debrief with the whole group: why does $k$ grow with the mass, and why can the manufacturer's value not be rejected on the basis of the mean?
- **33–43 min, Theory summary.** The teacher goes through the map of the six questions and the constant term of the graph (oil weighed in a beaker). The Common mistakes list is read and compared with the list from Think about it.
- **43–63 min, Apply.** In class, five tasks alone or in pairs (about four minutes each): Choosing a measuring instrument, Find the flaw in the plan, Which graph?, Repeated timing and Spring constant from a graph. Zero error, The zero of pressure and A long enough measurement are left for home.
- **63–75 min, Check your understanding and exit ticket.** Three checks alone, then the exit ticket.
- **At home: Tasks.** Everyone does at least one experiment plan (1 or 2), the report assessment (3) and the graph task (4) and assesses them with the marking scheme. Tasks 5 and 6 are extra practice. Task 4 is best drawn on graph paper or with a spreadsheet, as in the exam.

## Using the starting-point test
- More than two wrong answers in questions 1, 2 and 4 (kinds of error, resolution): send the student to the theory of the chapter *Measuring and the reliability of a measurement* before the Tasks.
- A wrong answer in question 5 (slope): the chapter *Graphing and fitting a straight line* and the Apply task “Spring constant from a graph”.
- A wrong answer in questions 3 and 6 (variables, conclusion): the chapter *Your own experimental investigation: planning, carrying out and reporting*.

## Pair work
- In the Investigate stage one pair looks for the gross error and the other for the systematic error, after which they swap and complete each other's answers.
- In the experiment plan tasks (1 and 2) a pair can write the plan and swap it with another pair: the other pair tries to “carry out the experiment” from the text alone and marks the places where they do not know what to do.

## Differentiation
- **Fast students** also do the three Apply tasks left for home in class. At home they also do task 6 (combining modules) and compare its part d with part d of task 4: both have a systematic error, but one shifts the line and the other changes the slope.
- **Slower students** do the choice and plan tasks of Apply and the repeated timing, and leave the rest for home. Of the Tasks, all of task 1 (experiment plan) and parts a and b of task 4 are enough.

## Assessment
- Written answers are assessed with the criteria of the model answer. In the experiment plan, the points require that the plan shows the measured quantity and instrument, the measuring method, the things kept constant, repetitions, calculating the result from a graph and at least two named sources of error with ways to reduce them.
- In the report assessment (task 3) a shortcoming is accepted only when its effect on the result or the conclusion is justified.

## Common difficulties
- The student writes the resolution as the uncertainty. Ask: “Who presses the button of the watch, and how precisely?”
- Systematic error and the constant term of the line get mixed up with random error. Show the figure in the theory: the beaker shifts every point by the same amount, so the line shifts but does not turn.
- “Human error” as a source of error. Always require three things: what happens, in which direction the result changes and how it is reduced.
