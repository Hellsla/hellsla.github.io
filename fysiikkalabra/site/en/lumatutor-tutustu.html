Visit science events

See how others do science education and collect ideas for your own workshop. Take part in at least one suitable science event.

Why visit?

As a LUMA tutor you do not only organise events. You also learn to look at how experienced science communicators and science educators do their work: how they start, what they ask and how they get the audience to try things.

On a visit you are an observer. You watch how it is done and how the audience reacts, not only the phenomena. No long report is needed: the aim is to collect thoughts and ideas for your own work.

What kind of events?

A suitable event is one where the public hears about science or gets to try things themselves. Examples:

  • Researchers' Night
  • Heureka's science events
  • science days and open days at universities
  • events at LUMA centres
  • public events at research institutes
  • science festivals
  • public lectures popularising science
  • STEAM and technology education events
  • other science events the teacher approves

The list gives examples of kinds of events. They are not agreed visits or partnerships. Always check the timing and suitability of an event with the teacher first.

Three ways to visit

  • Group visit

    The teacher arranges a visit for the whole tutor group. Agree in advance who observes what.

  • Your own visit

    You visit an event on your own, for example at a weekend. Tell the teacher in advance where you are going and make sure the event is suitable.

  • As an assistant

    You help a workshop leader at an event. You see the workshop from the inside, and this is often the best way to learn.

Before, during and after

Before

  • Agree on the visit with the teacher and check the event's programme.
  • Choose one or two things to observe in particular.
  • Print the observation template or copy it to your phone.

During

  • Take part yourself, try things and listen.
  • Watch the audience: when do they get excited, and when does their attention drift?
  • Make short notes as soon as something gives you an idea.
  • Do not photograph people without permission. You may photograph equipment and set-ups if the organiser allows it.

After

  • Complete the observation template on the same day.
  • Choose one idea to try in your own workshop.
  • Add your observations to the Visits to science events part of your portfolio and log the time you spent.

Observation questions

Look at the event from the point of view of science communication and science education. Answer the six questions in a few sentences.

  1. How was the science made understandable?
  2. How was the audience's interest sparked?
  3. How did the participants get to try and explore things themselves?
  4. What demonstrations or workshops were there at the event?
  5. What worked especially well?
  6. What could I use in my own science workshop?
