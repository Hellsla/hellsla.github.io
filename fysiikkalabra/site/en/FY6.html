FY6 · 2 cr · Optional

Electricity

How does electric current flow in a circuit and transfer energy?

16 lessons

Lessons

  1. Direct-current circuits

    chapters 1–5
  2. Chapter 1Electric circuit, current and voltage

    Why does the lamp of a torch light at the same moment as you press the switch, although the electrons in the wire move more slowly than a snail?

  3. Chapter 2Measurements in an electric circuit

    Why is an ammeter connected to a circuit in a different way from a voltmeter, and what happens if the connections get mixed up?

  4. Chapter 3Resistance and Ohm's law

    Why is the heating wire of a toaster made of thin special metal, while the conductors of an extension cord are thick copper?

  5. Chapter 4Resistors in series and in parallel

    Why do all the bulbs of an old string of fairy lights go out when one bulb breaks, while at home the floor lamp stays on even when the desk lamp is switched off?

  6. Chapter 5Kirchhoff's laws and potential in a circuit

    Why can a bird sit on a bare high-voltage wire unharmed, even though a current of hundreds of amperes flows in the wire?

  7. Electrical energy and sources

    chapters 6–9
  8. Chapter 6Electric power and the heating of a resistor

    Why do the resistance wires of a toaster glow red while the toaster's cord stays cool, even though the same current flows through both?

  9. Chapter 7Electrical energy at home

    Why is an electricity bill calculated from kilowatt-hours and not from the watts of the home's devices?

  10. Chapter 8Electromotive force and internal resistance

    Why do the headlights of a car dim for a moment when the engine is started on a cold winter morning?

  11. Chapter 9Batteries and the charging circuit

    Why does a phone battery charge quickly at the start, but the last few percent take a long time?

  12. Electric charge and field

    chapters 10–13
  13. Chapter 10Electric charge and Coulomb's law

    Why does a balloon rubbed on a wool sweater stick to a wall, although the wall has no electric charge?

  14. Chapter 11Uniform electric field

    Why do the dust particles of an air purifier end up on the surface of the plates of the device, although the air flows past the plates?

  15. Chapter 12Potential energy and potential in an electric field

    Why are the electrons plunging into the upper atmosphere in the aurora so fast that they make the air glow?

  16. Chapter 13The capacitor and its energy

    Why does the flash of a camera need a few seconds of charging time, although the flash itself lasts only a millisecond?

  17. Components and safety

    chapters 14–16
  18. Chapter 14Semiconductors, the diode, the LED and the solar cell

    Why does an LED light up only when it is connected one way round, and why must the current of an LED be limited, although a light bulb can be connected straight to a battery?

  19. Chapter 15Electrical safety at home and at work

    Why does the kitchen fuse trip when many devices are on at the same time, even though none of them is broken?

  20. Chapter 16Applied chapter: the electric car and the home electrical system

    Why should an electric car not be charged through an extension cord from an ordinary socket, and is the electricity of a home enough for the sauna and for charging the car at the same time?
