The findings below are non-blocking; findings 1 and 2 are worth fixing before publication.

1. Extrapolation tasks repeat the worked example: exercises.json:56 (L2-02) reuses the example's exact data (100 kPa at 0 °C, 137 kPa at 100 °C), and exercises.json:62-65 (L2-03) and :88-93 (L3-02) repeat its method (slope, then p = 0), only with other numbers. Suggest changing L2-02 to different data (for example interpolating at an awkward temperature), and replacing L3-02 with a reasoning task (for example, deciding which of two noisy data sets gives the better estimate, or explaining why the line cannot be measured down to 0 K).
2. The lesson never states the two proportionalities that L3-01, L3-03 and exit ticket 2 rely on: lesson.md:53-58 and :61 imply that p is proportional to T (the line passes through p = 0 at 0 K) and that mean kinetic energy is proportional to T, but neither is said in words, so the exercises (exercises.json:82, :98) have to supply them as premises. Add one sentence after the extrapolation (at lesson.md:58): with constant volume and amount of gas, the pressure is directly proportional to the kelvin temperature, and so is the mean kinetic energy. That fits FY3-3 and is not the gas law.
