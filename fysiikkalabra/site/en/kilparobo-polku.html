Competitive robotics

VEX V5 – the beginner's path

The path helps a new student get started with a VEX V5 robot. Go at your own pace with your mentor. The stages have no deadlines, and you can skip a stage when you already know its contents.

  • Start
  • Beginner's path
  • Season and diary
  • Mentoring
  • Safety

Each stage has a goal, a small practice, a tip for the mentor and a way to show what you learnt. When you have shown the skill to your mentor, record it in the development diary.

Before the first stage: complete the safety induction as the teacher instructs. Safety →

  2. Learn to drive
  3. Learn to program

1

Stage 1 Get to know the robot

Goal: You recognise the robot's main parts and know what each does and where it is connected.

What you learn

  • VEX V5 Robot Brain: the robot's control unit with a touchscreen, 21 Smart Ports for motors and sensors, and eight 3-Wire ports (A⁠–⁠H).
  • V5 Controller: the driver's controller. It connects wirelessly to the Brain through the V5 Robot Radio connected to the robot.
  • V5 Smart Motor (11W): the motor has an interchangeable gear cartridge that sets its top speed: red 100 rpm, green 200 rpm and blue 600 rpm.
  • Battery and power: the V5 Robot Battery is connected to the Brain, and the Brain powers the motors and sensors.
  • Sensors: for example the Inertial Sensor (heading and turning), Distance Sensor (distance), Optical Sensor (colour and proximity) and Rotation Sensor (a shaft's rotation).
  • Mechanical structure: metal chassis, shafts, bearings, gears and screw joints.

Practice

Go round the team's robot with your mentor. Find the Brain, the controller, the radio, the battery and every motor and sensor. Check on the Brain's screen which port each device is connected to, and draw a port map of the robot.

Tip for the mentor

Let the new student find the parts themselves. Ask for example: How do you know this is a motor and not a sensor? What would happen if this cable came loose in the middle of a match?

How you show the skill

Using the port map, you explain to your mentor what the robot's parts do and where they are connected.

  • Force on a current-carrying wire: the electric motor and the loudspeaker
2

Stage 2 Learn to drive

Goal: You start the robot safely and drive it under control with the controller.

What you learn

  • Starting safely: check the surroundings, put the robot on the floor or field and keep your hands away from moving parts.
  • The link between controller and Brain: how you see that the controller is connected to the robot.
  • Driving practice: driving straight, turning and handling game pieces.
  • Speed and accuracy: when is it worth driving slowly, and when fast?
  • First observations: how the robot behaves when the battery runs down, the surface changes or the load grows.

Practice

Make a small course with tape: a straight, two turns and a stopping area. Drive the course three times, time it and count how many times the robot hits a line. Then drive more slowly and compare the results.

Tip for the mentor

Show starting and switching off once, then let the new student do them. After driving, ask: What was hardest? What did the robot do that you did not expect?

How you show the skill

You start and switch off the robot correctly and drive the course three times without collisions. You record the times and one observation in the development diary.

Official guides

  • Pairing the V5 Controller with the V5 Brain (opens on a VEX or RECF site, in English)
  • Understanding the V5 Robot Battery (opens on a VEX or RECF site, in English)

Physics behind it

  • Weight, friction and the inclined plane
  • Measuring and the reliability of a measurement
3

Stage 3 Learn to program

Goal: You write a program that moves the robot and uses the reading of one sensor.

What you learn

  • VEXcode V5: the programming environment where you can code with blocks, Python or C++.
  • Configuring devices: motors and sensors are added to the program's device settings on the right ports.
  • Controlling motors: direction, speed, and turning angle or time.
  • Program structure: the order of commands, repetition, conditions and your own functions.
  • Using sensors: showing a reading on the Brain's screen and using it in a condition.
  • A simple autonomous action: the robot drives, turns and stops without a driver.

The length and rules of a match's autonomous period depend on the season's game. Check them in the current season's game manual.

Practice

Make a program that drives the robot straight forward about a metre, turns a right angle and stops. Measure with a tape measure how accurately the robot arrives. Then add a condition: the robot stops when the Distance Sensor detects an obstacle. Choose the limit yourself and record it.

Tip for the mentor

Do not write code for the new student. Ask them to read the program aloud and say what the robot should do. When something does not work, ask: What did you expect? What happened? What would you change first?

How you show the skill

You run the program for your mentor, explain its structure and show what happens when the sensor's limit is changed.

Official guides

  • VEXcode V5 (opens on a VEX or RECF site, in English)
  • Configuring Sensors in VEXcode V5 (opens on a VEX or RECF site, in English)
  • VEX API Reference (Blocks, Python, C++) (opens on a VEX or RECF site, in English)

Physics behind it

  • Uniform motion: speed, position and graphs
4

Stage 4 Build and improve

Goal: You make one reasoned mechanical change to the robot and show with a test what it changed.

What you learn

  • Drivetrain: how the motor's rotation is passed to wheels and mechanisms through shafts, gears and chains.
  • Gear ratios: when a small gear turns a large one, speed drops and torque grows. Choosing the gear cartridge works the same way.
  • Stiffness of structures: bracing, triangles and properly tightened joints keep the chassis from twisting.
  • Centre of mass: a low, central centre of mass keeps the robot from tipping over.
  • Grippers and other mechanisms: how a game piece is picked up, carried and released.
  • Testing and iteration: one change at a time and the same test before and after the change.

Practice

Choose one mechanism with your mentor, for example a lifting arm. First measure the starting point, for example the lifting time or the largest load the arm lifts. Change the gear ratio or the gear cartridge, measure again in the same way and compare.

Tip for the mentor

Let the new student suggest a change before you share your own idea. Ask: Why do you think this helps? How will we measure whether it helped? What do we lose if we gain speed?

How you show the skill

You present the before-and-after measurement and explain in your own words how the gear ratio or the structure made a difference.

Official guides

  • Using Gear Ratios with the V5 Motor (opens on a VEX or RECF site, in English)
  • Switching the V5 Motor Gear Cartridge (opens on a VEX or RECF site, in English)
  • Building with VEX V5 (opens on a VEX or RECF site, in English)

Physics behind it

  • Moment: the turning effect of a force
  • Equilibrium of a body: forces and moments
  • Newton's second law
  • Power: $P = \Delta E/\Delta t$
5

Stage 5 Take part in the team's project

Goal: You take one small development task from a plan to a tested and documented solution.

What you learn

  • Choose a development task from the team's task list or suggest your own.
  • Plan a solution: a sketch, the parts needed and a test that shows whether it worked.
  • Carry out the solution with your mentor's support.
  • Test and compare the result with the starting point.
  • Record the observation and the improvement in the development diary.

Practice

Examples of development tasks: improving a gripper's hold, making an autonomous program more accurate, routing cables so that nothing comes loose during a match, or sorting spare parts so that they are quick to find.

Tip for the mentor

Agree when you will check the work together. Let the new student present the work to the team themselves, even if it is not finished yet.

How you show the skill

You briefly present the work to the team and write a diary entry that would let someone else continue the work.
