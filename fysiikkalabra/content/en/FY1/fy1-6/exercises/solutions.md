## fy1-6-L1-01
**Given:** the ball starts from rest, $t = 3.0\,\text{s}$, acceleration of free fall $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the speed of the ball $v$ in $\text{m/s}$
**Principle:** Air resistance is ignored, so the ball is in free fall. Then the speed increases by the same amount every second, and the speed of an object that starts from rest is directly proportional to time.
**Formula:** $v = g\,t$. The formula is already solved for the speed we want: $v = g\,t$
**Conversions:** The time is in seconds and $g$ in $\text{m/s}^2$, so the result comes directly in $\text{m/s}$. No conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $v = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 3.0\,\text{s} = 29.43\,\text{m/s}$
**Answer:** $29\,\text{m/s}$, two significant figures as in the time $3.0\,\text{s}$.
**Reasonableness:** The speed increases by about $10\,\text{m/s}$ every second, so in three seconds it is about $3 \cdot 10\,\text{m/s} = 30\,\text{m/s}$, which matches the result. In addition, $29\,\text{m/s} \approx 106\,\text{km/h}$ is a motorway speed, which is a reasonable result for a three-second free fall.

## fy1-6-L1-02
**Given:** the apple starts from rest, $t = 0.80\,\text{s}$, $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the speed of the apple $v$ when it hits the ground, in $\text{m/s}$
**Principle:** Air resistance is ignored, so the apple is in free fall and starts from rest. Then the speed is the product of the acceleration of free fall and the elapsed time.
**Formula:** $v = g\,t$. The formula is already solved for the speed we want: $v = g\,t$
**Conversions:** The time is given in seconds, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $v = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 0.80\,\text{s} = 7.848\,\text{m/s}$
**Answer:** $7.8\,\text{m/s}$, two significant figures as in the time $0.80\,\text{s}$.
**Reasonableness:** In less than a second the speed has time to increase by less than $9.81\,\text{m/s}$, and $0.80 \cdot 9.81 \approx 8$ gives the same order of magnitude. A speed of $7.8\,\text{m/s} \approx 28\,\text{km/h}$ is reasonable after a fall of less than a second: it is faster than jogging but not yet the speed of cars.

## fy1-6-L1-04
**Given:** the line of the $(t, v)$ graph passes through the points $(t_1;\, v_1) = (0\,\text{s};\, 0\,\text{m/s})$ and $(t_2;\, v_2) = (5.0\,\text{s};\, 49\,\text{m/s})$
**Find:** the acceleration of free fall $g$ in $\text{m/s}^2$
**Principle:** The speed of a freely falling object increases by the same amount every second, so the $(t, v)$ graph is a straight line. The slope of the line is the change in speed divided by the change in time, and it is the acceleration of free fall.
**Reading the graph:** The two given points are on the line. The rise $\Delta v = v_2 - v_1$ is read in the vertical direction and the run $\Delta t = t_2 - t_1$ in the horizontal direction. The points are far apart, so the reading error stays small.
**Formula:** $g = \dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{v_2 - v_1}{t_2 - t_1}$
**Conversions:** The speeds are in $\text{m/s}$ and the times in seconds, so the result comes in $\text{m/s}^2$. No conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $g = \dfrac{49\,\text{m/s} - 0\,\text{m/s}}{5.0\,\text{s} - 0\,\text{s}} = \dfrac{49\,\text{m/s}}{5.0\,\text{s}} = 9.8\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Answer:** $9.8\,\text{m/s}^2$, two significant figures as in the numbers $49\,\text{m/s}$ and $5.0\,\text{s}$.
**Reasonableness:** The result is close to the value for Earth, $9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$, so the graph belongs to an object falling on Earth. The slope is positive, because the speed increases with time.

## fy1-6-L2-01
**Given:** the ball starts from rest, final speed $v = 12.0\,\text{m/s}$, $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the duration of the fall $t$ in $\text{s}$
**Principle:** Air resistance is ignored, so the ball is in free fall. The speed of an object that starts from rest is $v = g\,t$, and when the speed is known, the time is found by solving the formula for time.
**Formula:** $v = g\,t$, so $t = \dfrac{v}{g}$
**Conversions:** The speed is in $\text{m/s}$ and $g$ in $\text{m/s}^2$, so the unit of the quotient is $\dfrac{\text{m/s}}{\text{m/s}^2} = \text{s}$. No conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $t = \dfrac{12.0\,\text{m/s}}{9.81\,\text{m/s}^2} = 1.223\,\text{s}$
**Answer:** $1.22\,\text{s}$, three significant figures as in the speed $12.0\,\text{m/s}$.
**Reasonableness:** The speed increases by about $10\,\text{m/s}$ every second, so reaching $12\,\text{m/s}$ takes a little over a second. Back-calculation: $9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 1.22\,\text{s} = 12.0\,\text{m/s}$.

## fy1-6-L2-02
**Given:** the stone starts from rest, impact speed $v = 54.0\,\text{km/h}$, $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the time of the fall $t$ in $\text{s}$
**Principle:** Air resistance is ignored, so the stone is in free fall and the speed of an object that starts from rest is $v = g\,t$. The time is found by solving the formula for time after the speed has been converted to SI units.
**Formula:** $v = g\,t$, so $t = \dfrac{v}{g}$
**Conversions:** substitute $1\,\text{km} = 1000\,\text{m}$ and $1\,\text{h} = 3600\,\text{s}$: $54.0\,\text{km/h} = \dfrac{54.0\cdot1000\,\text{m}}{3600\,\text{s}} = 15.0\,\text{m/s}$.
**Substitution:** $t = \dfrac{15.0\,\text{m/s}}{9.81\,\text{m/s}^2} = 1.529\,\text{s}$
**Answer:** $1.53\,\text{s}$, three significant figures as in the speed $54.0\,\text{km/h}$.
**Reasonableness:** A speed of $15\,\text{m/s}$ is reached when the speed increases by about $10\,\text{m/s}$ every second, so it takes about one and a half seconds, which matches the result. This corresponds to falling from about the height of the fourth floor, so the situation is believable.

## fy1-6-L2-03
**Given:** initial speed $v_1 = 12.0\,\text{m/s}$, time interval $\Delta t = 1.50\,\text{s}$, $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$, no air resistance
**Find:** the speed $v_2$ in $\text{m/s}$ at the end of the time interval
**Principle:** Here the object does not start from rest, but the acceleration is still the constant $g$. So the speed increases by the same amount every second, and the change in speed over the interval is $\Delta v = g\,\Delta t$. The new speed is the old speed plus the change.
**Formula:** $v_2 = v_1 + \Delta v$ and $\Delta v = g\,\Delta t$, so $v_2 = v_1 + g\,\Delta t$
**Conversions:** The times are in seconds and the speeds in $\text{m/s}$, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $v_2 = 12.0\,\text{m/s} + 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 1.50\,\text{s} = 12.0\,\text{m/s} + 14.715\,\text{m/s} = 26.715\,\text{m/s}$
**Answer:** $26.7\,\text{m/s}$, three significant figures as in the data $12.0\,\text{m/s}$ and $1.50\,\text{s}$.
**Reasonableness:** The speed increases, so the new speed must be larger than $12.0\,\text{m/s}$. Estimate: one and a half seconds adds about $15\,\text{m/s}$, and $12 + 15 = 27$ fits the result.

## fy1-6-L2-04
**Given:** measurements $(t_1;\, v_1) = (1.0\,\text{s};\, 9.7\,\text{m/s})$ and $(t_2;\, v_2) = (3.0\,\text{s};\, 29.3\,\text{m/s})$
**Find:** the acceleration of free fall $g$ in $\text{m/s}^2$
**Principle:** In free fall the speed increases by the same amount every second. So $g$ is the change in speed divided by the change in time, and it can be calculated from any two measurements, even when neither is at the moment zero.
**Formula:** $g = \dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{v_2 - v_1}{t_2 - t_1}$
**Conversions:** The speeds are in $\text{m/s}$ and the times in seconds, so the result comes in $\text{m/s}^2$. No conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $g = \dfrac{29.3\,\text{m/s} - 9.7\,\text{m/s}}{3.0\,\text{s} - 1.0\,\text{s}} = \dfrac{19.6\,\text{m/s}}{2.0\,\text{s}} = 9.8\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Answer:** $9.8\,\text{m/s}^2$, two significant figures, because the time interval $2.0\,\text{s}$ is given with two figures.
**Reasonableness:** The result is close to the table value $9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$. Calculating from the first measurement by simple division, $\dfrac{9.7\,\text{m/s}}{1.0\,\text{s}} = 9.7\,\text{m/s}^2$, and from the second, $\dfrac{29.3\,\text{m/s}}{3.0\,\text{s}} = 9.8\,\text{m/s}^2$, gives almost the same, so the measurements fit a straight line through the origin.

## fy1-6-L3-01
**Given:** two balls in a vacuum, masses $m_1 = 0.50\,\text{kg}$ and $m_2 = 4.0\,\text{kg}$, both start from rest, $t = 1.5\,\text{s}$
**Find:** the ratio of the speeds $\dfrac{v_2}{v_1}$ (no unit)
**Principle:** In a vacuum there is no air resistance, so both balls are in free fall. The acceleration of free fall $g$ is the same for all objects regardless of mass, so the speed of each is $g\,t$.
**Formula:** $v_1 = g\,t$ and $v_2 = g\,t$, so $\dfrac{v_2}{v_1} = \dfrac{g\,t}{g\,t}$
**Conversions:** The times are in seconds, and the masses are not needed in the calculation. No conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $v_1 = v_2 = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 1.5\,\text{s} = 14.715\,\text{m/s}$, so $\dfrac{v_2}{v_1} = \dfrac{14.715\,\text{m/s}}{14.715\,\text{m/s}} = 1.0$
**Answer:** $1.0$, two significant figures as in the time $1.5\,\text{s}$. The ratio is exactly one, because the mass cancels out.
**Reasonableness:** If the heavier ball fell faster, mass would have to appear in the formula, but the formula $v = g\,t$ has no mass. This matches the hammer and feather experiment on the Moon, where the objects hit the ground at the same time.

## fy1-6-L3-02
**Given:** the planet has no atmosphere, the stone starts from rest, $v_1 = 14.8\,\text{m/s}$ at the moment $t_1 = 4.0\,\text{s}$, desired speed $v_2 = 30.0\,\text{m/s}$
**Find:** the time $t_2$ in $\text{s}$ at which the speed is $30.0\,\text{m/s}$
**Principle:** With no atmosphere the stone is in free fall, so the speed is directly proportional to time: $v = g\,t$. The $g$ of the planet is not known, so it is first solved from the given measurement and then used to find the time.
**Formula:** $g = \dfrac{v_1}{t_1}$ and $t_2 = \dfrac{v_2}{g}$, so $t_2 = \dfrac{v_2\,t_1}{v_1}$
**Conversions:** The speeds are in $\text{m/s}$ and the times in seconds, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $g = \dfrac{14.8\,\text{m/s}}{4.0\,\text{s}} = 3.70\,\text{m/s}^2$ and $t_2 = \dfrac{30.0\,\text{m/s}}{3.70\,\text{m/s}^2} = 8.108\,\text{s}$
**Answer:** $8.1\,\text{s}$, two significant figures, because the time $4.0\,\text{s}$ is given with two figures and limits the result.
**Reasonableness:** A speed of $30.0\,\text{m/s}$ is about twice the speed $14.8\,\text{m/s}$, so the time is about twice $4.0\,\text{s}$, that is, about $8\,\text{s}$. The result $8.1\,\text{s}$ matches the estimate. The planet's $g = 3.70\,\text{m/s}^2$ is similar to that of Mars.

## fy1-6-L3-03
**Given:** measured speed in air $v_\text{measured} = 25.0\,\text{m/s}$ at the moment $t = 3.00\,\text{s}$, $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the difference $\Delta v = v_\text{free} - v_\text{measured}$ in $\text{m/s}$
**Principle:** In free fall without air resistance the speed of an object that starts from rest would be $v_\text{free} = g\,t$. Air slows the ball, so the measured speed is smaller, and the difference tells the shortfall caused by air resistance.
**Formula:** $v_\text{free} = g\,t$ and $\Delta v = g\,t - v_\text{measured}$
**Conversions:** The time is in seconds and the speeds in $\text{m/s}$, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $\Delta v = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 3.00\,\text{s} - 25.0\,\text{m/s} = 29.43\,\text{m/s} - 25.0\,\text{m/s} = 4.43\,\text{m/s}$
**Answer:** $4.4\,\text{m/s}$. The time $3.00\,\text{s}$ gives the speed $29.43\,\text{m/s}$ three significant figures, but the difference has as many decimal places as the weakest number, that is, the measured value $25.0\,\text{m/s}$.
**Reasonableness:** The difference is positive, because air slows the ball, and it is a small part of the speed: $\dfrac{4.4}{29.4} \approx 15\,\%$. In a three-second fall air resistance is already significant but not dominant, which fits a ball.
