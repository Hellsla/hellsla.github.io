## Lesson plan (75 min)

- **0–5 min, Reflect.** Greenhouse grow lights and a dental X-ray. In pairs the students estimate which one can damage DNA and why the power does not decide it. The answer is not given yet: it is at the end of the Key idea part of the theory summary (red photon $1.9\,\text{eV}$, ionisation energy of water $12.6\,\text{eV}$, X-ray photon about $60\,\text{keV}$).
- **5–15 min, Predict (review test).** Six questions alone, without notes. Ask the students to write the numbers of the questions they got wrong in their notebook.
- **15–30 min, Investigate.** Three unknown sources in pairs: first the types of radiation from the table, then the half-value thickness of source C in lead. Go through it together: the background is subtracted before the ratio, and the share of gamma radiation is read behind the aluminium, because the aluminium stops the beta radiation.
- **30–40 min, Theory summary.** Go through the concept map one branch at a time: the photon and the atom (electronvolts), the nucleus (megaelectronvolts), radiation and people (dose). In each branch ask what one photon or one nucleus does and what the number changes. Return to Reflect with the energy-axis figure. The halving figure shows that the decay law and the attenuation of gamma radiation are the same law.
- **40–55 min, Apply.** Four multiple-choice questions in pairs (about $6\,\text{min}$), then the calculations *A dental X-ray* and *A gamma source in a storeroom* alone. The calculations *A dugout canoe found in a lake* and *Fusion of two deuterium nuclei* are homework for everyone. Fast students continue without hints and start them already in class; slower ones use the hints one at a time.
- **55–65 min, Check your understanding and exit ticket.** Alone: the concept and the calculation task and the exit ticket. The explanation task (fission and fusion) is homework, and the students assess it themselves with the criteria.
- **65–75 min, Problems.** Ten minutes is enough for the start of one long problem under exam conditions, without the model answer: recommended *Caesium in forest mushrooms* a–b (a calculation) or *The photoelectric effect and the Planck constant* a (a graph). The rest of the problem and the other five problems are homework, two or three at a time before the next lesson.

## Using the review test
The Predict questions are a diagnostic test, not a grade. Each question corresponds to one chapter:

- Question 1: the intensity does not change the photon energy (chapter *The photoelectric effect and the photon*).
- Question 2: an atom absorbs only a photon as large as the difference of levels (chapter *Energy levels of the hydrogen atom and the Bohr model*).
- Question 3: the threshold voltage of an LED and the colour of the light (chapter *Light from an LED and measuring the Planck constant*).
- Question 4: decay halves what is left (chapter *The decay law and half-life*).
- Question 5: the daughter nucleus of beta minus decay (chapter *The atomic nucleus and radioactive decay*).
- Question 6: the radon dose in the lungs (chapters *Radiation dose and radiation safety in Finland* and *Ionising radiation and the body*).

If more than a third of the group answers question 1 wrong (typical answer “bright light frees electrons”), show the energy-axis figure and ask how many red photons would be needed to free one electron if they could combine their energies. They cannot: one photon, one electron. The same idea returns in the answer to Reflect and in the long problem *The photoelectric effect and the Planck constant*. Mistakes in question 4 (“everything decays in two half-lives”) are taken up again in the halving figure and in the problem *A dugout canoe found in a lake*.

## Pair work
- Investigate: one student works out the types of radiation of sources A and B, the other calculates the half-value thickness of source C; they compare why the gamma radiation of C is read behind the aluminium.
- Apply multiple choice: each first chooses alone and only then justifies the choice to the partner. They answer only when the pair agrees.
- In the long problem *Radon at the housing company meeting* (a written one) pairs can write the answers together and assess them with another pair using the marking scheme.

## Fast and slow students
- **Fast students** do the Apply calculations without hints and go straight to the long problem *The photoelectric effect and the Planck constant* (graph and fit, $20$ points), which has the most steps. Extra question: how large would the stopping voltage be with $365\,\text{nm}$ light if the cathode were zinc, whose work function is $4.3\,\text{eV}$? (No current at all, because $3.4\,\text{eV} < 4.3\,\text{eV}$.)
- **Slow students** do the Apply calculations *A dental X-ray* and *A gamma source in a storeroom* with the hints, one hint at a time, and ask their partner before opening the solution. The *Review if needed* links of the theory summary lead straight to the original chapters, which have basic exercises.

## Assessment
The long problems are self-assessed with the marking scheme. The points follow the matriculation exam: the graph and fitting problem *The photoelectric effect and the Planck constant* is of the Part III type ($20$ points), the other five of the Part II type ($15$ points each), $95$ points in all. If the teacher assesses them, the same criteria work: choosing the right model and justifying its condition (for example one photon for one electron, a large set of nuclei or a point source), the symbolic solution, unit conversions by substitution (electronvolts into joules, years into seconds), accuracy and reasonableness; in a written part, justifying the claim with the name of the phenomenon and with numbers. All the data are invented examples, not real measurements.
