## Timing (75 minutes)

- **0–8 min, Think about it.** Read the hook aloud and ask the opening question; the review question on JET and JT-60.
- **8–13 min, Predict.** Both predictions individually, committed with a short reason. Do not discuss the answers yet.
- **13–40 min, Investigate.** Pairs: the timeline (era *From Geneva to ITER*) with notes, the text *Why one machine for the world* (prediction 1 opens), the site source and its question, the schedule section (prediction 2 opens). Then the pair discussion on the host country with the word bank.
- **40–52 min, Theory summary.** Members and organisation, challenges; the sorting task; back to the predictions.
- **52–65 min, Apply.** Put the story in order, find the errors, and the 60–80-word newsletter paragraph. The role-play is optional.
- **65–75 min, Check your understanding.** Three checks, the big-question answer, the summary and the French corner.

## Organisation and preparation
- Pairs share one device for the timeline; both keep their own notes. In the host discussion, one student argues for France and the EU, the other for the other Members; swap roles for the role-play if you use it.
- Check that the timeline loads; open the page from the web site, not as a local file.
- Optional: show ITER's site photographs or the live webcam on iter.org at the start.

## Assessment
- The newsletter paragraph (the short written task): two different reasons, one dated or numerical fact, 60–80 words, linking words, key terms used correctly.
- The big-question answer: one argument for, one against, a dated fact, plans marked as plans, a conclusion with a reason.
- Language: use of at least three word-bank terms in the pair discussion; sentence starters used to argue.
- Students self-assess with the criteria shown after they have written. For a grade, use the same criteria.

## Common difficulties
- Students treat ITER as French. Point to the cost shares (EU 45.6 %, the others 9.1 % each) and to in-kind contributions.
- Students repeat old dates (2025) from older videos and articles. Practise the habit: *which plan, made in which year?*
- "Member" means the seven parties (the EU is one Member), not the more than thirty countries.
- The timeline includes events from other machines; ask students to focus on the ITER cards first.

## Fast pairs and running out of time
- **Fast pairs:** the optional role-play *Moscow, June 2005*, or a comparison: find in the timeline when the EU, China, India and Korea joined, and explain why later members also wanted to join.
- **Short of time:** skip the order task and the role-play; do the newsletter paragraph as homework; keep the three checks and the big-question answer (the exit task of the lesson).

## Extension ideas
- Compare ITER with CERN's Large Hadron Collider as an international science project: membership, host country, how costs are shared.
- Follow ITER's news page for one month and report one milestone with its date.
- The facts in this unit were checked on 1 November 2026. Before teaching, check iter.org for a newer baseline or schedule.
