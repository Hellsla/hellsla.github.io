## fy2-3-L1-01
**Given:** energy $E = 1.80 \cdot 10^{5}\,\text{J}$, time $t = 90.0\,\text{s}$
**Find:** power $P$ in watts
**Principle:** Power is the quotient of the energy transformed and the time it takes. The kettle transforms energy steadily, so the whole energy and the whole time are enough.
**Formula:** $P = \dfrac{E}{t}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the energy is in joules and the time in seconds, so the result comes out in the unit $\text{J/s} = \text{W}$.
**Substitution:** $P = \dfrac{1.80 \cdot 10^{5}\,\text{J}}{90.0\,\text{s}} = 2000\,\text{J/s} = 2000\,\text{W}$
**Answer:** $2000\,\text{W}$ (that is $2.00 \cdot 10^{3}\,\text{W}$), three significant figures as in the given values.
**Reasonableness:** With rounded numbers $200\,000\,\text{J} : 100\,\text{s} = 2000\,\text{W}$. The typical power of a kettle is about $2\,\text{kW}$, so the result fits.

## fy2-3-L1-02
**Given:** power $P = 8.0\,\text{W}$, time $t = 5.0\,\text{s}$
**Find:** energy $E$ in joules
**Principle:** The lamp transforms energy at a steady power, so the energy is power times time.
**Formula:** $P = \dfrac{E}{t}$, from which $E = P\,t$
**Conversions:** Not needed: $1\,\text{W} = 1\,\text{J/s}$, so a watt times a second is a joule.
**Substitution:** $E = 8.0\,\text{W} \cdot 5.0\,\text{s} = 40\,\text{J}$
**Answer:** $40\,\text{J}$, two significant figures as in the given values.
**Reasonableness:** The lamp transforms $8\,\text{J}$ every second, and in five seconds five times as much, which is $40\,\text{J}$. The unit is the joule, a unit of energy.

## fy2-3-L1-03
**Given:** power $P = 1200\,\text{W}$, energy $E = 72\,000\,\text{J}$
**Find:** time $t$ in seconds
**Principle:** The oven transforms energy at a steady power, so the time is the energy divided by the power.
**Formula:** $P = \dfrac{E}{t}$, from which $t = \dfrac{E}{P}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: $\text{J} : \text{W} = \text{J} : (\text{J/s}) = \text{s}$.
**Substitution:** $t = \dfrac{72\,000\,\text{J}}{1200\,\text{W}} = 60\,\text{s}$
**Answer:** $60\,\text{s}$, two significant figures as in the energy $72\,000\,\text{J}$.
**Reasonableness:** The oven transforms $1200\,\text{J}$ in a second, and $60 \cdot 1200\,\text{J} = 72\,000\,\text{J}$. A minute is a typical heating time.

## fy2-3-L1-04
**Given:** power $P = 3.5\,\text{kW}$
**Find:** power in watts
**Principle:** The prefix kilo means a thousand, so a kilowatt is a thousand watts. The power does not change, only the unit does.
**Formula:** the prefix kilo is replaced by its value: $P = 3.5 \cdot 1000\,\text{W}$
**Conversions:** In place of the kilowatt we write its value in watts, $1000\,\text{W}$.
**Substitution:** $P = 3.5\,\text{kW} = 3.5 \cdot 1000\,\text{W} = 3500\,\text{W}$
**Answer:** $3500\,\text{W}$ (that is $3.5 \cdot 10^{3}\,\text{W}$), two significant figures as in the given value $3.5\,\text{kW}$.
**Reasonableness:** A watt is a thousand times smaller unit than a kilowatt, so the numerical value must be a thousand times greater: from 3.5 to 3500.

## fy2-3-L1-05
**Given:** power $P = 1.5\,\text{kW}$, time $t = 2.0\,\text{h}$
**Find:** energy $E$ in kilowatt-hours
**Principle:** The heater transforms energy at a steady power, so the energy is power times time. When the power is in kilowatts and the time in hours, the result comes out directly in kilowatt-hours.
**Formula:** $E = P\,t$
**Conversions:** Not needed: $\text{kW} \cdot \text{h} = \text{kWh}$. In joules the result would be $3.0 \cdot 1000\,\text{W} \cdot 3600\,\text{s} \approx 1.1 \cdot 10^{7}\,\text{J}$.
**Substitution:** $E = 1.5\,\text{kW} \cdot 2.0\,\text{h} = 3.0\,\text{kWh}$
**Answer:** $3.0\,\text{kWh}$, two significant figures as in the given values.
**Reasonableness:** In one hour $1.5\,\text{kWh}$ is used, so in two hours twice as much, $3.0\,\text{kWh}$. For a heater this is a normal amount.

## fy2-3-L2-01
**Given:** power $P = 6.0\,\text{kW}$, time $t = 150\,\text{min}$, price $0.20\,\text{€/kWh}$
**Find:** cost in euros
**Principle:** The sauna heater transforms energy at a steady power. First we calculate the energy consumed in kilowatt-hours, then its cost.
**Formula:** $E = P\,t$ and $\text{cost} = E \cdot \text{unit price}$
**Conversions:** The power is already in kilowatts. Time: $1\,\text{min} = 1\,\text{h} : 60$, so $150\,\text{min} = 150 \cdot (1\,\text{h} : 60) = 2.5\,\text{h}$. Price $20\,\text{c/kWh} = 0.20\,\text{€/kWh}$.
**Substitution:** $E = 6.0\,\text{kW} \cdot 2.5\,\text{h} = 15\,\text{kWh}$ and $\text{cost} = 15\,\text{kWh} \cdot 0.20\,\text{€/kWh} = 3.0\,\text{€}$
**Answer:** $3.0\,\text{€}$, two significant figures as in the power $6.0\,\text{kW}$.
**Reasonableness:** With rounded numbers $6\,\text{kW} \cdot 2.5\,\text{h} \approx 15\,\text{kWh}$, which costs about $15 \cdot 0.20\,\text{€} = 3.0\,\text{€}$. The cost of a sauna session is of the order of a few euros.

## fy2-3-L2-02
**Given:** energy of the battery $E = 15\,\text{Wh}$
**Find:** energy $E$ in joules
**Principle:** A watt-hour is power times time, so it is a unit of energy. The unit is converted by substituting $1\,\text{W} = 1\,\text{J/s}$ and $1\,\text{h} = 3600\,\text{s}$.
**Formula:** $E = P\,t$
**Conversions:** $1\,\text{Wh} = 1\,\text{W} \cdot 3600\,\text{s} = 3600\,\text{J}$.
**Substitution:** $E = 15\,\text{Wh} = 15 \cdot 3600\,\text{J} = 54\,000\,\text{J}$
**Answer:** $54\,000\,\text{J}$ (that is $5.4 \cdot 10^{4}\,\text{J}$), two significant figures as in the given value $15\,\text{Wh}$.
**Reasonableness:** A joule is a very small unit and a watt-hour a large one, so the numerical value must grow. If the battery is charged full in an hour, the charging power is $54\,000\,\text{J} : 3600\,\text{s} = 15\,\text{W}$, which is a typical charging power. In use a phone consumes about $2\,\text{W}$, so the battery lasts about $15\,\text{Wh} : 2\,\text{W} = 7.5\,\text{h}$.

## fy2-3-L2-03
**Given:** energy $E = 0.40\,\text{kWh}$, time $t = 20\,\text{min}$
**Find:** average power $P$ in watts
**Principle:** Power is energy divided by time. The units must be joules and seconds for the result to be in watts.
**Formula:** $P = \dfrac{E}{t}$
**Conversions:** $0.40\,\text{kWh} = 0.40 \cdot 1000\,\text{W} \cdot 3600\,\text{s} = 1\,440\,000\,\text{J}$ and $20\,\text{min} = 20 \cdot 60\,\text{s} = 1200\,\text{s}$.
**Substitution:** $P = \dfrac{1\,440\,000\,\text{J}}{1200\,\text{s}} = 1200\,\text{J/s} = 1200\,\text{W}$
**Answer:** $1200\,\text{W}$ (that is $1.2 \cdot 10^{3}\,\text{W}$), two significant figures as in the given values.
**Reasonableness:** In a third of an hour $0.40\,\text{kWh}$ is used, so in an hour $3 \cdot 0.40\,\text{kWh} = 1.2\,\text{kWh}$ would be used, which means the power is $1.2\,\text{kW}$. This is a typical power for a small household appliance.

## fy2-3-L2-04
**Given:** six lights, power of each $1.5\,\text{W}$, time $8760\,\text{h}$
**Find:** total energy $E$ in kilowatt-hours
**Principle:** The lights use a steady power, so the total power is the sum of the individual powers, and the energy is the total power times time.
**Formula:** $P_\text{tot} = 6 \cdot P$ and $E = P_\text{tot}\,t$
**Conversions:** $6 \cdot 1.5\,\text{W} = 9.0\,\text{W}$, and substituting $1\,\text{W} = 10^{-3}\,\text{kW}$ gives $9.0\,\text{W} = 9.0 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{kW} = 0.0090\,\text{kW}$.
**Substitution:** $E = 0.0090\,\text{kW} \cdot 8760\,\text{h} = 78.84\,\text{kWh}$
**Answer:** $79\,\text{kWh}$, two significant figures as in the power $1.5\,\text{W}$.
**Reasonableness:** Nine watts consume $9\,\text{W} \cdot 24\,\text{h} = 216\,\text{Wh} \approx 0.22\,\text{kWh}$ in a day, and in $365\,\text{days}$ about $80\,\text{kWh}$. The cost would be about $16\,\text{€}$ a year, so a small power costs money when it lasts the whole year.

## fy2-3-L3-01
**Given:** power of the incandescent bulb $60\,\text{W}$, of the LED $8.0\,\text{W}$, usage time $4.0\,\text{h}$ a day for $365\,\text{days}$, price $0.20\,\text{€/kWh}$
**Find:** the cost saved in a year in euros
**Principle:** The saving is the difference between the energy consumption of the two lamps times the price. Both have a steady power, so the energy is power times time.
**Formula:** $\Delta E = (P_1 - P_2)\,t$ and $\text{saving} = \Delta E \cdot \text{unit price}$
**Conversions:** $P_1 - P_2 = 60\,\text{W} - 8.0\,\text{W} = 52\,\text{W} = 52 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{kW} = 0.052\,\text{kW}$ and $t = 4.0\,\text{h} \cdot 365 = 1460\,\text{h}$.
**Substitution:** $\Delta E = 0.052\,\text{kW} \cdot 1460\,\text{h} = 75.92\,\text{kWh}$ and $\text{saving} = 75.92\,\text{kWh} \cdot 0.20\,\text{€/kWh} = 15.18\,\text{€}$
**Answer:** $15\,\text{€}$, two significant figures as in the power $8.0\,\text{W}$ and the time $4.0\,\text{h}$.
**Reasonableness:** With rounded numbers $0.05\,\text{kW} \cdot 1500\,\text{h} = 75\,\text{kWh}$, and $75 \cdot 0.20\,\text{€} = 15\,\text{€}$. An LED lamp costs a few euros in a shop, so a saving of about $15\,\text{€}$ a year pays back the lamp in less than a year. This is believable for a single lamp.

## fy2-3-L3-02
**Given:** energy $E = 3.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\text{kWh}$, time $t = 8760\,\text{h}$
**Find:** average power $P$ in watts
**Principle:** Average power is the energy consumed divided by time. Not all appliances are on at the same time, so the average power is small compared with the sum of the rated powers of the appliances.
**Formula:** $P = \dfrac{E}{t}$
**Conversions:** Kilowatt-hours and hours give the power in kilowatts, so finally $1\,\text{kW} = 1000\,\text{W}$.
**Substitution:** $P = \dfrac{3.0 \cdot 10^{3}\,\text{kWh}}{8760\,\text{h}} = 0.3425\,\text{kW} = 0.3425 \cdot 1000\,\text{W} = 342.5\,\text{W}$
**Answer:** $340\,\text{W}$ (that is $3.4 \cdot 10^{2}\,\text{W}$), two significant figures as in the energy.
**Reasonableness:** If a kettle ($2000\,\text{W}$) were on all the time, the average power would be $2000\,\text{W}$; the $340\,\text{W}$ of a household corresponds to about four televisions ($4 \cdot 80\,\text{W} = 320\,\text{W}$) running continuously. Consumption builds up from short moments of high power and long moments of low power, so the average power is small compared with the sum of the rated powers of the appliances.

## fy2-3-L3-03
**Given:** consumption $18\,\text{kWh}$ per hundred kilometres, charging at a power of $2.3\,\text{kW}$ for a time of $8.0\,\text{h}$, losses are ignored
**Find:** distance $s$ in kilometres
**Principle:** First we calculate the charging energy at a steady power, $E = P\,t$. Then we ask how many hundred-kilometre distances the energy is enough for when each consumes $18\,\text{kWh}$.
**Formula:** $E = P\,t$ and $s = \dfrac{E}{k} \cdot 100\,\text{km}$, where $k = 18\,\text{kWh}$ is the consumption per hundred kilometres
**Conversions:** Not needed: the power is in kilowatts and the time in hours, so the energy is in kilowatt-hours.
**Substitution:** $E = 2.3\,\text{kW} \cdot 8.0\,\text{h} = 18.4\,\text{kWh}$ and $s = \dfrac{18.4\,\text{kWh}}{18\,\text{kWh}} \cdot 100\,\text{km} = 102.2\,\text{km}$
**Answer:** $100\,\text{km}$ (that is $1.0 \cdot 10^{2}\,\text{km}$), two significant figures as in the power $2.3\,\text{kW}$.
**Reasonableness:** The charging energy $18.4\,\text{kWh}$ is slightly more than the consumption of one hundred kilometres, $18\,\text{kWh}$, so the distance is slightly over $100\,\text{km}$. Charging overnight from a wall socket therefore gives about a day's driving distance.
