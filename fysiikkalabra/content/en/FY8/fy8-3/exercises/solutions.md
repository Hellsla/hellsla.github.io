## fy8-3-L1-01
**Given:** energy levels of the hydrogen atom $E_n = -\tfrac{13.6\,\text{eV}}{n^2}$, level $n = 4$
**Asked:** the energy $E_4$ of level $n = 4$ in electron volts, with its sign
**Principle:** The energy of a hydrogen atom is quantised, and the energy of level $n$ is obtained from the given expression. The zero of energy is a free electron, so the energy of a bound electron is negative.
**Formula:** $E_n = -\dfrac{13.6\,\text{eV}}{n^2}$, so $E_4 = -\dfrac{13.6\,\text{eV}}{4^2}$
**Conversions:** Not needed, because the answer is asked in electron volts, as in the expression.
**Substitution:** $E_4 = -\dfrac{13.6\,\text{eV}}{16} = -0.850\,\text{eV}$
**Answer:** $-0.850\,\text{eV}$, three significant figures, as in the ground-state energy $13.6\,\text{eV}$.
**Reasonableness:** The value is negative and much closer to zero than the ground-state value $-13.6\,\text{eV}$, as it should be: the upper levels are close to zero and crowd together. On level $n = 2$ the energy is $-3.40\,\text{eV}$, so $-0.850\,\text{eV}$ is a quarter of that, because $4^2 = 16$ is four times $2^2 = 4$.

## fy8-3-L1-02
**Given:** energy levels of the hydrogen atom $E_n = -\tfrac{13.6\,\text{eV}}{n^2}$, upper level $n = 4$, lower level $n = 1$
**Asked:** the energy $hf$ of the photon in electron volts
**Principle:** Energy is conserved: when the electron moves to a lower level, the energy of the atom decreases by the difference between the levels, and one photon carries exactly this energy away.
**Formula:** $hf = E_\text{u} - E_\text{l}$, so $hf = E_4 - E_1$
**Conversions:** Not needed, because the energies of the levels and the answer are in electron volts.
**Substitution:** $hf = -\dfrac{13.6\,\text{eV}}{16} - \left(-\dfrac{13.6\,\text{eV}}{1}\right) = -0.850\,\text{eV} + 13.6\,\text{eV} = 12.75\,\text{eV}$
**Answer:** $12.8\,\text{eV}$, three significant figures; the result is rounded to one decimal place, as the value $13.6\,\text{eV}$.
**Reasonableness:** The energy is slightly smaller than the ionisation energy $13.6\,\text{eV}$, because level $n = 4$ is close to zero. The energy of the photon is greater than that of visible-light photons, $1.7$–$3.3\,\text{eV}$, so the photon is ultraviolet, like all transitions ending in the ground state.

## fy8-3-L1-03
**Given:** wavelength $\lambda = 486\,\text{nm}$, $h = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s}$, $c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$, $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$
**Asked:** the energy $E$ of the photon in electron volts
**Principle:** Light consists of photons, and the energy of one photon depends only on the wavelength of the light: the shorter the wavelength, the greater the energy.
**Formula:** $E = hf = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$
**Conversions:** $\lambda = 486\,\text{nm} = 486 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}$. Finally the joules are converted to electron volts by dividing by $1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$, because $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$.
**Substitution:** The product $hc = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s} \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s} = 1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}$. $E = \dfrac{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}}{486 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}} = 4.0874 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J} = \dfrac{4.0874 \cdot 10^{-19}}{1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}}\,\text{eV} = 2.5514\,\text{eV}$
**Answer:** $2.55\,\text{eV}$, three significant figures, as in the wavelength $486\,\text{nm}$.
**Reasonableness:** The energies of visible-light photons are about $1.7$–$3.3\,\text{eV}$, and cyan light is in the middle of the spectrum. The result is also the difference between the hydrogen levels $n = 4$ and $n = 2$: $-0.850\,\text{eV} + 3.40\,\text{eV} = 2.55\,\text{eV}$.

## fy8-3-L1-04

**Asked:** the energy $E_\text{ion}$ needed to free the electron, in electron volts
**Principle:** The zero of energy is a free electron at rest. Freeing the electron from level $n = 3$ raises the energy of the atom from $E_3$ to zero, so it requires the energy difference $0 - E_3$.
**Formula:** $E_\text{ion} = 0 - E_3 = \dfrac{13.6\,\text{eV}}{3^2}$
**Conversions:** Not needed, because the energies are in electron volts.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{ion} = 0 - \left(-\dfrac{13.6\,\text{eV}}{9}\right) = 1.5111\,\text{eV}$
**Answer:** $1.51\,\text{eV}$, three significant figures, as in the value $13.6\,\text{eV}$.
**Reasonableness:** An electron comes free from an excited state much more easily than from the ground state, where $13.6\,\text{eV}$ is needed: on level $n = 3$ the energy is only a ninth of the ground-state energy. A red-light photon with an energy of about $1.9\,\text{eV}$ is already enough to free the electron from this state.

## fy8-3-L1-05
**Given:** energy of the photon $E = 1.89\,\text{eV}$, $h = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s}$, $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$
**Asked:** the frequency $f$ of the photon in terahertz
**Principle:** The energy of a photon is proportional to its frequency, and the constant of proportionality is the Planck constant.
**Formula:** $E = hf$, so $f = \dfrac{E}{h}$
**Conversions:** $E = 1.89\,\text{eV} = 1.89 \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J} = 3.0278 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$. Finally $1\,\text{THz} = 10^{12}\,\text{Hz}$.
**Substitution:** $f = \dfrac{3.0278 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}}{6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s}} = 4.5695 \cdot 10^{14}\,\text{Hz} = 456.95\,\text{THz}$
**Answer:** $457\,\text{THz}$, that is $4.57 \cdot 10^{14}\,\text{Hz}$, three significant figures, as in the energy $1.89\,\text{eV}$.
**Reasonableness:** The frequencies of visible light are about $400$–$790\,\text{THz}$, and $457\,\text{THz}$ is near the red end. Check with the wavelength: $\lambda = \tfrac{c}{f} = \tfrac{2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}}{4.5695 \cdot 10^{14}\,\text{Hz}} \approx 656\,\text{nm}$, which is the red line of hydrogen. Unit: $\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{J}\,\text{s}} = \tfrac{1}{\text{s}} = \text{Hz}$.

## fy8-3-L2-01
**Given:** energy levels of the hydrogen atom $E_n = -\tfrac{13.6\,\text{eV}}{n^2}$, transition from level $n = 5$ to level $n = 2$, $h = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s}$, $c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$
**Asked:** the wavelength $\lambda$ of the light in nanometres
**Principle:** Energy is conserved: the energy of the atom decreases by the difference between the levels, and one photon, with energy $\tfrac{hc}{\lambda}$, carries it away.
**Formula:** $\dfrac{hc}{\lambda} = E_5 - E_2$, so $\lambda = \dfrac{hc}{E_5 - E_2}$
**Conversions:** The energy difference is calculated in electron volts and converted to joules by substituting $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$. Finally $1\,\text{m} = 10^{9}\,\text{nm}$.
**Substitution:** The product $hc = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s} \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s} = 1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}$. $E_5 - E_2 = -\dfrac{13.6\,\text{eV}}{25} + \dfrac{13.6\,\text{eV}}{4} = 2.856\,\text{eV} = 2.856 \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J} = 4.5753 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$ and $\lambda = \dfrac{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}}{4.5753 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}} = 4.3417 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\text{m} = 434.17\,\text{nm}$
**Answer:** $434\,\text{nm}$, three significant figures, as in the value $13.6\,\text{eV}$.
**Reasonableness:** The transition ends on level $n = 2$, so the line belongs to the Balmer series and is visible light. It is shorter than the line $486\,\text{nm}$ of the transition $4 \to 2$, because the energy difference is larger, and the hydrogen spectrum has a blue line exactly at $434\,\text{nm}$.

## fy8-3-L2-02
**Given:** wavelength $\lambda = 410\,\text{nm}$, lower level $n_\text{l} = 2$, $E_n = -\tfrac{13.6\,\text{eV}}{n^2}$, $h = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s}$, $c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$, $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$
**Asked:** the number $n$ of the upper level
**Principle:** The energy of a photon is the difference between levels. When the energy of the photon and the energy of the lower level are known, the energy of the upper level is their sum, and from it the number of the level.
**Formula:** $\dfrac{hc}{\lambda} = E_n - E_2$, so $E_n = E_2 + \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$, and solving from the expression $E_n = -\dfrac{13.6\,\text{eV}}{n^2}$ gives $n = \sqrt{\dfrac{13.6\,\text{eV}}{-E_n}}$
**Conversions:** $\lambda = 410 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}$; the energy of the photon is converted to electron volts by dividing by $1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$.
**Substitution:** The product $hc = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s} \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s} = 1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}$. $\dfrac{hc}{\lambda} = \dfrac{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}}{410 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}} = 4.8451 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J} = 3.0244\,\text{eV}$, $E_n = -3.40\,\text{eV} + 3.0244\,\text{eV} = -0.3756\,\text{eV}$ and $n = \sqrt{\dfrac{13.6\,\text{eV}}{0.3756\,\text{eV}}} = \sqrt{36.21} = 6.017$
**Answer:** $n = 6$. The number of a level is an integer, and the calculated $6.02$ differs from it only by rounding.
**Reasonableness:** Check in the other direction: $E_6 - E_2 = -0.378\,\text{eV} + 3.40\,\text{eV} = 3.02\,\text{eV}$, which corresponds to the wavelength $410\,\text{nm}$. The violet line is the shortest of the visible Balmer lines, so its upper level must be the highest of these four.

## fy8-3-L2-04
**Given:** wavelength of the photon $\lambda = 80.0\,\text{nm}$, ionisation energy of hydrogen from the ground state $E_\text{ion} = 13.6\,\text{eV}$, $h = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s}$, $c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$, $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$
**Asked:** the kinetic energy $E_\text{k}$ of the freed electron in electron volts
**Principle:** Energy is conserved. Of the energy of the photon, the ionisation energy is used to free the electron, and the rest stays as the kinetic energy of the free electron. **Conversions:** $\lambda = 80.0 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}$; the energy of the photon is converted to electron volts by dividing by $1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$.

**Answer:** $1.9\,\text{eV}$. The difference is given to one decimal place, like the ionisation energy $13.6\,\text{eV}$, so the result has two significant figures.

Check: the wavelength $80.0\,\text{nm}$ is shorter than the limit of the Lyman series, $91.2\,\text{nm}$, so the photon really can ionise an atom in the ground state.

## fy8-3-L3-01
**Given:** kinetic energy of the colliding electrons $12.5\,\text{eV}$, atoms in the ground state $n = 1$, $E_n = -\tfrac{13.6\,\text{eV}}{n^2}$
**Asked:** the number of different wavelengths that the excited atoms can emit
**Principle:** In a collision an atom can take only the energy that takes it to some higher level, so we find which levels are at most $12.5\,\text{eV}$ from the ground state. We assume that each atom receives energy from one collision while in the ground state; an electron hitting an already excited atom again could raise it higher, but that is not considered here. From an excited state the atom can return directly or through intermediate levels, and each different pair of levels gives its own wavelength.
**Formula:** excitation energy $E_n - E_1 = 13.6\,\text{eV} - \dfrac{13.6\,\text{eV}}{n^2}$; a level can be reached if $E_n - E_1 \leq 12.5\,\text{eV}$
**Conversions:** Not needed, because all energies are in electron volts.
**Substitution:** $E_2 - E_1 = 13.6\,\text{eV} - 3.40\,\text{eV} = 10.2\,\text{eV}$, $E_3 - E_1 = 13.6\,\text{eV} - 1.511\,\text{eV} = 12.09\,\text{eV}$ and $E_4 - E_1 = 13.6\,\text{eV} - 0.850\,\text{eV} = 12.75\,\text{eV}$. Levels $2$ and $3$ can be reached, level $4$ cannot, because $12.75\,\text{eV} > 12.5\,\text{eV}$. The possible transitions are $3 \to 1$, $3 \to 2$ and $2 \to 1$.
**Answer:** $3$ different wavelengths: $3 \to 1$ ($102\,\text{nm}$), $2 \to 1$ ($122\,\text{nm}$) and $3 \to 2$ ($656\,\text{nm}$).
**Reasonableness:** The gas would glow in visible light only red, because only the transition $3 \to 2$ ends on level $2$; the other two are ultraviolet. If the energy of the electrons were at least $12.75\,\text{eV}$, level $4$ and the cyan line would also come in.

## fy8-3-L2-05
**Given:** the four Balmer lines of the table ($n = 3, 4, 5, 6$ and $\lambda = 656.7$; $486.1$; $434.5$; $410.2\,\text{nm}$), $E_n = -\tfrac{E_\text{ion}}{n^2}$, $c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$, $h = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s}$, $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$
**Asked:** the ionisation energy $E_\text{ion}$ of hydrogen in electron volts
**Principle:** The energy of the photon is the difference between the levels, $hf = E_n - E_2 = \tfrac{E_\text{ion}}{4} - \tfrac{E_\text{ion}}{n^2}$. The frequency is therefore a linear function of $\tfrac{1}{n^2}$, $f = \tfrac{E_\text{ion}}{4h} - \tfrac{E_\text{ion}}{h} \cdot \tfrac{1}{n^2}$, and the slope of the line is $-\tfrac{E_\text{ion}}{h}$. Because of measurement uncertainty the points do not usually lie exactly on a straight line, so a line is fitted and the slope is read from the line, not from two neighbouring points.
**Reading the graph:** The frequencies $f = \tfrac{c}{\lambda}$ are $456.5$; $616.7$; $690.0$ and $730.9\,\text{THz}$ at $\tfrac{1}{n^2} = 0.111$; $0.0625$; $0.0400$ and $0.0278$. The line fitted to the points passes, within the measured range, through the points $(0.0300;\ 723.3\,\text{THz})$ and $(0.1100;\ 460.2\,\text{THz})$, so the slope is $k = \dfrac{460.2\,\text{THz} - 723.3\,\text{THz}}{0.1100 - 0.0300} = -3289\,\text{THz}$.
**Formula:** $k = -\dfrac{E_\text{ion}}{h}$, so $E_\text{ion} = -h k$

The constant term of the line, $822\,\text{THz}$, corresponds to the energy $h \cdot 822\,\text{THz} = 3.40\,\text{eV}$, which is $\tfrac{13.6\,\text{eV}}{4}$, and the corresponding wavelength $\tfrac{c}{822\,\text{THz}} \approx 365\,\text{nm}$ is the limit of the Balmer series.
