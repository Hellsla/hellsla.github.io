## fy8-2-L1-01
**Given:** wavelength of the helium line $\lambda = 502\,\text{nm}$, $h = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s}$, $c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$, $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$
**Find:** the energy difference $\Delta E = E_\text{u} - E_\text{l}$ in electronvolts
**Principle:** An atom emits a photon when it moves from a higher energy state to a lower one. Energy is conserved, so the energy $hf$ of the photon is the difference between the energies of the states, and the frequency is obtained from the wavelength with the equation $c = f\lambda$.
**Formula:** $\Delta E = hf$ and $f = \tfrac{c}{\lambda}$, so $\Delta E = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$
**Conversions:** $502\,\text{nm} = 502 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}$. The result in joules is converted to electronvolts by dividing by $1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{eV}}$.
**Substitution:** $hc = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s} \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s} = 1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}$ and $\Delta E = \dfrac{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}}{502 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}} = 3.9571 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J} = \dfrac{3.9571 \cdot 10^{-19}}{1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}}\,\text{eV} = 2.4701\,\text{eV}$
**Answer:** $2.47\,\text{eV}$, three significant figures like the wavelength $502\,\text{nm}$.
**Reasonableness:** With the estimate $hc \approx 1240\,\text{eV}\,\text{nm}$ we get $\tfrac{1240}{502}\,\text{eV} \approx 2.5\,\text{eV}$. The energy of a photon of green light is between those of red ($1.9\,\text{eV}$) and violet ($3.0\,\text{eV}$), as it should be.

**Principle:** An atom of a gas can absorb only a photon whose energy is exactly the difference between two energy states. **Reasonableness:** The energy is greater than $-3.40\,\text{eV}$ and less than zero, where the electron would be freed, as is right in absorption. It is the energy of the state $n = 4$ of hydrogen, because $-13.6\,\text{eV} : 16 = -0.85\,\text{eV}$.

## fy8-2-L2-07
**Given:** the lines of mercury and the readings of the scale from the table, the reading of the unknown line $x = 16.4\,\text{mm}$
**Find:** a) the spectra of the sources and how to tell them apart, b) the wavelength $\lambda$ of the unknown line in nanometres
**Principle:** An incandescent lamp emits a continuous spectrum, that is, an unbroken band of colour without separate lines, whereas the thin gas of a gas discharge tube emits a line spectrum: separate bright lines on a dark background. The scale of the spectroscope is almost linear: the deviations of the fitted line from the data points are at most $0.8\,\text{nm}$, which is small compared with the measuring range of $173\,\text{nm}$. The wavelength is therefore a linear function of the reading of the scale, $\lambda = kx + b$. The known lines of mercury calibrate the scale, and the unknown line is read from the fitted line.
**Reading the graph:** The points $(x, \lambda)$ of the graph are the four lines of mercury: $(8.7\,\text{mm}; 404.7\,\text{nm})$ and $(23.1\,\text{mm}; 577.0\,\text{nm})$ bound the measuring range. ## fy8-2-L3-04

**Find:** a) the energies of the photons of the lines, b) the energies of the excited states and the energy level diagram, c) the wavelength $\lambda_6$ of the sixth line in nanometres
**Principle:** Each line is the difference between two states, $\tfrac{hc}{\lambda}$. The energies of the excited states above the ground state are $E_1 < E_2 < E_3$, and the six lines are $E_1$, $E_2$, $E_3$, $E_2 - E_1$, $E_3 - E_2$ and $E_3 - E_1$. The shortest wavelength is the greatest energy, $E_3$. The sixth line is the transition $E_1 \to 0$, that is, the energy $E_1$, so it is not among the measured ones. The pairs $E_1$ and $E_3 - E_1$, and $E_2$ and $E_3 - E_2$, each have the sum $E_3$; because $E_1$ is missing, its pair $E_3 - E_1$ has been measured without its pair, and the other pair has been measured completely.
**Formula:** $E = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$ and $E_1 = E_3 - (E_3 - E_1)$, so $\lambda_6 = \dfrac{hc}{E_1}$

## fy8-2-L3-05
**Given:** absorbed light $\lambda_\text{abs}$ and fluorescent light $\lambda_\text{fl}$: A $365\,\text{nm}$ and $450\,\text{nm}$, B $405\,\text{nm}$ and $520\,\text{nm}$, C $450\,\text{nm}$ and $530\,\text{nm}$, $h = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s}$, $c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$, $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$
**Find:** the share $p$ of the energy converted to heat of the energy of the absorbed photon, in per cent, the greatest of the three
**Principle:** The dye absorbs a photon and emits one fluorescence photon. Energy is conserved, so the energy of the absorbed photon is the sum of the energy of the emitted photon and the energy $Q$ converted to heat. The fluorescence photon has a longer wavelength, so its energy is smaller.
**Formula:** $E = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$, $Q = E_\text{abs} - E_\text{fl}$ and $p = \dfrac{Q}{E_\text{abs}} = 1 - \dfrac{\lambda_\text{abs}}{\lambda_\text{fl}}$
**Conversions:** $1\,\text{nm} = 10^{-9}\,\text{m}$; joules are converted to electronvolts by dividing by $1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{eV}}$. In the share $p$ the constants $h$ and $c$ cancel.
**Substitution:** The energies of dye B are $E_\text{abs} = \dfrac{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}}{405 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}} = 3.0617\,\text{eV}$ and $E_\text{fl} = \dfrac{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}}{520 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}} = 2.3846\,\text{eV}$, so $Q = 0.6771\,\text{eV}$ and $p = \dfrac{0.6771\,\text{eV}}{3.0617\,\text{eV}} = 0.2211 = 22.11\,\%$. In part d) $E_\text{abs} = \dfrac{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}}{400 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}} = 3.1000\,\text{eV}$, $E_\text{fl} = 3.1000\,\text{eV} - 0.50\,\text{eV} = 2.6000\,\text{eV}$ and $\lambda_\text{fl} = \dfrac{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}}{2.6000 \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}} = 4.769 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\text{m} = 477\,\text{nm}$. For dyes A and C the corresponding energies are $3.397\,\text{eV}$ and $2.756\,\text{eV}$, and $2.756\,\text{eV}$ and $2.340\,\text{eV}$, and the shares are $1 - \tfrac{365}{450} = 0.189$ and $1 - \tfrac{450}{530} = 0.151$.
**Answer:** $22.1\,\%$ in dye B, three significant figures like the wavelengths. In the others the share is smaller. The wavelength of the fluorescent light of dye D is $477\,\text{nm}$ (part d).
**Reasonableness:** The estimate $1 - \tfrac{405}{520} \approx 1 - 0.78 = 0.22$ gives the same result. The energy $2.38\,\text{eV}$ of the fluorescence photon is smaller than the $3.06\,\text{eV}$ of the absorbed one, as it should be for light of a longer wavelength.
**Model answer:** The molecule that has absorbed a photon is in an excited state. Before the photon is emitted, part of its energy is converted to the energy of the thermal motion of the molecule and its surroundings, so the energy of the emitted photon is smaller than that of the absorbed one.
