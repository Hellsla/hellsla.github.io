Quick start

This induction is gone through before the first flight. The guide is a draft: the school's responsible staff approve Dronelabra's safety instructions before the course starts, and the teacher's instructions always take priority over this page.

Every flight has three roles:

  1. The pilot controls the drone and is responsible for the flight.
  2. The observer stands next to the pilot and watches the drone and the flight area with their own eyes the whole time.
  3. The safety officer goes through the checklist, keeps spectators behind the safety line and gives permission for take-off.

The shared commands, which are shouted, are Take-off, Stop and Land. Anyone may shout Stop, and then the pilot stops the drone at once.

Try

Practise the roles and the commands with your group without a drone. Show how each of the two drones is stopped in an emergency.

Modify

Make your group's own checklist based on this guide and test it on another group.

Challenge yourself

Find out from Traficom's website what flying outdoors would require of the school's drones, and record three rules with their sources.

This part goes through the work thoroughly: why it works, the exact steps, measurements and solving problems.

Challenge

A drone is small, but its propellers spin fast and its battery holds a lot of energy in a small space. A safe flight does not come from the pilot's skill but from the whole group acting in the agreed way every time. The goal is that you can act in each of the three roles.

Flying indoors is not automatically safe. Aviation rules do not apply to indoor spaces in the same way as to flying outdoors, but the responsibility for people and for the space remains, and a cramped or dim space makes flying harder.

Learning goals

After the induction you can

  • do the pre-flight inspection with the help of a checklist
  • mark out the flight area and act as pilot, observer and safety officer
  • stop each of the two drones in an emergency
  • handle and charge batteries safely
  • tell what filming and flying outdoors require.

Starting level and prior skills

No earlier experience of drones is needed. Ahjo's general instructions are on the page Safety.

Equipment and materials

Safety and induction

See also the topics on Ahjo's safety page: propellers and motors, batteries, flight area and roles, emergency stop, FPV flight, rules and filming. They are in preparation until the school has approved them.

Propellers

  • The Air:bit's propellers are fitted only when the assembly and programming are done and starting and stopping the motors has been tried without propellers.
  • Propellers are fitted and removed only with the battery disconnected.
  • Do not touch spinning propellers. Keep your distance when the drone is started and when it is flying.
  • Long hair is tied back, and hanging cords and jewellery are taken off.
  • Use propeller guards whenever the drone has them.

Batteries

  • A battery is charged only under supervision, according to the manufacturer's instructions and on a non-flammable surface.
  • Do not use a battery that is swollen, dented or punctured or that has been in a hard crash. Take it to the teacher.
  • Let the battery cool down after a flight before charging.
  • Always disconnect the Air:bit's battery when the drone is not in use.
  • If a battery gets hot, smokes or smells: tell the teacher at once and move further away. The teacher acts according to the school's instructions.

Flight area

  • The flight area is marked out with tape or cones. Spectators stay behind the safety line.
  • One drone at a time flies in the flight area, unless the teacher has decided otherwise.
  • Flying is done low. With the Air:bit the hovering height is $10$–$30\,\text{cm}$ above a soft surface.
  • Windows and doors are kept closed so that the drone cannot get outside.
  • Nobody flies over people or towards people.

Emergency stop

Drone How you stop it
Air:bit 2 Shake the controller: the motors stop and the drone falls. That is why flying is done low.
Camera drone with the motion controller Press the lock button once: the drone brakes and stays hovering. Hold the button down while hovering: the drone lands.
Camera drone, real emergency Four presses of the lock button stop the motors in the air, and the drone falls. Used only if the drone has crashed or does not obey.

After a crash the drone is inspected before the next flight, and the damage is reported to the teacher.

FPV flight

  • With the goggles on, the pilot cannot see around them. That is why the observer stands next to them and keeps the drone in sight the whole time without any aids.
  • The pilot sits or stands still in a clear space, far from stairs and windows.
  • Flying is done only in Normal mode.
  • Stop at once if you feel dizzy or unwell. Take breaks.
  • Tell the teacher beforehand if you have an illness or another reason why using the goggles does not suit you. You can also do all the tasks without the goggles.
  • The goggles are cleaned between users.

Rules and filming

  • Flying a camera drone outdoors requires that the drone's user (the UAS operator) has registered with Traficom and that the registration number is marked on the drone. The UAS operator must be an adult or an organisation. Outdoors, flying is done at a height of at most $120\,\text{m}$, the drone is kept in sight, and nobody flies over crowds of people. Airspace restrictions are checked before the flight.
  • When flying with the goggles, there must be an observer next to the pilot who sees the drone directly.
  • In Dronelabra the Air:bit 2 is flown only indoors.
  • The drone's camera is used to film only the flight area and the drone's own flight. Nobody is filmed without permission, and recordings are not published.

Steps

  1. Read this guide and watch the teacher's demonstration flight from behind the safety line.
  2. Draw the flight area with your group: the take-off point, the safety line, the pilot's and the observer's place and the spectators' place.
  3. Make your group's checklist based on the model below.
  4. Practise the roles and the commands without a drone. Swap roles so that everyone is in each role once.
  5. Show the teacher how each of the two drones is stopped.
  6. The teacher signs off the induction. Only after that does the group get a flight turn.

Test and measure

The group is ready when every member can, without paper,

  • say the three roles and their tasks
  • show the emergency stop on each of the two drones
  • tell what is done if a battery is swollen or the drone crashes.

Time it: how fast does the group go through the checklist carefully? Record the time and which item was most easily forgotten.

If something does not work

  • The drone does not obey the controller: stop it with the emergency stop. Do not try to catch a flying drone by hand.
  • The drone crashed: disconnect the battery or switch the drone off, inspect the propellers and the frame and tell the teacher.
  • Someone came into the flight area: anyone shouts Stop, and the pilot stops the drone.
  • The battery is warm or swollen: do not charge it and do not use it. Take it to the teacher.
  • You feel dizzy in the goggles: say Stop, stop the drone, take the goggles off and sit down.

Extension

Find out from Traficom's Unmanned aviation pages and from the airspace map service whether there are UAS geographical zones near the school where flying is restricted. Record where you found the information and on which day you checked it.

Document your work

Attach to your project diary

  • the group's checklist
  • the drawing of the flight area and the roles
  • the day on which the teacher signed off the induction.

For the teacher: preparation

This guide is a draft, and it does not replace the school's own risk assessment. The information was checked on 12 November 2026 from the manufacturers' user and safety instructions and from the Traficom and EASA websites. Check it again before the course starts.

  • Indoor spaces. The EU drone regulation does not apply to flying indoors when the drone cannot get outside. The responsibility for people, the space and damage still remains. Agree on the use of the space with the principal and the property management, and check whether the school's liability insurance covers flying.
  • Camera drones outdoors. A camera drone of under $250\,\text{g}$ requires the UAS operator to register, and the registration number is marked on every drone. The UAS operator must be an adult or an organisation. The minimum age of a remote pilot in Finland is 12 years. Class C0 has no training requirement, but the user manual must be read and the A1/A3 online theory exam is recommended. UAS geographical zones are checked on the day of the flight.
  • Air:bit 2 outdoors. A drone assembled from a construction kit is not a privately built drone in the sense of the regulation. Its class marking or toy status must be established before flying outdoors. That is why it is flown only indoors here.
  • The manufacturers' restrictions. The camera drone's manufacturer states that the product is not intended for children, and gives no age limit. The goggles do not meet the requirement of direct visual line of sight. The manufacturer urges particular caution indoors: without positioning the drone can drift, and white walls, glass, mirrors and repeating floor patterns interfere with its sensors. Obstacle avoidance does not detect moving people, and it does not work in Sport mode.
  • Air:bit 2. According to the manufacturer, children under 8 and animals are kept at least five metres away indoors too, the battery is not charged unsupervised, and flying is done low above a soft surface. The school's kits have propeller guards: they are always fitted before flying.
  • Filming. The school is the data controller, so filming follows the school's own permissions and instructions. Agree where the recordings are stored and when they are deleted.
  • Batteries. Agree on the charging place, supervision and storage with the person responsible for the school's fire safety. Tukes's guidance on the safe use of lithium-ion batteries is worth reading directly on the Tukes website.
