## fy3-14-L1-01
**Given:** ice, $m = 0.050\,\text{kg}$, temperature $0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$; from the table $s = 334\,\tfrac{\text{kJ}}{\text{kg}}$
**Find:** the quantity of heat $Q$ needed to melt the ice, in kilojoules
**Principle:** The ice is already at its melting temperature and melts completely into water that is also at $0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$. The temperature does not change, so the heat is used only for the change of state, and the latent heat of fusion is proportional to the mass.
**Formula:** $Q = s m$, so the quantity to find is directly $Q = s m$
**Conversions:** The result is wanted in kilojoules and $s$ is already in kilojoules per kilogram, so no conversion is needed.
**Substitution:** $Q = 334\,\tfrac{\text{kJ}}{\text{kg}} \cdot 0.050\,\text{kg} = 16.7\,\text{kJ}$
**Answer:** $17\,\text{kJ}$, two significant figures like the mass $0.050\,\text{kg}$.
**Reasonableness:** A kilogram of ice needs $334\,\text{kJ}$, and the mass is one twentieth of a kilogram, so $334\,\text{kJ} / 20 \approx 17\,\text{kJ}$. $Q$ is positive, because the ice takes in heat. The formula has no temperature change, because the temperature stays constant.

## fy3-14-L2-01
**Given:** water $m_\text{w} = 0.35\,\text{kg}$, $T_\text{w} = 30\,{}^\circ\text{C}$; ice $m_\text{i} = 0.060\,\text{kg}$, $T_\text{i} = 0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$; from the table $c_\text{w} = 4190\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{kg}\cdot\text{K}}$ and $s = 334\,\tfrac{\text{kJ}}{\text{kg}}$
**Find:** the final temperature $T$ in degrees Celsius
**Principle:** In an insulated calorimeter energy is conserved: the heat given out by the water is equal to the heat taken in by the ice. The ice is already at $0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, so it does not warm before melting. The problem says that all the ice melts, and this must be checked by comparing the quantities of heat.
**Formula:** When the water cools to $0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ it gives out a quantity of heat $Q_\text{w} = c_\text{w} m_\text{w} T_\text{w}$. If $Q_\text{w} > s m_\text{i}$, the surplus warms the whole amount of water: $Q_\text{w} - s m_\text{i} = c_\text{w} (m_\text{w} + m_\text{i}) T$, so $T = \dfrac{Q_\text{w} - s m_\text{i}}{c_\text{w} (m_\text{w} + m_\text{i})}$
**Conversions:** Measured from $0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, the temperature is the same in kelvins and in degrees Celsius, so $T_\text{w} = 30\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ corresponds to a fall of $30\,\text{K}$. The latent heat of fusion is $334\,\tfrac{\text{kJ}}{\text{kg}} = 334\,000\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{kg}}$.
**Substitution:** $Q_\text{w} = 4190 \cdot 0.35 \cdot 30\,\text{J} = 43\,995\,\text{J}$ and $s m_\text{i} = 334\,000 \cdot 0.060\,\text{J} = 20\,040\,\text{J}$. Because $43\,995 > 20\,040$, all the ice melts. $T = \dfrac{43\,995\,\text{J} - 20\,040\,\text{J}}{4190\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{kg}\cdot\text{K}} \cdot 0.41\,\text{kg}} = \dfrac{23\,955\,\text{J}}{1717.9\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{K}}} = 13.94\,{}^\circ\text{C}$
**Answer:** $14\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, two significant figures, because the mass of the water $0.35\,\text{kg}$, its temperature $30\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ and the mass of the ice $0.060\,\text{kg}$ all have two figures; the difference of the quantities of heat $43\,995 - 20\,040$ does not add figures.
**Reasonableness:** The result is between $0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ and $30\,{}^\circ\text{C}$. Without the latent heat the result would be $\dfrac{0.35 \cdot 30}{0.41} \approx 26\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, so melting takes almost half of the heat given out by the water. Energy balance: the water gives out $4190 \cdot 0.35 \cdot (30 - 13.944)\,\text{J} \approx 23.55\,\text{kJ}$ and the ice takes in $20\,040\,\text{J} + 4190 \cdot 0.060 \cdot 13.944\,\text{J} \approx 23.55\,\text{kJ}$, so the balances agree.

## fy3-14-L3-03
**Given:** water $m_\text{w} = 0.100\,\text{kg}$, $T_\text{w} = 10.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$; ice $m_\text{i} = 0.500\,\text{kg}$, $T_\text{i} = -40.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$; from the table $c_\text{w} = 4190\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{kg}\cdot\text{K}}$, $c_\text{i} = 2100\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{kg}\cdot\text{K}}$ and $s = 334\,\tfrac{\text{kJ}}{\text{kg}}$
**Find:** the final temperature $T$ in degrees Celsius
**Principle:** Energy is conserved in the insulated container. The water can give out at most the heat released when it cools to $0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ and freezes. If this is less than the heat needed to warm the ice to $0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, all the water freezes and the whole content becomes ice at a temperature below $0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$.
**Formula:** If all the water freezes, the water gives out three quantities of heat: it cools to $0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ ($c_\text{w} m_\text{w} T_\text{w}$), it freezes ($s m_\text{w}$) and the ice formed cools further to the temperature $T$ ($c_\text{i} m_\text{w} (0 - T)$). The original ice takes in, when it warms from the temperature $T_\text{i}$ to the temperature $T$, a quantity of heat $c_\text{i} m_\text{i} (T - T_\text{i})$. Conservation of energy: $c_\text{w} m_\text{w} T_\text{w} + s m_\text{w} - c_\text{i} m_\text{w} T = c_\text{i} m_\text{i} T - c_\text{i} m_\text{i} T_\text{i}$. Moving the terms that contain $T$ to the right: $c_\text{w} m_\text{w} T_\text{w} + s m_\text{w} + c_\text{i} m_\text{i} T_\text{i} = c_\text{i} (m_\text{i} + m_\text{w}) T$, so $T = \dfrac{c_\text{w} m_\text{w} T_\text{w} + s m_\text{w} + c_\text{i} m_\text{i} T_\text{i}}{c_\text{i} (m_\text{i} + m_\text{w})}$
**Conversions:** The temperature differences are the same in kelvins: the water falls by $10\,\text{K}$ and the ice rises by $0 - (-40) = 40\,\text{K}$. The latent heat of fusion is $334\,\tfrac{\text{kJ}}{\text{kg}} = 334\,000\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{kg}}$.
**Substitution:** The water can give out at most $4190 \cdot 0.100 \cdot 10.0\,\text{J} + 334\,000 \cdot 0.100\,\text{J} = 37\,590\,\text{J}$, but warming the ice to $0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ needs $2100 \cdot 0.500 \cdot 40.0\,\text{J} = 42\,000\,\text{J}$, so all the water freezes.

The terms in the numerator are $4190\,\text{J}$, $33\,400\,\text{J}$ and $2100 \cdot 0.500 \cdot (-40.0)\,\text{J} = -42\,000\,\text{J}$, and the denominator is $2100\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{kg}\cdot\text{K}} \cdot 0.600\,\text{kg} = 1260\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{K}}$, so

$$\begin{aligned} T &= \dfrac{4190\,\text{J} + 33\,400\,\text{J} - 42\,000\,\text{J}}{1260\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{K}}} \\ &= \dfrac{-4410\,\text{J}}{1260\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{K}}} \\ &= -3.5\,{}^\circ\text{C} \end{aligned}$$

**Answer:** $-3.5\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, two significant figures, because the difference in the numerator $37\,590 - 42\,000 = -4410\,\text{J}$ is known only to the nearest hundred joules (the latent heat $334\,000 \cdot 0.100 = 33\,400\,\text{J}$ limits it), so the result keeps two figures even though the initial values have three.
**Reasonableness:** The result is below $0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ and above the initial temperature of the cold ice, $-40\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, as it should be. Although there is five times the mass of ice, the temperature rises close to $0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, because the freezing water gives out $33.4\,\text{kJ}$, which is most of the $42\,\text{kJ}$ needed to warm the ice; only $4.4\,\text{kJ}$ is missing. Energy balance: the ice takes in $2100 \cdot 0.50 \cdot 36.5\,\text{J} = 38\,325\,\text{J}$ and the water gives out $37\,590\,\text{J} + 2100 \cdot 0.10 \cdot 3.5\,\text{J} = 38\,325\,\text{J}$.
