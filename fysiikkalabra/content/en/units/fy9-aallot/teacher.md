## Lesson plan (75 min)

- **0–5 min, Reflect.** The lifeguard's whistle. In pairs the students estimate in words what changes in the sound when it passes from air into water. The answer is not given yet: it is at the end of the Key idea part of the theory summary (the frequency stays $3.0\,\text{kHz}$, the speed $350\,\text{m/s} \to 1500\,\text{m/s}$, the wavelength $0.12\,\text{m} \to 0.50\,\text{m}$).
- **5–15 min, Predict (review test).** Six questions alone, without notes. Ask the students to write the numbers of the questions they got wrong in their notebook.
- **15–25 min, Investigate.** The resonance frequencies of two cardboard tubes in pairs: which tube is open and which closed, and what is the speed of sound. Go through it together: the frequencies of the open tube are all the multiples of the fundamental frequency, those of the closed tube only the odd ones, and both give about $343\,\text{m/s}$.
- **25–35 min, Theory summary.** Go through the concept map one branch at a time: oscillators, waves, sound. At each formula ask which quantity stays constant (the frequency when the medium changes, the mass in a pendulum). Return to Reflect with the whistle figure.
- **35–55 min, Apply.** Four multiple-choice questions in pairs (about $6\,\text{min}$), then four calculations alone. Fast students carry on without hints; slower ones use the hints one at a time and do only the calculations *Car suspension* and *Waves from a motorboat* in the lesson, the others at home. If time runs out, the calculations *A train passes the platform* and *Loudspeaker at an open-air concert* are homework for everyone.
- **55–65 min, Check your understanding and exit ticket.** Alone: the concept and calculation tasks and the exit ticket. The explanation task (spring or pendulum) is homework, and the students assess it themselves with the criteria.
- **65–75 min, Problems.** In ten minutes there is time for the start of one long problem under exam conditions, without the model answer: recommended *A guitar string and tuning* a–b (calculation) or *The period of a pendulum and the acceleration of free fall* a (graph). The rest of the problem and the other five problems are homework, two or three at a time before the next lesson.

## Using the review test
The Predict questions are a diagnostic test, not a grade. Each question matches one chapter:

- Question 1: the period of a pendulum does not depend on the mass (chapter *The mass–spring oscillator and the pendulum*).
- Question 2: amplitude and period (chapters *Spring force and spring energy* and *The mass–spring oscillator and the pendulum*).
- Question 3: the frequency stays the same when the medium changes (chapter *How waves form and travel*).
- Question 4: the path difference and destructive interference (chapter *When waves combine: superposition and interference*).
- Question 5: the sound level of two equally loud sources (chapter *Intensity and the decibel scale*).
- Question 6: the Doppler effect from a passing source (chapter *Sound as a wave: pitch, timbre and the Doppler effect*).

If more than a third of the group answers question 5 wrongly (a typical answer is $160\,\text{dB}$), show on the board that the intensities are added and that twice the intensity raises the level by only $10\,\text{dB} \cdot \lg 2 \approx 3\,\text{dB}$. The Apply multiple-choice question *Justified claim: two machines* returns to the same mistake. The mistakes in question 3 ("the frequency changes in water") are returned to in the Investigate task and in the answer to Reflect.

## Pair work
- Investigate: one partner studies the frequency series of tube A, the other that of tube B; they compare the ratios and calculate the speed of sound from each.
- Apply multiple-choice questions: each partner first chooses alone and only then justifies the choice to the other. They answer only when they agree.
- In the long problem *A festival in the lakeside park* (written) pairs can write the answers together and assess them with another pair using the marking scheme.

## Differentiation
- **Fast students** do the Apply calculations without hints and go straight to the long problem *The period of a pendulum and the acceleration of free fall* (graph and linearisation, $20$ points), which has the most stages. Extra question: how long is a pendulum whose period on the Moon ($g = 1.62\,\text{m/s}^2$) is $2.0\,\text{s}$? (About $0.16\,\text{m}$.)
- **Slower students** do the Apply calculations *Car suspension* and *Waves from a motorboat* with the hints and leave the calculations *A train passes the platform* and *Loudspeaker at an open-air concert* for home. The *Review if needed* links of the theory summary lead straight to the original chapters, which have basic-level exercises.

## Assessment
The long problems are self-assessed with the marking scheme. The points follow the division of the matriculation exam: the graph and fitting problem *The period of a pendulum and the acceleration of free fall* is of the Part III type ($20$ points), the other five of the Part II type ($15$ points each), $95$ points in total. If the teacher assesses them, the same criteria work: choosing the right model and justifying its condition (for example a small angle of swing or a medium at rest), the symbolic solution, unit conversions in the substitution, precision and reasonableness; in a written part, justifying the claim with the name of the phenomenon and with numbers. All the data are invented examples, not real measurements.
