A weight oscillates on a spring and three data sets are simulated measurements of it (51 points over 0–5 s, each with a fixed error of at most ±0,25 cm); the student fits the model x = A sin(2πt/T) by setting A (0,5–12 cm) and T (0,40–3,00 s) and watches the model curve over the points and the fit's deviation d (the mean vertical distance of the points from the curve), finding that A moves the curve vertically and is the equilibrium-to-peak displacement (A = 6,0 cm, not the 12 cm between the extremes, gives the smallest d), that a larger T spreads the peaks (fewer full oscillations N, smaller f = 1/T), and that a good fit of an unknown set (A = 8,5 cm, T = 2,20 s) leaves d at the size of the measurement error, never zero; playback moves the weight as the data and a blue marker as the model, so a wrong T shows as a marker drifting out of step.
