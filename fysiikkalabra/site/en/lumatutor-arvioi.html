Tutor portfolio

The portfolio has six parts. Fill it in as you go, not only at the end. Short, honest answers are better than long, general ones.

  1. Your starting point

    Right at the start

    • Why do I want to be a LUMA tutor?
    • What strengths do I have?
    • What do I want to learn?

  2. Induction

    During the induction

    • Exercises completed
    • Main insights about guiding

  3. Visits to science events

    After every visit

    • Where did I go?
    • What did I observe?
    • What did I learn about science communication?
    • What could I use myself?

  4. Planning the workshop

    During planning and testing

    • The workshop's topic and audience
    • My plan
    • Test results
    • Changes made to the plan

  5. Running events

    After every event

    • Event and date
    • My role
    • Working time
    • What worked?
    • What would I improve?

  6. Final self-assessment

    At the end

    • How did my guiding skills develop?
    • How well can I get others interested in science?
    • What did I learn about cooperation?
    • What would I do differently next time?

The whole portfolio is one portfolio template that you can copy into your own file.

Self-assessment

After every event, assess your work with the tutor's self-assessment template. At the end you assess your development over the whole course using the same areas the teacher uses to assess completion:

  • Guiding skills

    You let the participants do things themselves, you ask before you tell, and you notice who is left out.

  • Preparation

    You have tried the workshop in advance, the equipment is ready and you know what to do if something does not work.

  • Cooperation

    You agree on tasks with the other tutors and the teacher and keep to what was agreed.

  • Responsibility

    You follow the safety instructions, respect the participants and do not collect personal data about them.

  • Active participation

    You are at the events for the whole agreed time and take on tasks on your own initiative.

  • Development and reflection

    Your portfolio shows what you learned, what changed and what you would do differently next time.

Handing in

Hand in your portfolio and your working time log in the learning environment the school names separately. Ruukkilabra does not save anything you write: make the portfolio in your own file.

After handing in, you have a short feedback talk with the teacher. You go through the portfolio, the events and your self-assessment, and the teacher explains the grounds for the assessment.

The course is assessed as pass or fail. Filling the hours is not enough on its own: the teacher assesses your work and your portfolio in the following areas.

Before handing in, check that

  • all six parts of the portfolio have answers
  • the working time log is added up
  • every event has a role, working time and self-assessment
  • the portfolio has no participants' names or other personal data
