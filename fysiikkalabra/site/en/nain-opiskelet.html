How to learn physics

You learn physics by experimenting, thinking, calculating and talking things through. You don't need to understand everything straight away. What matters most is to try for yourself, check, and try again.

How you study in Ruukkilabra

Every chapter goes the same way. The numbers are the numbers on the chapter's stage bar. You can go back to any step.

  1. Chapter stage 1

    Reflect

    Get to know the phenomenon, recall what you already know, and see what the chapter will teach you.

  2. Chapter stage 2

    Predict

    Make a reasoned prediction before you try anything. Later you compare it with what you observe.

  3. Chapter stage 3

    Investigate

    Experiment or explore a simulation. Change one thing at a time, record what you see and interpret it.

  4. Chapter stage 4

    Theory

    Connect your observations to concepts, models and laws. Also ask where a model works and where it does not.

  5. Chapter stage 5–6

    Examples and exercises

    Study worked solutions and practise with hints. Step by step, move on to solving on your own.

  6. Chapter stage 7

    Test and assess

    Test what you know, notice what is still unclear, and decide what to practise next.

Back to practice

Self-assessment and feedback show what to revisit. Go back to the theory, the examples or the exercises, and try again.

The core path includes investigations, explanations and graphs as well as calculations. Exercises marked Optional give extra practice.

How to learn more effectively

No method works equally well everywhere. A beginner usually needs more support: read the examples closely and use the hints. As you get better, use less support. Start with one or two of these.

  • Recall before you look

    Try to remember, and write down from memory what you understood. Only then check. It teaches more than rereading.

  • Spread your practice out

    Practise a little on several days instead of everything at once. Come back to older chapters after a week or two.

  • Mix up the exercises

    Once the basics feel solid, take exercises from different chapters and of different kinds. In each, first decide which principle fits.

  • Learn from examples, then reduce support

    Study a worked solution step by step and ask why it goes that way. Then use hints, and finally solve without them.

  • Explain and justify

    Say in your own words why a phenomenon happens and why you chose this formula. Giving reasons shows what is still unclear.

  • Study your mistakes

    Don't just correct the answer. Find where it went wrong: the concept, the diagram, the calculation or the unit. Write down the corrected idea.

  • Check like a physicist

    Draw a force diagram or sketch a graph. Check the units and ask whether the result is reasonable: are the size and direction believable?

  • Make your solution visible

    On paper or digitally: draw a diagram, list the given data, the principle, the intermediate steps and the answer with its unit, and justify your conclusion. What matters is that your thinking shows.

When you are stuck

  • Draw and list

    What do you know? What do you need to find? A diagram alone often gets you moving.

  • Go back to the theory, an example or a hint

    Reread the part you need, look at a similar example, or open one hint at a time. If you looked at a model solution, try again afterwards without it.

  • Solve it together

    Take turns explaining your thinking and ask each other why. Compare intermediate steps. Don't just copy an answer.

  • Ask for help precisely

    Say what you need to find, what you have already tried and where you got stuck. Then it is easy to help you. You can ask for help right away.

How to use feedback and assess what you know

Feedback and self-assessment tell you what to do next.

Make use of feedback

Your teacher may regularly give you feedback on how correct your exercises are and how good your solutions are, for example as points.

When you get feedback:

  • Find out where you succeeded.
  • Look at your mistakes and any missing reasoning.
  • Correct your solutions and practise more if needed.
  • Follow how your skills develop.

A single score does not describe everything you know. It only shows how certain solutions went that time.

Assess your own learning

In a chapter's last stage you first answer in your own words and only then open the model answer. After that you mark how well you knew it: Still practising, Partly, or I can do this. On the module page, My self-assessment shows how you have rated the chapter's goals.

Then decide your next step:

  • I can do this: move on and review it again later.
  • Partly: do more exercises and write down what is still uncertain.
  • Still practising: go back to the theory and examples, or ask for help.

Your marks are saved in this browser. They help you plan your practice.

Physics is not just memorising formulas. The aim is to understand phenomena and learn to solve problems.
