## fy2-2-L1-01
**Given:** energy $E = 54\,\text{kJ}$
**Find:** energy $E$ in joules
**Principle:** The prefix kilo (k) means a thousand, so a kilojoule is a thousand joules. The energy stays the same; only the way of writing it changes.
**Formula:** $1\,\text{kJ} = 1000\,\text{J}$, so $E = \text{value} \cdot 1\,\text{kJ} = \text{value} \cdot 1000\,\text{J}$
**Conversions:** The conversion is the task itself: $\text{kJ}$ is replaced with the value $1000\,\text{J}$.
**Substitution:** $E = 54 \cdot 1000\,\text{J} = 54\,000\,\text{J}$
**Answer:** $54\,000\,\text{J}$, two significant figures as in the given value $54\,\text{kJ}$.
**Reasonableness:** The joule is a much smaller unit than the kilojoule, so the number must grow: $54$ is less than $54\,000$. The battery contains tens of thousands of joules, which fits the fact that the energy of a match is about $1000\,\text{J}$.

## fy2-2-L1-02

**Find:** number of chocolate bars $N$
**Principle:** Every bar has the same energy, so the number is obtained by dividing the energy of a day's food by the energy of one bar. The energies are in the same unit, so they can be divided directly.
**Formula:** $N \cdot E_\text{chocolate} = E_\text{food}$, so $N = E_\text{food} : E_\text{chocolate}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: both energies are given in megajoules, and the unit cancels.
**Substitution:** $N = 10\,\text{MJ} : 4.4\,\text{MJ} = 2.27$
**Answer:** About $2.3$ chocolate bars, two significant figures as in the data $4.4\,\text{MJ}$ and $10\,\text{MJ}$.
**Reasonableness:** Estimate with round numbers: $10 : 5 = 2$, so a little over two bars is the right order of magnitude. Two bars ($8.8\,\text{MJ}$) fall slightly short of a day's food, and three ($13.2\,\text{MJ}$) go over it.

## fy2-2-L1-04
**Given:** chemical energy $E_\text{start} = 12\,\text{MJ}$ and energy transferred to the water $E_\text{water} = 9.0\,\text{MJ}$
**Find:** energy transferred to the air $E_\text{air}$ in megajoules
**Principle:** Energy is conserved, so the chemical energy of the gas is transferred entirely to the water and the air. The chemical energy does not disappear anywhere.
**Formula:** $E_\text{start} = E_\text{water} + E_\text{air}$, so $E_\text{air} = E_\text{start} - E_\text{water}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: both energies are given in megajoules.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{air} = 12\,\text{MJ} - 9.0\,\text{MJ} = 3.0\,\text{MJ} \approx 3\,\text{MJ}$
**Answer:** $3\,\text{MJ}$, to the accuracy of a whole megajoule: in a subtraction the least precise value decides the accuracy, and the energy of the gas, $12\,\text{MJ}$, is given only in whole megajoules.
**Reasonableness:** Most goes to the water, $9.0\,\text{MJ}$ out of twelve. Check: $9.0\,\text{MJ} + 3\,\text{MJ} = 12\,\text{MJ}$, which is the same total as at the start.

## fy2-2-L1-05
**Given:** energies read from the figure: AA battery $E_\text{b} = 10^{4}\,\text{J}$ and lightning $E_\text{l} = 10^{9}\,\text{J}$ (orders of magnitude)
**Find:** number of batteries $N$ whose total energy equals that of the lightning flash
**Principle:** Every battery has the same energy, so the number is obtained by dividing the energy of the lightning flash by the energy of one battery. On a power-of-ten scale every step is a factor of ten, so the power of ten of an energy is read by counting steps.
**Reading the graph:** The AA battery point is one small step to the right of $1\,\text{kJ} = 10^{3}\,\text{J}$, so its energy is $10 \cdot 10^{3}\,\text{J} = 10^{4}\,\text{J}$. The lightning point is at $1\,\text{GJ} = 10^{9}\,\text{J}$. There are five small steps between the points.
**Formula:** $N \cdot E_\text{b} = E_\text{l}$, so $N = E_\text{l} : E_\text{b}$
**Conversions:** Both energies were read in joules, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $N = 10^{9}\,\text{J} : 10^{4}\,\text{J} = 10^{9-4} = 10^{5} = 100\,000$
**Answer:** About $100\,000$ batteries. The result is an order of magnitude, because the energies in the figure are orders of magnitude.
**Reasonableness:** Five steps of a factor of ten means multiplying by ten five times: $10 \cdot 10 \cdot 10 \cdot 10 \cdot 10 = 100\,000$. The energy of lightning is large, but it is released in an instant; the same energy takes an enormous number of small batteries.

## fy2-2-L2-03
**Given:** electrical energy $E_\text{electric} = 6.0\,\text{kJ}$, energy turned into light $E_\text{light} = 1.5\,\text{kJ}$; the light is absorbed by the surfaces of the room
**Find:** energy that turned into thermal energy in the room $E_\text{heat}$ in kilojoules
**Principle:** Energy is conserved. The thermal energy of the lamp is $E_\text{electric} - E_\text{light}$, and when the light is absorbed, it also turns into thermal energy. No energy leaves the room as light.
**Formula:** $E_\text{heat} = (E_\text{electric} - E_\text{light}) + E_\text{light} = E_\text{electric}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: all energies are given in kilojoules.
**Substitution:** $E_\text{heat} = (6.0\,\text{kJ} - 1.5\,\text{kJ}) + 1.5\,\text{kJ} = 4.5\,\text{kJ} + 1.5\,\text{kJ} = 6.0\,\text{kJ}$
**Answer:** $6.0\,\text{kJ}$, two significant figures as in the given electrical energy.
**Reasonableness:** All the electrical energy ends up as thermal energy, as at the end of a chain whenever nothing is stored. The light is not stored anywhere but heats the surfaces.

The daily deficit is multiplied by the number of days and divided by the energy of fat per kilogram.
**Formula:** $E_\text{use} = E_\text{basic} + s \cdot E_\text{km}$, $\Delta E = E_\text{use} - E_\text{food}$ and $m = \dfrac{30 \cdot \Delta E}{37\,\text{MJ/kg}}$
**Conversions:** All to megajoules: $3200\,\text{kJ} = 3.2\,\text{MJ}$, $650\,\text{kJ} = 0.65\,\text{MJ}$, $0.0042\,\text{GJ} = 4.2\,\text{MJ}$, $900\,\text{kJ} = 0.90\,\text{MJ}$ and walking $1250\,\text{kJ} = 1.25\,\text{MJ}$.
**Substitution:** The meals together are $1.4 + 3.2 + 0.65 + 4.2 + 0.90 = 10.35$, so $E_\text{food} = 10.35\,\text{MJ}$. The use is $E_\text{use} = 10.2\,\text{MJ} + 1.25\,\text{MJ} = 11.45\,\text{MJ}$. The deficit is $\Delta E = 11.45\,\text{MJ} - 10.35\,\text{MJ} = 1.10\,\text{MJ}$ per day. The deficit of thirty days is $30 \cdot 1.10\,\text{MJ} = 33\,\text{MJ}$, and $m = 33\,\text{MJ} : 37\,\text{MJ/kg} = 0.89\,\text{kg}$.
**Answer:** About $0.89\,\text{kg}$ of fat is used up in a month. The chemical energy of the fat does not disappear: it turns into the motion of the muscles and mostly into thermal energy, which is transferred to the surroundings as heat. Two significant figures as in the data.
**Reasonableness:** The deficit is only about $10\,\%$ of the day's energy, so losing a little under a kilogram in a month is believable. Estimate: $30 \cdot 1 : 37 \approx 0.8$. The tolerance of five percent covers the rounding of the deficit $1.10\,\text{MJ}$.
