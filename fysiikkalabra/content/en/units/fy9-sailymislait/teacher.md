## Lesson plan (75 min)

- **0–5 min, Think about it.** Coupling railway wagons. Students first estimate in words with a partner how fast the wagons move on and whether kinetic energy is conserved. The answer is not given yet: it is at the end of the Key idea part of the Theory summary ($0.80\,\text{m/s}$; kinetic energy $41\,\text{kJ} \to 20\,\text{kJ}$).
- **5–15 min, Predict (starting-point test).** Six questions alone, without notes. Ask the students to write in their notebooks the numbers of the questions they got wrong.
- **15–25 min, Investigate.** The three collisions on the air track in pairs: each pair calculates the momentum and the kinetic energy before and after for all three. Discuss together: momentum is conserved in all of them, kinetic energy almost only with the spring buffer, and with the hook-and-loop strip almost $m_\text{B} : (m_\text{A} + m_\text{B})$ is lost.
- **25–35 min, Theory summary.** Go through the concept map and, for each branch, ask the students when the law holds. Return to Think about it with the wagon figure.
- **35–55 min, Apply.** Four multiple-choice questions in pairs (about $6\,\text{min}$), then four calculations alone. Fast students continue without hints; slower students use the hints one at a time and do only the calculations *A small hydropower plant* and *A seat belt in a crash* in the lesson, the others at home.
- **55–65 min, Check your understanding and exit ticket.** Alone: the concept and calculation questions and the exit ticket. The explanation question (head-on collision or wall) is homework, and students assess it themselves with the criteria.
- **65–75 min, Problems.** Ten minutes is enough for the start of one long problem in exam conditions, without the model answer: recommended *A curling throw* a–b (calculation problem) or *A pile driver and a warming pile* a–b (combining modules). The rest of the problem and the other five problems are homework, two or three at a time before the next lesson.

## Using the starting-point test
The Predict questions are a diagnostic test, not a grade. Each question corresponds to one chapter:

- Question 1: kinetic energy and the square of the speed (chapter *Kinetic energy, potential energy and mechanical energy*).
- Questions 2 and 3: conservation of mechanical energy and the work done by friction (chapter *Conservation of mechanical energy and the work–energy principle*).
- Question 4: impulse and stopping time (chapter *Momentum and impulse*).
- Question 5: what is conserved in a collision (chapter *Conservation of momentum and one-dimensional collisions*).
- Question 6: the power of a wind turbine (chapter *Hydro and wind power as energy converters*).

If more than a third of the group answers question 5 wrong, go through the difference between momentum and kinetic energy on the board before the Investigate task: two identical trolleys stick together, the momentum is shared by twice the mass, and the kinetic energy is halved. The Investigate task usually corrects this mistake with numbers. The mistakes in question 3 (“friction consumes energy”) are revisited in the Apply calculation *A cyclist going downhill*.

## Pair work
- Investigate: one partner calculates the momenta, the other the kinetic energies; they swap after each collision and compare at the end.
- Apply multiple-choice questions: each partner first chooses on their own and only then justifies the choice to the other. They answer only when the pair agrees.
- In the long problem *Does momentum disappear?* (assessing a report) pairs can write the experimental plan of part c together and assess it with another pair using the marking scheme.

## Fast and slow students
- **Fast students** do the Apply calculations without hints and go straight on to the long problem *A braking test on wet asphalt* (graph and linearisation, $20$ points), which has the most steps. Extra question: how long would the stopping distance be from $80\,\text{km/h}$ on an icy road where $\mu = 0.10$? (Reaction distance about $19\,\text{m}$, braking distance $\tfrac{(22.2\,\text{m/s})^2}{2 \cdot 0.981\,\text{m/s}^2} \approx 252\,\text{m}$, in total about $270\,\text{m}$.)
- **Slower students** do the Apply calculations *A small hydropower plant* and *A seat belt in a crash* with the hints and leave the calculations *A cyclist going downhill* and *A body check from opposite directions* for home. The *Review if needed* links of the Theory summary lead straight to the original chapters, which have basic-level exercises.

## Assessment
Students assess the long problems themselves with the marking scheme. The points follow the division of the matriculation exam: the graph and fitting problem *A braking test on wet asphalt* is of the Part III type ($20$ points), the other five of the Part II type ($15$ points each), $95$ points in total. If the teacher assesses them, the same criteria work: choosing the conservation law and justifying its condition, signs, a symbolic solution, unit conversions by substitution, precision and reasonableness; in the written part, assessing the claim with the numbers of the data. All data are invented examples, not real measurements.
