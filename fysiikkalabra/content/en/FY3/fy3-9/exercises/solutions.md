## fy3-9-L1-01
**Given:** $V_1 = 30.0\,\text{ml}$, $p_1 = 101\,\text{kPa}$, $V_2 = 45.0\,\text{ml}$, temperature constant, no air enters or leaves the syringe
**Find:** the air pressure $p_2$ in kilopascals
**Principle:** The temperature and the amount of air stay the same, so this is an isothermal change and Boyle's law holds: the pressure is inversely proportional to the volume, that is, the product $pV$ stays constant.
**Formula:** $p_1 V_1 = p_2 V_2$, so $p_2 = \dfrac{p_1 V_1}{V_2}$
**Conversions:** No units need to be converted: both volumes are in millilitres, so $\text{ml}$ cancels, and the pressure comes out in the same unit as $p_1$.
**Substitution:** $p_2 = \dfrac{101\,\text{kPa} \cdot 30.0\,\text{ml}}{45.0\,\text{ml}} = 67.333\,\text{kPa}$
**Answer:** $p_2 \approx 67.3\,\text{kPa}$, three significant figures. All three initial values ($101\,\text{kPa}$, $30.0\,\text{ml}$ and $45.0\,\text{ml}$) have three figures, so nothing limits the result to fewer than three.
**Reasonableness:** The volume becomes 1.5 times larger, so the pressure must decrease by the same factor: $101\,\text{kPa} / 1.5 \approx 67\,\text{kPa}$. The pressure is below atmospheric pressure, so the outside air pushes the plunger inwards, which you can feel when pulling the plunger. The product $pV$ before and after is $3030\,\text{kPa}\cdot\text{ml}$ in both cases.

The line is placed so that the points are spread evenly on both sides of it, and its slope is approximately the mean of these values, $\dfrac{12\,013}{6}\,\text{kPa}\cdot\text{ml} \approx 2000\,\text{kPa}\cdot\text{ml}$. **Reasonableness:** The values of the product $pV$ are $1980$–$2030\,\text{kPa}\cdot\text{ml}$, so they differ from the mean by less than $2\,\%$. ## fy3-9-L3-01
**Given:** $h = 20.0\,\text{m}$, $V_1 = 2.0\,\text{cm}^3$, $p_0 = 101\,\text{kPa}$, $\rho = 1000\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$, $g = 9.81\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2}$, temperature constant, air does not dissolve in water
**Find:** the volume $V_2$ of the bubble at the surface in cubic centimetres
**Principle:** Deep down, the bubble is subject to the hydrostatic pressure of the water in addition to the atmospheric pressure. A rising bubble is in balance with the surrounding water, so its pressure is always the pressure of its surroundings. The temperature and the amount of air stay the same, so Boyle's law holds.
**Formula:** $p_1 = p_0 + \rho g h$ and $p_1 V_1 = p_0 V_2$, so $V_2 = \dfrac{p_1 V_1}{p_0} = \dfrac{(p_0 + \rho g h) V_1}{p_0}$
**Conversions:** The hydrostatic pressure in pascals: $\rho g h = 196\,200\,\text{Pa} = 196.2\,\text{kPa}$, because $1\,\text{kPa} = 1000\,\text{Pa}$. The volumes are in the same unit, so they do not need to be converted.
**Substitution:** $p_1 = 101\,\text{kPa} + 1000\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3} \cdot 9.81\,\tfrac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^2} \cdot 20.0\,\text{m} = 101\,\text{kPa} + 196.2\,\text{kPa} = 297.2\,\text{kPa}$. $V_2 = \dfrac{297.2\,\text{kPa} \cdot 2.0\,\text{cm}^3}{101\,\text{kPa}} = 5.885\,\text{cm}^3$
**Answer:** $V_2 \approx 5.9\,\text{cm}^3$, two significant figures. The limiting value is the volume of the bubble, $2.0\,\text{cm}^3$ (two figures); the depth $20.0\,\text{m}$ and the atmospheric pressure $101\,\text{kPa}$ have three figures, and the density of water, $1000\,\tfrac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3}$, is a known table value that does not limit the result.
**Reasonableness:** Every ten metres of water adds about one atmosphere of pressure, so at $20\,\text{m}$ the pressure is about $3 p_0$ and the volume at the surface is about three times as large: $3 \cdot 2.0\,\text{cm}^3 \approx 6\,\text{cm}^3$. The same applies to the lungs: if a person diving with compressed air held their breath while rising, the air in the lungs would try to expand to almost three times its volume. The lungs cannot stretch that much, and lung tissue can be damaged by far smaller expansion. You must never hold your breath when rising: breathe normally throughout the ascent, so that the expanding air can leave the lungs.

## fy3-9-L1-06
**Find:** what the $(V, p)$ graph of an isothermal change looks like.
**Principle:** The amount of sealed air stays the same and its temperature stays the same, so Boyle's law holds: $pV$ is constant. Then $p = \text{constant} \cdot \dfrac{1}{V}$, that is, the pressure is inversely proportional to the volume.
**Model answer:** The graph is a falling curve, because the product $pV$ is constant: when the volume doubles, the pressure halves, and when the volume halves, the pressure doubles. The curve approaches the axes but never meets them. A falling straight line would be wrong, because the pressure would not decrease by the same amount for every added millilitre. A rising graph is wrong, because the amount of air is the same all the time, and when the volume increases the particles hit the walls less often. A horizontal graph is wrong, because a constant temperature does not mean a constant pressure: when the volume changes, the pressure changes.
**Reading the graph:** The shape of the graph is deduced from the product $pV = \text{constant}$: choose the volumes $V$, $2V$ and $4V$, and the corresponding pressures are $p$, $\dfrac{p}{2}$ and $\dfrac{p}{4}$. The points do not lie on one straight line but form a falling curve.

## fy3-9-L1-08
