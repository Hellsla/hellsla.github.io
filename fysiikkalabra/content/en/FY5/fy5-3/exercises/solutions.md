## fy5-3-L1-01
**Given:** radius of the path $r = 2.5\,\text{m}$, period $T = 6.0\,\text{s}$
**Find:** the speed $v$ of the child in $\text{m/s}$
**Principle:** The roundabout turns steadily, so the child is in uniform circular motion. In one revolution the child travels the circumference $2\pi r$ in the time $T$, so the speed is the distance divided by the time.
**Formula:** $v = \dfrac{2\pi r}{T}$
**Conversions:** None needed: the radius is in metres and the time in seconds.
**Substitution:** $v = \dfrac{2\pi \cdot 2.5\,\text{m}}{6.0\,\text{s}} = \dfrac{15.71\,\text{m}}{6.0\,\text{s}} = 2.618\,\text{m/s}$
**Answer:** $2.6\,\text{m/s}$, two significant figures like the radius $2.5\,\text{m}$ and the period $6.0\,\text{s}$.
**Reasonableness:** The circumference is about $16\,\text{m}$, and it is travelled in six seconds, so the speed is just under $3\,\text{m/s}$, or about $9\,\text{km/h}$: faster than brisk walking, which fits the edge of a roundabout.

## fy5-3-L1-02
**Given:** distance of the tip from the axis $r = 0.20\,\text{m}$, rotation frequency $f = 15\,\tfrac{1}{\text{s}}$
**Find:** the speed $v$ of the tip of the blade in $\text{m/s}$
**Principle:** The tip of the blade is in uniform circular motion whose radius is the distance of the tip from the axis. In every revolution the tip travels the distance $2\pi r$, and there are $f$ revolutions per second, so the distance travelled per second is $2\pi r f$.
**Formula:** $v = 2\pi r f$
**Conversions:** None needed: $\text{m} \cdot \tfrac{1}{\text{s}} = \text{m/s}$.
**Substitution:** $v = 2\pi \cdot 0.20\,\text{m} \cdot 15\,\tfrac{1}{\text{s}} = 1.257\,\text{m} \cdot 15\,\tfrac{1}{\text{s}} = 18.85\,\text{m/s}$
**Answer:** $19\,\text{m/s}$, two significant figures like the radius $0.20\,\text{m}$ and the rotation frequency $15\,\tfrac{1}{\text{s}}$.
**Reasonableness:** A check the other way, through the period: $T = \dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{15\,\tfrac{1}{\text{s}}} = 0.0667\,\text{s}$, so $v = \dfrac{2\pi \cdot 0.20\,\text{m}}{0.0667\,\text{s}} = 18.85\,\text{m/s}$, the same as above. The order of magnitude fits: the circumference is about $1.3\,\text{m}$, and $15$ revolutions per second gives about $19\,\text{m}$ per second, or about $70\,\text{km/h}$.

## fy5-3-L1-03
**Given:** period $T = 4.0\,\text{s}$
**Find:** the rotation frequency $f$ in $\tfrac{1}{\text{s}}$
**Principle:** The rotation frequency tells how many revolutions are made per second. When one revolution takes the time $T$, $\tfrac{1}{T}$ of a revolution is made per second, so the rotation frequency is the reciprocal of the period.
**Formula:** $f = \dfrac{1}{T}$
**Conversions:** None needed: the period is in seconds, so $f$ is obtained in $\tfrac{1}{\text{s}}$.
**Substitution:** $f = \dfrac{1}{4.0\,\text{s}} = 0.25\,\tfrac{1}{\text{s}}$
**Answer:** $0.25\,\tfrac{1}{\text{s}}$, two significant figures like the period $4.0\,\text{s}$.
**Reasonableness:** One revolution takes $4\,\text{s}$, so a quarter of a revolution is made in a second. In a minute the sails turn $0.25 \cdot 60 = 15$ revolutions, which is a usual pace for a windmill.

## fy5-3-L1-04
**Given:** radius of the path $r = 1.5\,\text{m}$, speed $v = 3.0\,\text{m/s}$, time after the break $t = 2.0\,\text{s}$
**Find:** the distance $d$ of the puck from the centre of the path in $\text{m}$
**Principle:** When the string breaks, no force acts on the puck horizontally, so the puck continues straight with the velocity it had at the moment of the break. The velocity is along the tangent of the path, that is, perpendicular to the radius, so the puck moves along the tangent the distance $s = vt$. From the centre to the point of the break the distance is $r$, and from there onwards perpendicular to it the distance is $s$, so by the Pythagorean theorem $d^2 = r^2 + s^2$.
**Formula:** $d = \sqrt{r^2 + (vt)^2}$
**Conversions:** None needed: the radius is in metres, the time in seconds and the speed in $\text{m/s}$.
**Substitution:** $d = \sqrt{(1.5\,\text{m})^2 + (3.0\,\text{m/s} \cdot 2.0\,\text{s})^2} = \sqrt{2.25\,\text{m}^2 + 36\,\text{m}^2} = 6.185\,\text{m}$
**Answer:** $6.2\,\text{m}$, two significant figures like the data $1.5\,\text{m}$, $3.0\,\text{m/s}$ and $2.0\,\text{s}$.
**Reasonableness:** The puck travels straight $s = 6.0\,\text{m}$, and the distance from the centre is slightly greater than this, because the starting point is already $1.5\,\text{m}$ away. If the puck left outwards in the direction of the radius, the distance would be $r + s = 7.5\,\text{m}$; along the tangent it is smaller than this, as the result $6.2\,\text{m}$ is.

## fy5-3-L2-02
**Given:** diameter $d = 40\,\text{m}$, speed of the gondola $v = 0.35\,\text{m/s}$
**Find:** the period $T$ in minutes
**Principle:** The gondola is in uniform circular motion whose radius is half of the diameter. The speed is the distance $2\pi r$ of one revolution divided by the period, so the period is obtained by solving it from the formula of the speed.
**Formula:** $v = \dfrac{2\pi r}{T}$, so $T = \dfrac{2\pi r}{v}$, where $r = \dfrac{d}{2}$
**Conversions:** $r = \dfrac{40\,\text{m}}{2} = 20\,\text{m}$. Finally seconds are converted to minutes by substituting $1\,\text{s} = \tfrac{1}{60}\,\text{min}$.
**Substitution:** $T = \dfrac{2\pi \cdot 20\,\text{m}}{0.35\,\text{m/s}} = \dfrac{125.66\,\text{m}}{0.35\,\text{m/s}} = 359.0\,\text{s} = 359.0 \cdot \tfrac{1}{60}\,\text{min} = 5.98\,\text{min}$
**Answer:** $6.0\,\text{min}$, two significant figures like the speed $0.35\,\text{m/s}$ and the diameter $40\,\text{m}$.
**Reasonableness:** The circumference is about $126\,\text{m}$, and the gondola goes round it at about a third of a metre per second, so a revolution takes about $360\,\text{s}$. A revolution of a Ferris wheel usually takes a few minutes, so the result is plausible.

**Answer:** about $4.5 \cdot 10^{3}\,\tfrac{1}{\text{min}}$, that is, $4500$ revolutions per minute, two significant figures like the speed $45\,\text{m/s}$ and the radius $9.5\,\text{cm}$.
**Reasonableness:** The circumference is about $0.6\,\text{m}$, so $45\,\text{m}$ per second is about $75$ revolutions per second. Laboratory centrifuges typically turn thousands of revolutions per minute, so the result is plausible.

## fy5-3-L2-04
**Given:** distance of the tip $r_1 = 52\,\text{m}$ and speed $v_1 = 65\,\text{m/s}$, distance of the other point $r_2 = 20\,\text{m}$
**Find:** the speed $v_2$ of the point in $\text{m/s}$
**Principle:** The blade is rigid, so all its points complete a revolution in the same time $T$. By the formula $v = \tfrac{2\pi r}{T}$ the speed is then directly proportional to the distance from the axis.
**Formula:** $T = \dfrac{2\pi r_1}{v_1} = \dfrac{2\pi r_2}{v_2}$, so $v_2 = \dfrac{r_2}{r_1}\, v_1$
**Conversions:** None needed: the unit of the distances cancels, and the speed is already in $\text{m/s}$.
**Substitution:** $v_2 = \dfrac{20\,\text{m}}{52\,\text{m}} \cdot 65\,\text{m/s} = 0.3846 \cdot 65\,\text{m/s} = 25.0\,\text{m/s}$
**Answer:** $25\,\text{m/s}$, two significant figures like the distances and the speed of the tip.
**Reasonableness:** The point is at less than half the distance of the tip, so its speed is less than half of the $65\,\text{m/s}$ of the tip. The period is $T = \tfrac{2\pi \cdot 52\,\text{m}}{65\,\text{m/s}} = 5.03\,\text{s}$, and $\tfrac{2\pi \cdot 20\,\text{m}}{5.03\,\text{s}} = 25\,\text{m/s}$ gives the same result.

## fy5-3-L3-01
**Given:** inner arc $r_1 = 36.5\,\text{m}$, speed of the inner runner $v_1 = 7.0\,\text{m/s}$, outer arc $r_2 = 40.2\,\text{m}$; the arcs are concentric semicircles
**Find:** the speed $v_2$ of the outer runner in $\text{m/s}$
**Principle:** Runners who run side by side are all the time on the same radius as seen from the common centre, so they run the semicircle in the same time $t$. Each is in uniform circular motion, and the distance of half a revolution is $\pi r$. When the time is the same, the speed is directly proportional to the radius.
**Formula:** $t = \dfrac{\pi r_1}{v_1} = \dfrac{\pi r_2}{v_2}$, so $v_2 = \dfrac{r_2}{r_1}\, v_1$
**Conversions:** None needed: the unit of the radii cancels.
**Substitution:** $v_2 = \dfrac{40.2\,\text{m}}{36.5\,\text{m}} \cdot 7.0\,\text{m/s} = 1.1014 \cdot 7.0\,\text{m/s} = 7.710\,\text{m/s}$
**Answer:** $7.7\,\text{m/s}$, two significant figures like the speed of the inner runner $7.0\,\text{m/s}$.
**Reasonableness:** The outer arc is about $10\,\%$ longer, so the speed must also be about $10\,\%$ greater. For both, the duration of the bend is $t = \tfrac{\pi \cdot 36.5\,\text{m}}{7.0\,\text{m/s}} = 16.4\,\text{s}$, because staying side by side means that both arrive at the end of the bend at the same time. In a race the starting positions of the outer lanes are staggered forwards so that arcs of different lengths even out; in this task the runners start side by side and no staggering is needed.

## fy5-3-L3-02
**Given:** speed $v = 36\,\text{km/h}$, the car goes round a quarter of the circle
**Find:** the magnitude of the change of velocity $|\Delta\overline{v}|$ in $\text{m/s}$
**Principle:** The speed stays the same, but the velocity is always along the tangent of the path, perpendicular to the radius. In a quarter of a revolution the radius turns $90^\circ$, so the velocity also turns $90^\circ$: $\overline{v}_1$ and $\overline{v}_2$ are perpendicular to each other. When they are drawn from the same point, the change $\Delta\overline{v} = \overline{v}_2 - \overline{v}_1$ is the hypotenuse of a right triangle whose legs are $v$ and $v$.
**Formula:** $|\Delta\overline{v}|^2 = v^2 + v^2$, so $|\Delta\overline{v}| = \sqrt{2}\, v$
**Conversions:** $v = 36\,\tfrac{\text{km}}{\text{h}} = \dfrac{36 \cdot 1000\,\text{m}}{3600\,\text{s}} = 10\,\text{m/s}$
**Substitution:** $|\Delta\overline{v}| = \sqrt{2} \cdot 10\,\text{m/s} = 14.14\,\text{m/s}$
**Answer:** $14\,\text{m/s}$, two significant figures like the speed $36\,\text{km/h}$.
**Reasonableness:** The speed did not change at all, but the change of velocity is greater than the speed itself, because the direction turned through a right angle. In half a revolution the velocity would turn to the opposite direction, and the change would be $2v = 20\,\text{m/s}$; the result for a quarter of a revolution lies between these, as it should.

## fy5-3-L3-03
**Given:** radius of the front sprocket $r_\text{f} = 10.0\,\text{cm}$, radius of the rear sprocket $r_\text{r} = 4.0\,\text{cm}$, radius of the rear wheel $R = 0.34\,\text{m}$, rotation frequency of the front sprocket $f_\text{f} = 1.0\,\tfrac{1}{\text{s}}$; the tyre does not slip
**Find:** the speed $u$ of the bicycle in $\text{km/h}$
**Principle:** The chain moves on the rim of both sprockets at the same speed $v_\text{c}$, so $2\pi r_\text{f} f_\text{f} = 2\pi r_\text{r} f_\text{r}$. The rear wheel turns at the same frequency $f_\text{r}$ as the rear sprocket. When the tyre does not slip, the point of the tyre touching the ground is momentarily at rest relative to the ground, so in one revolution the bicycle advances the circumference $2\pi R$ of the tyre, and the speed is $u = 2\pi R f_\text{r}$.
**Formula:** $f_\text{r} = \dfrac{r_\text{f}}{r_\text{r}}\, f_\text{f}$ and $u = 2\pi R f_\text{r}$, so $u = 2\pi R\, \dfrac{r_\text{f}}{r_\text{r}}\, f_\text{f}$
**Conversions:** The unit of the radii of the sprockets cancels. Finally $1\,\text{m/s} = \dfrac{0.001\,\text{km}}{\frac{1}{3600}\,\text{h}} = 3.6\,\tfrac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}$.
**Substitution:** $f_\text{r} = \dfrac{10.0\,\text{cm}}{4.0\,\text{cm}} \cdot 1.0\,\tfrac{1}{\text{s}} = 2.5\,\tfrac{1}{\text{s}}$, $u = 2\pi \cdot 0.34\,\text{m} \cdot 2.5\,\tfrac{1}{\text{s}} = 5.341\,\text{m/s} = 5.341 \cdot 3.6\,\tfrac{\text{km}}{\text{h}} = 19.23\,\tfrac{\text{km}}{\text{h}}$
**Answer:** $19\,\text{km/h}$, two significant figures like the radius of the rear sprocket $4.0\,\text{cm}$ and the radius of the rear wheel $0.34\,\text{m}$.
**Reasonableness:** In one turn of the pedals the rear wheel turns $2.5$ revolutions, and one revolution of the wheel takes the bicycle about $2.1\,\text{m}$ forward, so about $5.3\,\text{m}$ is covered per second. A calm pedalling pace gives a usual cycling speed of about $20\,\text{km/h}$, so the result is plausible.

## fy5-3-L1-06
**Find:** the direction of the velocity of a body in uniform circular motion.
**Principle:** The velocity is always in the direction of the motion of the body. On a circular path the direction of motion is at every moment the direction of the tangent of the path, that is, perpendicular to the radius. The body goes round the path, so the direction of the tangent keeps turning, and the velocity changes even though the speed stays the same.
**Model answer:** The velocity is along the tangent of the path, because the body moves in the direction of the path at every moment. The velocity does not point towards the centre: the body stays at the same distance from the centre, so it does not move towards it. Nor does it point away from the centre, because no force pushes the body outwards; when the string breaks, the body continues along the tangent. The direction of the velocity does not stay fixed but turns through a whole revolution during one revolution, even though the speed is constant.

## fy5-3-L1-07
**Given:** rotation frequency $f = 3000\,\tfrac{1}{\text{min}}$
**Find:** the period $T$ in seconds
**Principle:** The rotation frequency tells the number of revolutions per unit of time, and the period is the duration of one revolution. They are reciprocals of each other. The rotation frequency is first converted to revolutions per second.
**Formula:** $T = \dfrac{1}{f}$
**Conversions:** $f = 3000\,\tfrac{1}{\text{min}} = \dfrac{3000}{60\,\text{s}} = 50\,\tfrac{1}{\text{s}}$
**Substitution:** $T = \dfrac{1}{50\,\tfrac{1}{\text{s}}} = 0.020\,\text{s}$
**Answer:** $0.020\,\text{s}$, two significant figures.
**Reasonableness:** When one revolution takes $0.020\,\text{s}$, the number of revolutions per second is $\dfrac{1\,\text{s}}{0.020\,\text{s}} = 50$, which is the same as the converted rotation frequency. The sound of an engine is a fast buzz, so a revolution takes a fraction of a second.

## fy5-3-L1-09
**Find:** how the tangential speed $v$ of the child changes when the radius increases from $1.2\,\text{m}$ to $2.4\,\text{m}$ and the frequency of rotation decreases from $16\,\tfrac{1}{\text{min}}$ to $8.0\,\tfrac{1}{\text{min}}$.
**Principle:** In both situations the child is in uniform circular motion, and the tangential speed is $v = 2\pi r f$. The tangential speed is therefore directly proportional to both the radius and the frequency of rotation, and the changes of both must be taken into account. When the ratio is calculated, the frequency of rotation does not need to be converted to the unit $\tfrac{1}{\text{s}}$, because the conversion factor cancels.
**Model answer:** The radius doubles:

$\dfrac{r_2}{r_1} = \dfrac{2.4\,\text{m}}{1.2\,\text{m}} = 2.0$

The frequency of rotation is halved:

$\dfrac{f_2}{f_1} = \dfrac{8.0\,\tfrac{1}{\text{min}}}{16\,\tfrac{1}{\text{min}}} = 0.50$

The ratio of the tangential speeds:

$\dfrac{v_2}{v_1} = \dfrac{2\pi r_2 f_2}{2\pi r_1 f_1}$

$\dfrac{v_2}{v_1} = 2.0 \cdot 0.50$, that is, $\dfrac{v_2}{v_1} = 1.0$

The tangential speed stays the same. Check: at the start $f_1 = \tfrac{16}{60\,\text{s}} \approx 0.267\,\tfrac{1}{\text{s}}$ and $v_1 = 2\pi \cdot 1.2\,\text{m} \cdot 0.267\,\tfrac{1}{\text{s}} \approx 2.0\,\text{m/s}$. At the end $f_2 = \tfrac{8.0}{60\,\text{s}} \approx 0.133\,\tfrac{1}{\text{s}}$ and $v_2 = 2\pi \cdot 2.4\,\text{m} \cdot 0.133\,\tfrac{1}{\text{s}} \approx 2.0\,\text{m/s}$. The increase of the radius alone would double the tangential speed, and the slowing down alone would halve it. The period doubles, but the tangential speed is proportional to the frequency of rotation and not to the period.

## fy5-3-L2-05
**Given:** times for ten revolutions $t_{10}$: $41.2\,\text{s}$, $40.6\,\text{s}$, $41.8\,\text{s}$, $40.9\,\text{s}$ and $41.5\,\text{s}$; distance of the seat from the axis $r = 2.1\,\text{m}$
**Find:** the period $T$ and the speed $v$ of the seat; $v$ in $\text{m/s}$ goes in the answer box
**Principle:** The time of a single revolution is hard to measure accurately, because starting and stopping the clock include an error from reaction time. When ten revolutions are measured at once, the same error is shared among ten revolutions and the error of the period is one tenth. The mean of the measurements evens out random scatter. The period is the mean time divided by the number of revolutions, and the speed is then calculated with the formula $v = \tfrac{2\pi r}{T}$.
**Formula:** $t_{\text{av}} = \dfrac{\text{sum of measurements}}{5}$, $\quad T = \dfrac{t_{\text{av}}}{10}$, $\quad v = \dfrac{2\pi r}{T}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the time is in seconds and the distance in metres.
**Substitution:** The sum of the measurements is $206.0\,\text{s}$, so
```latex
\begin{aligned}
t_{\text{av}} &= \frac{206.0\,\text{s}}{5} = 41.2\,\text{s} \\
T &= \frac{41.2\,\text{s}}{10} = 4.12\,\text{s} \\
v &= \frac{2\pi \cdot 2.1\,\text{m}}{4.12\,\text{s}} \\
&= 3.203\,\text{m/s}
\end{aligned}
```
**Answer:** a) The period is $T = 4.12\,\text{s}$, and many revolutions are measured so that the reaction-time error is shared among several revolutions. b) The speed is $3.2\,\text{m/s}$, two significant figures like the radius $2.1\,\text{m}$.
**Reasonableness:** In one revolution the seat travels the circumference $2\pi \cdot 2.1\,\text{m} = 13.2\,\text{m}$, and at the speed $3.2\,\text{m/s}$ this takes $\dfrac{13.2\,\text{m}}{3.2\,\text{m/s}} = 4.1\,\text{s}$, as the period. The speed is about $12\,\text{km/h}$, which fits a roundabout.

## fy5-3-L2-06

**Substitution:** Substituting into the formula $v = \dfrac{2\pi r}{T}$
```latex
\begin{aligned}
v_{\text{low}} &= \frac{2\pi \cdot 6.92 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{m}}{5730\,\text{s}} \\
&= 7588\,\text{m/s} \\
v_{\text{nav}} &= \frac{2\pi \cdot 26.56 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{m}}{43\,074\,\text{s}} \\
&= 3874\,\text{m/s} \\

```
**Answer:** a) The speeds are $7.59\,\text{km/s}$, $3.87\,\text{km/s}$ and, for the geostationary satellite, $3.07\,\text{km/s}$, three significant figures like the values in the table. b) The satellite farther away is slower: the radius of the orbit grows from top to bottom in the table, but the speed decreases. The answer box gets $3.07\,\text{km/s}$.

## fy5-3-L3-04
**Given:** latitudes $\varphi_{\text{Kourou}} = 5.2\,{}^\circ$, $\varphi_{\text{Canaveral}} = 28.5\,{}^\circ$ and $\varphi_{\text{Plesetsk}} = 62.7\,{}^\circ$; radius of the Earth $R = 6371\,\text{km}$, period $T = 86\,164\,\text{s}$, speed in low orbit $7.7\,\text{km/s}$

**Principle:** The Earth rotates as a rigid body, so every place goes round the axis of the Earth in uniform circular motion with the period $T$. The path of a place is a circle of latitude whose radius is $r = R\cos\varphi$: the radius $R$ drawn from the centre of the Earth to the place makes the angle $\varphi$ with the plane of the equator, and the distance of the place from the axis is the part of this radius in the direction of the equator, $R\cos\varphi$. A rocket launched eastwards gets the speed of the place as its initial speed.
**Formula:** $r = R\cos\varphi$, $\quad v = \dfrac{2\pi r}{T}$
**Conversions:** $R = 6371\,\text{km} = 6.371 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{m}$ and $7.7\,\text{km/s} = 7700\,\text{m/s}$
**Substitution:** The radii of the circles of latitude are $r_{\text{Kourou}} = 6.371 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{m} \cdot \cos 5.2\,{}^\circ = 6.345 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{m}$, $r_{\text{Canaveral}} = 5.599 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{m}$ and $r_{\text{Plesetsk}} = 2.922 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{m}$. The speeds are

v_{\text{Canaveral}} &= \frac{2\pi \cdot 5.599 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{m}}{86\,164\,\text{s}} \\

v_{\text{Plesetsk}} &= \frac{2\pi \cdot 2.922 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{m}}{86\,164\,\text{s}} \\

\end{aligned}
```

At the pole the radius is zero and so is the speed, so the speed decreases as the latitude increases. The extra speed from the rotation is thus only a small part of the speed in low orbit.

## fy5-3-L3-05
**Given:** speed $v = 1.2\,\text{m/s}$, start of the groove $r_1 = 25\,\text{mm}$, end of the groove $r_2 = 58\,\text{mm}$, spacing of the turns of the groove $1.6\,\mu\text{m}$

&= 20\,625 \\

&= 5378\,\text{m} \\
t &= \frac{5378\,\text{m}}{1.2\,\text{m/s}} \\

```
