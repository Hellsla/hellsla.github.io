FY3 · 2 cr · Optional

Energy and heat

How is energy transferred as heat and work, and how does it change matter?

16 lessons

Lessons

  1. Force, work and temperature

    chapters 1–4
  2. Chapter 1Force as a measure of interaction and weight $G = mg$

    Why does an astronaut weigh only one sixth as much on the Moon as on Earth, even though no part of the astronaut has gone missing?

  3. Chapter 2Work and energy

    Why is carrying a heavy backpack along a level corridor not work in the physics sense, even though your shoulders get tired?

  4. Chapter 3Thermodynamic system, state variables and temperature

    Why does a bicycle tyre feel harder on a hot summer day than on a frosty day, even though no more air has been pumped into it?

  5. Chapter 4Determining absolute zero

    Why can the lowest possible temperature be found in a school laboratory, even though a thermometer does not come anywhere near it?

  6. Pressure, heat and phases

    chapters 5–9
  7. Chapter 5Pressure and hydrostatic pressure

    Why is the wall of a dam much thicker at the bottom than at the top, even though the water pressing on it is the same water all the way down?

  8. Chapter 6Internal energy, heat and conservation of energy

    Why does a bicycle pump get hot when you pump up a tyre, even though nothing is heating the pump?

  9. Chapter 7Heating and cooling of matter: $Q = cm\Delta T$

    Why does the sand on a beach burn your feet already in the late morning, when the water is still cool, even though the Sun has shone on both for the same time?

  10. Chapter 8Changes of state

    Why does the temperature of water boiling at normal atmospheric pressure stay at about $100\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, even though energy keeps flowing into the pan?

  11. Chapter 9Thermal balance during a change of state

    Why do ice cubes cool a drink so effectively, even though the ice itself is only at zero degrees? And why can some ice be left at the bottom of the glass, even though the drink was at room temperature?

  12. Expansion and gases

    chapters 10–13
  13. Chapter 10Thermal expansion

    Why is there a comb-shaped steel joint at the end of a long bridge, which makes the car tyres thump as they cross it?

  14. Chapter 11Changes of state of a gas and Boyle's law

    Why does a sealed bag of crisps swell up tight on an aeroplane, even though nothing has been added to it?

  15. Chapter 12Ideal gas law $pV = nRT$

    Why is a helium balloon much lighter than an air balloon of the same size? Does it contain less gas?

  16. Chapter 13Work done by a gas and the heat engine

    Why does a thermal power plant get less than half of the energy of its fuel as electricity, and where does the rest of the energy go?

  17. Energy and the environment

    chapters 14–16
  18. Chapter 14Energy production and the efficiency of power plants

    Why does a thermal power plant need about $2.5$ times as much fuel energy as the electrical energy it produces, and how could the rest of the energy be put to use?

  19. Chapter 15Heat transfer, energy balance and climate change

    Why is the mean temperature of the Earth about $+15\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ and not below freezing, even though the Earth radiates as much energy into space as it absorbs from the Sun?

  20. Chapter 16Applied lesson: heat phenomena in everyday life

    Why does the ice pack in a cool bag keep drinks cold for hours in hot weather, but once the ice has melted, the drinks warm up quickly?

  3. 4 goalsModule goals

    The student

    • deepens their understanding of energy as a central concept of physics
    • can investigate phenomena related to the thermodynamic state of matter and to changes of state
    • can apply the concepts and models of thermodynamics when examining energy production solutions and building a sustainable future
    • recognises the importance of energy balance and heat transfer in climate change
