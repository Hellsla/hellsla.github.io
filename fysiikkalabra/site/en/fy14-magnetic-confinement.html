Charged particles and magnetic confinement

Why can an invisible magnetic field hold a fuel that would melt or vaporise any solid container?

In this lesson, you will learn to

  • calculate the radius and period of a charged particle's circular orbit in a magnetic field.
  • explain how a magnetic field holds particles across field lines but not along them, using the mirror and the torus.
  • evaluate what the single-particle model shows and what the collective behaviour of a plasma adds.

Prior knowledge

  • A plasma is a quasi-neutral mixture of ions and free electrons that reacts strongly to magnetic fieldschapter Plasma: the fourth state of matter
  • Uniform circular motion needs a centripetal force $F = mv^2/r$, and kinetic energy is $E_\text{k} = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$ (earlier physics courses)

Core path: follow the steps in order. Besides calculations, the core path includes investigations, explanations and graphs. Tasks marked Optional give extra practice. Optional tasks: 2.

1Think about it

A fusion plasma is about $1.5 \cdot 10^{8}\ \text{K}$ hot. No material can touch it: even tungsten, the metal with the highest melting point, melts at about $3400\ ^\circ\text{C}$, and a plasma that touches a wall cools down at once. So the plasma must float inside its vessel without touching anything. In the previous unit you learnt that a plasma is made of free ions and electrons. Charged particles feel magnetic forces.

How could a magnetic field, which you cannot see or touch, keep millions of degrees away from the walls?

Review: plasma and magnetic fields

In the unit Plasma: the fourth state of matter you saw that a plasma reacts strongly to magnetic fields, while ordinary air hardly does. Why?

  • Temperature alone does not make anything magnetic: a very hot iron bar even loses its magnetism. What matters is that a plasma has free charges.

  • A plasma is quasi-neutral: its positive and negative charges balance. The field acts on each moving charge separately, not on a net charge.

  • Right. A magnetic field acts on a moving charge. Neutral air molecules carry no net charge, so the field hardly affects them; the free ions and electrons of a plasma are pushed by it.

Prediction 1: a stronger field

A proton moves at $2.0 \cdot 10^{6}\ \text{m/s}$ perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field and goes round in a circle. You make the field twice as strong, from $1.0\ \text{T}$ to $2.0\ \text{T}$.What happens to the radius of the circle?

  • The model values show the opposite: about $20.9\ \text{mm}$ at $1.0\ \text{T}$ and $10.4\ \text{mm}$ at $2.0\ \text{T}$. The magnetic force points to the centre of the circle, and a stronger force bends the path more sharply.

  • The force $|q|vB$ grows in proportion to $B$, not $B^2$. The model values show the radius halving: about $20.9\ \text{mm}$ at $1.0\ \text{T}$, $10.4\ \text{mm}$ at $2.0\ \text{T}$.

  • The magnetic force is perpendicular to the velocity, so it never changes the speed. It changes the direction, and the model values show that the circle shrinks from about $20.9\ \text{mm}$ to $10.4\ \text{mm}$.

Prediction 2: a magnetic mirror

In a magnetic mirror the field is four times stronger at both ends than in the middle. Protons start in the middle with the same speed but different directions. The pitch angle $\theta$ is the angle between a proton's velocity and the field line. Which protons will turn back before the ends?

  • The model values support this: with this mirror, protons with $\theta \geq 30^\circ$ are trapped and those with smaller angles escape. In the stronger field more of the motion turns into circling, and the motion along the field stops and reverses.

  • The model values show the opposite: protons with small pitch angles, for example $10^\circ$ or $20^\circ$, escape through the ends. Their motion is mostly along the field, and the stronger field at the end cannot stop it.

  • A magnetic force does not change the speed, but it can turn the direction of motion along the field around. In the model values protons with $\theta \geq 30^\circ$ were reflected.

  • Not all of them: in the model values protons with small pitch angles, such as $10^\circ$ or $20^\circ$, escape through the ends. A mirror always has a loss cone.

    Review the theory.

    4Theory

    The magnetic force on a moving charge

    A magnetic field $\overline{B}$ pushes on a charge $q$ only when the charge moves. The force is called the magnetic part of the Lorentz force. In the formula below, $v$ is the speed and $\alpha$ is the angle between the velocity $\overline{v}$ and the field $\overline{B}$.

    $$F = |q|\,v B \sin\alpha$$

    A charge that moves along the field feels no force.

    The force is always perpendicular both to $\overline{v}$ and to $\overline{B}$. For a positive charge, the right-hand rule gives its direction; a negative charge is pushed the opposite way. Because the force is perpendicular to the motion, it does no work: it changes the direction of the particle, not its speed.

    Radius and period of the orbit

    When $\overline{v}$ is perpendicular to a uniform $\overline{B}$, the magnetic force is a constant-size force towards a centre, so the particle moves on a circle. The magnetic force is the centripetal force:

    $$\begin{aligned} |q|\,v B &= \frac{m v^2}{r} \\[8pt] r &= \frac{m v}{|q| B} \\[8pt] T &= \frac{2 \pi r}{v} = \frac{2 \pi m}{|q| B} \end{aligned}$$

    The radius $r$ is called the gyroradius. A stronger field gives a smaller circle. The period $T$ does not depend on the speed: a faster particle runs on a larger circle in the same time.

    Along the field: helices and magnetic mirrors

    Usually the velocity has a part along the field, $v_{\parallel} = v\cos\theta$, and a part across it, $v_{\perp} = v\sin\theta$. The angle $\theta$ is the pitch angle. The field does not affect $v_{\parallel}$, so the particle circles around a field line and slides along it: its path is a helix. The particle is tied to its field line, but it can run freely along it.

    In a magnetic mirror the field is stronger at both ends. As a particle moves into the stronger field, more of its motion turns into circling and $v_{\parallel}$ shrinks. If $v_{\parallel}$ reaches zero, the particle turns back. The condition for this is below: $B_0$ is the field in the middle and $B_{\max}$ the field at the ends.

    $$\sin^2\theta \geq \frac{B_0}{B_{\max}}$$

    Particles with smaller pitch angles, inside the loss cone, escape through the ends.

    The Earth's magnetic field is a natural mirror: it traps charged particles in the radiation belts, where they bounce between the north and the south. Particles that do reach the upper air near the poles make the northern lights.

    Why a ring, and why it is still hard

    A straight field always has ends, and every particle with $v_{\parallel} \neq 0$ eventually leaks out. Bending the field into a ring, a torus, closes the field lines, so there are no ends.

    But in a ring the coils are closer together on the inside, so the field is stronger near the centre and weaker on the outside, and the field lines are curved. A circling particle then drifts slowly across the field: ions one way, electrons the other. The charges separate, an electric field forms, and the whole plasma drifts outward into the wall. The cure is to twist the field lines, which is the topic of the chapter From Early Machines to Modern Reactors.

    One particle is not a plasma

    The simulation follows one particle in a fixed field. A fusion plasma has about $10^{20}$ ions and as many electrons in every cubic metre. They collide, which lets them jump from one field line to another. Their currents change the magnetic field that holds them. Small waves and swirls, turbulence, carry heat out much faster than single-particle theory predicts. Large-scale instabilities can bend or break up the whole plasma: the kinks and wriggles of early pinch machines such as ZETA in 1958 are an example. Single-particle motion explains why magnetic confinement is possible; the collective behaviour of the plasma explains why it is so difficult.

      Example 1

      In a tokamak with a magnetic field of $5.0\ \text{T}$, a deuteron (the nucleus of deuterium) moves at $1.0 \cdot 10^{6}\ \text{m/s}$ perpendicular to the field. Calculate the radius and the period of its circular motion. The mass of a deuteron is $3.344 \cdot 10^{-27}\ \text{kg}$ and its charge is $e = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\ \text{C}$.

      Step 1/7

      Given: $m = 3.344 \cdot 10^{-27}\ \text{kg}$, $q = e = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\ \text{C}$, $v = 1.0 \cdot 10^{6}\ \text{m/s}$, $B = 5.0\ \text{T}$, $\overline{v}$ perpendicular to $\overline{B}$. Asked: the radius $r$ and the period $T$.

      Step 2/7

      Principle: The magnetic force $|q|vB$ is perpendicular to the velocity, so it keeps the speed constant and acts as the centripetal force $mv^2/r$ of a circular motion.

      Step 3/7

      Formula: From $|q|vB = mv^2/r$:

      $$\begin{aligned} r &= \frac{m v}{|q| B} \\[8pt] T &= \frac{2 \pi m}{|q| B} \end{aligned}$$

      Step 4/7

      Unit conversions: All quantities are in SI units. $1\ \text{T} = 1\ \text{kg}/(\text{C} \cdot \text{s})$, so $\text{kg} \cdot (\text{m/s}) : (\text{C} \cdot \text{T}) = \text{m}$.

      Step 5/7

      Substitution: $$\begin{aligned} r &= \frac{3.344 \cdot 10^{-27}\ \text{kg}}{1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\ \text{C} \cdot 5.0\ \text{T}} \\ &\quad \cdot 1.0 \cdot 10^{6}\ \text{m/s} \\ &= 4.17 \cdot 10^{-3}\ \text{m} \\[6pt] T &= \frac{2 \pi \cdot 3.344 \cdot 10^{-27}\ \text{kg}}{1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\ \text{C} \cdot 5.0\ \text{T}} \\ &= 2.62 \cdot 10^{-8}\ \text{s} \end{aligned}$$

      Step 6/7

      Answer: $r \approx 4.2\ \text{mm}$ and $T \approx 26\ \text{ns}$. Two significant figures, as in the speed and the field.

      Step 7/7

      Reasonableness: The deuteron circles about 38 million times a second on a circle of only a few millimetres, tiny compared with a plasma about $2\ \text{m}$ across. Check with $T = 2\pi r / v$:

      $2\pi \cdot 4.17 \cdot 10^{-3}\ \text{m} : 1.0 \cdot 10^{6}\ \text{m/s}$
      $= 2.62 \cdot 10^{-8}\ \text{s}$

      You can test the result in the simulation: deuteron, $v = 1.0 \cdot 10^{6}\ \text{m/s}$, $B = 5.0\ \text{T}$.

      Example 2: Apply: can the plasma hold its alpha particles?

      Each deuterium–tritium reaction makes an alpha particle (a helium-4 nucleus) with a kinetic energy of $3.5\ \text{MeV}$. Its mass is $6.645 \cdot 10^{-27}\ \text{kg}$ and its charge $2e$. Calculate the radius of its circle in ITER's field $B = 5.3\ \text{T}$, assuming that it moves perpendicular to the field. Give the answer in centimetres.

      $\text{cm}$
      Hint 1: a guiding question

      First find the speed from $E_\text{k} = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$. Change $3.5\ \text{MeV}$ into joules.

      Hint 2: what is the connection

      $1\ \text{MeV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-13}\ \text{J}$, and $v = \sqrt{2E_\text{k}/m}$ gives about $1.3 \cdot 10^{7}\ \text{m/s}$.

      Hint 3: an intermediate step

      Then $r = mv/(|q|B)$ with

      $|q|$$= 2e$
      $= 3.204 \cdot 10^{-19}\ \text{C}$
      Show the solution

      Given

      ${E_\text{k} = 3.5\ \text{MeV}}$, ${m = 6.645 \cdot 10^{-27}\ \text{kg}}$, ${|q| = 2e = 2 \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\ \text{C}}$, ${B = 5.3\ \text{T}}$, velocity perpendicular to the field

      Asked

      the radius $r$ of the circle, in centimetres

      Principle

      The magnetic force is the centripetal force, so $r = mv/(|q|B)$. The speed comes from the kinetic energy; the speed is far below the speed of light (about $4\ \%$), so $E_\text{k} = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$ is accurate enough.

      Formula

      $$v = \sqrt{\frac{2E_\text{k}}{m}}, \qquad r = \frac{m v}{|q| B}$$

      Unit conversions

      $$\begin{aligned} E_\text{k} &= 3.5 \cdot 10^{6} \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\ \text{J} \\ &= 5.607 \cdot 10^{-13}\ \text{J} \end{aligned}$$

      Substitution

      $$\begin{aligned} v &= \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 5.607 \cdot 10^{-13}\ \text{J}}{6.645 \cdot 10^{-27}\ \text{kg}}} \\ &= 1.299 \cdot 10^{7}\ \text{m/s} \\[6pt] r &= \frac{6.645 \cdot 10^{-27}\ \text{kg} \cdot 1.299 \cdot 10^{7}\ \text{m/s}}{3.204 \cdot 10^{-19}\ \text{C} \cdot 5.3\ \text{T}} \\ &= 5.08 \cdot 10^{-2}\ \text{m} \\ &= 5.08\ \text{cm} \end{aligned}$$

      Answer

      $r \approx 5.1\ \text{cm}$

      Two significant figures as in the energy and the field.

      Reasonableness

      The alpha particle has about $340$ times the energy of the deuteron in the worked example (

      $mv^2/2$$= 1.67 \cdot 10^{-15}\ \text{J}$
      $\approx 10\ \text{keV}$

      ), but its circle is still only about $5\ \text{cm}$, small compared with ITER's plasma, which is about $4\ \text{m}$ across. So the field can hold the alpha particles long enough for them to give their energy to the plasma by collisions, which keeps the plasma hot.

      Optional Example 3: Find the error
      A student writes: “An electron and a proton move at the same speed perpendicular to the same field.The electron has the same charge, so it moves on a circle of the same size, only the other way round.” What is wrong?

      • $F = |q|vB$ is the same for both, because they have charges of the same size and the same speed. The electron's circle is smaller because its mass is much smaller.

      • Right. The size of the charge is the same, but $r = mv/(|q|B)$ depends on the mass too. In the simulation at $2.0 \cdot 10^{6}\ \text{m/s}$ and $1.0\ \text{T}$ the proton circles at $20.9\ \text{mm}$, the electron at about $0.011\ \text{mm}$.

      • The student is right about the direction: the electron's charge is negative, so the force on it points the other way and it circles in the opposite sense. The error is the size of the circle.

      • Look at $r = mv/(|q|B)$: with the same $v$, $|q|$ and $B$, the radius is proportional to the mass, and the electron is about 1836 times lighter.

      Common misconception

      "The magnetic field pushes the particles towards the centre of the machine." It does not. The force on each particle points to the centre of its own small circle, not to the centre of the plasma. A magnetic field cannot stop motion along the field lines at all, and it holds particles across the field lines only because they circle around them. That is why the shape of the field lines matters so much: closed (a torus), twisted, and without ends.

      A second mistake is to think that the magnetic force speeds the particles up or heats them. It does no work, because it is always perpendicular to the velocity. A plasma is heated in other ways, for example by its own current, by beams of fast atoms or by radio waves.

      1. Concept task: Force and speed

      A deuteron circles in a uniform magnetic field with no other forces. Which statement is correct?

      • Right. A force perpendicular to the motion does no work, so the kinetic energy and the speed stay constant. Only the direction changes.

      • In a uniform field and without collisions or radiation, the radius $r = mv/(|q|B)$ stays constant, because $v$ does not change.

      • A static magnetic field cannot give or take energy from a moving charge: the force is perpendicular to the velocity, so it does no work.

      3. Concept task: Model and reality

      In the simulation a proton circles for ever in a uniform field. Why does this not prove that a real fusion plasma would stay confined?

      4. Explanation task: Why a ring?

      A younger student asks: “Why are fusion machines shaped like a doughnut and not like a long tube?” Answer in four or five sentences. Mention what a magnetic field does to a charged particle, why a tube does not work, and one problem that remains in the ring.

      Model answer

      A charged particle in a magnetic field circles around a field line, so the field holds it across the field lines, but it can still slide freely along them. In a long tube the field lines end, so particles that move along them leak out at the ends, even with magnetic mirrors, because of the loss cone. Bending the tube into a ring closes the field lines, so there are no ends. However, in a ring the field is stronger on the inside than on the outside, so ions and electrons drift apart and the plasma is pushed outward. That is why the field lines must also be twisted.
