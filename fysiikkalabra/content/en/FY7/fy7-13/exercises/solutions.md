## fy7-13-L1-01
**Given:** line density $N = 300$ lines per millimetre
**Find:** the grating spacing $d$ in micrometres
**Principle:** The grating spacing is the distance between adjacent lines. When $N$ evenly spaced lines fit into a millimetre, the spacing of the lines is a millimetre divided by the number of lines.
**Formula:** $d = \dfrac{1\,\text{mm}}{N}$
**Conversions:** $1\,\text{mm} = 1 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{m}$ and finally $1\,\mu\text{m} = 1 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m}$.
**Substitution:** $d = \dfrac{1 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{m}}{300} = 3.333 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m} = 3.333\,\mu\text{m}$
**Answer:** $3.33\,\mu\text{m}$, three significant figures like the line density $300$.
**Reasonableness:** The grating spacing is a few wavelengths of visible light ($3.33\,\mu\text{m} = 3330\,\text{nm}$), so diffraction and interference are clearly visible. Check: $300 \cdot 3.333\,\mu\text{m} = 1000\,\mu\text{m} = 1\,\text{mm}$.

## fy7-13-L1-02
**Given:** wavelength $\lambda = 650\,\text{nm}$, grating spacing $d = 2.00\,\mu\text{m}$, order $k = 1$
**Find:** the angle $\theta$ of the first-order maximum in degrees
**Principle:** Light arrives perpendicular to the grating, so the directions of the maxima obey the grating equation: a maximum forms in the direction where the path difference $d\sin\theta$ of adjacent slits is a whole number of wavelengths.
**Formula:** $d\sin\theta = k\lambda$, so $\theta = \sin^{-1}\dfrac{k\lambda}{d}$
**Conversions:** $650\,\text{nm} = 650 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}$ and $2.00\,\mu\text{m} = 2.00 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m}$.
**Substitution:** $\theta = \sin^{-1}\dfrac{1 \cdot 650 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}}{2.00 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m}} = \sin^{-1} 0.325 = 18.97^\circ$
**Answer:** $19.0^\circ$, three significant figures like the grating spacing $2.00\,\mu\text{m}$.
**Reasonableness:** The wavelength is about a third of the grating spacing, so $\sin\theta$ is about $0.33$ and the angle about $19^\circ$, clearly less than $90^\circ$. Check: $2.00\,\mu\text{m} \cdot \sin 18.97^\circ = 0.650\,\mu\text{m}$.

## fy7-13-L1-03
**Given:** grating spacing $d = 2.50\,\mu\text{m}$, order $k = 2$, angle of the maximum $\theta = 30.0^\circ$
**Find:** the wavelength $\lambda$ of the light in nanometres
**Principle:** Light arrives perpendicular to the grating, so the direction of the maximum obeys the grating equation. In the second-order maximum the path difference of adjacent slits is two wavelengths.
**Formula:** $d\sin\theta = k\lambda$, so $\lambda = \dfrac{d\sin\theta}{k}$
**Conversions:** $2.50\,\mu\text{m} = 2.50 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m}$ and finally $1\,\text{nm} = 1 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}$.
**Substitution:** $\lambda = \dfrac{2.50 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m} \cdot \sin 30.0^\circ}{2} = \dfrac{2.50 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m} \cdot 0.500}{2} = 6.25 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\text{m} = 625\,\text{nm}$
**Answer:** $625\,\text{nm}$, three significant figures like the grating spacing and the angle.
**Reasonableness:** The wavelength is in the range of visible light, $400$–$700\,\text{nm}$; $625\,\text{nm}$ is orange-red light. If the order had been forgotten, the result $1250\,\text{nm}$ would be infrared, so dividing by two is necessary. Check: $2 \cdot 625\,\text{nm} = 1250\,\text{nm} = 2500\,\text{nm} \cdot 0.500$.

## fy7-13-L1-04
**Given:** distance from the grating to the screen $L = 1.20\,\text{m}$, distance of the first maximum from the central maximum $x = 30.0\,\text{cm}$
**Find:** the angle $\theta$ of the maximum from the direction of the central maximum in degrees
**Principle:** The screen is perpendicular to the laser beam, so the grating, the central maximum and the maximum form a right-angled triangle whose legs are $L$ and $x$. The tangent of the angle is the ratio of the opposite leg to the adjacent leg.
**Formula:** $\tan\theta = \dfrac{x}{L}$, so $\theta = \tan^{-1}\dfrac{x}{L}$
**Conversions:** $x = 30.0\,\text{cm} = 30.0 \cdot 0.01\,\text{m} = 0.300\,\text{m}$.
**Substitution:** $\theta = \tan^{-1}\dfrac{0.300\,\text{m}}{1.20\,\text{m}} = \tan^{-1} 0.250 = 14.04^\circ$
**Answer:** $14.0^\circ$, three significant figures like the distances $1.20\,\text{m}$ and $30.0\,\text{cm}$.
**Reasonableness:** The maximum is a quarter of the distance of the screen to the side, so the angle must be clearly less than $45^\circ$, at which $x$ would equal $L$. Check: $1.20\,\text{m} \cdot \tan 14.04^\circ = 0.300\,\text{m}$.

## fy7-13-L1-05
**Given:** wavelength $\lambda = 633\,\text{nm}$, order $k = 1$, angle of the maximum $\theta = 22.0^\circ$
**Find:** the grating spacing $d$ in micrometres
**Principle:** Light arrives perpendicular to the grating, so the direction of the maximum obeys the grating equation. In the first-order maximum the path difference of adjacent slits is one wavelength.
**Formula:** $d\sin\theta = k\lambda$, so $d = \dfrac{k\lambda}{\sin\theta}$
**Conversions:** $633\,\text{nm} = 633 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}$ and finally $1\,\mu\text{m} = 1 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m}$.
**Substitution:** $d = \dfrac{1 \cdot 633 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}}{\sin 22.0^\circ} = \dfrac{633 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}}{0.3746} = 1.690 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m} = 1.690\,\mu\text{m}$
**Answer:** $1.69\,\mu\text{m}$, three significant figures like the wavelength and the angle.
**Reasonableness:** The grating spacing must be larger than the wavelength for a first maximum to form, and $1.69\,\mu\text{m}$ is about $2.7$ times $0.633\,\mu\text{m}$. It corresponds to about $590$ lines per millimetre, which is a common school grating.

## fy7-13-L2-01
**Given:** wavelength $\lambda = 450\,\text{nm}$, line density $N = 600$ lines per millimetre
**Find:** the number of dots of light that form on the screen
**Principle:** Each maximum is one dot of light. According to the grating equation $\sin\theta = k\lambda : d$, and since the sine is at most $1$, a maximum forms only for orders for which $k\lambda < d$. On each side of the central maximum there are equally many maxima.
**Formula:** $d = \dfrac{1\,\text{mm}}{N}$ and $k < \dfrac{d}{\lambda}$; the number of dots is $2k_\text{max} + 1$, where $k_\text{max}$ is the largest whole number that satisfies the condition.
**Conversions:** $d = 1 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{m} : 600 = 1.667 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m}$ and $450\,\text{nm} = 450 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}$.
**Substitution:** $\dfrac{d}{\lambda} = \dfrac{1.667 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m}}{450 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}} = 3.70$, so $k_\text{max} = 3$ and the number of dots is $2 \cdot 3 + 1 = 7$.
**Answer:** $7$ dots of light: the central maximum and the orders $\pm 1$, $\pm 2$ and $\pm 3$. The answer is a whole number, so no rounding is needed.
**Reasonableness:** In the third-order maximum $\sin\theta = 3 \cdot 0.450 : 1.667 = 0.81$, that is $\theta = 54^\circ$, which is possible. In the fourth $\sin\theta = 1.08$, which is impossible, so a fourth-order maximum does not form.

## fy7-13-L2-02
**Given:** wavelength $\lambda = 650\,\text{nm}$, line density $N = 100$ lines per millimetre, distance of the screen $L = 2.50\,\text{m}$, order $k = 1$
**Find:** the distance $x$ of the first maximum from the central maximum in centimetres
**Principle:** Light arrives perpendicular to the grating, so the angle of the maximum comes from the grating equation. The screen is perpendicular to the beam, so the position of the maximum comes from the right-angled triangle whose legs are $L$ and $x$.
**Formula:** $d = \dfrac{1\,\text{mm}}{N}$, $d\sin\theta = k\lambda$ and $\tan\theta = \dfrac{x}{L}$, so $\theta = \sin^{-1}\dfrac{k\lambda}{d}$ and $x = L\tan\theta$
**Conversions:** $d = 1 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{m} : 100 = 1.00 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\text{m}$, $650\,\text{nm} = 650 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}$ and finally $1\,\text{m} = 100\,\text{cm}$.
**Substitution:** $\theta = \sin^{-1}\dfrac{650 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}}{1.00 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\text{m}} = \sin^{-1} 0.0650 = 3.727^\circ$ and $x = 2.50\,\text{m} \cdot \tan 3.727^\circ = 2.50\,\text{m} \cdot 0.06514 = 0.1628\,\text{m} = 16.28\,\text{cm}$
**Answer:** $16.3\,\text{cm}$, three significant figures like the distance $2.50\,\text{m}$.
**Reasonableness:** The angle for a sparse grating is small, so $\tan\theta \approx \sin\theta = 0.065$ and $x \approx 2.50\,\text{m} \cdot 0.065 = 16\,\text{cm}$. The spacing of the dots is easy to measure with a tape measure.

## fy7-13-L2-03
**Given:** wavelength $\lambda = 532\,\text{nm}$, distance of the screen $L = 1.00\,\text{m}$, separation of the maxima $\pm 1$ $66.8\,\text{cm}$, order $k = 1$
**Find:** the line density $N$ of the grating, that is the number of lines per millimetre
**Principle:** The maxima are symmetric on both sides of the central maximum, so the first maximum is half of the separation of the dots from the central maximum. The angle comes from the right-angled triangle, the grating spacing from the grating equation and the line density from the grating spacing.
**Formula:** $\tan\theta = \dfrac{x}{L}$, $d\sin\theta = k\lambda$ and $d = \dfrac{1\,\text{mm}}{N}$, so $d = \dfrac{k\lambda}{\sin\theta}$ and $N = \dfrac{1\,\text{mm}}{d}$
**Conversions:** $x = 66.8\,\text{cm} : 2 = 33.4\,\text{cm} = 33.4 \cdot 0.01\,\text{m} = 0.334\,\text{m}$, $532\,\text{nm} = 532 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}$ and $1\,\text{mm} = 1 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{m}$.
**Substitution:** $\theta = \tan^{-1}\dfrac{0.334\,\text{m}}{1.00\,\text{m}} = 18.47^\circ$, $d = \dfrac{532 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}}{\sin 18.47^\circ} = \dfrac{532 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}}{0.3168} = 1.679 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m}$ and $N = \dfrac{1 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{m}}{1.679 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m}} = 595.5$
**Answer:** about $596$ lines per millimetre, three significant figures like the distances. The grating is probably the manufacturer's $600$-line-per-millimetre grating, and the difference is due to measurement uncertainty.
**Reasonableness:** With $600$ lines per millimetre the first maximum of green light is at an angle of $18.6^\circ$, which is close to the obtained $18.5^\circ$. Check: $1.679\,\mu\text{m} \cdot 0.3168 = 0.532\,\mu\text{m}$.

## fy7-13-L2-04
**Given:** wavelengths $\lambda_\text{v} = 400\,\text{nm}$ (violet edge) and $\lambda_\text{r} = 700\,\text{nm}$ (red edge), line density $N = 300$ lines per millimetre, distance of the screen $L = 1.00\,\text{m}$, order $k = 1$
**Find:** the width $\Delta x$ of the first-order spectrum on the screen in centimetres
**Principle:** Each wavelength is deviated to its own angle according to the grating equation, so the spectrum begins at the maximum of violet light and ends at the maximum of red light. Each position comes from a right-angled triangle, and the width is their difference.
**Formula:** $d = \dfrac{1\,\text{mm}}{N}$, $\theta = \sin^{-1}\dfrac{k\lambda}{d}$, $x = L\tan\theta$ and $\Delta x = x_\text{r} - x_\text{v}$
**Conversions:** $d = 1 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\text{m} : 300 = 3.333 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m}$, wavelengths $400 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}$ and $700 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}$, finally $1\,\text{m} = 100\,\text{cm}$.
**Substitution:** $\theta_\text{v} = \sin^{-1}\dfrac{400 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}}{3.333 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m}} = \sin^{-1} 0.120 = 6.892^\circ$, $\theta_\text{r} = \sin^{-1} 0.210 = 12.12^\circ$, $x_\text{v} = 1.00\,\text{m} \cdot \tan 6.892^\circ = 0.1209\,\text{m}$, $x_\text{r} = 1.00\,\text{m} \cdot \tan 12.12^\circ = 0.2148\,\text{m}$ and $\Delta x = 0.2148\,\text{m} - 0.1209\,\text{m} = 0.0939\,\text{m} = 9.39\,\text{cm}$
**Answer:** $9.39\,\text{cm}$, three significant figures like the distance $1.00\,\text{m}$ and the wavelengths.
**Reasonableness:** At small angles $x$ is approximately proportional to the wavelength, so the width must be a little over $(700 - 400) : 700$, that is $43\,\%$, of the distance $21.5\,\text{cm}$ of the red maximum: $9.39\,\text{cm}$ is $44\,\%$, as it should be.

## fy7-13-L3-01
**Given:** wavelength of the second order ($k = 2$) $\lambda_2 = 700\,\text{nm}$, third order $k = 3$, wavelengths of white light $400$–$700\,\text{nm}$
**Find:** the wavelength $\lambda_3$ whose third-order maximum is in the same direction, and what this tells about the spectra
**Principle:** In the same direction $\theta$ the left-hand side $d\sin\theta$ of the grating equation is the same for both wavelengths, so the right-hand sides are equal too. The grating spacing is not needed, because it cancels: the result holds for every grating.
**Formula:** $d\sin\theta = 2\lambda_2$ and $d\sin\theta = 3\lambda_3$, so $3\lambda_3 = 2\lambda_2$ and $\lambda_3 = \dfrac{2\lambda_2}{3}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the wavelengths stay in nanometres, because the unit cancels in the ratio $2 : 3$.
**Substitution:** $\lambda_3 = \dfrac{2 \cdot 700\,\text{nm}}{3} = 466.7\,\text{nm}$
**Answer:** $467\,\text{nm}$, three significant figures like the wavelength $700\,\text{nm}$. Blue $467\,\text{nm}$ is present in white light, and the third-order spectrum begins from violet, $400\,\text{nm}$, at an even smaller angle. So the red end of the second-order spectrum and the violet and blue part of the third-order spectrum overlap.
**Reasonableness:** The first- and second-order spectra do not overlap, because the second-order violet, $2 \cdot 400\,\text{nm} = 800\,\text{nm}$, is more than the first-order red, $700\,\text{nm}$. Between the second and third orders $3 \cdot 400\,\text{nm} = 1200\,\text{nm}$ is less than $2 \cdot 700\,\text{nm} = 1400\,\text{nm}$, so overlap is to be expected.

## fy7-13-L3-02
**Given:** wavelength $\lambda = 650\,\text{nm}$, distance of the screen from the disc $L = 10.0\,\text{cm}$, separation of the maxima $\pm 1$ $89\,\text{mm}$, order $k = 1$
**Find:** the track spacing $d$ in micrometres
**Principle:** The evenly spaced tracks of a CD act as a reflection grating: for light arriving perpendicularly the reflected maxima obey the same grating equation as in a transmission grating, and $d$ is the track spacing. The screen is parallel to the disc, that is perpendicular to the incident beam, so the angle comes from a right-angled triangle. The maximum is half of the separation of the dots from the hole.
**Formula:** $\tan\theta = \dfrac{x}{L}$ and $d\sin\theta = k\lambda$, so $d = \dfrac{k\lambda}{\sin\theta}$
**Conversions:** $x = 89\,\text{mm} : 2 = 44.5\,\text{mm} = 4.45\,\text{cm}$; for the angle it is enough that $x$ and $L$ are in the same unit. $650\,\text{nm} = 0.650\,\mu\text{m}$.
**Substitution:** $\theta = \tan^{-1}\dfrac{4.45\,\text{cm}}{10.0\,\text{cm}} = \tan^{-1} 0.445 = 23.99^\circ$ and $d = \dfrac{1 \cdot 0.650\,\mu\text{m}}{\sin 23.99^\circ} = \dfrac{0.650\,\mu\text{m}}{0.4066} = 1.599\,\mu\text{m}$
**Answer:** $1.6\,\mu\text{m}$, two significant figures like the separation of the dots $89\,\text{mm}$.
**Reasonableness:** The track spacing given in the CD standard is $1.6\,\mu\text{m}$, so the measurement hits the mark. The track spacing is about $2.5$ times the wavelength, so there are only two orders on each side, and the first-order angle of $24^\circ$ is reasonable.

## fy7-13-L3-03
**Given:** wavelength of the red laser $\lambda_\text{r} = 650\,\text{nm}$, distance of the screen $L = 1.00\,\text{m}$, distance of the first maximum of red $x_\text{r} = 19.9\,\text{cm}$ and of green $x_\text{g} = 16.2\,\text{cm}$, order $k = 1$
**Find:** the wavelength $\lambda_\text{g}$ of the green laser in nanometres
**Principle:** The grating is the same for both lasers, so the measurement of the red laser, whose wavelength is known, first gives the grating spacing; that is, the grating is calibrated. After that the wavelength of green light comes from the same grating equation. The angles come from right-angled triangles, because the screen is perpendicular to the beam.
**Formula:** $\tan\theta = \dfrac{x}{L}$ and $d\sin\theta = k\lambda$, so $d = \dfrac{\lambda_\text{r}}{\sin\theta_\text{r}}$ and $\lambda_\text{g} = d\sin\theta_\text{g}$
**Conversions:** $x_\text{r} = 19.9 \cdot 0.01\,\text{m} = 0.199\,\text{m}$, $x_\text{g} = 0.162\,\text{m}$ and $650\,\text{nm} = 650 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}$.
**Substitution:** $\theta_\text{r} = \tan^{-1}\dfrac{0.199\,\text{m}}{1.00\,\text{m}} = 11.25^\circ$, $d = \dfrac{650 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}}{\sin 11.25^\circ} = \dfrac{650 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}}{0.1952} = 3.330 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m}$, $\theta_\text{g} = \tan^{-1} 0.162 = 9.202^\circ$ and $\lambda_\text{g} = 3.330 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m} \cdot \sin 9.202^\circ = 3.330 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\text{m} \cdot 0.1599 = 5.326 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\text{m} = 532.6\,\text{nm}$
**Answer:** $533\,\text{nm}$, three significant figures like the distances $19.9\,\text{cm}$ and $16.2\,\text{cm}$.
**Reasonableness:** The stated wavelength of a green laser pointer is $532\,\text{nm}$, and the green maximum is closer to the central maximum than the red one, because a shorter wavelength is deviated less. The grating spacing $3.33\,\mu\text{m}$ corresponds to $300$ lines per millimetre, which is a common school grating.
