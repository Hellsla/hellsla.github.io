## fy6-7-L1-01
**Given:** power $P = 45\,\text{W}$, time of use $t = 4.0\,\text{h}$
**Find:** the electrical energy $E$ in kilowatt-hours
**Principle:** The power of the computer is assumed to be constant, so the energy it uses is power times time. When the power is in kilowatts and the time in hours, the energy comes out directly in kilowatt-hours.
**Formula:** $E = Pt$
**Conversions:** $P = 45\,\text{W} = 45 \cdot 0.001\,\text{kW} = 0.045\,\text{kW}$
**Substitution:** $E = 0.045\,\text{kW} \cdot 4.0\,\text{h} = 0.18\,\text{kWh}$
**Answer:** $0.18\,\text{kWh}$, two significant figures like the power $45\,\text{W}$ and the time $4.0\,\text{h}$.
**Reasonableness:** In watt-hours the energy is $45\,\text{W} \cdot 4.0\,\text{h} = 180\,\text{Wh}$, and $1000\,\text{Wh} = 1\,\text{kWh}$, so the result $0.18\,\text{kWh}$ agrees. The energy is small, because the power of a computer is small compared with, for example, a kettle.

## fy6-7-L1-02
**Given:** energy $E = 0.15\,\text{kWh}$
**Find:** the energy in megajoules
**Principle:** The kilowatt-hour and the joule are units of the same quantity, energy. The conversion is done by substituting for the kilowatt-hour what the unit means: kilowatt times hour.
**Formula:** $E = Pt$: a kilowatt-hour is the power of a kilowatt for an hour, so to convert to joules we substitute watts for the kilowatt and seconds for the hour.
**Conversions:** $1\,\text{kWh} = 1000\,\text{W} \cdot 3600\,\text{s} = 3\,600\,000\,\text{J} = 3.6\,\text{MJ}$
**Substitution:** $E = 0.15 \cdot 3.6\,\text{MJ} = 0.54\,\text{MJ}$
**Answer:** $0.54\,\text{MJ}$, two significant figures like the energy $0.15\,\text{kWh}$.
**Reasonableness:** A kilowatt-hour is $3.6\,\text{MJ}$, so a little under a fifth of it is a little over half a megajoule. In joules the number is larger than in kilowatt-hours, because the joule is a much smaller unit.

## fy6-7-L1-03
**Given:** rated power $P = 1200\,\text{W}$, voltage $U = 230\,\text{V}$
**Find:** the current $I$ in amperes
**Principle:** Electric power is voltage times current. The mains voltage $230\,\text{V}$ is the effective value of an alternating voltage, so the power of a heating device is calculated with it as with a direct voltage.
**Formula:** $P = UI$, so $I = \dfrac{P}{U}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: $1\,\text{W} : 1\,\text{V} = 1\,\text{A}$.
**Substitution:** $I = \dfrac{1200\,\text{W}}{230\,\text{V}} = 5.217\,\text{A}$
**Answer:** $5.2\,\text{A}$, two significant figures like the voltage $230\,\text{V}$.
**Reasonableness:** The estimate $1200\,\text{W} : 200\,\text{V} = 6\,\text{A}$ is a little larger, because the divisor is smaller. The current is under $10\,\text{A}$, so the iron suits a circuit protected by a $10\,\text{A}$ or $16\,\text{A}$ fuse when no other high-power devices are on at the same time.

## fy6-7-L1-04
**Given:** energy $E = 350\,\text{kWh}$, price $a = 15.0\,\text{c/kWh}$
**Find:** the cost $K$ in euros
**Principle:** The same price is paid for every kilowatt-hour of energy and its transmission, so the cost is price times energy. The basic charges are left out as the task says.
**Formula:** $K = aE$
**Conversions:** $a = 15.0\,\text{c/kWh} = 15.0 \cdot 0.01\,\text{€/kWh} = 0.150\,\text{€/kWh}$
**Substitution:** $K = 0.150\,\tfrac{\text{€}}{\text{kWh}} \cdot 350\,\text{kWh} = 52.5\,\text{€}$
**Answer:** $52.5\,\text{€}$, three significant figures like the price $15.0\,\text{c/kWh}$ and the energy $350\,\text{kWh}$.
**Reasonableness:** In cents the cost is $350 \cdot 15\,\text{c} = 5250\,\text{c}$, that is $52.50\,\text{€}$. At ten cents per kilowatt-hour the bill would be $35\,\text{€}$ and at twenty cents $70\,\text{€}$; the result is between them.

## fy6-7-L1-05
**Given:** power $P = 6.0\,\text{W}$, time of use $5.0\,\text{h}$ per day, $365$ days
**Find:** the energy of a year $E$ in kilowatt-hours
**Principle:** The power of the lamp is constant, so the energy is power times the total burning time. With kilowatts and hours the energy comes out directly in kilowatt-hours.
**Formula:** $E = Pt$
**Conversions:** $P = 6.0\,\text{W} = 0.0060\,\text{kW}$; burning time in a year $t = 365 \cdot 5.0\,\text{h} = 1825\,\text{h}$
**Substitution:** $E = 0.0060\,\text{kW} \cdot 1825\,\text{h} = 10.95\,\text{kWh}$
**Answer:** $11\,\text{kWh}$, two significant figures like the power $6.0\,\text{W}$ and the time $5.0\,\text{h}$.
**Reasonableness:** In a day the lamp uses $6\,\text{W} \cdot 5\,\text{h} = 30\,\text{Wh}$, and $365 \cdot 30\,\text{Wh} \approx 11\,000\,\text{Wh} = 11\,\text{kWh}$. The lighting of a whole year with one LED lamp uses roughly the same amount of energy (slightly more) as one sauna session of an hour and a half with a $6\,\text{kW}$ stove ($9.0\,\text{kWh}$).

## fy6-7-L2-01
**Given:** the powers and times of the three phases of the wash program from the graph $(t, P)$
**Find:** the whole energy of the program $E$ in kilowatt-hours
**Principle:** In each phase the power is constant, so the energy of the phase is power times the duration of the phase, that is the area of the rectangle under the graph. The whole energy is the sum of the energies of the phases.
**Reading the graph:** In the heating phase the power is $2.0\,\text{kW}$ between $0$ and $0.25\,\text{h}$, so the duration is $0.25\,\text{h}$. In the wash the power is $0.2\,\text{kW}$ between $0.25$ and $1.25\,\text{h}$, that is $1.0\,\text{h}$. In the spin the power is $0.4\,\text{kW}$ between $1.25$ and $1.5\,\text{h}$, that is $0.25\,\text{h}$.
**Formula:** $E = P_1 t_1 + P_2 t_2 + P_3 t_3$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the powers are read in kilowatts and the times in hours, so the energy comes out in kilowatt-hours.
**Substitution:** $E = 2.0\,\text{kW} \cdot 0.25\,\text{h} + 0.2\,\text{kW} \cdot 1.0\,\text{h} + 0.4\,\text{kW} \cdot 0.25\,\text{h} = 0.80\,\text{kWh}$
**Answer:** $0.80\,\text{kWh}$, two significant figures, because the values are read from the grid lines of $0.2\,\text{kW}$ and $0.25\,\text{h}$.
**Reasonableness:** The heating is short but uses over half of the energy, $0.50\,\text{kWh}$, because its power is ten times that of the wash. A program of an hour and a half at an average power of about $0.5\,\text{kW}$ would give $0.8\,\text{kWh}$.

## fy6-7-L2-02
**Given:** voltage $U = 230\,\text{V}$, current $I = 8.7\,\text{A}$, time $t = 4.0\,\text{min}$
**Find:** the electrical energy $E$ in kilojoules
**Principle:** The power of the kettle is voltage times current, and at constant power the energy is power times time. When the voltage is in volts, the current in amperes and the time in seconds, the energy comes out in joules.
**Formula:** $P = UI$ and $E = Pt$, so $E = UIt$
**Conversions:** $t = 4.0\,\text{min} = 4.0 \cdot 60\,\text{s} = 240\,\text{s}$; finally $1\,\text{kJ} = 1000\,\text{J}$
**Substitution:** $E = 230\,\text{V} \cdot 8.7\,\text{A} \cdot 240\,\text{s} = 480\,240\,\text{J} = 480.24\,\text{kJ}$
**Answer:** $480\,\text{kJ}$, two significant figures like the current $8.7\,\text{A}$ and the time $4.0\,\text{min}$.
**Reasonableness:** The power of the kettle is $230\,\text{V} \cdot 8.7\,\text{A} \approx 2000\,\text{W}$, that is $2\,\text{kJ}$ per second, and in $240$ seconds it is about $480\,\text{kJ}$. In kilowatt-hours this is $0.13\,\text{kWh}$, which fits an ordinary boiling of a kettle.

## fy6-7-L2-03
**Given:** standby power $P = 4.5\,\text{W}$ all year, $365$ days, price $a = 17\,\text{c/kWh}$
**Find:** the cost of a year $K$ in euros
**Principle:** The standby power is small but constant, and the devices are in that state around the clock. Energy is power times time, and the cost is price times energy.
**Formula:** $E = Pt$ and $K = aE$, so $K = aPt$
**Conversions:** $P = 4.5\,\text{W} = 0.0045\,\text{kW}$; $t = 365 \cdot 24\,\text{h} = 8760\,\text{h}$; $a = 17\,\text{c/kWh} = 0.17\,\text{€/kWh}$
**Substitution:** $E = 0.0045\,\text{kW} \cdot 8760\,\text{h} = 39.42\,\text{kWh}$ and $K = 0.17\,\tfrac{\text{€}}{\text{kWh}} \cdot 39.42\,\text{kWh} = 6.70\,\text{€}$
**Answer:** $6.7\,\text{€}$, two significant figures like the power $4.5\,\text{W}$ and the price $17\,\text{c/kWh}$.
**Reasonableness:** One watt for a whole year is about $9\,\text{kWh}$, so $4.5\,\text{W}$ is about $40\,\text{kWh}$, and $40 \cdot 0.17\,\text{€} \approx 7\,\text{€}$. The sum is small compared with heating, but the standby mode can be removed with no effort using a power switch.

## fy6-7-L2-04
**Given:** power of the filament lamp $P_1 = 60\,\text{W}$, power of the LED lamp $P_2 = 8.0\,\text{W}$, burning time $4.0\,\text{h}$ per day, $365$ days
**Find:** the energy saving of a year $\Delta E$ in kilowatt-hours
**Principle:** The lamps burn equally long, so the saving is the difference of the powers times the burning time. There is no need to calculate the energy of each separately.
**Formula:** $\Delta E = P_1 t - P_2 t$, so $\Delta E = (P_1 - P_2)t$
**Conversions:** $P_1 - P_2 = 60\,\text{W} - 8.0\,\text{W} = 52\,\text{W} = 0.052\,\text{kW}$; $t = 365 \cdot 4.0\,\text{h} = 1460\,\text{h}$
**Substitution:** $\Delta E = 0.052\,\text{kW} \cdot 1460\,\text{h} = 75.92\,\text{kWh}$
**Answer:** $76\,\text{kWh}$, two significant figures like the powers $60\,\text{W}$ and $8.0\,\text{W}$ and the burning time $4.0\,\text{h}$.
**Reasonableness:** In a year the filament lamp uses $0.060\,\text{kW} \cdot 1460\,\text{h} \approx 88\,\text{kWh}$ and the LED lamp about $12\,\text{kWh}$; the difference is about $76\,\text{kWh}$. The saving is almost the whole consumption of the filament lamp, because the power of the LED lamp is only about a seventh of it.

## fy6-7-L3-01
**Given:** power of the kettle $P_1 = 2200\,\text{W}$, time $t_1 = 3.0\,\text{min}$, power of the lamp $P_2 = 15\,\text{W}$
**Find:** the burning time of the lamp $t_2$ in hours
**Principle:** The lamp uses the same energy as the kettle. The energy of each is power times time, so with a smaller power the same energy lasts for a longer time.
**Formula:** $P_2 t_2 = P_1 t_1$, so $t_2 = \dfrac{P_1 t_1}{P_2}$
**Conversions:** The unit W of the powers cancels, so the time comes out in minutes; finally $1\,\text{h} = 60\,\text{min}$.
**Substitution:** $t_2 = \dfrac{2200\,\text{W} \cdot 3.0\,\text{min}}{15\,\text{W}} = 440\,\text{min} = \tfrac{440}{60}\,\text{h} = 7.33\,\text{h}$
**Answer:** $7.3\,\text{h}$, two significant figures like the time $3.0\,\text{min}$ and the power $15\,\text{W}$.
**Reasonableness:** The power of the kettle is about $150$ times that of the lamp, so the lamp burns about $150$ times as long: $150 \cdot 3\,\text{min} = 450\,\text{min} \approx 7.5\,\text{h}$. The energy of both is $0.11\,\text{kWh}$.

## fy6-7-L3-02
**Given:** total of the bill $K = 64.20\,\text{€}$, basic charges $K_\text{base} = 8.50\,\text{€}$, price $a = 15.0\,\text{c/kWh}$
**Find:** the energy of the month $E$ in kilowatt-hours
**Principle:** The bill consists of basic charges that do not depend on consumption and a consumption charge, which is price times energy. When the basic charges are subtracted from the total, the consumption charge is left, and the energy follows by dividing by the price.
**Formula:** $K = aE + K_\text{base}$, so $E = \dfrac{K - K_\text{base}}{a}$
**Conversions:** $a = 15.0\,\text{c/kWh} = 0.150\,\text{€/kWh}$
**Substitution:** $E = \dfrac{64.20\,\text{€} - 8.50\,\text{€}}{0.150\,\text{€/kWh}} = \dfrac{55.70\,\text{€}}{0.150\,\text{€/kWh}} = 371.3\,\text{kWh}$
**Answer:** $371\,\text{kWh}$, three significant figures like the price $15.0\,\text{c/kWh}$.
**Reasonableness:** Check: $371\,\text{kWh} \cdot 0.150\,\text{€/kWh} = 55.65\,\text{€}$, and with the basic charges the total is $64.15\,\text{€} \approx 64.20\,\text{€}$. The consumption suits an apartment in a block of flats without electric heating. If the basic charges had been forgotten, the energy would have come out too large, $428\,\text{kWh}$.

## fy6-7-L3-03
**Given:** resistance of the heating resistor $R = 26.5\,\Omega$, voltage $U = 230\,\text{V}$, time $t = 2.50\,\text{h}$
**Find:** the electrical energy $E$ in kilowatt-hours
**Principle:** By Ohm's law the current of a resistor is the voltage divided by the resistance. The power of the resistor is voltage times current, and since the resistance stays constant, the power is constant and the energy is power times time. The electrical energy becomes the internal energy of the resistor, which is transferred as heat to the water.
**Formula:** $I = \dfrac{U}{R}$, $P = UI$ and $E = Pt$, so $E = UIt$
**Conversions:** The power is converted to kilowatts ($1\,\text{kW} = 1000\,\text{W}$), so that the energy calculated with hours comes out in kilowatt-hours.
**Substitution:** $I = \dfrac{230\,\text{V}}{26.5\,\Omega} = 8.679\,\text{A}$, $P = 230\,\text{V} \cdot 8.679\,\text{A} = 1996\,\text{W} = 1.996\,\text{kW}$ and $E = 1.996\,\text{kW} \cdot 2.50\,\text{h} = 4.99\,\text{kWh}$
**Answer:** $4.99\,\text{kWh}$, three significant figures like the resistance $26.5\,\Omega$ and the time $2.50\,\text{h}$.
**Reasonableness:** The power of the heater is about $2\,\text{kW}$, and in two and a half hours it uses about $2\,\text{kW} \cdot 2.5\,\text{h} = 5\,\text{kWh}$. The current $8.7\,\text{A}$ is of the same order as that of a kettle, but the heater is on for hours, so its energy is many times as large.
