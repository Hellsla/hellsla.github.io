## Aim and level

The last lesson of the French chapter. It is a reading lesson, and its goal is confidence: students discover that
after a few weeks of French they can find the topic and the main facts of a news text. Names, numbers, look-alike
words, word endings and context make this possible. Both texts were **written for this course** (A2–B1, in the
style of a regional newspaper); they are not copied from any source. Their facts were checked on 1 November 2026
against the CEA's announcement (via EUROfusion and AFP), iter.org and the ITER press release of 29 July 2026.

## Before the lesson

- **Check the changing facts** in the main text: is WEST's 1,337-second plasma (12 February 2025) still described
  correctly? Is the ITER schedule (research operation from 2034) still current on iter.org? If a newer record or
  milestone exists, keep the text as it is (it is dated) and mention the news as a discussion point: *"What has
  changed since the text was written?"*
- The texts appear on screen. If students prefer paper, print the two texts. Students who mark look-alikes with a
  pen often find more of them.

## Timing (75 minutes)

- **0–7 min, Think about it.** Hook; pairs share experiences of reading in an unknown language and translate the five words.
- **7–10 min, Predict.** Both predictions individually (how much they will understand; *<span lang="fr">actuel</span>*).
- **10–14 min, Step 1.** Two minutes for title, names and numbers; the *What is the text about?* question.
- **14–26 min, Step 2.** Read the whole text silently; the look-alike match individually; partners compare.
- **26–32 min, Step 3.** The true / false / not in the text match.
- **32–42 min, Theory summary.** Read the strategies and the false friends together; collect more look-alikes on the board (English, Swedish, Finnish columns); back to the predictions.
- **42–48 min, Apply.** Sort the words (look-alike, false friend, context).
- **48–58 min, Summary.** Pairs tell each other the text in English, then write the summary.
- **58–65 min, Transfer.** The second text, read and summarised individually.
- **65–75 min, Check.** Three checks individually; check 4 as homework if time is short.

**Fast pairs:** the extension (a real French text from iter.org or the CEA), or write three more false friends they find in a French–English dictionary.

**Running out of time:** do step 3 orally, give the transfer text as homework and keep the summary short (three sentences). Do not cut step 1 and step 2. The discovery that partial comprehension works is the point of the lesson.

## Organisation

Individual reading first, then pairs. Strong readers may try the text without the matching tasks and then check
their understanding with them. Students with German, Spanish or Latin will find even more look-alikes: invite
them to share.

## Assessment (formative)

- **The English summary:** facts right (what, where, when, why it matters, ITER's progress), no invented facts, clear English.
- **The transfer:** topic and two facts of the second text; four look-alikes and one word from context.
- **Check 4:** three strategies, each with a correct example.

## Common difficulties

- Students give up at the first unknown word. Model aloud how you read on and come back to it.
- Trusting false friends: *<span lang="fr">actuel</span>* read as *actual*, *<span lang="fr">expérience</span>* as *experience*, *<span lang="fr">milliard</span>* as million.
- Reading numbers: *<span lang="fr">1 337</span>* with a space (French thousands separator), *<span lang="fr">25 %</span>* with a space before the sign.
- Adding facts that are not in the text (for example that ITER will make electricity in 2034).

## Follow-up

This lesson ends the French chapter. A short oral at the end of the chapter can combine the units: introduce
yourself, order at the café, ask the way at the research centre, and tell in English what a French news text says.
