## fy2-3-L1-01
**Given:** energy $E = 1.80 \cdot 10^{5}\,\text{J}$, time $t = 90.0\,\text{s}$
**Find:** power $P$ in watts
**Principle:** Power is the quotient of the energy transformed and the time it takes. The kettle transforms energy steadily, so the whole energy and the whole time are enough.
**Formula:** $P = \dfrac{E}{t}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the energy is in joules and the time in seconds, so the result comes out in the unit $\text{J/s} = \text{W}$.
**Substitution:** $P = \dfrac{1.80 \cdot 10^{5}\,\text{J}}{90.0\,\text{s}} = 2000\,\text{J/s} = 2000\,\text{W}$
**Answer:** $2000\,\text{W}$ (that is $2.00 \cdot 10^{3}\,\text{W}$), three significant figures as in the given values.
**Reasonableness:** With rounded numbers $200\,000\,\text{J} : 100\,\text{s} = 2000\,\text{W}$. The typical power of a kettle is about $2\,\text{kW}$, so the result fits.

**Reasonableness:** The lamp transforms $8\,\text{J}$ every second, and in five seconds five times as much, which is $40\,\text{J}$. The unit is the joule, a unit of energy.

## fy2-3-L1-03
**Given:** power $P = 1200\,\text{W}$, energy $E = 72\,000\,\text{J}$
**Find:** time $t$ in seconds
**Principle:** The oven transforms energy at a steady power, so the time is the energy divided by the power.
**Formula:** $P = \dfrac{E}{t}$, from which $t = \dfrac{E}{P}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: $\text{J} : \text{W} = \text{J} : (\text{J/s}) = \text{s}$.
**Substitution:** $t = \dfrac{72\,000\,\text{J}}{1200\,\text{W}} = 60\,\text{s}$
**Answer:** $60\,\text{s}$, two significant figures as in the energy $72\,000\,\text{J}$.
**Reasonableness:** The oven transforms $1200\,\text{J}$ in a second, and $60 \cdot 1200\,\text{J} = 72\,000\,\text{J}$. A minute is a typical heating time.

## fy2-3-L1-04
**Given:** the lines A and B of the graph, which show the energy $E$ transformed by the devices as a function of time $t$
**Find:** the power $P$ in watts of the device whose line rises more steeply
**Principle:** The device transforms energy steadily, so its power is the energy transformed divided by the time, $P = \Delta E : \Delta t$. In the graph this is the slope of the line, so the more steeply rising line A belongs to the device with the greater power.
**Reading the graph:** Line A passes through the grid points $(40\,\text{s};\ 60\,\text{kJ})$ and $(80\,\text{s};\ 120\,\text{kJ})$. Between these points $\Delta E = 120\,\text{kJ} - 60\,\text{kJ} = 60\,\text{kJ}$ and $\Delta t = 80\,\text{s} - 40\,\text{s} = 40\,\text{s}$.
**Formula:** $P = \dfrac{\Delta E}{\Delta t}$
**Conversions:** The power comes out in watts when the energy is in joules and the time in seconds: $60\,\text{kJ} = 60 \cdot 1000\,\text{J} = 60\,000\,\text{J}$.
**Substitution:** $P = \dfrac{60\,000\,\text{J}}{40\,\text{s}} = 1500\,\tfrac{\text{J}}{\text{s}} = 1500\,\text{W}$
**Answer:** $1500\,\text{W}$, that is $1.5\,\text{kW}$, two significant figures, because the values are read from the gridlines to two-digit precision.
**Reasonableness:** Line A rises $30\,\text{kJ}$ in $20\,\text{s}$, that is $1.5\,\text{kJ} = 1500\,\text{J}$ per second, which is the same result. Line B rises only $40\,\text{kJ}$ in $80\,\text{s}$, so its power is $500\,\text{W}$, a third of the power of A, and its line is less steep. Such a power is typical of a microwave oven.

**Principle:** Power is the quotient of the transformed energy and time, $P = \Delta E : \Delta t$, so $1\,\text{W} = 1\,\text{J/s}$. Power tells how much energy is transformed per unit of time. The energy consumed also depends on time: $E = P\,t$.

## fy2-3-L1-07
**Find:** what the slope of an energy–time line starting from the origin tells you.
**Principle:** The slope of the line is $\Delta E : \Delta t$, and this quotient is power. The device transforms energy steadily, so energy is directly proportional to time and the slope of the line is the same everywhere.
**Model answer:** The slope tells the power of the device. The more steeply the line rises, the more energy is transformed per second. ## fy2-3-L3-04

**Reasonableness:** The power of a hydropower plant is hundreds of megawatts, so $800\,\text{MW}$ is the combined power of several plants, which is believable for a system the size of Finland. ## fy2-3-L3-05
**Given:** the periods in the table, in which the compressor runs for $11.5$, $12.0$, $11.5$ and $12.0\,\text{min}$ and is off for $18.0$, $18.5$, $17.5$ and $18.0\,\text{min}$; power when running $P = 100\,\text{W}$; annual consumption of the new fridge $130\,\text{kWh}$; price $0.20\,\text{€/kWh}$; year $8760\,\text{h}$
**Find:** the average power and annual consumption of the old fridge and the saving in euros from changing to a new one; an assessment of whether it is worth it
**Principle:** The compressor runs at $100\,\text{W}$ for only part of the time, so the average power is the running power multiplied by the fraction of the total time spent running. The annual consumption is the average power times the time in a year, and the saving is the difference in consumption times the price. The model assumes that the measured periods describe the whole year.
**Formula:** $\text{fraction} = \dfrac{t_\text{on}}{t_\text{on} + t_\text{off}}$, $P_\text{avg} = \text{fraction} \cdot P$, $E = P_\text{avg}\,t$ and $\text{saving} = (E - E_\text{new}) \cdot \text{unit price}$
**Conversions:** The average power is converted to kilowatts by substituting $1\,\text{W} = 10^{-3}\,\text{kW}$.
**Substitution:** The sum of the running times is $11.5 + 12.0 + 11.5 + 12.0 = 47.0\,\text{min}$ and the sum of the off times $18.0 + 18.5 + 17.5 + 18.0 = 72.0\,\text{min}$. The total time is $47.0\,\text{min} + 72.0\,\text{min} = 119.0\,\text{min}$, so the fraction is $\dfrac{47.0}{119.0} = 0.395$. The average power is $P_\text{avg} = 0.395 \cdot 100\,\text{W} = 39.5\,\text{W} = 0.0395\,\text{kW}$. The annual consumption is $E = 0.0395\,\text{kW} \cdot 8760\,\text{h} = 346\,\text{kWh}$. The saving is $(346\,\text{kWh} - 130\,\text{kWh}) \cdot 0.20\,\text{€/kWh} = 216\,\text{kWh} \cdot 0.20\,\text{€/kWh} = 43.2\,\text{€}$.
**Answer:** The average power is about $40\,\text{W}$, the annual consumption about $350\,\text{kWh}$ and the saving about $43\,\text{€}$ a year, two significant figures. A new fridge costing $600\,\text{€}$ would pay for itself only in a time of $600\,\text{€} : 43\,\text{€/a} \approx 14\,\text{a}$, so buying it for the saving in electricity alone is not worth it if the old fridge works. The assumptions that affect the result: the four measured periods are representative, and the room temperature, opening the door or the build-up of ice do not change the running time over the year, and other consumption such as the light and defrosting is ignored.
**Reasonableness:** The average power of $40\,\text{W}$ is less than half of the running power, because the compressor runs about two fifths of the time. An annual consumption of a few hundred kilowatt-hours is typical of an old appliance, and the electricity price of $0.20\,\text{€/kWh}$ gives an annual cost of a few tens of euros.

## fy2-3-L2-08
**Find:** how the power $P$ of the stove changes when the same energy $E$ is transformed in half of the time.
**Principle:** The stove transforms energy steadily, so power is energy divided by time, $P = E : t$. The energy is the same both times, so only the time affects the power, and the time is in the denominator of the quotient.
**Model answer:** The first time $P_1 = 6.0\,\text{kWh} : 3.0\,\text{h} = 2.0\,\text{kW}$.

The second time $P_2 = 6.0\,\text{kWh} : 1.5\,\text{h} = 4.0\,\text{kW}$.

So the power doubles: $P_2 : P_1 = 4.0\,\text{kW} : 2.0\,\text{kW} = 2.0$. When the time is halved and the energy stays the same, the power doubles. The power is not halved, because the time is in the denominator and not a factor. It does not stay the same, because power describes the rate of the transformation and not the energy alone. It does not quadruple, because the time was halved only once.

## fy2-3-L1-09
**Given:** power $P = 5.0\,\text{hp}$ and time $\Delta t = 30\,\text{min}$; from the table (MAFY table, unit conversions, power, horsepower) $1\,\text{hp} = 735.5\,\text{W}$
**Asked:** the energy converted $\Delta E$ in megajoules
**Principle:** Power tells how much energy is converted per second. When the power is steady, the energy converted is power times time. Power and time must first be converted to SI units, watts and seconds.
**Formula:** $P = \dfrac{\Delta E}{\Delta t}$, so $\Delta E = P\,\Delta t$
**Conversions:** $P = 5.0 \cdot 735.5\,\text{W} = 3677.5\,\text{W}$ and $\Delta t = 30 \cdot 60\,\text{s} = 1800\,\text{s}$. Finally $1\,\text{MJ} = 10^{6}\,\text{J}$.
**Substitution:** $\Delta E = 3677.5\,\text{W} \cdot 1800\,\text{s} = 6\,619\,500\,\text{J} \approx 6.6\,\text{MJ}$
**Answer:** The motor converts about $6.6\,\text{MJ}$ of energy. The value $735.5\,\text{W}$ of the horsepower is from the MAFY table (unit conversions); tables differ slightly, and an answer within about $\pm 4\,\%$ is accepted.
**Reasonableness:** A rough estimate: $3.7\,\text{kW} \cdot 1800\,\text{s} \approx 6.7\,\text{MJ}$. The power is about $3.7\,\text{kW}$, a little more than a kettle, and in half an hour about $1.8\,\text{kWh}$ is converted, which is a sensible amount.

## fy2-3-L1-10
**Given:** points read from the less steep line B: $(0\,\text{s};\ 0\,\text{kJ})$ and $(80\,\text{s};\ 40\,\text{kJ})$
**Asked:** the power $P$ of device B in watts
**Principle:** When a device converts energy steadily, the slope of the line in the energy-time graph is the power of the device: power is the energy converted divided by the time.
**Reading the graph:** The less steep line is B. It starts at the origin and passes through the point $(80\,\text{s};\ 40\,\text{kJ})$. The point is at a crossing of gridlines, so the values can be read exactly.
**Formula:** $P = \dfrac{\Delta E}{\Delta t}$
**Conversions:** $\Delta E = 40\,\text{kJ} = 40 \cdot 1000\,\text{J} = 40\,000\,\text{J}$.
**Substitution:** $P = \dfrac{40\,000\,\text{J}}{80\,\text{s}} = 500\,\text{W}$
**Answer:** The power of device B is $500\,\text{W}$, or $0.50\,\text{kW}$.
**Reasonableness:** The other point $(40\,\text{s};\ 20\,\text{kJ})$ gives $20\,000\,\text{J} : 40\,\text{s} = 500\,\text{W}$, the same. Device A rises more steeply and has the greater power, so the smaller power of B fits the graph.
