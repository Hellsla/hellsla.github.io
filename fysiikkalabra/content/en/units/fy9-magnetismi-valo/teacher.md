## Lesson plan (75 min)

- **0–5 min, Think about it.** The island's submarine cable. In pairs, students estimate why a high voltage is used in the cable. The answer is not given yet: it is at the end of the Key idea part of the theory summary ($230\,\text{V}$: current $174\,\text{A}$ and loss $15\,\text{kW}$, or $38\,\%$; $20\,\text{kV}$: current $2.0\,\text{A}$ and loss $2.0\,\text{W}$).
- **5–15 min, Predict (diagnostic test).** Six questions alone, without notes. Ask the students to write in their notebooks the numbers of the questions they got wrong.
- **15–25 min, Investigate.** A magnet in four tubes in pairs: why the metal tubes brake although the magnet does not stick to them, why copper brakes more than aluminium and why the slit removes the braking. Debrief together: the time for the plastic tube is the free-fall time, and in steady fall the whole decrease in potential energy turns into internal energy of the tube.
- **25–35 min, Theory summary.** Go through the concept map one branch at a time: forces in a field, induction, light. For each formula ask what its condition is (perpendicular velocity, long wire, alternating voltage, $n_1 > n_2$, polarised light). Return to Think about it with the transmission figure.
- **35–55 min, Apply.** Four multiple-choice questions in pairs (about $6\,\text{min}$), then four calculations alone. Fast students continue without hints; slower students use the hints one at a time and do only the calculations *The wings of an aeroplane* and *The diver's window* in class, the others at home. If time runs out, the calculations *The temperature of Sirius* and *An oil film on a puddle* are homework for everyone. The minimum for everyone in class is four tasks: the multiple-choice questions *Read the graph: the voltage of a generator* and *Which particle: a curving path* and the calculations *The wings of an aeroplane* and *The diver's window*.
- **55–65 min, Check your understanding and exit ticket.** Alone: the concept and calculation tasks and the exit ticket. The explanation task (the jumping ring) is homework, and students assess it themselves with the criteria.
- **65–75 min, Problems.** In ten minutes there is time for the start of one long problem under exam conditions, without the model answer: recommended *Electricity from a small hydropower plant* a–b (calculation) or *The magnetic field of a straight wire* a (graph). The rest of the problem and the other five problems are homework, two or three at a time before the next lesson.

## Using the review test
The Predict questions are a diagnostic test, not a grade. Each question corresponds to one chapter:

- Question 1: the magnetic force does not change the speed (chapter *Charged particle in electric and magnetic fields*).
- Question 2: a voltage arises only from a change of flux (chapter *Electromagnetic induction*).
- Question 3: Lenz's law opposes the change, not the field (chapter *Lenz's law and eddy currents*).
- Question 4: currents in the same direction attract each other (chapter *Force on a current-carrying wire: the electric motor and the loudspeaker*).
- Question 5: total internal reflection at an angle of incidence larger than the critical angle (chapter *Total internal reflection and optical fibre*).
- Question 6: in a grating red deviates most (chapter *Interference and diffraction of light*).

If more than a third of the group answers question 2 wrong (typical answer “a large voltage”), show the law of induction on the board and ask what changes when the magnet is at rest: the flux is large but constant, so $\tfrac{\Delta\Phi}{\Delta t} = 0$. Check your understanding question 1 and the explanation task return to the same mistake. Mistakes in question 3 (“the ring always repels”) are revisited in the Investigate task and in the explanation of the jumping ring, and the mistake in question 6 (“violet deviates most”) in the long problem *Colours in a bubble, a rainbow and a CD*.

## Pair work
- Investigate: one explains the difference between the plastic and copper tubes, the other the difference between the copper tube and the slit tube; then the power is calculated together.
- Apply multiple choice: each first chooses alone and only then justifies the choice to the partner. They answer only when the pair agrees.
- In the long problem *Colours in a bubble, a rainbow and a CD* (written explanation) the pairs can write the answers together and assess them with another pair using the marking scheme.

## Differentiation
- **Fast students** do the Apply calculations without hints and move directly to the long problem *The magnetic field of a straight wire* (graph and linearisation, $20$ points), which has the most steps. Extra question: how large a current would have to flow in the wire for the field $5.0\,\text{cm}$ away to be as large as the Earth's field $50\,\mu\text{T}$? (About $13\,\text{A}$.)
- **Slower students** do the Apply calculations *The wings of an aeroplane* and *The diver's window* with the hints and leave the calculations *The temperature of Sirius* and *An oil film on a puddle* for home. The *Review if needed* links of the theory summary lead directly to the original chapters, which have basic-level exercises.

## Assessment
Students assess the long problems themselves with the marking scheme. The points follow the division of the matriculation exam: the graph and fitting problem *The magnetic field of a straight wire* is of the Part III type ($20$ points), the other five of the Part II type ($15$ points each), $95$ points in total. If the teacher assesses them, the same criteria work: choosing the right model and justifying its condition (for example an ideal transformer, the critical-angle condition $n_1 > n_2$ or classical mechanics in the cyclotron), the symbolic solution, unit conversions by substitution, precision and reasonableness; in the written part, justifying the claim with the name of the phenomenon and with numbers. All data are invented examples, not real measurements.
