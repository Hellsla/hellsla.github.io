## Aim and level

The fourth lesson of the French chapter. Students introduce themselves at a research centre, say where people come
from, ask simple questions, ask for help and follow directions. They also learn who does research around Cadarache.
Instructions are in English; the French voice of the browser is a model, not a native speaker. The visit, the
building, its plan and the people in it are invented. The facts about the CEA, the CNRS, Cadarache, WEST and ITER are
real and dated (checked 1 November 2026).

**A real visit:** the ITER site admits visitors aged 16 or over, and groups book weeks ahead (iter.org, *Visiting
ITER*). Access to the CEA's Cadarache centre is separate; ask the CEA well in advance.

## Before the lesson

- **The optional warm-up video** (Alexandra Siopi, *Saluer - Prendre congé*, 19 November 2014) could not be played
  from the course's development environment, and its length and description were not readable. Watch it and check
  that it suits your class; the task only asks students to note greeting and leave-taking expressions. Leave it out
  if it does not fit.
- **Changing facts:** check that WEST's 1,337-second plasma (12 February 2025) is still described correctly, and
  that the ITER schedule (start of research operation 2034, 2024 baseline) is still current on iter.org.

## Timing (75 minutes)

- **0–7 min, Think about it.** Hook; pairs list what to say at a workplace and discuss why many nationalities work at ITER.
- **7–10 min, Predict.** Both predictions individually (who runs Cadarache; asking a stranger for help).
- **10–18 min, Investigate: listen.** The phrase list in chorus, once slowly, once at normal speed.
- **18–28 min, Investigate: dialogue and order.** The reception dialogue in three steps; the meeting with Ms Sharma in order; *What did you notice?*
- **28–40 min, Theory summary.** Read *Science in France* and *Cadarache, ITER and Provence* with the map; read *Asking for help* with the plan, drill the directions with gestures (point left, right, straight on); back to the predictions.
- **40–48 min, Apply.** The plan match and the gap fill individually; partners compare.
- **48–65 min, Role-play.** Two rounds with the four cards; two pairs perform, and another pair follows their directions on the plan.
- **65–75 min, Check.** Three checks individually; the message (check 4) as homework if time is short.

**Fast pairs:** the cultural investigation (optional), or a round 3 where the visitor asks for two places at once.

**Running out of time:** read the culture sections quickly with the map and skip the plan drill, do one round of the
role-play, and give the gap fill and check 4 as homework. Keep the plan match and one role-play round: following
and giving directions is the core of the lesson.

## Organisation

- Pairs throughout; mix confident and hesitant speakers. Partners must not see each other's role cards (print them
  or cover half the screen).
- Directions practice works well standing up: one student gives directions in French and the other walks them in the classroom.

## Assessment of speaking (formative)

Use the criteria under the role-play: **communicates the message** (introduction, request), **polite**
(*<span lang="fr">vous</span>*, *<span lang="fr">Excusez-moi</span>*, thanks), **directions right** (they match the plan), **checks understanding**
(asks for a repetition or repeats the way). Note one strength and one point to work on for each pair.

## Common difficulties

- *<span lang="fr">Je viens de Japon</span>*, *<span lang="fr">je viens de Inde</span>*: practise *<span lang="fr">de / d' / du / des</span>* with the countries of ITER's members.
- *<span lang="fr">Droit</span>* (straight) and *<span lang="fr">droite</span>* (right) sound alike; the final *t* is heard only in *<span lang="fr">droite</span>*.
- The French *<span lang="fr">premier étage</span>* taken as the Finnish first floor.
- ITER and Cadarache confused, or ITER thought to belong to the CNRS or the CEA.

## Follow-up

The last unit, *Reading French news without a dictionary*, uses a short news text about WEST and ITER. The names
and facts from this lesson (CEA, Cadarache, ITER, the seven members) make it much easier to read.
