## fy8-7-L1-01
**Given:** wavelength of the laser light $\lambda = 633\,\text{nm}$, $h = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s}$, $c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$, $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$
**Find:** the energy $E$ of one laser photon in electronvolts
**Principle:** All the photons of a laser are produced in the same transition, so they have the same energy, and the energy of one photon depends only on the wavelength of the light.
**Formula:** $E = hf$ and $f = \tfrac{c}{\lambda}$, so $E = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$
**Conversions:** $\lambda = 633\,\text{nm} = 633 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}$. Finally joules are converted to electronvolts by substituting $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$.
**Substitution:** $E = (6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s} \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}) : (633 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}) = 3.1382 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J} = \dfrac{3.1382 \cdot 10^{-19}}{1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}}\,\text{eV} = 1.9589\,\text{eV}$
**Answer:** $1.96\,\text{eV}$, three significant figures like the wavelength $633\,\text{nm}$.
**Reasonableness:** The energies of photons of visible light are about $1.7$–$3.3\,\text{eV}$, and red light is at the low-energy end of the range, like the result. The photon of the red line of hydrogen, $656\,\text{nm}$, has the energy $1.89\,\text{eV}$; at a slightly shorter wavelength the energy is slightly greater.

## fy8-7-L1-03
**Given:** energy difference of the levels of the laser transition $E_2 - E_1 = 1.79\,\text{eV}$, $h = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s}$, $c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$, $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$
**Find:** the wavelength $\lambda$ of the laser light in nanometres
**Principle:** Energy is conserved: when an ion drops from the upper laser level to the lower, its energy decreases by the difference of the levels, and exactly this energy is carried away by one photon.
**Formula:** $\dfrac{hc}{\lambda} = E_2 - E_1$, from which $\lambda = \dfrac{hc}{E_2 - E_1}$
**Conversions:** $E_2 - E_1 = 1.79\,\text{eV} = 1.79 \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J} = 2.8676 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$; finally $1\,\text{m} = 10^{9}\,\text{nm}$.
**Substitution:** $\lambda = (6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s} \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}) : (2.8676 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}) = \dfrac{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}}{2.8676 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}} = 6.9274 \cdot 10^{-7}\,\text{m} = 692.74\,\text{nm}$
**Answer:** $693\,\text{nm}$, three significant figures like the energy difference $1.79\,\text{eV}$.
**Reasonableness:** The result is at the red end of visible light, and the light of a ruby laser is dark red with a wavelength of about $694\,\text{nm}$. The energy $1.79\,\text{eV}$ is at the low end of the range of visible light, $1.7$–$3.3\,\text{eV}$, which fits red light.

**Answer:** $75.9\,\%$, three significant figures like the wavelength $808\,\text{nm}$.
**Reasonableness:** The share is below $100\,\%$, because the wavelength of the laser photon is longer and its energy therefore smaller than that of the pump photon. As energies, $\tfrac{1.17\,\text{eV}}{1.53\,\text{eV}} \approx 0.76$, the same result. The real efficiency is lower still, because not all pump photons produce a laser photon.

## fy8-7-L3-01

## fy8-7-L1-06
**Find:** what distinguishes stimulated emission from spontaneous emission
**Principle:** In both an excited atom drops from the upper level $E_2$ to the lower level $E_1$ and emits a photon whose energy is $E_2 - E_1$. The difference is in what triggers the emission and what the photon produced is like.
**Model answer:** In spontaneous emission the atom emits a photon on its own, and its direction or phase has no connection with other photons: the direction is random. In stimulated emission a photon with the energy $E_2 - E_1$ hits the atom and makes it emit a new photon. The new photon leaves in the same direction and in the same phase as the incoming photon, and the incoming photon continues on its way. That is why stimulated emission amplifies the light and makes it directed and coherent. The atom does not absorb a photon in stimulated emission, and its energy does not rise in either of these events, and the energy of the photon produced is the same $E_2 - E_1$ in both.

## fy8-7-L1-08
**Find:** what population inversion means
**Principle:** An incoming photon with the energy $E_2 - E_1$ can either be absorbed by an atom on the lower level or make an atom on the upper level emit a new photon. Which happens more often depends on which level has more atoms.
**Model answer:** Population inversion is a state in which there are more atoms on the upper laser level than on the lower, $N_2 > N_1$. Then more stimulated emissions than absorptions take place, and the light passing through is amplified. Usually the situation is the opposite: almost all the atoms are on the lower level, so absorption wins and the light is attenuated. Inversion does not mean that the directions of the photons are the same or that the level is the other way round, but how the atoms are distributed among the levels. It is achieved by pumping atoms to the upper level.

## fy8-7-L1-09
**Find:** which of the five statements about the laser and quantum dots A and B are true.
**Principle:** The energy of a photon depends only on the wavelength, $hf = \tfrac{hc}{\lambda}$, not on what kind of light source emits it. Laser light is produced in stimulated emission, which requires population inversion $N_2 > N_1$; it is achieved by pumping a material that has at least three levels. In a quantum dot the energy gap grows as the dot gets smaller.
**Model answer:** The energy of a $650\,\text{nm}$ photon of both light sources is

$hf = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$

$$\begin{aligned}
&hc = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s} \\
&\quad \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s} \\
&= 1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}$$

$$\begin{aligned}
&hf = \dfrac{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}}{650 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}} \\
&= 3.056 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}$$

In electronvolts, when $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$:

$$\begin{aligned}
&hf = \dfrac{3.056 \cdot 10^{-19}}{1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}}\,\text{eV} \\
&= 1.91\,\text{eV}
\end{aligned}$$

1 false: the energies of the photons are the same, $1.91\,\text{eV}$. The danger of a laser comes from the whole power travelling in a narrow beam, which the eye focuses to a small spot.

2 true: a stimulated photon gets the energy, direction and phase of the incoming photon, so the waves of laser light are in step.

3 false: in the two-level model the pumping light causes stimulated emission in atoms of the upper state as probably as it excites atoms of the ground state, so at most half of the atoms can be brought to the upper state. In atomic and ion lasers, population inversion is made by pumping a material that has three or four levels and a long-lived upper laser level.

4 false: dot A is smaller, so its energy gap is greater and the energy of the photon is greater. So dot A emits light of a shorter wavelength than dot B.

5 true: light along the axis travels through the material between the mirrors many times and is amplified, and the beam comes out through the partly transmitting mirror. Photons that go sideways leave the material and do not have time to multiply.

## fy8-7-L3-04
**Given:** wavelength of the pump light $\lambda_\text{p} = 976\,\text{nm}$ and power $P_\text{p} = 20.0\,\text{W}$, wavelength of the laser light $\lambda_\text{L} = 1070\,\text{nm}$ and power $P_\text{L} = 15.0\,\text{W}$, all the pump light is absorbed, the rest of the energy is heat, $h = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s}$, $c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$
**Find:** the quantum efficiency $\eta_\text{q} = \tfrac{N_\text{L}}{N_\text{p}}$ as a percentage
**Principle:** The power is the number of photons per second multiplied by the energy of a photon $\tfrac{hc}{\lambda}$, so the numbers of photons are obtained from the powers. Energy is conserved, so the thermal power is the pump power minus the laser power. Heat is produced for two reasons: the energy of a laser photon is smaller than the energy of a pump photon, and some of the pump photons do not produce a laser photon.
**Formula:** $\dfrac{N}{t} = \dfrac{P\lambda}{hc}$, so $\eta_\text{q} = \dfrac{P_\text{L}\lambda_\text{L}}{P_\text{p}\lambda_\text{p}}$, thermal power $P_\text{heat} = P_\text{p} - P_\text{L}$ and the unavoidable part $P_\text{unav} = \dfrac{N_\text{L}}{t}\left(\dfrac{hc}{\lambda_\text{p}} - \dfrac{hc}{\lambda_\text{L}}\right)$
**Conversions:** $\lambda_\text{p} = 976 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}$ and $\lambda_\text{L} = 1070 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}$; the powers are already in watts.
**Substitution:**

a) $hc = 1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}$. Pump photons: $\tfrac{N_\text{p}}{t} = \dfrac{20.0\,\text{W} \cdot 976 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}}{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}} = 9.826 \cdot 10^{19}\,\tfrac{1}{\text{s}}$. Laser photons: $\tfrac{N_\text{L}}{t} = \dfrac{15.0\,\text{W} \cdot 1070 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}}{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}} = 8.080 \cdot 10^{19}\,\tfrac{1}{\text{s}}$.

b) $\eta_\text{q} = \dfrac{8.080 \cdot 10^{19}\,\tfrac{1}{\text{s}}}{9.826 \cdot 10^{19}\,\tfrac{1}{\text{s}}} = 0.8222 = 82.2\,\%$.

c) The thermal power is $20.0\,\text{W} - 15.0\,\text{W} = 5.0\,\text{W}$. The energy difference of the photons is $\dfrac{hc}{\lambda_\text{p}} - \dfrac{hc}{\lambda_\text{L}} = 2.0354 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J} - 1.8566 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J} = 1.788 \cdot 10^{-20}\,\text{J}$, so the unavoidable part is $P_\text{unav} = 8.080 \cdot 10^{19}\,\tfrac{1}{\text{s}} \cdot 1.788 \cdot 10^{-20}\,\text{J} = 1.44\,\text{W}$, that is, about $29\,\%$ of the thermal power.

The difference between the energies of a pump photon and a laser photon remains in the medium as heat, even if every pump photon produced a laser photon.

**Reasonableness:** The quantum efficiency is less than $100\,\%$, because no more laser photons are produced than pump photons. The upper limit of the energy efficiency is $\tfrac{976}{1070} = 91.2\,\%$, and the actual $75.0\,\%$ is below it, as it should be. The unavoidable heat $1.44\,\text{W}$ is smaller than the whole thermal power $5.0\,\text{W}$, as it should be.

## fy8-7-L3-05
**Given:** wavelength of the pump light $\lambda_\text{p} = 808\,\text{nm}$, wavelength of the laser light $\lambda_\text{L} = 1064\,\text{nm}$ and the output powers as a function of the pump power (table)
**Find:** the number of laser photons per additional pump photon above the threshold, $\tfrac{\Delta N_\text{L}}{\Delta N_\text{p}}$
**Principle:** Above the threshold the output power grows directly with the pump power, so the points lie on a straight line. The power is the number of photons multiplied by the energy of a photon, so the change in the numbers of photons is obtained from the change in the powers by dividing each power by the energy of its own photon. Below the threshold the light is not amplified more than it is lost through the mirror and elsewhere; inversion is a necessary condition, but the threshold is the balance of gain and losses.
**Reading the graph:** The points $(10;\ 0.0)$, $(20;\ 0.1)$, $(30;\ 2.1)$, $(40;\ 6.1)$, $(50;\ 10.0)$, $(60;\ 13.9)$ and $(70;\ 18.1)$ of the table are plotted in a coordinate system ($P_\text{p}$ in milliwatts on the horizontal axis, $P_\text{L}$ in milliwatts on the vertical axis). The points $P_\text{p} \geq 30\,\text{mW}$ lie on a straight line apart from small variation, and the first two points are close to zero. The equation of the fitted line is $P_\text{L} = 0.398 \cdot P_\text{p} - 9.86\,\text{mW}$. The line crosses the horizontal axis when $P_\text{L} = 0$, that is, $P_\text{p} = \tfrac{9.86\,\text{mW}}{0.398} = 24.8\,\text{mW}$. The threshold power is thus about $25\,\text{mW}$. The slope of the line is $\tfrac{\Delta P_\text{L}}{\Delta P_\text{p}} = 0.398$. At the threshold the gain is equal to the losses, and above the threshold additional pumping turns into laser light.
