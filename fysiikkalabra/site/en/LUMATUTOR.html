LUMA tutor

Science belongs to everyone.

Inspire others to explore science! As a LUMA tutor you visit science events, design your own science workshops and guide others to investigate phenomena in the natural sciences, mathematics and technology.

At a glance

  • What do you do in the course?You learn about science education, visit a science event, plan your own science workshop and guide others to explore and try things out.
  • How do you complete the course?You work through the five parts at your own pace during the school year. The key evidence is guiding a real science workshop or event.
  • How much work is it?About $28.5\,\text{h}$, planned as $23 \cdot 75\,\text{min}$. That is the total workload, not 23 lessons in a classroom.
  • Where do I start?With the induction. Read its first topic and start your portfolio with your starting point.
  • What do I need to document?In your portfolio you record your exercises, what you observed on a visit, your workshop plan, the times you guided, your working time and your self-assessment.

Planning decision The scope of the course, the split of the workload and the assessment model are a planning decision. They apply only once they have been confirmed in Kiviruukki upper secondary school's local curriculum. Ready guide, not yet piloted: Electric wonder – build a circuit, Whose paper bridge holds the most?, Secret codes and mathematics, Explore light and colours, Climate data detectives.

What is a LUMA tutor?

A LUMA tutor works as a science tutor: they plan science events and take part in them. LUMA comes from the Finnish words for natural sciences and mathematics, and technology belongs to it too.

The course is not tutoring in mathematics, physics or chemistry. Its subject is science education and science communication: how to get children, young people and other students excited about phenomena, asking questions and trying things out themselves.

LUMA tutor is Kiviruukki upper secondary school's own programme. It complements Ruukkilabra, where you study science yourself, and Ahjo Makerspace, where you build and make. As a LUMA tutor you take science to others.

How you progress

The course has five parts. Work through them in order, but the parts may overlap with the events of the school year: you can, for example, visit a science event while you are still in the induction.

  1. 1Get readyYou learn how to spark interest, ask, explain and guide a group safely. Ten short topics with exercises.Workload: $3 \cdot 75\,\text{min}$
  2. 2Visit science eventsYou visit at least one science event and look at it through the eyes of a science communicator. You collect ideas for your own workshop.Workload: $4 \cdot 75\,\text{min}$
  3. 3Plan a workshopYou choose a workshop from the workshop bank or plan your own, test it and improve it based on feedback.Workload: $4 \cdot 75\,\text{min}$
  4. 4Run itYou run science workshops and help organise science events. This is the most important evidence in the course.Workload: $9 \cdot 75\,\text{min}$
  5. 5Assess and hand inYou put together your tutor portfolio, log your working time and assess how you have developed. Finally, a feedback talk with the teacher.Workload: $3 \cdot 75\,\text{min}$

Goals

After the course you can

  • say what science education and science communication are and why they are needed
  • explain a scientific phenomenon with language and pictures that suit the audience
  • ask questions that make a participant think and try things out themselves
  • guide a group so that everyone gets to take part
  • plan, test and improve a hands-on science workshop
  • take care of safety as your role requires and know when to call an adult
  • work as part of an event's organising team and take responsibility for your share
  • assess your own work and develop it based on feedback

Workload

The total workload of the course is about $38 \cdot 45\,\text{min} = 1\,710\,\text{min}$, or $28.5\,\text{h}$. That equals $22.8$ lessons of $75\,\text{min}$. For planning, the workload is rounded to $23 \cdot 75\,\text{min}$.

$23 \cdot 75\,\text{min} = 1\,725\,\text{min}$, or $28\,\text{h}\ 45\,\text{min}$. This is $15\,\text{min}$ more than the exact workload. The rounding is intentional.

  • The workload is the student's total workload, not 23 separate contact lessons.
  • It includes the shared induction meetings, independent preparation, event visits, planning and testing the workshop, working at events, and the portfolio and self-assessment.
  • Events vary in length, so you log your actual working time in minutes on the working time log.
  • There may be reasonable flexibility between the parts if you agree on it with the teacher. The total scope and the goals stay the same.

Completion

To pass, you need all six of these:

  1. You complete the tutor induction.
  2. You take part in at least one suitable visit to a science event.
  3. You plan and test a science workshop.
  4. You take part in guiding a science workshop or science event in practice.
  5. You document your participation and assess your work in a portfolio.
  6. Your total workload matches the scope of the course.

Assessment

The course is assessed as pass or fail. Filling the hours is not enough on its own: the teacher assesses your work and your portfolio in the following areas.

  • Guiding skills

    You let the participants do things themselves, you ask before you tell, and you notice who is left out.

  • Preparation

    You have tried the workshop in advance, the equipment is ready and you know what to do if something does not work.

  • Cooperation

    You agree on tasks with the other tutors and the teacher and keep to what was agreed.

  • Responsibility

    You follow the safety instructions, respect the participants and do not collect personal data about them.

  • Active participation

    You are at the events for the whole agreed time and take on tasks on your own initiative.

  • Development and reflection

    Your portfolio shows what you learned, what changed and what you would do differently next time.

Responsibility and safety

The teacher is responsible for supervision and for organising safety. You are never alone the person responsible at an event for minors. No personal data is collected from the children who take part in the workshops: no names and no identifiable photos.

Pedagogical models

The structure of the course draws on ideas that work in the following programmes. Ruukkilabra's text is its own, and none of the programmes is a partner of LUMA tutor.

  • OHSU Science Ambassadors (opens in a new tab) Oregon Health & Science UniversityUpper secondary students learn current science, develop their leadership skills and take science activities to younger students.
  • Peer Tutor Training Program (opens in a new tab) University of PennsylvaniaUpper secondary students are trained as tutors before they guide younger ones: training and guiding run side by side.
  • PASS (opens in a new tab) Monash UniversityA student guides their peers in a group. The leader does not lecture again but guides the group to solve things on their own and together.
  • LUMAlab Gadolin (opens in a new tab) University of HelsinkiHands-on science workshops where research-based science education turns into doing.
  • Tiedeluokka SOLU (opens in a new tab) Päijät-Häme LUMA CentreA group visits a science classroom: an expert guides, and the group's own teacher is there the whole time.
