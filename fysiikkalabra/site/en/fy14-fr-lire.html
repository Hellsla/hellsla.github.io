Reading French news without a dictionary

How much of a French news text about fusion can you understand after a few weeks of French?

Newspapers, websites and signs in Provence often write about ITER and fusion. In this lesson you read a short French news text, written for this course in the style of a regional newspaper, with real and checked facts. You read it without a dictionary and without a translation app.

In this lesson, you will learn to

  • find information in a short French news text without a dictionary.
  • use look-alike words in English, Swedish and Finnish, word endings and the context to work out new words.
  • recognise false friends: French words that look like English ones but mean something else.

Can you find out what the article says, and how?

The French words mean temperature, a reactor, international, physics and a kilometre: they look almost the same in English, and Swedish and Finnish have similar words (temperatur, reaktor, fysiikka, kilometri).

    Make your predictions before investigating 2 questions

    Prediction 1: how much will you understand?

    You will read a French news text of about 200 words, with no dictionary and five minutes of time.How much do you think you will understand? Commit now; your reading and the theory will show you.

    • Speaking and reading are different skills. Names, dates, numbers and look-alike words (record, physiciens, projet) give most readers the topic and the main facts even after a few weeks of French.

    • This is what most beginners manage: names, numbers and international words carry the topic and most of the facts. The theory shows why: thousands of French words have relatives in English, Swedish and Finnish.

    • A few words have no look-alike (aimants, chantier) and some grammar is new, so word-by-word comprehension is unlikely. But you do not need it: the topic and the main facts are enough.

    • You will understand much more than names: numbers, dates and many look-alike words (record, physiciens, projet) carry the facts.

    3Investigate

    Step 1: a first look (two minutes)

    Read only the title, the place, the names and every number. Do not read the sentences yet. Then answer the question below.

    Fusion nucléaire : record de durée à Cadarache

    Saint-Paul-lez-Durance (Bouches-du-Rhône). Le 12 février 2025, dans leur tokamak WEST, installé au centre de Cadarache, les physiciens du CEA ont maintenu un plasma pendant 1 337 secondes, soit plus de 22 minutes. Selon le CEA, ils ont battu un record mondial de durée : c'est 25 % de plus que le record du tokamak chinois EAST, établi quelques semaines plus tôt.

    Un plasma est un gaz très chaud, à plusieurs dizaines de millions de degrés. Aucun matériau ne résiste à cette température : la machine utilise donc des aimants très puissants pour garder le plasma loin des parois.

    Selon les chercheurs, ce résultat est important pour ITER, le grand projet international construit juste à côté. Sept membres participent au projet : la Chine, l'Union européenne, l'Inde, le Japon, la Corée, la Russie et les États-Unis. Leur objectif : produire 500 mégawatts de puissance de fusion avec seulement 50 mégawatts de chauffage. Attention : ITER ne produira pas d'électricité. C'est une expérience scientifique.

    Le chantier avance. En juillet 2026, les équipes ont installé le sixième des neuf modules qui forment le cœur de la machine, avec presque six mois d'avance sur le calendrier. Selon le calendrier actuel, les premières expériences scientifiques commenceront en 2034.

    The text was written for this course in the style of a newspaper; the facts were checked against the CEA, EUROfusion and ITER in November 2026.

    What is the text about?

    From the title, the names and the numbers only: what is the text about?

    • Yes. Record, WEST, 1 337 secondes, ITER, 2026 and 2034 already give you the topic: a long plasma at Cadarache and the ITER worksite.

    • The text speaks of a tokamak and of ITER, which ne produira pas d'électricité. It is a scientific record and a worksite, not a power station.

    • Seven countries and the EU are named, but as ITER's members, not at a meeting. The title speaks of a record at Cadarache.

    • Nothing in the title or the numbers suggests an accident: record, secondes and projet point to a scientific success and a building project.

    Step 2: hunt for look-alikes

    Now read the sentences. Underline in your head (or on paper) every word that looks like a word you know in English, Swedish or Finnish. Then work out the words below.

    Step 3: read for detail

    4Theory

    Why you understand more than you think

    You can already recognise many French words, especially through English, Swedish and international scientific vocabulary. English took a very large number of words from French and Latin, Swedish took many from French too (trottoar from trottoir, paraply from parapluie, byrå from bureau), and Finnish took international words through Swedish (insinööri from Swedish ingenjör, which comes from French ingénieur). Science uses the same international words everywhere: plasma, tokamak, physiciens, projet.

    Good readers of a foreign language use five strategies:

    1. Start with what is easy: the title, the place, the names, the dates and the numbers. They tell you the topic before you read a single sentence.
    2. Use look-alike words in English, Swedish or Finnish. Read them aloud if the spelling hides them.
    3. Use word endings. Many French endings have a regular English partner: ‑ité = ‑ity (électricité), ‑ique = ‑ic (scientifique), ‑eur = ‑or (réacteur), ‑ment = ‑ly (seulement, only).
    4. Guess from the context. What does the sentence need? In la machine utilise des aimants très puissants pour garder le plasma loin des parois, the aimants hold a hot gas, so they must be magnets.
    5. Skip what you do not need. You rarely need every word to get the message.

    False friends

    Some French words look like English words but mean something else. They are called false friends:

    French Means (and not)
    actuel, actuellement current, currently (not actual, actually)
    une expérience an experiment, or an experience
    un milliard a billion, $10^{9}$ (not a million)
    un billion $10^{12}$, a trillion (not a billion)
    éventuellement possibly (not eventually)
    une librairie a bookshop (not a library)
    le calendrier a calendar, but also a schedule

    When a look-alike gives a sentence a strange meaning, check it against the context.

    Summary

    Strategy Example from the news text
    title, names, numbers first Cadarache, WEST, 12 février 2025, 1 337 secondes
    look-alikes (English, Swedish, Finnish) record, physiciens, matériau, projet
    word endings électricité (‑ity), scientifique (‑ic), seulement (‑ly)
    context aimants = magnets, chantier = construction site
    watch for false friends actuel = current, expérience = experiment

    Partial comprehension is real comprehension: the topic, the facts and the numbers are already most of a news text.

      Write: the text in English

      A friend who does not read French asks what the article says. Write a summary in English in four or five sentences: what happened, where and when, why it matters for ITER, and what the text says about ITER's progress.

      Tell your partner first, in English, without looking at the text; then write the summary together.

      Model answer

      On 12 February 2025, physicists of the French CEA kept a plasma in their tokamak WEST at Cadarache for 1,337 seconds, more than 22 minutes. The CEA called it a world record: 25 % longer than China's EAST a few weeks before. The machine uses strong magnets to keep the very hot plasma away from the walls. The researchers say the result matters for ITER, the international project next door, which aims to produce 500 MW of fusion power from 50 MW of heating but will not produce electricity. In July 2026 six of the nine core modules of ITER were installed, almost six months ahead of schedule, and research operation is planned to start in 2034.

          6Self-assessment

          Finally, assess whether you have reached the chapter's goals. If an answer is wrong, you get a suggestion of what to review.

          1. Concept task: A false friend

          A French news text says: « Actuellement, 5 000 personnes travaillent sur le site. » What does actuellement mean?

          • Actuellement is about now: currently. Finally is finalement.

          • The number is a round figure, but the word means currently, at present. Exactly is exactement.

          • Yes: actuellement is a false friend. The sentence says that about 5,000 people work on the site at present.

          • This is the trap: actuellement looks like actually, but it means currently. Actually is en fait in French.

          Suggestion: one answer does not show everything you can do, but review the theory.

          2. Concept task: A big number

          A French article says that a project costs « plusieurs milliards d'euros ». What does un milliard mean?

          • That is the French un billion, another false friend. Un milliard is $10^{9}$, a billion.

          • A million is un million. Un milliard is a thousand times more: $10^{9}$, a billion.

          • Yes: un milliard is a thousand million, the English billion (Swedish miljard, Finnish miljardi).

          • A thousand is mille. Un milliard is $10^{9}$, a billion: Swedish miljard, Finnish miljardi.

          Suggestion: one answer does not show everything you can do, but review the theory.

          3. Concept task: An unknown word

          In a French text you meet les aimants, and it looks like no word you know. What is the best first step?

          • Skipping one word can be fine, but not the whole paragraph: it has the key facts. Read on, and the context shows that aimants are magnets.

          • Yes. The sentence says the aimants are very strong and keep the hot plasma away from the walls: they must be magnets. Context often solves a word.

          • A guess from the spelling alone can mislead: aimants has nothing to do with aims. The context (strong, keeping plasma from the walls) shows they are magnets.

          • Looking a word up is fine later, but stopping at every word loses the meaning of the text. Read on first: the context shows that aimants are magnets.

          Suggestion: one answer does not show everything you can do, but review the theory.

          4. Explanation task: Write: how I read French

          Explain to a classmate who starts French next year how to read a short French text without a dictionary. Name three strategies and give one example of each from today's texts (four to six sentences, in English).

          Model answer

          First read the title, the names and the numbers: Cadarache, WEST, 12 février 2025 and 1 337 secondes already tell you that the text is about a record. Then look for words that look like English, Swedish or Finnish words, such as physiciens (physicists, fysiker) or projet (project, projekti). When a word looks like nothing you know, read on and use the context: aimants keep a very hot plasma away from the walls, so they must be magnets. Be careful with false friends: actuel means current, not actual.
