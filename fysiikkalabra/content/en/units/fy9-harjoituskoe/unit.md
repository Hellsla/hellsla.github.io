## Exam instructions {Ohjeet}

### Structure
The practice exam is built like the physics matriculation exam. You have six hours. The exam has $11$ problems in three parts, and you answer seven:

| Part | What you answer | Points |
|---|---|---|
| Part I | compulsory, $10$ short items | $20$ p |
| Part II | $4$ problems out of seven | $4 \cdot 15$ p |
| Part III | $2$ data-based problems out of three | $2 \cdot 20$ p |

The maximum score of the exam is $120$ p. If you answer more problems in Part II or III than the instructions say, count only the ones you chose before opening the model answers when you assess the practice exam.

### Points in Part I
Each of the ten items of Part I is worth $2$ p, $20$ p in total:

- items 1–4, multiple choice: $4 \cdot 2$ p $= 8$ p;
- items 5–7, statement and justification: $3 \cdot 2$ p $= 6$ p;
- items 8–10, short calculations: $3 \cdot 2$ p $= 6$ p.

### Parts II and III
The problems of Parts II and III are at the end of the page under Problems, and their title says which part each problem belongs to. The problems of Part II are worth $15$ p and the data-based problems of Part III $20$ p. The points of each part of a problem are marked on it. Write a complete solution for every calculation: the given and requested quantities, the principle, the formula solved for the unknown, the substitution with units, the answer to the right precision and a reasonableness check.

### Aids
Use the same aids as in the real exam: the computer's calculator program, a spreadsheet, a graphing program and a formula editor, together with the exam's tables and formulas. A separate calculator or a printed book of tables is not allowed in the physics exam. Your notes, the textbook, the internet and the model answers on this page stay closed throughout the exam. The programs and materials allowed in the real exam are given in the regulations of the Matriculation Examination Board, so check them before the exam.

- **Read and choose, $15\,\text{min}$.** Read all the problems. From Part II choose the four, and from Part III the two, whose phenomenon you recognise and whose every part you can begin to answer.
- **Part I, $50\,\text{min}$.** About five minutes per item. If an item does not open up, move on to the next one and come back to it at the end.
- **Part II, $160\,\text{min}$.** About $40\,\text{min}$ per problem.
- **Part III, $110\,\text{min}$.** About $55\,\text{min}$ per problem. In a data-based problem drawing the graph takes time, so start with it.
- **Check, $25\,\text{min}$.** Go through the units, the significant figures and that every question has been answered.

During the exam, write down how long each problem took. In the self-assessment you will see where the time went.

## Self-assessment {Itsearviointi}

Assess the exam only when the six hours have passed or you have answered all the problems you chose.

### Part I
The page checks the items of Part I. Give yourself $2$ p for a multiple-choice item and a statement if your first choice was correct, otherwise $0$ p. In the real exam you cannot change your answer after seeing feedback, so do not change your choice. A short calculation gives $2$ p if the result is correct and your solution has the formula, the substitution with units and the answer with its unit, and $1$ p if the method is correct but the result is wrong or the unit is missing.

### Parts II and III
1. Open the model answer and marking scheme of a problem only after you have marked the problem as done.
2. Go through the marking scheme criterion by criterion. Give the points of a criterion only if your answer contains what the criterion requires. A good intention or a correct number without justification is not enough.
3. Read the section *Common mistakes*: if you made one of them, check that it did not earn you points. The section *Also accepted* tells which other solutions are correct.
4. Add up the points of each problem and of the whole exam, at most $120$ p.

### What next
Every problem has a list of the chapters whose skills it uses. If you got less than half the points of a problem, review these chapters and do one more problem from them. Also compare your use of time with the plan: if some part took much more time, practise its type of problem before the real exam.
