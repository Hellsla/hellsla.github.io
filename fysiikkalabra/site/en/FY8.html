Matter, radiation and quantisation

Why is the energy of an atom quantised, and where does radiation come from?

5 lessons available · 11 more coming

Lessons

Quantisation and the atom

  1. Chapter 1The photoelectric effect and the photon

    Why does even weak ultraviolet light release electrons from a zinc plate, while bright red light does not release a single one?

  2. Chapter 2Spectra reveal the energy levels of atoms

    Why do narrow dark lines cross the rainbow colours of sunlight, and how can they tell us which elements there are in the Sun?

  3. Chapter 3Energy levels of the hydrogen atom and the Bohr model

    Why does the light of a lamp containing hydrogen split on a grating into just four clearly visible coloured lines, and why exactly at the wavelengths $656\,\text{nm}$, $486\,\text{nm}$, $434\,\text{nm}$ and $410\,\text{nm}$?

  4. Chapter 4Light and electrons as waves and particles

    Why does very weak light or an electron beam draw first random single points on the screen, from which a fringe pattern arises only after thousands of hits?

  5. Chapter 5The wave-mechanical model of the atom and orbitals

    Why is the electron of an atom drawn in a chemistry book as a fuzzy cloud and not as a small ball going round the nucleus along an orbit?

Module goals

Show all 3 goals

The student

  • knows the effects of ionising radiation and becomes familiar with the safe use of radiation
  • becomes familiar with the worldview based on quantum physics, from elementary particle physics to cosmology
  • understands the importance of technology based on quantisation in modern society
