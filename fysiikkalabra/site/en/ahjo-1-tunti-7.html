Lesson 7 · Electronics and Arduino

Light without a program

Circuits and components

Power source, resistor, LED and breadboard.

In this lesson you learn: You connect an LED and a series resistor and explain the difference between series and parallel circuits.

Start right away (5–10 min)

Connect an LED and a 330 Ω resistor on the breadboard as in the wiring picture and take the wires to the Arduino's 5V and GND pins. The LED lights when you plug in the USB cable. No program is needed.

An LED circuit without a program →

Try · Modify · Challenge yourself

  1. Try for everyone

    Build the circuit as in the wiring picture. Always make changes with the USB cable unplugged.

  2. Modify your own ideas

    Add a second LED first in series and then in parallel. In parallel each LED gets its own 330 Ω resistor. In which circuit do the LEDs shine brighter, and why?

  3. Challenge yourself optional

    Size the series resistor with Ohm's law and compare the measured current with your calculation.

What did you make?

Basic level: A working LED circuit and an observation of the difference between series and parallel.

Challenge yourself: The series resistor worked out with Ohm's law and the matching measured current.

You need

  • Arduino Uno and USB cable (as a power source only)
  • breadboard and 4–6 wires
  • two LEDs and two 330 Ω resistors
  • multimeter for the challenge

Step-by-step guide

  • An LED circuit without a program
  • Resistance and Ohm's law
  • Small extra challenge: A sewn light-up badge
