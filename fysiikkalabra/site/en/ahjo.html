Ahjo Makerspace

Ahjo is Kiviruukin lukio's shared space for making: a cross-curricular STEAM learning environment where ideas become models, prototypes and solutions.

Kiviruukin lukio opens in autumn 2027. Ahjo's equipment, guides and safety instructions will be ready before that, and this page grows as they are completed.

Ahjo 1Technology basics

Being planned

Basics of 3D modelling, 3D printing, electronics and robotics.

Contents

  • 3D modelling
  • 3D printing
  • Electronics and Arduino
  • Basics of programming and robotics
  • Getting to know the equipment and working safely

Draft: the goals and the lesson outline await the teacher's approval.

Goals

In this course, you will learn to

  • work safely in Ahjo and use its equipment as you were introduced to it
  • model objects to measure and make them with a 3D printer
  • build simple circuits and measure them with a multimeter
  • program a microcontroller and use sensors and actuators
  • build and program a simple robot
  • document your work and assess how well it succeeded
16 lessons
  1. Welcome to Ahjo The space, the equipment and how we work; the rules and the induction.Example: Map Ahjo's equipment and pick one real problem you could solve with it during the course, such as gear for your hobby or a tool that helps you study.
  2. Working safely Hand and power tools, protective equipment and an orderly workstation.Example: A risk assessment as at a workplace: choose one machine, identify its hazards and write instructions someone else can work safely from.
  3. Basics of 3D modelling Basic shapes, joining and cutting in a modelling program.Example: Model an ergonomic handle, a case or a spare part for something broken and justify your design choices.
  4. A model to measure Dimensions, tolerances and fitting parts together.Example: Design a two-part joint (snap fit or hinge) and find by experiment the clearance at which the parts fit together.
  5. From model to print Slicing, print settings and using the printer.Example: An optimisation challenge: the lightest printed truss that carries 2 kg. Compare how infill, layer height and print orientation affect strength and material use.
  6. Judging a print and 3D scanning Print defects and how to fix them; scanning an object into a model.Example: Scan an object, repair the model and print a scale copy. How accurate is it, and where do the differences come from?
  7. Circuits and components Power source, resistor, LED and breadboard.Example: Size an LED's series resistor with Ohm's law, build the circuit and compare the measured current with your calculation. What happens without the resistor?
  8. Measuring with a multimeter Measuring voltage, current and resistance connected the right way.Example: Measure the current drawn by a phone charger, an Arduino and a motor in different situations and estimate how long a battery would power your device.
  9. A first Arduino program The programming environment, digital outputs and a blinking LED.Example: Program a reaction-time game that measures reaction time in milliseconds and computes the class's mean and spread.
  10. Sensors and inputs Button, light and distance sensors; conditions and loops.Example: Build a measuring device: a classroom CO₂, noise or temperature logger that records data. Plot a graph of a lesson's data.
  11. Motors and actuators Servo and DC motor; power supply and control.Example: Use a servo to turn a solar panel towards the light and measure how much power tracking adds compared with a fixed panel.
  12. Soldering Using a soldering station and soldering a small circuit.Example: Solder your measuring device onto stripboard so that it lasts. Test the joints with a multimeter and compare reliability with the breadboard.
  13. Building a robot The structure of a robotics kit, drive train and sensors.Example: Build a robot that carries a load around a course as fast as possible. Compare how the gear ratio affects speed and pulling force.
  14. Programming a robot Movement, sensor control and solving a task with a program.Example: Program a robot to find its way past obstacles with its sensors. Compare simple and proportional (P) control in line following.
  15. Mini project A small device of your own with the methods learned: design and build.Example: A small device of your own with the methods learned, such as a plant moisture monitor, a bike light system or a sports performance meter.
  16. Presentation and self-assessment Presenting and documenting the mini project and assessing your own learning.Example: Present your device as in a product review: goal, solution, measurements and what you would do in the next version.

Ahjo 2Design and build

Being planned

Design, build and test your own prototype.

Contents

  • Ideation and defining the problem
  • Design and prototyping
  • Building
  • Testing and improving
  • Documentation and assessment

Draft: the goals and the lesson outline await the teacher's approval.

Goals

In this course, you will learn to

  • define a problem and the user's needs
  • come up with possible solutions and choose between them with reasons
  • plan and schedule a project in a group
  • build a prototype with Ahjo's equipment and methods
  • test a solution and improve it on the basis of tests and feedback
  • document and present the project and assess your own and the group's work
16 lessons
  1. Starting the project How Ahjo 2 runs, teams and finding a project topic.Example: Look at a few student invention and enterprise projects and collect problems that matter to you, the school or the neighbourhood.
  2. Defining the problem Whose problem, what need, and how success is recognised.Example: Interview at least three users and write a precise problem definition: who, in what situation, and by what measurable criterion the solution succeeds.
  3. Ideation Ideation methods and producing several possible solutions.Example: Ideate through constraints: what if it cost at most €20, worked without electricity, or had to be repairable by the user?
  4. Judging and choosing ideas Criteria, comparison and a justified choice.Example: Rank the ideas with a weighted decision matrix that also covers sustainability: materials, energy use and life cycle.
  5. Project plan Goals, roles, schedule, materials and risks.Example: Write a plan with milestones, responsibilities, a budget and a risk assessment, as in a real product development project.
  6. Design Sketches, dimensioned drawings and digital models.Example: Make a dimensioned drawing and a 3D model from which another group could build the same part without explanations.
  7. Materials and methods Choosing suitable materials, equipment and ways of making.Example: Choose a material with reasons: compare strength, cost, making time and recyclability for at least two options.
  8. A first prototype A quick, simple model to try out the idea.Example: Build the smallest working prototype that tests the riskiest assumption of your project.
  9. Testing the prototype A test plan, measuring and recording observations.Example: Write a test plan: what is measured, how precisely, and what result counts as success. Collect the data and present it as a graph.
  10. Feedback and improvement Feedback from users and the group; planning changes.Example: Run a user test in which an outsider uses the prototype without instructions. Record what you see and decide what to change and why.
  11. Building 1 Making the parts of the solution according to the plan.Example: Make the parts with different machines and share the work so that every member masters at least one new method.
  12. Building 2 Assembling the parts; electronics, programming or finishing as needed.Example: Add a sensor, a microcontroller or a printed textile part that measurably improves how it works.
  13. Final testing Does the solution work: tests, measurements and comparison with the goals.Example: Test the final solution with the same measures as the prototype and judge whether the original goal was met.
  14. Finishing Last fixes, appearance and usability.Example: Finish it for presentation: a case, labels and instructions that let the user manage without the makers.
  15. Documentation The project's stages, decisions and results into a portfolio.Example: Put together a portfolio that tells your decisions and failures honestly; it also serves an upper secondary diploma and applications to further studies.
  16. Presentation and assessment Presenting the project and assessing your own and the group's work.Example: Pitch the project to an audience or a partner company and answer questions. Finally, assess your own and the group's work.
