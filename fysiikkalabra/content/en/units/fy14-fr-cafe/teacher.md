## Aim and level

The third lesson of the French chapter, for beginners who know greetings, *<span lang="fr">tu</span>* and *<span lang="fr">vous</span>* and the numbers to 100.
The goal is a café visit from start to finish without a script. The lesson is not a translation worksheet. Students
read and hear a model, practise it in three steps of support, and then speak freely with role cards that give each
partner information the other does not have. Instructions are in English; the browser's French voice is a model, not
a native speaker. The café, its menu and its prices are invented.

## Before the lesson: check the videos

The videos could not be played from the course's development environment. The tasks use only what their YouTube
metadata shows (checked 1 November 2026). Watch them once and check:

- **La prof Elisabeth, *Commander au café en français*** (29 January 2022, 2 min 44 s; a simulated DELF A1
  dialogue with English subtitles): what is ordered, whether prices are said, and how the visit ends. Find the
  spelling mistake that the author mentions (*<span lang="fr">eau gazeuse</span>*). Check that the subtitles are available on your
  network. If the dialogue uses *<span lang="fr">tu</span>* or an unusual phrase, mention it when you compare it with the lesson's dialogue.
- **Easy French, *How to Order Coffee in a French Café*** (optional): its date and length were not readable. Check
  that it is suitable for the class (language, length, any adverts) before you point fast pairs to it. If it is not,
  leave the extension out.

## Timing (75 minutes)

- **0–6 min, Think about it.** Hook; pairs list the steps of a café visit and compare with a Finnish café.
- **6–10 min, Predict.** Both predictions individually (the first words, getting the bill). Their feedback stays closed until *At a French café* and *Polite ordering* have been read.
- **10–14 min, Investigate: the menu.** Pairs read the menu and pick out the words they understand.
- **14–20 min, Investigate: listen.** The phrase list in chorus, once slowly, once at normal speed.
- **20–30 min, Investigate: dialogue and video.** Pairs work through the dialogue in the three steps (read, practise, perform). Then the video once, for the four notes.
- **30–34 min, Investigate: order.** The ordering task and *What did you notice?*
- **34–42 min, Theory summary.** Read the two sections and the table; back to the predictions.
- **42–48 min, Apply.** Gap fill and the waiter match, individually; partners compare.
- **48–66 min, Role-play.** Round 1 from cues (3 min), round 2 with role cards (5 min), round 3 with roles swapped (5 min). Two pairs perform round 2 or 3 for the class. Pairs write their round 2 dialogue (the `explain` task) while you listen to others.
- **66–75 min, Check.** Three checks individually; check 4 as homework if time is short.

**Fast pairs:** the Easy French video (optional extension), or invent a round 4 card of their own (a customer
who orders for three, or a waiter whose café has only cold drinks today).

**Running out of time:** skip the video on the first viewing and show it at the start of the next lesson, do round
3 only if time allows, and give the gap fill and check 4 as homework. Keep round 2 with the role cards. It is the step
from supported to independent speaking that the lesson is built for.

## Organisation and preparation

- Pairs throughout; in rounds 2 and 3 partners must not see each other's cards. Print the four cards if you prefer
  paper (one set per pair), or let partners cover half of the screen.
- Arrange two chairs face to face as a café table; a real menu card from the page (printed) makes the scene real.
- Students with more French can be the waiter first and add questions (*<span lang="fr">Sur place ou à emporter ?</span>*,
  *<span lang="fr">Vous voulez de l'eau ?</span>*).

## Assessment of speaking (formative)

The four criteria that students see under the role-play:

- **Communicates the message:** greets, orders, asks a question, asks for the bill, pays, thanks.
- **Polite:** *<span lang="fr">vous</span>*, *<span lang="fr">je voudrais</span>* or *<span lang="fr">je vais prendre</span>*, *<span lang="fr">s'il vous plaît</span>*; no *<span lang="fr">Garçon !</span>* or *<span lang="fr">je veux</span>*.
- **Reacts:** deals with the surprise on the partner's card (no croissants, cash only) in French.
- **Numbers right:** prices and total match the menu.

Note one strength and one thing to work on for each pair; correct only what blocks understanding during the
role-play.

## Common difficulties

- *<span lang="fr">Je veux</span>* instead of *<span lang="fr">je voudrais</span>*; *<span lang="fr">tu</span>* with the waiter.
- *<span lang="fr">S'il te plaît</span>* to the waiter, or *<span lang="fr">s'il vous plaît</span>* to a friend (both are understood; the first is the problem).
- *<span lang="fr">L'addition</span>* confused with *<span lang="fr">la carte</span>* (the menu).
- Pronouncing silent letters: *croissanT*, *eT*, *prendS*.
- Waiting for the bill: some students expect it to arrive on its own, as in many other countries.
