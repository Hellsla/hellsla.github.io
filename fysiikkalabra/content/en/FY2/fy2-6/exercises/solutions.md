## fy2-6-L1-01
**Given:** average power $P = 2.5\,\text{MW}$, time $t = 168\,\text{h}$
**Find:** the energy $E$ produced in a week, in megawatt-hours
**Principle:** The average power is the power at which the plant would produce the same energy steadily all the time, so the energy is the average power multiplied by time.
**Formula:** $E = P t$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the power is in megawatts and the time in hours, so the product is directly in megawatt-hours.
**Substitution:** $E = 2.5\,\text{MW} \cdot 168\,\text{h} = 420\,\text{MWh}$
**Answer:** $420\,\text{MWh}$, two significant figures like the power $2.5\,\text{MW}$.
**Reasonableness:** Estimate: $2.5\,\text{MW} \cdot 170\,\text{h} \approx 425\,\text{MWh}$. A week is about $1 : 52$ of a year, so at the same average power the plant would produce in a year about $52 \cdot 420\,\text{MWh} \approx 22\,000\,\text{MWh}$, that is, tens of gigawatt-hours, which fits the size of one wind turbine.

## fy2-6-L1-02
**Given:** power $P = 120\,\text{MW}$, time $t = 8.0\,\text{h}$
**Find:** the energy produced $E$, in megawatt-hours
**Principle:** The plant runs at constant power, so the energy is the power multiplied by the running time.
**Formula:** $E = P t$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the power is in megawatts and the time in hours, so the product is directly in megawatt-hours.
**Substitution:** $E = 120\,\text{MW} \cdot 8.0\,\text{h} = 960\,\text{MWh}$
**Answer:** $960\,\text{MWh}$, two significant figures like the time $8.0\,\text{h}$ and the power $120\,\text{MW}$.
**Reasonableness:** Eight hours is a third of a day, so the energy is a third of the energy of a day, $120\,\text{MW} \cdot 24\,\text{h} = 2880\,\text{MWh}$, and $2880 : 3 = 960$.

## fy2-6-L1-03
**Given:** power $P = 1.6\,\text{GW}$, time $t = 24\,\text{h}$
**Find:** the energy $E$ produced in a day, in gigawatt-hours
**Principle:** The plant runs at the same power all the time, so the energy is the power multiplied by time.
**Formula:** $E = P t$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the power is in gigawatts and the time in hours, so the product is in gigawatt-hours.
**Substitution:** $E = 1.6\,\text{GW} \cdot 24\,\text{h} = 38.4\,\text{GWh}$
**Answer:** $38\,\text{GWh}$, two significant figures like the power $1.6\,\text{GW}$.
**Reasonableness:** Estimate: $1.6 \cdot 25 = 40$, so $38\,\text{GWh}$ is of the right order of magnitude. When one plant produces $38\,\text{GWh}$ in a day, it produces over $10\,\text{TWh}$ in a year.

## fy2-6-L1-04
**Given:** wind power $20.2\,\text{TWh}$, total production $79.9\,\text{TWh}$
**Find:** the share of wind power as a percentage
**Principle:** A share tells you what part of the whole a part is, so the part is divided by the whole and the result is given as a percentage.
**Formula:** $\text{share} = \dfrac{E_\text{wind}}{E_\text{tot}}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: both are in terawatt-hours, so the unit cancels. As a percentage, $1 = 100\,\%$.
**Substitution:** $\text{share} = 20.2\,\text{TWh} : 79.9\,\text{TWh} = 0.2528 = 25.28\,\%$
**Answer:** $25.3\,\%$, three significant figures like the energies $20.2\,\text{TWh}$ and $79.9\,\text{TWh}$.
**Reasonableness:** $20 : 80 = 0.25$, so a quarter is correct. The share of wind power is clearly less than a half but greater than the share of hydropower.

## fy2-6-L1-05
**Given:** power $P = 4.0\,\text{kW}$, time $t = 3.5\,\text{h}$
**Find:** the energy $E$ produced, in kilowatt-hours
**Principle:** The power of the panels stays the same all the time, so the energy is the power multiplied by time.
**Formula:** $E = P t$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the power is in kilowatts and the time in hours, so the product is directly in kilowatt-hours.
**Substitution:** $E = 4.0\,\text{kW} \cdot 3.5\,\text{h} = 14\,\text{kWh}$
**Answer:** $14\,\text{kWh}$, two significant figures like the power $4.0\,\text{kW}$ and the time $3.5\,\text{h}$.
**Reasonableness:** Estimate: $4\,\text{kW} \cdot 4\,\text{h} = 16\,\text{kWh}$, and the time is a little under four hours, so a slightly smaller $14\,\text{kWh}$ fits. It is roughly the electricity consumption of a detached house in one day without electric heating.

## fy2-6-L2-01
**Given:** rated power $P_\text{nom} = 25\,\text{MW}$, average power $32\,\%$ of the rated power, time $t = 8760\,\text{h}$
**Find:** the energy $E$ produced in a year, in gigawatt-hours
**Principle:** The power of a wind farm varies, but the annual energy is the same as if the farm ran at its average power all year. The average power is the given share of the rated power, and the energy is the average power multiplied by time.
**Formula:** $P_\text{avg} = 0.32 \cdot P_\text{nom}$ and $E = P_\text{avg}\, t$
**Conversions:** $32\,\% = 0.32$. Finally megawatt-hours to gigawatt-hours: $70\,080\,\text{MWh} = 70.08 \cdot 1000\,\text{MWh} = 70.08\,\text{GWh}$.
**Substitution:** $P_\text{avg} = 0.32 \cdot 25\,\text{MW} = 8.0\,\text{MW}$ and $E = 8.0\,\text{MW} \cdot 8760\,\text{h} = 70\,080\,\text{MWh} = 70.08\,\text{GWh}$
**Answer:** $70\,\text{GWh}$, two significant figures like the rated power $25\,\text{MW}$ and the share $32\,\%$.
**Reasonableness:** At full power the farm would produce $25\,\text{MW} \cdot 8760\,\text{h} = 219\,000\,\text{MWh} \approx 220\,\text{GWh}$, and about a third of that is about $70\,\text{GWh}$. The result is smaller than the energy at rated power, because the wind varies.

## fy2-6-L2-02
**Given:** energy $E = 14\,\text{TWh}$, power $P = 1.6\,\text{GW}$
**Find:** the time $t$ in hours
**Principle:** The plant produces at constant power, so the energy is the power multiplied by time. The time is found by dividing the energy by the power.
**Formula:** $E = P t$, so $t = \dfrac{E}{P}$
**Conversions:** $14\,\text{TWh} = 14 \cdot 10^{9}\,\text{kWh}$ and $1.6\,\text{GW} = 1.6 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{kW}$.
**Substitution:** $t = 14 \cdot 10^{9}\,\text{kWh} : (1.6 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{kW}) = 8750\,\text{h}$
**Answer:** $8800\,\text{h}$, that is, $8.8 \cdot 10^{3}\,\text{h}$, two significant figures like the power $1.6\,\text{GW}$.
**Reasonableness:** A year has $8760\,\text{h}$, so the result is almost the whole year: one large nuclear power plant running at full power would produce the annual energy of all Finnish hydropower.

## fy2-6-L2-03
**Given:** energy of nuclear power $31\,\text{TWh}$, annual energy of one wind turbine $12\,\text{GWh}$
**Find:** the number of wind turbines $N$
**Principle:** The total energy is the number of turbines times the energy of one turbine, so the number is found by dividing the energy of nuclear power by the energy of one wind turbine.
**Formula:** $E_\text{nuc} = N E_\text{wind}$, so $N = \dfrac{E_\text{nuc}}{E_\text{wind}}$
**Conversions:** $31\,\text{TWh} = 31 \cdot 10^{9}\,\text{kWh}$ and $12\,\text{GWh} = 12 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{kWh}$.
**Substitution:** $N = 31 \cdot 10^{9}\,\text{kWh} : (12 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{kWh}) = 2583$
**Answer:** $2600$ wind turbines, two significant figures like the energy of a wind turbine, $12\,\text{GWh}$.
**Reasonableness:** Estimate: $30\,000\,\text{GWh} : 12\,\text{GWh} = 2500$. The number is in the thousands, because one wind turbine produces in a year only about $0.012\,\text{TWh}$, while nuclear power produces thirty terawatt-hours.

## fy2-6-L2-04
**Given:** power $P = 1.6\,\text{GW}$, time $t = 8760\,\text{h}$, consumption of one household $5000\,\text{kWh}$
**Find:** the number of households, in millions
**Principle:** First the annual energy of the plant is calculated with the formula $E = P t$, because the power is constant. Then the energy is divided by the consumption of one household.
**Formula:** $N = \dfrac{P t}{E_\text{home}}$
**Conversions:** $1.6\,\text{GW} = 1.6 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{kW}$, so the product with an hour is in kilowatt-hours.
**Substitution:** $N = 1.6 \cdot 10^{6}\,\text{kW} \cdot 8760\,\text{h} : 5000\,\text{kWh} = 14\,016\,000\,000\,\text{kWh} : 5000\,\text{kWh} = 2\,803\,200$
**Answer:** $2.8$ million households, two significant figures like the power $1.6\,\text{GW}$.
**Reasonableness:** The result is of the same order of magnitude as the number of households in Finland (about $2.86$ million in 2024, Statistics Finland). One plant still does not cover the electricity of the whole country, because industry and services use a large part of it.

## fy2-6-L3-01
**Given:** consumption $11\,\text{GW}$, nuclear power $3.5\,\text{GW}$, rated power of wind power $9.0\,\text{GW}$ and actual power $4.0\,\%$ of it
**Find:** the power $P_\text{other}$ that other power plants and imports must produce, in gigawatts
**Principle:** In the electricity grid, production and consumption must be equal at every moment, so the missing power is the consumption minus the power of weather-dependent and steady production.
**Formula:** $P_\text{other} = P_\text{cons} - P_\text{nuc} - 0.040 \cdot P_\text{wind,nom}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: all powers are in gigawatts. The percentage is $4.0\,\% = 0.040$.
**Substitution:** $P_\text{other} = 11\,\text{GW} - 3.5\,\text{GW} - 0.040 \cdot 9.0\,\text{GW} = 11\,\text{GW} - 3.5\,\text{GW} - 0.36\,\text{GW} = 7.14\,\text{GW}$
**Answer:** $7.1\,\text{GW}$, two significant figures like the rated power of wind power, $9.0\,\text{GW}$.
**Reasonableness:** In weak wind, wind power covers only about $0.36 : 11 \approx 3\,\%$ of consumption, so most of the missing power must be produced by controllable means: hydropower, fuels or imports. This is why weather-dependent production needs controllable production alongside it.

## fy2-6-L3-02
**Given:** rated power $600\,\text{MW}$ for both, average power of the wind farm $33\,\%$ and of the nuclear power plant $90\,\%$ of the rated power
**Find:** the ratio of the annual energies $E_\text{nuc} : E_\text{wind}$
**Principle:** The annual energy of each is the average power times the same length of year, $E = P_\text{avg} t$. The rated power and the time are the same, so the ratio depends only on the shares of the average powers.
**Formula:** $\dfrac{E_\text{nuc}}{E_\text{wind}} = \dfrac{0.90 \cdot 600\,\text{MW} \cdot t}{0.33 \cdot 600\,\text{MW} \cdot t}$
**Conversions:** Not needed: the rated power and the time cancel.
**Substitution:** $\dfrac{E_\text{nuc}}{E_\text{wind}} = 0.90 : 0.33 = 2.727$
**Answer:** $2.7$ times, two significant figures like the shares $0.90$ and $0.33$.
**Reasonableness:** Check with the annual energies: nuclear power $0.90 \cdot 600\,\text{MW} \cdot 8760\,\text{h} \approx 4.7\,\text{TWh}$ and the wind farm $0.33 \cdot 600\,\text{MW} \cdot 8760\,\text{h} \approx 1.7\,\text{TWh}$, and $4.7 : 1.7 \approx 2.7$. The same rated power does not mean the same annual energy.

## fy2-6-L3-03
**Given:** rated power of Olkiluoto 3 $P_\text{O} = 1600\,\text{MW}$ and average power $90\,\%$ of it, average power of the solar park $11\,\%$ of its rated power, the same time $t$ (a year) for both
**Find:** the rated power $P_\text{a}$ of the solar park in gigawatts, and whether the park would replace Olkiluoto 3 on a January evening
**Principle:** The annual energy is the average power multiplied by time, $E = P_\text{avg} t$, and the average power is the given share $o$ of the rated power. The time is the same year for both, so the annual energies are equal when the average powers are equal.
**Formula:** $o_\text{a} P_\text{a}\, t = o_\text{O} P_\text{O}\, t$, so $P_\text{a} = \dfrac{o_\text{O} P_\text{O}}{o_\text{a}}$
**Conversions:** $o_\text{O} = 90\,\% = 0.90$ and $o_\text{a} = 11\,\% = 0.11$. Finally megawatts to gigawatts: $13\,091\,\text{MW} = 13.091 \cdot 1000\,\text{MW} = 13.1\,\text{GW}$.
**Substitution:** $P_\text{a} = 0.90 \cdot 1600\,\text{MW} : 0.11 = 1440\,\text{MW} : 0.11 = 13\,091\,\text{MW} = 13.1\,\text{GW}$
**Answer:** $13\,\text{GW}$, two significant figures like the shares $11\,\%$ and $90\,\%$.
**Reasonableness:** Estimate: $90 : 11 \approx 8$, so about eight times as much rated power is needed, $8 \cdot 1.6\,\text{GW} \approx 13\,\text{GW}$. On a January evening the Sun has set, so the park produces nothing at all, even if its annual energy were the same as that of Olkiluoto 3. Then controllable production, such as hydropower and fuel-fired power plants, or imported electricity and energy storage, would be needed in place of Olkiluoto 3. The same annual energy therefore does not mean that one power plant replaces another at every moment.
