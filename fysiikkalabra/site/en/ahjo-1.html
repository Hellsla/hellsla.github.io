Goals

Draft: the goals and the lesson outline await the teacher's approval.

In this course, you will learn to

  • work safely in Ahjo and use its equipment as you were introduced to it
  • model objects to measure and make them with a 3D printer
  • build simple circuits and measure them with a multimeter
  • program a microcontroller and use sensors and actuators
  • build and program a simple robot
  • document your work and assess how well it succeeded

How the lessons work

Every lesson starts with making. The first success comes within 5–10 minutes, and the teacher has prepared the devices and example code in advance. Devices are used only after the induction.

  1. Try You get a first working version from a ready-made example or model.
  2. Modify You change it: a number, a shape, a colour or a sound. Using ready-made code and models is allowed and encouraged.
  3. Understand You find out why it works. The technology, physics and programming are explained in Ahjo's guides and the physics chapters.
  4. Create your own You design it yourself, first in the lesson's challenge and finally in the mini project.

Welcome to Ahjo The space, the equipment and how we work; the rules and the general induction.Make: A torch from found objectsThe first 10 minutes: Connect an LED to a coin cell and it lights up. Turn the LED round and see what happens.Try Make the LED light and explain why it lights only one way round.Modify Change how the light looks with a ping-pong ball, paper, foil or a plastic bottle, and pick your favourite.Challenge yourself Walk round Ahjo and record one real problem you would like to solve during the course.Output: Project journal set up and one problem recorded.Guides: Project journal
  2. Working safely Device induction for the 3D printer and hand tools; protective equipment and an orderly workstation.Make: Ahjo's own instruction cardThe first 10 minutes: Find five things in the space that have to do with safety, such as the fire extinguisher, the first aid kit and safety glasses, and photograph them.Try Take the device induction for the 3D printer and the hand tools.Modify Make an illustrated instruction card for one device in your own style, for example as a drawing or a comic.Challenge yourself Write a risk assessment for the device: the hazard, how likely it is, the consequence and how to protect yourself.Output: A risk assessment of one device.
  3. Basics of 3D modelling Basic shapes, joining and holes in Tinkercad.Make: Your own key ringThe first 10 minutes: Open the teacher's ready-made template in Tinkercad and type your name on it. Your first model is done.Try Finish the key ring and save it as an STL file in the print queue.Modify Make the shape your own: add your own symbol or pattern and move the hole.Challenge yourself Model something that fixes or improves something in your everyday life, such as a cable holder or a pen stand.Output: Your own model in the print queue.Guides: Basics of 3D modelling
  4. A model to measure Dimensions, clearance and fitting parts together.Make: A lid that snaps shutThe first 10 minutes: Guess the sizes of three objects, then measure them with a calliper. Who guessed closest?Try Print a ready-made fit test with five clearances and find which peg fits the hole.Modify Model a lid or a stopper for your own jar, pen or bottle with the clearance from the fit test.Challenge yourself Find out how the clearance depends on the print orientation, and present the results as a table.Output: Fit test result: a clearance that works.Guides: Basics of 3D modelling
  5. From model to print Slicing in PrusaSlicer, print settings and using the printer.Make: A beam that holdsThe first 10 minutes: Open a ready-made beam in PrusaSlicer and change the infill. How do the print time and the mass change?Try Slice and print the beam with the settings your teacher gives.Modify Choose the infill and the orientation yourself and justify your choice.Challenge yourself Does the beam hold a 2 kg mass, a weight of about 20 N? Compare two beams and work out which holds more per gram of its own mass.Output: Strength test results as a table.Guides: Getting started with 3D printing
  6. Judging and fixing a print The most common print defects, their causes and how to fix them.Make: A museum of mistakesThe first 10 minutes: Pick three failed prints from the box of mistakes, name their defects and guess the causes.Try Measure your own print with a calliper and compare it with the model.Modify Change one print setting and print a corrected version.Challenge yourself Put together an illustrated guide to the class's defects: the defect, the cause and the fix.Output: The measured difference between model and print.Guides: Getting started with 3D printing
  7. Circuits and components Power source, resistor, LED and breadboard.Make: Light without a programThe first 10 minutes: Put an LED and a resistor on the breadboard as in the ready-made drawing. The LED lights from the Arduino's 5 volts with no program.Try Build the circuit as in the drawing.Modify Add a second LED, first in series and then in parallel. In which connection do the LEDs shine brighter?Challenge yourself Size the series resistor with Ohm's law and compare the measured current with your calculation.Output: Circuit diagram and resistor calculation.Guides: First steps with Arduino, Resistance and Ohm's law
  8. Measuring with a multimeter Measuring voltage, current and resistance connected the right way.Make: How long does the battery last?The first 10 minutes: Measure the voltage of a new and a used battery. Which is which?Try Measure the voltages across the LED and the resistor, and the current in the circuit.Modify Choose a device yourself, such as an LED, a motor or a buzzer, and measure its current in different situations.Challenge yourself From your measurements, estimate how long a battery would power your device.Output: Current and voltage measurements with units.Guides: Measurements in an electric circuit
  9. A first Arduino program The programming environment, digital outputs and a blinking LED.Make: Your own light rhythmThe first 10 minutes: Upload a ready-made blink program to the Arduino and change one number in it. The LED now blinks to your beat.Try Make the LED blink with the example program.Modify Turn the blinking into a heartbeat, a lighthouse or your own name in Morse code.Challenge yourself Program a reaction-time game that measures reaction time in milliseconds, and work out the class's mean and spread.Output: A working program and the class mean.Guides: First steps with Arduino
  10. Sensors and inputs Button, light and distance sensors; conditions and loops.Make: A measuring device that drawsThe first 10 minutes: Connect a light sensor, upload a ready-made program and cover the sensor with your hand. The serial plotter draws a curve at once.Try Send the sensor's readings to the serial port and save them.Modify Turn the sensor into an instrument or a light that reacts to its surroundings, for example a buzzer whose pitch rises as your hand comes closer.Challenge yourself Calibrate the sensor, collect a series of measurements and plot a graph with units.Output: A graph of your own measurement data.Guides: Anturit ja mittausohjelma in Finnish
  11. Motors and actuators Servo and DC motor; power supply and control.Make: A moving figureThe first 10 minutes: Connect a servo and a potentiometer to a ready-made program and turn the servo with the knob.Try Program the servo to turn to three different angles.Modify Build a cardboard figure on the servo that waves, nods or points.Challenge yourself Make the servo follow the light, steered by two light-dependent resistors.Output: A light-following servo.Guides: Force on a current-carrying wire: the electric motor and the loudspeaker
  12. Building a robot The structure of a robotics kit, drive train and sensors.Make: A robot that carries a loadThe first 10 minutes: Put the robot base together from ready-made parts and drive it forwards and backwards with a test program.Try Build the robot as in the instructions.Modify Give the robot its own body or load space made of cardboard or printed.Challenge yourself Compare two gear ratios: how do they affect speed and pulling force?Output: A working robot base and a gear ratio comparison.
  13. Programming a robot Movement, sensor control and solving a task with a program.Make: A course blind first, then with sensorsThe first 10 minutes: Program the robot to drive a square. How close to the start does it come back?Try Drive the course using only distances and angles calculated in advance, as when steering a space probe.Modify Add a sensor to the robot and let it find the route itself.Challenge yourself Compare the rounds: measure the time and the accuracy with three repetitions.Output: A comparison of the two rounds.
  14. Planning the mini project The problem, a measurable measure of success, a materials list and a sketch.Make: Your own idea on paperThe first 10 minutes: Browse the project bank and your own course work and pick three ideas that excite you.Try Choose one idea and record its problem, measure and materials.Modify Combine two earlier exercises from the course into a new device.Challenge yourself Make a cardboard mock-up and try the idea on another group before building.Output: An approved mini project plan.Guides: Project journal
  15. Mini project A small device of your own with the methods learned: building and testing.Make: Your own deviceThe first 10 minutes: Gather the materials and get the first part working, for example a sensor or a light.Try Build a working version by combining examples from the course.Modify Make the device your own: a case, a colour, a sound or a shape.Challenge yourself Test the device three times with the measure of success and improve its weakest point.Output: A working device and test results.Guides: Project journal
  16. Presentation and self-assessment Presenting and documenting the mini project and assessing your own learning.Make: A small exhibitionThe first 10 minutes: Film a 30-second video of your device working.Try Present your device: the goal, the solution and the result.Modify Choose your own way to present: a demo, a poster, a video or an exhibition stand.Challenge yourself Say what you would do in the next version, and assess your learning with the project journal's criteria.Output: A presentation and a completed project journal.Guides: Project journal

Optional deepening

Optional extra work for those who get further or want to go deeper. It is not part of the outline everyone follows.

  • 3D scanning Scan an object, repair the model and print a scale copy. How accurate is the copy, and where do the differences come from?
  • Soldering Solder your measuring device onto stripboard once you have had the soldering station induction. Test the joints with a multimeter.
  • Solar panel power Measure with two multimeters how much more power a light-following panel gives than a fixed one.
  • P control Improve line following so that the robot's turning depends on how far it is from the line. Compare lap times.
