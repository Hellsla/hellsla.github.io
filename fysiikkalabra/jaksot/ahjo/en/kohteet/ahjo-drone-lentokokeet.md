## Quick start

A flight test is a measurement, not a show. You decide beforehand what you measure and how, and you repeat the flight in the same way so many times that you see the variation in the results.

1. Write a measurement rule: what is measured, from which point and with which tool.
2. Assign the roles: pilot, observer or safety officer, and recorder.
3. Go through the checklist and fly.
4. Record the result in the flight log straight after the flight.

### Try

Do three short hovers with the Air:bit and record for each one the duration and the direction and distance of the drift.

### Modify

Change one thing, for example the pilot or the battery, and repeat the same test. Keep everything else the same.

### Challenge yourself

Design a test with which you can tell apart the drift caused by the drone and the drift caused by the pilot.

## Challenge

Two flights are never exactly alike. The battery runs down, the air moves and the pilot learns. That is why one flight proves nothing. The goal is to measure in such a way that you can say whether a difference is due to the change you made or to ordinary variation.

## Learning goals

After the work you can

- write a measurement rule and follow it
- repeat a measurement and calculate the mean and the range
- change one thing at a time
- name the biggest sources of error in your measurement.

## Safety and induction

See the topics [pre-flight inspection](ahjo-turvallisuus.html#drone-tarkastus), [flight area and roles](ahjo-turvallisuus.html#drone-lentoalue) and [emergency stop](ahjo-turvallisuus.html#drone-hatapysaytys). They are in preparation until the school has approved them.

- The checklist is gone through before every measurement series.
- With the Air:bit, flying is done low: the hovering height is $10$–$30\,\text{cm}$. The drone has no altitude hold and no position hold.
- Measuring is done only when the propellers have stopped. Nobody goes into the flight area during a flight.
- The flight is ended at once when the battery warning comes.
- A changed drone or program is tested first without propellers.

## Steps

### Hovering the Air:bit

1. Tape a take-off point on the floor and place the drone on it with the micro:bit's display towards the pilot.
2. Connect the battery and wait until the drone has calibrated. Do not move it.
3. Start the motors by pressing A and B together.
4. Add throttle one step at a time. According to the manufacturer the drone rises when the throttle is about 50–60 per cent.
5. When the drone starts to rise, reduce the throttle slightly so that the height stays the same.
6. Hover for the agreed time, for example five seconds, and bring the throttle down.
7. Measure how far from the take-off point the drone landed and in which direction.

### Precision landing

1. Tape a landing mark and a take-off point at an agreed distance from each other.
2. Fly from the take-off point to the mark and land.
3. Measure the distance from the centre of the mark to the middle of the drone.
4. Repeat at least five times.

## Diagrams and example code

A row of the flight log can look like this. The numbers are a made-up example.

| Flight | Pilot | Battery | Duration (s) | Drift (cm) | Direction | Observation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A | full | 5 | 40 | to the left | rose at a slant straight away |
| 2 | A | full | 5 | 25 | to the left | |
| 3 | A | used | 5 | 55 | backwards | throttle had to be added |

The mean is calculated by dividing the sum of the results by their number. The range is the smallest and the largest result.

## Test and measure

A good measurement series meets these conditions:

- Every flight is recorded, a failed one too.
- The same person measures with the same rule.
- The series has at least three, preferably five repetitions.
- Only one thing has been changed between the series.
- Every number has a unit.

Compare two series: if the difference between the means is smaller than the variation within a series, the difference cannot be considered certain.

## If something does not work

The advice is from the manufacturer's troubleshooting.

- **The motors spin, but the drone does not rise:** check that every motor has a propeller that spins the right way and that the battery is charged. Check also that a motor is not damaged.
- **The drone tips over at once:** a propeller or a motor is in the wrong corner. Compare with the parts map.
- **The drone always drifts in the same direction:** disconnect the battery, place the drone on a level surface and start again so that it calibrates. Check that the propellers are intact and the battery is in the middle.
- **The results vary a lot:** shorten the hovering time, use a full battery and keep the same pilot.

## For the teacher: preparation

- Mark out the flight area and have a soft surface ready. According to the manufacturer a high space is not needed, and a carpet is better than a hard floor.
- Decide the length of the flight turns and the rotation order. One drone at a time flies in one flight area. The other groups act as recorders or process data.
- Charging the battery takes about an hour according to the manufacturer. The manufacturer does not state the flight time in minutes, so measure it yourself before the lesson and size the turns accordingly.
- The manufacturer's instructions have no means of correcting drift other than calibration on a level surface.
- The emergency stop is a shake of the controller. The manufacturer warns against using it from a height of over two metres, because the drone falls.
