**Model answer:** The points from $0.4\,\text{s}$ onwards lie on a line, so a line is fitted to them: the ball rolls steadily on the floor. At $0.4\,\text{s}$ the ball is at the lower end of the ramp (about $16\,\text{cm}$) and has already reached its speed on the floor. The first two points are from the ramp, where the speed is still increasing: they stay above the line and are not part of the fit. The points are not joined into a broken line, because every measurement has a small error.

### b)
**Given:** the graph from part a and the points taken on the floor, $t = 0.4\text{–}2.0\,\text{s}$
**Find:** the speed $v$ of the ball on the floor
**Principle:** On the floor the points lie on a line, so the ball moves uniformly: its position is $s = s_0 + vt$, and the speed is the slope of the fitted line. The points from the ramp are left out, because there the speed is still increasing.
**Reading the graph:** Two points far apart are read from the line, $(0.6\,\text{s};\ 0.32\,\text{m})$ and $(2.0\,\text{s};\ 1.44\,\text{m})$. They are points of the line, not measurement points.
**Formula:** $v = \dfrac{\Delta s}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{s_2 - s_1}{t_2 - t_1}$
**Conversions:** The positions have already been read in metres: $320\,\text{mm} = 0.32\,\text{m}$ and $1440\,\text{mm} = 1.44\,\text{m}$.
**Substitution:** $v = \dfrac{1.44\,\text{m} - 0.32\,\text{m}}{2.0\,\text{s} - 0.6\,\text{s}} = \dfrac{1.12\,\text{m}}{1.4\,\text{s}} = 0.80\,\text{m/s}$
**Answer:** $v \approx 0.80\,\text{m/s}$. The precision is justified in part c.
**Reasonableness:** On the floor the ball travels about $16\,\text{cm}$ during every $0.20\,\text{s}$, that is, $0.16\,\text{m} : 0.20\,\text{s} = 0.80\,\text{m/s}$. The speed is of the order of a slow walk, which fits a ball rolling down a low ramp.

### c)
**Find:** the real precision of the position and the justified precision of the speed
**Principle:** A measurement cannot be more precise than its smallest distinguishable detail. In a video that is one pixel, and the position of the edge of the ball can be off by a pixel or two.
**Model answer:** One pixel corresponds to $2.5\,\text{mm}$, so the position of the ball is known to a precision of about $\Delta s \approx 3\,\text{mm}$. The app calculates the centre of the ball from the pixels of its edge and can give the position in parts of a pixel, but the edge of a moving ball is slightly blurred. The $0.1\,\text{mm}$ digit shown by the app comes from calculation, when pixels are converted to millimetres, and it tells nothing about the precision of the measurement: even the millimetre digit is uncertain. The same can be seen in the scatter of the points: on the floor the points deviate from the fitted line by a few millimetres. The speed was determined from a distance of $1.12\,\text{m}$, whose uncertainty is a few millimetres, that is, less than one per cent, so the speed is given as $v = 0.80\,\text{m/s}$. A least-squares fit gives $0.799\,\text{m/s}$: the third significant figure can still be justified, but the fourth cannot.

### d)
**Find:** an evaluation of the extrapolation and an explanation for the constant term of the line
**Principle:** The model has been tested only over the interval that was measured. The constant term of the fitted line is the point where the line crosses the $s$-axis.
**Model answer:** The conclusion is not reliable. The line has been tested only over the interval $0.4\text{–}2.0\,\text{s}$, that is, over $1.6\,\text{s}$, and the conclusion extends it more than six times further. In reality rolling resistance gradually slows the ball, and over a distance of $7.8\,\text{m}$ the ball would probably hit a wall or a piece of furniture. So the model of uniform motion can continue only if it is tested with a longer measurement.
The constant term of the fitted line is about $s_0 = -0.16\,\text{m}$. It is not the position of the ball at the moment $0\,\text{s}$, because the ball started from $s = 0$. On the ramp the ball first moved more slowly than on the floor. If it had moved all the time at its floor speed of $0.80\,\text{m/s}$, it would have got further. So the line describing the motion on the floor has shifted downwards and crosses the $s$-axis below the origin.

### Yleisiä virheitä
- The points are joined into a broken line, or one line is fitted to all the points including the ramp points. Then the slope is too small.
- The slope is calculated from two adjacent measurement points, so the error of a single point has a large effect on the result.
- The speed is given with all the digits of the app, for example $0.7993\,\text{m/s}$.
- The constant term of the line is interpreted as the starting position of the ball, although the ball started from the origin.

## fy1-s2

### a)
**Find:** four significant weaknesses, why each weakens the investigation and how it is fixed
**Principle:** In a fair test only one quantity is varied at a time, the measurements are repeated, the uncertainty is estimated and the conclusion is drawn only from what the measurements show.
**Model answer:** Any four of the following, each justified and fixed:
1. *Other conditions changed besides the mass.* The plastic ball was pushed to get it moving, it set off only from roughly the same spot and the ramp became lower in between. The difference in time may be due to these and not to the mass. Fix: both balls are released from rest from the same marked spot with the same slope.
2. *Each ball was measured only once.* A time taken by hand has random error, so a single measurement does not show how much the result varies. Fix: at least five repeats for each ball and their mean.
3. *The results are given too precisely.* The stopwatch app shows thousandths of a second, but a person's reaction time varies by about $0.1\text{–}0.2\,\text{s}$. Fix: the times are given to the nearest hundredth of a second and the uncertainty is estimated from the variation of the repeats.
4. *The uncertainty was not estimated.* The difference of $0.33\,\text{s}$ may be entirely measurement error. Fix: the uncertainty is estimated from the repeats and the difference is compared with it.
5. *The conclusion generalises too much.* Nothing can be concluded about all objects from one ramp and two balls. Fix: the conclusion is limited to the conditions studied.
6. *The research question is vague.* It does not say what "faster" means or what is measured. Fix: "Does the time a ball takes to roll down a ramp depend on its mass when the balls are of the same size and solid?"

### b)
**Given:** the times of the steel ball $2.31$; $2.45$; $2.38$; $2.29$; $2.42\,\text{s}$ and the times of the plastic ball $2.40$; $2.33$; $2.47$; $2.36$; $2.44\,\text{s}$
**Find:** the means $\bar{t}$ and their uncertainties $\Delta t$, and a conclusion about the difference
**Principle:** The mean of the repeats is the best estimate of the rolling time, and half of the range is a simple estimate of its uncertainty. A difference is real only if it is clearly larger than the uncertainties.
**Formula:** $\bar{t} = \dfrac{t_1 + t_2 + t_3 + t_4 + t_5}{5}$ and $\Delta t = \dfrac{t_\text{max} - t_\text{min}}{2}$
**Conversions:** Not needed, all the times are in seconds.
**Substitution:** Steel: $\bar{t} = \dfrac{11.85\,\text{s}}{5} = 2.37\,\text{s}$ and $\Delta t = \dfrac{2.45\,\text{s} - 2.29\,\text{s}}{2} = 0.08\,\text{s}$.
Plastic: $\bar{t} = \dfrac{12.00\,\text{s}}{5} = 2.40\,\text{s}$ and $\Delta t = \dfrac{2.47\,\text{s} - 2.33\,\text{s}}{2} = 0.07\,\text{s}$.
The difference of the means is $2.40\,\text{s} - 2.37\,\text{s} = 0.03\,\text{s}$.
**Answer:** Steel ball $t = (2.37 \pm 0.08)\,\text{s}$ and plastic ball $t = (2.40 \pm 0.07)\,\text{s}$, to the nearest hundredth of a second like the measurements. The difference of $0.03\,\text{s}$ is smaller than either uncertainty, and the intervals overlap. Based on the measurements, the rolling times do not differ.
**Reasonableness:** The variation of the repeats, about $0.1\,\text{s}$, is of the order of the reaction time when timing by hand, so the size of the uncertainty is believable.

### c)
**Find:** a conclusion that answers the research question and takes the uncertainty into account
**Principle:** A conclusion answers the research question, gives the result with its uncertainties and limits the inference to the conditions in which the measurements were made.
**Model answer:** The mass of the ball did not affect the rolling time within the measurement precision. The rolling time of the steel ball ($110\,\text{g}$) was $(2.37 \pm 0.08)\,\text{s}$ and that of the plastic ball ($16\,\text{g}$) was $(2.40 \pm 0.07)\,\text{s}$. The difference of $0.03\,\text{s}$ is smaller than the uncertainty of the measurements, so the hypothesis that a heavy ball rolls faster received no support. The result applies to solid balls of the same size on this ramp. To detect a smaller difference, more precise timing would be needed, for example light gates.

### Hyväksytään myös
- The uncertainty can also be estimated in another justified way, for example with the standard error of the mean. The conclusion is the same: the difference is smaller than the uncertainty.

### Yleisiä virheitä
- Something that does not weaken the investigation is named as a weakness, for example the length of the ramp.
- A weakness is named, but no fix is given or the fix does not remove the problem.
- The mean is given with all the digits of the calculator, for example $2.370\,\text{s}$, or the uncertainty is left out.
- The conclusion "mass does not affect anything" generalises too much, in the same way as the original one.
