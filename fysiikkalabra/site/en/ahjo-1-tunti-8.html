Lesson 8 · Electronics and Arduino

How long does the battery last?

Measuring with a multimeter

Measuring voltage, current and resistance connected the right way.

In this lesson you learn: You measure voltage and current with a multimeter connected the right way and estimate a battery's life.

Start right away (5–10 min)

Measure the voltage of a new and a used AA battery. Which is which?

Measurements in an electric circuit →

Try · Modify · Challenge yourself

  1. Try for everyone

    Connect an LED and a 330 Ω resistor to the battery holder. Measure the voltage by connecting the multimeter in parallel with the battery and the current by connecting it in series in the circuit on the mA range. Then estimate the battery life: the capacity in milliampere-hours divided by the current in milliamperes gives the hours. The result is a rough estimate, because a battery's real capacity depends on the current and the temperature, and its voltage drops as it runs down.

  2. Modify your own ideas

    Choose a device yourself, such as an LED, a motor or a buzzer, and measure its current in different situations.

  3. Challenge yourself optional

    Measure the current with a motor or a buzzer too and compare the estimates. Think about why the real life can differ from the estimate.

What did you make?

Basic level: Voltage and current measurements with units, and a rough estimate of how many hours the battery would last.

Challenge yourself: Estimates for several devices and a reflection on why an estimate is not an exact forecast.

You need

  • multimeter
  • a new and a used AA battery
  • battery holder for 2 × AA with wires
  • LED and 330 Ω resistor

Step-by-step guide

  • Measurements in an electric circuit
  • Small extra challenge: A measurement poster

Starter files

  • Battery life estimate (CSV) spreadsheet template

Safety

Measure voltage by connecting the meter in parallel with the part you measure. Measure current only by connecting the meter in series in the circuit. Never connect a meter set to a current range straight across the battery's terminals: that is a short circuit and can blow the meter's fuse.
