## About the articles (checked 1 November 2026)

- **Helsingin Sanomat 2018** (dynamic.hs.fi/2018/iter/): the brief's main article. Its page could not be opened from the development environment, so this unit says nothing about its content. Read it before the lesson, check that students can open it (it may need the school's subscription), and check the students' reading notes on it yourself. In 2018 ITER's official plan was the 2016 baseline: first plasma in 2025, D–T operation in 2035; in December 2017 ITER said it was halfway to first plasma, at a cost of about 20 billion euros.
- **Yle 14 January 2020** (yle.fi/a/3-11146330) and **Yle 28 June 2005** (yle.fi/a/3-5217376) were read and checked; the claim sorting task and the model answer use them.
- **The link yle.fi/a/20-153568 in the course brief is not about ITER.** It is an Yle Oppiminen French lesson, *Osa 4: Ravintolat, kahvilat, baarit* (2012, updated 2016), on restaurants and cafés. It is not used here; it suits the French unit *Au café: a role-play*.

## Timing (75 minutes)

- **0–6 min, Think about it.** Hook; review question on where fusion energy comes from.
- **6–10 min, Predict.** Two predictions, committed with a reason.
- **10–35 min, Investigate.** Students choose an article (stronger readers: HS 2018; others: Yle 2020; Yle 2005 for a short reading), skim, read and make notes under the six headings; sort the Yle claims; read *ITER then and now* (both predictions open); compare two pieces of information.
- **35–45 min, Theory summary.** Key idea and the physics reminder; pair discussion with a partner who read another article; back to the predictions.
- **45–70 min, Apply.** The 300-word writing task (start in class, finish at home if needed); find the error. Peer feedback is optional.
- **70–75 min, Check your understanding.** Three checks and the correction note; summary and French corner.

## Organisation and preparation
- Reading is in Finnish, writing in English (mediation). Allow a dictionary for the writing, not for the first skim.
- Group students so that each pair has read two different articles.
- If the HS story cannot be opened, use the Yle 2020 article as the main article and Yle 2005 as the second one.

## Assessment of the writing task
Use the seven criteria shown on the page (the six content points and language). A suggested scale for each content point: 0 = missing, 1 = present but vague or partly wrong, 2 = correct and precise with a date or number. Language: 0–3 (length and structure, linking words, key terms, own words). The model answer is for the Yle 2020 article; for HS 2018 judge points 5 and 6 against the article.

## Common difficulties
- Students copy dates from the article as current facts. Insist on *as of …* and on naming the plan's year.
- Students mix *plan* and *result*; the sorting task and check 1 practise this.
- Point 5 needs the article's information **and** the current information; many students give only one.
- The physics points are often vague ("fusion is hot"). Ask for the mass decrease and $E = mc^2$, and for two concrete difficulties.

## Fast pairs and running out of time
- **Fast pairs:** read a second article and add a paragraph comparing the two; or the optional peer feedback.
- **Short of time:** finish the writing at home; keep the claim sorting, the table and checks 1–2 in class.

## Extension ideas
- Find a French news report on ITER (for example from *La Provence*) and identify three words you understand through English.
- Write a short letter to the editor in English suggesting how newspapers could mark dates in articles about long projects.
