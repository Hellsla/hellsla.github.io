## fy4-12-L1-01
**Given:** $x_\text{A} = 4.0\,\text{m} + 2.0\,\text{m/s} \cdot t$ and $x_\text{B} = 24\,\text{m} - 3.0\,\text{m/s} \cdot t$ in the same coordinate system
**Find:** the time of the meeting $t$ in $\text{s}$
**Principle:** The carts meet when they are at the same place at the same moment, so $x_\text{A} = x_\text{B}$. The positions are linear functions of time, so the equation has one solution.
**Formula:** $x_\text{A} = x_\text{B}$, so $4.0 + 2.0\,t = 24 - 3.0\,t$. Terms in $t$ to the left and numbers to the right: $5.0\,t = 20$, so $t = \dfrac{24 - 4.0}{2.0 + 3.0}$
**Conversions:** The positions are in metres and the velocities in $\text{m/s}$, so the result comes out in seconds. No conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $t = \dfrac{24\,\text{m} - 4.0\,\text{m}}{2.0\,\text{m/s} + 3.0\,\text{m/s}} = \dfrac{20\,\text{m}}{5.0\,\text{m/s}} = 4.0\,\text{s}$
**Answer:** $4.0\,\text{s}$, two significant figures like the velocities ($2.0\,\text{m/s}$ and $3.0\,\text{m/s}$).
**Reasonableness:** Substituting into both positions: $x_\text{A} = 4.0\,\text{m} + 2.0\,\text{m/s} \cdot 4.0\,\text{s} = 12\,\text{m}$ and $x_\text{B} = 24\,\text{m} - 3.0\,\text{m/s} \cdot 4.0\,\text{s} = 12\,\text{m}$. The positions are equal, so the carts meet. The time is positive, so the meeting happens after the start.

## fy4-12-L1-02
**Given:** cyclist $x_0 = 0$, $v_\text{C} = 8.0\,\text{m/s}$; runner $x_0 = 60\,\text{m}$, $v_\text{R} = 5.0\,\text{m/s}$; both uniform and in the same direction
**Find:** the time $t$ when the cyclist catches up with the runner, in $\text{s}$
**Principle:** Catching up is a meeting: the positions are equal. Both move uniformly, so $x = x_0 + v\,t$ for each. The cyclist is faster, so she catches up with the runner.
**Formula:** $v_\text{C}\,t = x_{0\text{R}} + v_\text{R}\,t$, so $(v_\text{C} - v_\text{R})\,t = x_{0\text{R}}$ and $t = \dfrac{x_{0\text{R}}}{v_\text{C} - v_\text{R}}$
**Conversions:** All quantities are in SI units, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $t = \dfrac{60\,\text{m}}{8.0\,\text{m/s} - 5.0\,\text{m/s}} = \dfrac{60\,\text{m}}{3.0\,\text{m/s}} = 20\,\text{s}$
**Answer:** $20\,\text{s}$, two significant figures like the velocities.
**Reasonableness:** The cyclist gains $3.0\,\text{m}$ on the runner every second, so $60\,\text{m}$ takes $20\,\text{s}$. Check: the position of the cyclist is $8.0\,\text{m/s} \cdot 20\,\text{s} = 160\,\text{m}$ and that of the runner is $60\,\text{m} + 5.0\,\text{m/s} \cdot 20\,\text{s} = 160\,\text{m}$.

## fy4-12-L1-03
**Given:** $x_0 = 0$, $v_0 = 0$, $a = 3.0\,\text{m/s}^2$, $t = 4.0\,\text{s}$
**Find:** the position $x$ of the car in $\text{m}$
**Principle:** The acceleration is constant, so the position comes from the position function of uniformly accelerated motion. The car starts from rest at the origin, so the initial position and the initial velocity are zero.
**Formula:** $x = x_0 + v_0\,t + \tfrac12\,a\,t^2$, and when $x_0 = 0$ and $v_0 = 0$, $x = \tfrac12\,a\,t^2$
**Conversions:** All quantities are in SI units, and $\text{m/s}^2 \cdot \text{s}^2 = \text{m}$. No conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $x = \tfrac12 \cdot 3.0\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot (4.0\,\text{s})^2 = \tfrac12 \cdot 3.0\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 16\,\text{s}^2 = 24\,\text{m}$
**Answer:** $24\,\text{m}$, two significant figures like the given values.
**Reasonableness:** If the car moved all the time at its final velocity $3.0\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot 4.0\,\text{s} = 12\,\text{m/s}$, it would advance $48\,\text{m}$. In accelerated motion the average velocity is half of the final velocity, so the distance is half of that, $24\,\text{m}$.

In this time the car drives $25\,\text{m/s} \cdot 30\,\text{s} = 750\,\text{m}$ and the bus $150\,\text{m} + 20\,\text{m/s} \cdot 30\,\text{s} = 750\,\text{m}$.

## fy4-12-L2-02
**Given:** distance between the stations $D = 12\,\text{km}$, velocities $v_1 = 90\,\text{km/h}$ and $v_2 = 54\,\text{km/h}$ toward each other, starting at the same time
**Find:** the time $t$ in $\text{min}$
**Principle:** The trains meet when they are at the same place at the same moment. The origin is at the first station and the positive direction is toward the second station: $x_1 = v_1\,t$ and $x_2 = D - v_2\,t$. The equation $x_1 = x_2$ gives the time.
**Formula:** $v_1\,t = D - v_2\,t$, so $t = \dfrac{D}{v_1 + v_2}$
**Conversions:** We keep the length in kilometres and the velocity in $\text{km/h}$, so the time comes out in hours: $t = \dfrac{12\,\text{km}}{144\,\text{km/h}}$. Finally $1\,\text{h} = 60\,\text{min}$.
**Substitution:** $t = \dfrac{12\,\text{km}}{90\,\text{km/h} + 54\,\text{km/h}} = \dfrac{12\,\text{km}}{144\,\text{km/h}} = 0.0833\,\text{h} = 0.0833 \cdot 60\,\text{min} = 5.0\,\text{min}$
**Answer:** $5.0\,\text{min}$, two significant figures like the velocities ($54\,\text{km/h}$).
**Reasonableness:** The trains approach each other at $144\,\text{km/h} = 2.4\,\text{km/min}$, so $12\,\text{km}$ takes $12 : 2.4\,\text{min} = 5.0\,\text{min}$. The faster train travels $90\,\text{km/h} \cdot \tfrac{1}{12}\,\text{h} = 7.5\,\text{km}$ and the slower one $4.5\,\text{km}$, together $12\,\text{km}$.

## fy4-12-L3-01
**Given:** runner $x_{0\text{R}} = -8.0\,\text{m}$, $v = 5.0\,\text{m/s}$ (uniform); bus $x_0 = 0$, $v_0 = 0$, $a = 1.0\,\text{m/s}^2$
**Find:** the first moment $t$ when the runner is level with the bus, in $\text{s}$
**Principle:** Meeting: $x_\text{B} = x_\text{R}$. The position of the bus is $\tfrac12\,a\,t^2$ and that of the runner is $x_{0\text{R}} + v\,t$. The equation is quadratic, and both positive roots are meetings: the first time the runner catches up with the bus, and the second time the bus, already faster, passes the runner.
**Formula:** $\tfrac12\,a\,t^2 = x_{0\text{R}} + v\,t$, that is, $\tfrac12\,a\,t^2 - v\,t - x_{0\text{R}} = 0$. The coefficients are $\tfrac12\,a$, $-v$ and $-x_{0\text{R}}$, so the quadratic formula gives $t = \dfrac{v \pm \sqrt{v^2 + 2\,a\,x_{0\text{R}}}}{a}$, and the first meeting is the smaller root
**Conversions:** All quantities are in SI units, so no conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** The expression under the root is $v^2 + 2\,a\,x_{0\text{R}} = (5.0\,\text{m/s})^2 + 2 \cdot 1.0\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot (-8.0\,\text{m}) = 9.0\,\text{m}^2/\text{s}^2$. So $t = \dfrac{5.0\,\text{m/s} - 3.0\,\text{m/s}}{1.0\,\text{m/s}^2} = 2.0\,\text{s}$
**Answer:** $2.0\,\text{s}$, two significant figures like the given values. The larger root $5.0 + 3.0 = 8.0\,\text{s}$ is the moment when the bus passes the runner.
**Reasonableness:** At $2.0\,\text{s}$ the runner is at $-8.0\,\text{m} + 5.0\,\text{m/s} \cdot 2.0\,\text{s} = 2.0\,\text{m}$, and the bus is at $\tfrac12 \cdot 1.0\,\text{m/s}^2 \cdot (2.0\,\text{s})^2 = 2.0\,\text{m}$. The velocity of the bus is then only $2.0\,\text{m/s}$, so the runner is clearly faster, as is right when catching up.

## fy4-12-L3-02

**Principle:** Two ideas: a meeting requires a non-negative expression under the root, and if there is no meeting, the distance is smallest when the velocities are equal (before that the runner is closing in, after that the bus pulls away).
**Formula:** The meeting $\tfrac12\,a\,t^2 = x_{0\text{R}} + v\,t$ gives $\tfrac12\,a\,t^2 - v\,t - x_{0\text{R}} = 0$, and the expression under the root is $v^2 + 2\,a\,x_{0\text{R}}$. ## fy4-12-L1-06
**Find:** what the intersection point of the $(t,v)$ graphs tells.
**Principle:** A point of a $(t,v)$ graph tells the velocity of the object at the moment $t$. When two graphs intersect, both objects have the same velocity at that moment. The position is determined by all the earlier motion, that is, by the area under the velocity graph, not by a single value of the velocity.
**Model answer:** The velocities of the cars are equal at the moment $4.0\,\text{s}$, but the positions may differ. A meeting cannot be read from this graph, because that needs a $(t,x)$ graph or a comparison of the position functions. The claim that the cars are at the same place mixes up velocity and position. The claim of the same distance travelled does not hold, because the distance is an area and not a single value. The claim of equal accelerations does not hold, because acceleration is the slope, and for intersecting lines the slopes are different.
**Reading the graph:** On the horizontal axis of the graph is time and on the vertical axis velocity. The vertical coordinate of the intersection point is the common velocity of both cars, and the horizontal coordinate is the moment $4.0\,\text{s}$.

## fy4-12-L1-08
**Find:** what condition determines the moment of passing.
**Principle:** Passing is a meeting: at the moment of passing, A and B are at the same place. Therefore the position of each is written as a function of time and the positions are set equal, $x_\text{A}(t) = x_\text{B}(t)$. The solution of the equation gives the moment.
**Model answer:** At the moment of passing the positions of the objects are equal at the same moment, that is, they are at the same point. Equal velocities do not determine the passing, because then the lead is at its greatest or smallest, not zero. Equal accelerations are not related to position, and for B the acceleration is zero. Equal distances travelled are not enough if the starting places are different, because the position is the starting place plus the distance travelled.
