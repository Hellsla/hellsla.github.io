Safety in Ahjo

This section will collect Ahjo's rules, the induction and the safety instructions for each kind of equipment.

  • Ahjo
  • Courses
  • Resource bank
  • Projects
  • Equipment
  • Safety

Ahjo's safety instructions are being prepared. The school's responsible staff will write and approve them before Ahjo is taken into use. This page has no approved instructions yet: until then, follow your teacher's instructions.

  1. Rules of the space

    Being prepared

    Covers: When and how Ahjo is used, order and tidiness at the workstation, the visit and loan records, and bringing your own devices into the space.

  2. Student induction

    Being prepared

    Covers: What the induction covers before using the space and each kind of equipment.

  3. Supervision and independent use

    Being prepared

    Covers: What is done under a teacher's supervision, on what terms the space can be used independently, and why nobody works there alone. Independent projects need the Makerspace licence (Ahjo 1).

  4. Safe use of tools and machines

    Being prepared

    Covers: General safety principles for hand and power tools, borrowing tools and reporting a broken machine.

  5. Soldering and electronics work

    Being prepared

    Covers: Using a soldering station, solder fumes, power supplies and electrical safety: voltage and energy limits for self-built devices, mains power and lithium batteries.

  6. 3D printing safety

    Being prepared

    Covers: Hot parts, materials, ventilation and keeping an eye on the printer.

  7. Drill and other mechanical tools

    Being prepared

    Covers: Clamping the workpiece, safety glasses, hair and clothing, handling bits and blades.

  8. Safe and hygienic use of VR equipment

    Being prepared

    Covers: A safe play area, breaks, motion sickness and cleaning the equipment.

  9. Ventilation, protective equipment and fire safety

    Being prepared

    Covers: Local exhaust, safety glasses, hearing protection and other protection, chemical warning labels, fire hazards and extinguishers.

  10. Storing projects

    Being prepared

    Covers: Labelling and storing unfinished work, and when a project counts as finished and is taken apart.

  11. Recycling and waste

    Being prepared

    Covers: Sorting materials, electronic scrap (WEEE), hazardous waste and returning components for reuse.

  12. What to do in accidents and dangerous situations

    Being prepared

    Covers: First aid, raising the alarm, stopping machines and reporting what happened.
