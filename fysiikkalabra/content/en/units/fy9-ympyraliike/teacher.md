## Lesson plan (75 min)

- **0–5 min, Think about it.** A satellite dish and a satellite that speeds along at about $3\,\text{km/s}$, yet the dish does not need turning. Students estimate in words with a partner why this is so and how far away the satellite must be. The answer is not given yet: it is at the end of the Key idea part of the theory summary ($r \approx 42\,200\,\text{km}$, about $35\,800\,\text{km}$ above the Earth's surface, $v \approx 3.07\,\text{km/s}$).
- **5–15 min, Predict (starting-level test).** Six questions alone, without notes. Ask the students to write in their notebook the numbers of the questions they got wrong.
- **15–25 min, Investigate.** Three satellites on the same orbital-speed curve $v(r)$ in pairs: the pair reads the radius of the orbit and the orbital speed of the GPS satellite from the graph and calculates the period (about $12\,\text{h}$). Discuss together: further away the orbit is longer and the orbital speed smaller, so the period grows quickly. The orbital speed depends only on the radius of the orbit and the mass of the Earth, not on the mass of the satellite.
- **25–35 min, Theory summary.** Go through the concept map: every branch starts from the free-body diagram. For each branch ask whether the body is in equilibrium or has an acceleration, and in which direction. Return to Think about it with the figure *geo*.
- **35–55 min, Apply.** Four multiple-choice questions in pairs (about $6\,\text{min}$), then four calculations alone. Fast students continue without hints; slower ones use the hints one at a time and do only the calculations *The moment on a pedal* and *A phone on a roundabout* in class, the others at home.
- **55–65 min, Check your understanding and exit ticket.** Alone: the concept and the calculation question and the exit ticket. The explanation question (why the mass of the satellite does not affect the orbital speed) is homework, and students assess it themselves with the criteria.
- **65–75 min, Problems.** Ten minutes is enough for the start of one long problem in exam conditions, without the model answer: recommended *A boat trailer and the nose weight* a–b (a calculation problem) or *The loop of a roller coaster* a–b (combining modules). The rest of the problem and the other five problems are homework, two or three at a time before the next lesson.

## Using the starting-level test
The Predict questions are a diagnostic test, not a grade. Each question matches one chapter:

- Question 1: the lever arm (chapter *Moment: the turning effect of a force*).
- Question 2: normal forces and the moment condition (chapter *Equilibrium of a body: forces and moments*).
- Question 3: period and speed on the same record (chapter *Moving in a circle: period and speed*).
- Question 4: friction as the centripetal force on a bend (chapter *Centripetal acceleration and the equation of motion in a circle*).
- Question 5: gravitation at twice the distance (chapter *The gravitational force between bodies*).
- Question 6: weightlessness in orbit (chapter *Satellites and planets in circular orbits*).

If over a third of the group answers question 4 or 6 wrong, draw two free-body diagrams on the board before the Investigate task: a car on a bend and a satellite. Neither has a separate “centripetal force” or “centrifugal force”; a real force (friction or gravitation) gives the centripetal acceleration. Weightlessness does not mean that there is no gravitation, but that there is no normal force. The error of question 5 (“half as large”) is taken up again in the Apply calculation *The period of a science satellite*.

## Pair work
- Investigate: one partner reads the values from the graph, the other calculates the orbital speed from the formula $v = \sqrt{\tfrac{\gamma M}{r}}$ and compares it with the reading; then the period $T = \tfrac{2\pi r}{v}$ is calculated together.
- Apply multiple-choice questions: each partner first chooses alone and only then justifies the choice to the other. The answer is given only when the pair agrees.
- In the long problem *The mass of a stone with a metre stick* (assessing the report) pairs can write the plan of the experiment together and assess it with another pair using the marking scheme.

## Fast and slower students
- **Fast students** do the Apply calculations without hints and move straight on to the long problem *Centripetal force on a rotating platform* (graph and linearisation, $20$ points), which has the most stages. Extra question: at what speed would a passenger at the top of the loop be just weightless when $r = 10\,\text{m}$? (The condition $v^2/r = g$ gives $v = \sqrt{gr} \approx 9.9\,\text{m/s}$.)
- **Slower students** do the calculations *The moment on a pedal* and *A phone on a roundabout* of the Apply part with the hints and leave the calculations *A snow shovel held level* and *The period of a science satellite* for home. The *Review if needed* links of the theory summary lead straight to the original chapters, which have basic-level exercises.

## Assessment
Students assess the long problems themselves with the marking scheme. The points follow the division of the matriculation exam: the graph and fitting problem *Centripetal force on a rotating platform* is of the Part III type ($20$ points), the other five of the Part II type ($15$ points each), $95$ points in all. If the teacher assesses them, the same criteria work: the free-body diagram, the choice of the equilibrium conditions or the equation of motion, the lever arms and directions of turning of the moments, the symbolic solution, unit conversions by substitution, precision and reasonableness; in the written part, assessing the claim with numbers. All the data are invented examples, not real measurements.
