## fy3-3-L1-01
**Given:** $\theta = 21\,{}^\circ\text{C}$
**Find:** temperature in kelvins, $T$
**Principle:** A kelvin and a degree Celsius are the same size, and the scales differ only in their zero point: $0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ is $273.15\,\text{K}$. So you get the reading in kelvins by adding $273.15$ to the Celsius reading.
**Formula:** $T = \theta + 273.15$, and this is already solved for the quantity to find, $T$
**Conversions:** The Celsius temperature is converted to kelvins in this formula; no other unit conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $T = 21 + 273.15 = 294.15\,\text{K}$
**Answer:** $294\,\text{K}$, in whole kelvins, because the temperature $21\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ is given in whole degrees.
**Reasonableness:** Room temperature is about $20\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, so the estimate is $20 + 273 \approx 293\,\text{K}$, and the result is close to it. Check by converting back: $294.15 - 273.15 = 21$, so the conversion returns the starting value. A temperature in kelvins is positive, as it should be.

## fy3-3-L1-02
**Given:** $T = 350\,\text{K}$
**Find:** temperature in degrees Celsius, $\theta$
**Principle:** The intervals of the Kelvin and Celsius scales are the same size, and the difference between the zero points is $273.15$. So you get the Celsius reading by subtracting $273.15$ from the reading in kelvins.
**Formula:** $T = \theta + 273.15$, so $\theta = T - 273.15$
**Conversions:** The temperature in kelvins is converted to degrees Celsius with this formula; no other unit conversions are needed.
**Substitution:** $\theta = 350 - 273.15 = 76.85\,{}^\circ\text{C}$
**Answer:** $77\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, in whole degrees, because $350\,\text{K}$ is given in whole kelvins.
**Reasonableness:** The boiling point of water is $373\,\text{K}$, or $100\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, and $350\,\text{K}$ is $23\,\text{K}$ below it, so the estimate is $100 - 23 = 77\,{}^\circ\text{C}$. The result is also above zero, because $350\,\text{K} > 273\,\text{K}$. Check by converting back: $76.85 + 273.15 = 350$.

**Principle:** The intervals of the Celsius and Kelvin scales are the same size, and the difference between the zero points is $273.15$. So $\theta$ is found by subtracting $273.15$ from the reading in kelvins. The formula also works when the result is negative.
**Formula:** $T = \theta + 273.15$, so $\theta = T - 273.15$

Check by converting back: $-195.75 + 273.15 = 77.4\,\text{K}$. Estimate: $77 - 273 \approx -196$.

## fy3-3-L1-04
**Given:** $\theta_1 = 20\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, $\theta_2 = 45\,{}^\circ\text{C}$
**Find:** change in temperature in kelvins, $\Delta T$
**Principle:** A degree Celsius and a kelvin are the same size, and the zero points of the scales differ only by the constant $273.15$. The constant cancels in the subtraction, so a change in temperature is the same on both scales.
**Formula:** $\Delta T = T_2 - T_1 = (\theta_2 + 273.15) - (\theta_1 + 273.15)$, so $\Delta T = \theta_2 - \theta_1$
**Conversions:** No conversion is needed, because a change in temperature is the same in degrees Celsius and in kelvins; only the unit changes.
**Substitution:** $\Delta T = 45 - 20 = 25\,\text{K}$
**Answer:** $25\,\text{K}$, two significant figures like the given temperatures.
**Reasonableness:** Check in kelvins: $T_1 = 293.15\,\text{K}$ and $T_2 = 318.15\,\text{K}$, so the difference is $318.15 - 293.15 = 25\,\text{K}$. The sign is positive, because the water warms up.

## fy3-3-L2-01
**Given:** $T_1 = 288\,\text{K}$, temperature rise $\Delta T = 45\,\text{K}$
**Find:** final temperature in degrees Celsius, $\theta_2$
**Principle:** A change in temperature is the same in kelvins and in degrees Celsius, so the rise can be added directly to the temperature in kelvins. Only at the end is the result converted to degrees Celsius.
**Formula:** $T_2 = T_1 + \Delta T$ and $\theta_2 = T_2 - 273.15$, so $\theta_2 = T_1 + \Delta T - 273.15$
**Conversions:** The rise $45\,\text{K}$ is added as it is. The final temperature is converted from kelvins to degrees Celsius by subtracting $273.15$.
**Substitution:** $T_2 = 288 + 45 = 333\,\text{K}$, and $\theta_2 = 333 - 273.15 = 59.85\,{}^\circ\text{C}$
**Answer:** $60\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, in whole degrees, because the initial values are given in whole kelvins.
**Reasonableness:** The initial temperature $288\,\text{K}$ is about $15\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, and a rise of $45\,\text{K}$ gives about $15 + 45 = 60\,{}^\circ\text{C}$. Exact check: $288 - 273.15 = 14.85\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ and $14.85 + 45 = 59.85\,{}^\circ\text{C}$. The gas warms up, so the final temperature is higher than the initial temperature.

## fy3-3-L2-02
**Given:** $(\theta_1,\, p_1) = (0\,{}^\circ\text{C},\ 90.0\,\text{kPa})$ and $(\theta_2,\, p_2) = (100\,{}^\circ\text{C},\ 123\,\text{kPa})$; the pressure depends linearly on the temperature
**Find:** pressure $p$ at the temperature $\theta = 35\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ in kilopascals
**Principle:** The container is closed and rigid, so the amount of gas and the volume stay the same and the pressure changes only with the temperature. The problem allows a linear model, so the points determine a straight line, and you read the pressure at $35\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ from the line.
**Formula:** slope $k = \dfrac{p_2 - p_1}{\theta_2 - \theta_1}$ and line $p = p_1 + k\theta$, because $\theta_1 = 0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$; so $p = p_1 + \dfrac{p_2 - p_1}{\theta_2 - \theta_1}\,\theta$
**Conversions:** No conversions are needed: the pressures are in the same unit, and the temperature difference $\theta_2 - \theta_1$ is the same in degrees Celsius and in kelvins. The unit of the slope is $\tfrac{\text{kPa}}{{}^\circ\text{C}}$.
**Substitution:** $k = \dfrac{(123 - 90.0)\,\text{kPa}}{(100 - 0)\,{}^\circ\text{C}} = 0.33\,\tfrac{\text{kPa}}{{}^\circ\text{C}}$, and $p = 90.0\,\text{kPa} + 0.33\,\tfrac{\text{kPa}}{{}^\circ\text{C}} \cdot 35\,{}^\circ\text{C} = 101.55\,\text{kPa}$
**Answer:** $102\,\text{kPa}$. The pressure difference $\Delta p = 123 - 90.0 = 33\,\text{kPa}$ has two figures, so $k = 0.33\,\tfrac{\text{kPa}}{{}^\circ\text{C}}$ and $k\theta \approx 12\,\text{kPa}$; the sum $90.0 + 12 = 102\,\text{kPa}$ is rounded to whole kilopascals.
**Reasonableness:** The temperature $35\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ is $35\,\%$ of the way from $0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ to $100\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, so the pressure must be $35\,\%$ of the way through the range $90.0\ldots123\,\text{kPa}$: $90.0 + 0.35 \cdot 33 = 101.55\,\text{kPa}$. The result is between the pressures of the points and below the mean of the range, $106.5\,\text{kPa}$.

## fy3-3-L2-03
**Given:** $(\theta_1,\, p_1) = (10\,{}^\circ\text{C},\ 110\,\text{kPa})$ and $(\theta_2,\, p_2) = (80\,{}^\circ\text{C},\ 137\,\text{kPa})$; the pressure depends linearly on the temperature
**Find:** pressure $p$ at the temperature $\theta = -50\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ in kilopascals
**Principle:** In a rigid closed container the amount of gas and the volume stay the same, so the pressure depends only on the temperature. When the points are placed on a straight line, the line can be continued outside the measured range, that is, extrapolated.
**Reading the graph:** On the graph the points $(10\,{}^\circ\text{C};\ 110\,\text{kPa})$ and $(80\,{}^\circ\text{C};\ 137\,\text{kPa})$ are on the same line. The line is continued to the left to $-50\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, which is $60\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ to the left of the point $\theta_1$, and the pressure is read there.
**Formula:** $k = \dfrac{p_2 - p_1}{\theta_2 - \theta_1}$ and $p = p_1 + k(\theta - \theta_1)$, so $p = p_1 + \dfrac{p_2 - p_1}{\theta_2 - \theta_1}\,(\theta - \theta_1)$
**Conversions:** No conversions are needed: the pressures are in kilopascals, and the temperature differences are the same in degrees Celsius and in kelvins.
**Substitution:** $k = \dfrac{(137 - 110)\,\text{kPa}}{(80 - 10)\,{}^\circ\text{C}} = 0.385714\,\tfrac{\text{kPa}}{{}^\circ\text{C}}$, and $p = 110\,\text{kPa} + 0.385714\,\tfrac{\text{kPa}}{{}^\circ\text{C}} \cdot (-50 - 10)\,{}^\circ\text{C} = 110\,\text{kPa} - 23.14\,\text{kPa} = 86.86\,\text{kPa}$
**Answer:** $87\,\text{kPa}$. The pressure difference $\Delta p = 137 - 110 = 27\,\text{kPa}$ has two figures, so $k = 0.39\,\tfrac{\text{kPa}}{{}^\circ\text{C}}$ and $k\,\Delta\theta \approx 23\,\text{kPa}$; the difference $110 - 23 = 87\,\text{kPa}$ is rounded to whole kilopascals (a difference of readings has the figures of the difference).
**Reasonableness:** When the temperature is below the measured range, the pressure must be lower than the lowest measured value, $110\,\text{kPa}$, and it is, but clearly above zero. An independent check: if the pressure is proportional to the temperature in kelvins, $p \approx 110\,\text{kPa} \cdot \dfrac{223.15\,\text{K}}{283.15\,\text{K}} = 86.7\,\text{kPa}$, which is almost the same.

## fy3-3-L2-04
**Given:** $\theta_\text{night} = -173\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ and $\theta_\text{day} = 127\,{}^\circ\text{C}$
**Find:** ratio of the temperatures in kelvins $\dfrac{T_\text{day}}{T_\text{night}}$
**Principle:** The ratio is asked for the temperatures in kelvins, because only the zero of the Kelvin scale is absolute zero, and the kinetic energy of the particles is proportional to the temperature in kelvins. The ratio of Celsius temperatures has no physical meaning.
**Formula:** $T = \theta + 273.15$, and the ratio $\dfrac{T_\text{day}}{T_\text{night}} = \dfrac{\theta_\text{day} + 273.15}{\theta_\text{night} + 273.15}$
**Conversions:** Both temperatures are converted to kelvins by adding $273.15$: $T_\text{night} = -173 + 273.15 = 100.15\,\text{K}$ and $T_\text{day} = 127 + 273.15 = 400.15\,\text{K}$.
**Substitution:** $\dfrac{T_\text{day}}{T_\text{night}} = \dfrac{400.15\,\text{K}}{100.15\,\text{K}} = 3.9955$
**Answer:** $4.00$ times. Whole degrees give temperatures of $100\,\text{K}$ and $400\,\text{K}$ to the nearest kelvin, three significant figures in each, so the quotient also has three.
**Reasonableness:** In kelvins the temperatures are about $100\,\text{K}$ and $400\,\text{K}$, so the ratio is about $4$. The day side is warmer, so the ratio is above $1$. The ratio of the Celsius temperatures would be $\tfrac{127}{-173} \approx -0.73$, which is a meaningless comparison.

## fy3-3-L3-01
**Given:** $p_1 = 300\,\text{kPa}$ (total pressure), $\theta_1 = 15\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, $\theta_2 = 35\,{}^\circ\text{C}$
**Find:** pressure $p_2$ at the temperature $\theta_2$ in kilopascals
**Principle:** The tyre does not leak and its volume does not change, so the amount of air and the volume are constant. Then the pressure is directly proportional to the temperature in kelvins: the particles of the warmer gas move faster and hit the wall more often and harder. The proportionality does not hold for the Celsius temperature.
**Formula:** $\dfrac{p_2}{p_1} = \dfrac{T_2}{T_1}$, so $p_2 = p_1\,\dfrac{T_2}{T_1}$
**Conversions:** The temperatures are converted to kelvins: $T_1 = 15 + 273.15 = 288.15\,\text{K}$ and $T_2 = 35 + 273.15 = 308.15\,\text{K}$. The pressure stays in kilopascals, because it appears only in a ratio.
**Substitution:** $p_2 = 300\,\text{kPa} \cdot \dfrac{308.15\,\text{K}}{288.15\,\text{K}} = 320.8\,\text{kPa}$
**Answer:** $321\,\text{kPa}$, three significant figures like the given values.
**Reasonableness:** The temperature in kelvins rises by $20\,\text{K}$, or about $\dfrac{20}{288} \approx 6.9\,\%$, so the pressure rises by about $0.069 \cdot 300\,\text{kPa} \approx 21\,\text{kPa}$, and $300 + 21 = 321\,\text{kPa}$. The temperature rises, so the pressure must increase. The ratio of Celsius temperatures $\tfrac{35}{15}$ would give an unreasonable $700\,\text{kPa}$.

## fy3-3-L3-02
**Given:** $p_1 = 150\,\text{kPa}$, $\theta_1 = 27.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, $p_2 = 200\,\text{kPa}$
**Find:** final temperature in degrees Celsius, $\theta_2$
**Principle:** The container is closed and rigid, so the volume and the amount of gas stay the same, and the pressure is directly proportional to the temperature in kelvins. So the ratio of the pressures equals the ratio of the temperatures in kelvins.
**Formula:** $\dfrac{p_2}{p_1} = \dfrac{T_2}{T_1}$, so $T_2 = T_1\,\dfrac{p_2}{p_1}$, and $\theta_2 = T_2 - 273.15$
**Conversions:** The initial temperature is converted to kelvins: $T_1 = 27.0 + 273.15 = 300.15\,\text{K}$. At the end the result is converted to degrees Celsius by subtracting $273.15$. The pressures do not need converting, because they are in the same unit.
**Substitution:** $T_2 = 300.15\,\text{K} \cdot \dfrac{200\,\text{kPa}}{150\,\text{kPa}} = 400.2\,\text{K}$, and $\theta_2 = 400.2 - 273.15 = 127.05\,{}^\circ\text{C}$
**Answer:** $127\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, three significant figures like the value $27.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$.
**Reasonableness:** The pressure increases by a factor of $\tfrac{4}{3}$, so the temperature in kelvins must increase by the same factor: about $300\,\text{K} \to 400\,\text{K}$, or $127\,{}^\circ\text{C}$. Check: $\tfrac{p}{T}$ at the start is $\tfrac{150}{300.15} = 0.4998\,\tfrac{\text{kPa}}{\text{K}}$ and at the end $\tfrac{200}{400.2} = 0.4998\,\tfrac{\text{kPa}}{\text{K}}$. The ratio of Celsius temperatures would wrongly give $36\,{}^\circ\text{C}$.

## fy3-3-L3-03
**Given:** $\theta_1 = 20.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ and average kinetic energy $E_2 = \tfrac{1}{2}E_1$
**Find:** final temperature in degrees Celsius, $\theta_2$
**Principle:** The average kinetic energy of the particles is directly proportional to the temperature in kelvins, because the zero of the Kelvin scale is absolute zero, where the kinetic energy is at its smallest (zero in the model). When the energy is halved, the temperature in kelvins is also halved. You must not halve the Celsius temperature.
**Formula:** $\dfrac{E_2}{E_1} = \dfrac{T_2}{T_1} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so $T_2 = \dfrac{T_1}{2}$, and $\theta_2 = T_2 - 273.15$
**Conversions:** The initial temperature is converted to kelvins: $T_1 = 20.0 + 273.15 = 293.15\,\text{K}$. At the end $T_2$ is converted back to degrees Celsius by subtracting $273.15$.
**Substitution:** $T_2 = \dfrac{293.15\,\text{K}}{2} = 146.575\,\text{K}$, and $\theta_2 = 146.575 - 273.15 = -126.575\,{}^\circ\text{C}$
**Answer:** $-127\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, three significant figures like the value $20.0\,{}^\circ\text{C}$.
**Reasonableness:** The gas must cool, so the result is below $20\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, and it is above absolute zero, $-273.15\,{}^\circ\text{C}$. Check: $\tfrac{146.575\,\text{K}}{293.15\,\text{K}} = 0.5$. Halving the Celsius temperature would give $10\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, but then the temperature in kelvins would be only $283\,\text{K}$, or $97\,\%$ of the initial value, not half.
