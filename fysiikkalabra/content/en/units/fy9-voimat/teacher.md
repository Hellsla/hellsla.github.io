## Lesson plan (75 min)

- **0–5 min, Think about it.** The car and the trailer. Pairs first explain in words why force and counterforce do not prevent setting off. The answer is not given yet: it is at the end of the Key idea part of the theory summary (the motion of the trailer is decided only by the forces acting on the trailer). What moves the car forwards is left for the long task *What moves you forwards?*.
- **5–15 min, Predict (diagnostic test).** Six questions alone, without notes. The students write the numbers of the questions they got wrong in their notebooks.
- **15–23 min, Investigate.** The graph of the two skydivers in pairs. Debrief together: what the horizontal curve says about the net force, and why the heavier skydiver's terminal velocity is larger.
- **23–33 min, Theory summary.** Go through the concept map: draw the free-body diagram on a slope on the board and ask which arrows are forces and which are components. Return to Think about it.
- **33–55 min, Apply.** Four multiple-choice questions in pairs (about $8\,\text{min}$), then the calculations *A crane lowers a load* and *A steep sledging hill* alone. *Pulling a sledge at an angle* and *A volleyball hit upwards* are optional in class for fast students; the others do them at home. In every calculation the free-body diagram is drawn in the notebook before the equation; check the diagrams by walking around the class.
- **55–65 min, Check your understanding and exit ticket.** Alone. The explanation task is self-assessed with the criteria.
- **65–75 min, Tasks.** The start of one long task in exam conditions without the model answer: recommended *Winching a boat* a–b (free-body diagram and calculation) or *A snowmobile and two sledges* a–b (connected bodies). The rest of the task and the other five tasks are homework, two or three at a time before the next lesson.

## Using the diagnostic test
The Predict questions are a diagnostic test, not a grade. Each question corresponds to one chapter:

- Questions 1 and 2: the first and third laws (chapter *Interaction and force: Newton's first and third laws*).
- Question 3: the direction of the net force (chapter *Newton's second law*).
- Question 4: oblique pull and the normal force (chapter *Free-body diagrams and the combined effect of forces*).
- Question 5: tension in connected bodies (chapter *Equation of motion for connected bodies*).
- Question 6: static friction (chapter *Weight, friction and the inclined plane*).

If more than a third of the group answers questions 1 or 3 wrongly, draw the free-body diagrams of a sliding object and a decelerating lift on the board before the Apply part and ask for the cause of each force: a "force of motion" has no cause. Mistakes in question 5 are usually corrected in the Apply task *Justified claim: pulley*.

## Free-body diagrams and assessment
In the exam, points for the free-body diagram are lost for a missing force, an extra force or a force drawn in the wrong direction. In practice the feedback names each mistake separately. Remind the students that velocity and acceleration are not drawn as forces and that components are drawn with dashed lines.

## Pair work
- Investigate: one reads the graph and says it out loud, the other draws the free-body diagram; the roles are swapped in the multiple-choice questions of the Apply part.
- Apply task *Find the mistake: a box on a belt*: each first chooses on their own and only then justifies the choice to their partner.
- In the long task *What moves you forwards?* (written explanation) pairs can write part b together and assess it with another pair using the marking scheme.

## Fast and slow
- **Fast students** do the Apply calculations without hints and move on to the long task *A cart and a force sensor* (graph and fit, $20$ points). Extra question: how large a mass must hang from the end of the string for the cart to accelerate at $1.0\,\text{m/s}^2$? (With the hanging mass $m_\text{p}$, $m_\text{p} g - F_\mu = (m + m_\text{p})a$, so $m_\text{p} = \tfrac{ma + F_\mu}{g - a} \approx 0.12\,\text{kg}$.)
- **Slow students** do the Apply calculations *A crane lowers a load* and *A steep sledging hill* with hints and leave *A volleyball hit upwards* for home. The *Review if needed* links of the theory summary lead straight to the original chapters, which have the steps of drawing a free-body diagram and basic exercises.

## Assessment
The long tasks are self-assessed with the marking scheme. The calculation tasks, the written explanation task, the experimental task and the task combining modules are $15$-point tasks (exam part II), the graph task *A cart and a force sensor* a $20$-point task (part III). If the teacher assesses them, the free-body diagram and the equations of motion are assessed separately, so that a wrong diagram does not cost an otherwise correct calculation. All data are invented examples; the curves of the Investigate graph are values from the simulation's model.
