FY2 · 1 cr · Compulsory

Physics, environment and society

Where does our energy come from, and how does using it affect the environment?

Lessons

  1. Energy and power

    chapters 1–4
  2. Chapter 1Forms of energy and conservation of energy

    Why does a bouncing ball eventually come to rest on the floor?

  3. Chapter 2Energy transformations and the joule

    Why does a bicycle move, where does its energy ultimately come from and how can the amount of energy be given as a single number?

  4. Chapter 3Power: $P = \Delta E/\Delta t$

    Why can you not work out an electricity bill from the power of a device alone?

  5. Chapter 4Efficiency

    Why does an incandescent lamp get so hot that it can burn your finger, even though its job is to give light, and why does an LED lamp give just as much light with much less electricity?

  6. Energy production and the environment

    chapters 5–8
  7. Chapter 5Fuels and heat of combustion $E = Hm$

    Why do a kilogram of petrol and a kilogram of air-dried firewood release different amounts of energy when they burn, even though both are just burning matter?

  8. Chapter 6Ways of producing energy and how they compare

    Why do nuclear power, hydropower, wind power and fuel-fired power plants all run in the Finnish electricity grid at the same time, even though one type of plant could in principle produce all the electricity?

  9. Chapter 7Transferring energy: electricity and heat

    Why is the voltage in power lines hundreds of kilovolts when a wall socket gives only $230\,\text{V}$, and why are district heating pipes wrapped in thick insulation?

  10. Chapter 8Energy production, the environment and climate change

    Why does the way we produce electricity decide how much carbon dioxide ends up in the atmosphere, even though the electricity from a socket at home is always the same?

  3. 4 goalsModule goals

    The student

    • becomes familiar with energy as a central concept of physics
    • knows different forms of energy and ways of producing energy
    • can compare the orders of magnitude of different ways of producing energy and their environmental effects
    • gains the skills to take part in discussion and decision-making about the environment and technology from the point of view of a sustainable energy economy
