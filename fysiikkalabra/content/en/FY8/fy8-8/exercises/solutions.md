## fy8-8-L1-01
**Given:** the nucleus of uranium-235 ${}^{235}_{92}\text{U}$: mass number $A = 235$, atomic number $Z = 92$
**Find:** the number of neutrons $N$
**Principle:** The mass number is the number of all nucleons of the nucleus and the atomic number is the number of protons. The remaining nucleons are neutrons.
**Formula:** $A = Z + N$, from which $N = A - Z$
**Conversions:** None are needed, because the numbers are counts.
**Substitution:** $N = 235 - 92 = 143$
**Answer:** $143$. The result is the exact number of neutrons, because nucleons are counted as pieces.
**Reasonableness:** There are more neutrons than protons, as in heavy nuclei: $143 : 92 \approx 1.6$. Uranium-238 has three more neutrons, $146$, because isotopes have the same $Z$ but a different $N$.

## fy8-8-L1-02
**Given:** the parent nucleus ${}^{222}_{86}\text{Rn}$, alpha decay; the alpha particle is ${}^{4}_{2}\text{He}$
**Find:** the mass number $A'$ of the daughter nucleus
**Principle:** In a decay equation the mass number is conserved: the nucleons of the parent nucleus are shared between the daughter nucleus and the alpha particle, which has four nucleons.
**Formula:** $A = A' + 4$, from which $A' = A - 4$
**Conversions:** None are needed, because the mass numbers are counts.
**Substitution:** $A' = 222 - 4 = 218$
**Answer:** $218$. The result is an exact integer. The daughter nucleus is polonium-218 ${}^{218}_{84}\text{Po}$, because at the same time $Z$ decreases by two.
**Reasonableness:** The equation ${}^{222}_{86}\text{Rn} \to {}^{218}_{84}\text{Po} + {}^{4}_{2}\text{He}$ balances: $222 = 218 + 4$ and $86 = 84 + 2$. The same step can be seen in the series of uranium-238, from radon to polonium.

## fy8-8-L1-03
**Given:** the parent nucleus ${}^{14}_{6}\text{C}$, beta minus decay; the electron is ${}^{0}_{-1}\text{e}$
**Find:** the atomic number $Z'$ of the daughter nucleus
**Principle:** In beta minus decay a neutron changes into a proton and the electron that is created leaves. The charge number is conserved when the charge number $-1$ of the electron is counted; the antineutrino is neutral.
**Formula:** $Z = Z' + (-1)$, from which $Z' = Z + 1$
**Conversions:** None are needed, because the atomic numbers are counts.
**Substitution:** $Z' = 6 + 1 = 7$
**Answer:** $7$. The result is an exact integer. The atomic number $7$ is nitrogen, so the daughter nucleus is ${}^{14}_{7}\text{N}$.
**Reasonableness:** The mass number stays at $14$ and the charge number balances: $6 = 7 - 1$. Carbon-14 has eight neutrons and six protons, that is, more neutrons than suits a light stable nucleus, so beta minus decay moves it towards the band of stable nuclei.

## fy8-8-L1-04
**Given:** the energy of the gamma photon $E_\gamma = 59.54\,\text{keV}$, $h = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s}$, $c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$, $1\,\text{keV} = 1000\,\text{eV}$, $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$
**Find:** the wavelength $\lambda$ of the photon in picometres
**Principle:** Gamma radiation is electromagnetic radiation, so the energy of a photon is $\tfrac{hc}{\lambda}$, as for a photon of visible light.
**Formula:** $E_\gamma = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$, from which $\lambda = \dfrac{hc}{E_\gamma}$
**Conversions:** $E_\gamma = 59.54\,\text{keV} = 59.54 \cdot 1000 \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J} = 9.5383 \cdot 10^{-15}\,\text{J}$; finally $1\,\text{m} = 10^{12}\,\text{pm}$.
**Substitution:** The product $hc = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s} \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s} = 1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}$. $\lambda = \dfrac{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}}{9.5383 \cdot 10^{-15}\,\text{J}} = 2.0826 \cdot 10^{-11}\,\text{m} = 20.826\,\text{pm}$
**Answer:** $20.8\,\text{pm}$, three significant figures; the energy is given with four figures, and $20.83\,\text{pm}$ is just as correct.
**Reasonableness:** The caesium photon of the example, $661.7\,\text{keV}$, has about $11$ times more energy, and its wavelength of $1.874\,\text{pm}$ is about $11$ times shorter: $20.8 : 1.874 \approx 11$. The wavelength is smaller than the diameter of an atom, about $100\,\text{pm}$.

## fy8-8-L1-05
**Given:** the parent nucleus ${}^{18}_{9}\text{F}$, beta plus decay; the positron is ${}^{0}_{+1}\text{e}$
**Find:** the neutron number $N'$ of the daughter nucleus
**Principle:** In beta plus decay a proton changes into a neutron, and a positron and a neutrino leave the nucleus. The mass number is conserved, and the charge number is conserved when the charge number $+1$ of the positron is counted.
**Formula:** $A' = A$ and $Z = Z' + 1$, so $Z' = Z - 1$ and $N' = A' - Z' = A - (Z - 1)$
**Conversions:** None are needed, because the numbers are counts.
**Substitution:** $Z' = 9 - 1 = 8$ and $N' = 18 - 8 = 10$
**Answer:** $10$. The result is the exact number of neutrons. The daughter nucleus is oxygen-18 ${}^{18}_{8}\text{O}$.
**Reasonableness:** The parent nucleus has $18 - 9 = 9$ neutrons, and a proton changing into a neutron adds one: $9 + 1 = 10$. The equation ${}^{18}_{9}\text{F} \to {}^{18}_{8}\text{O} + {}^{0}_{+1}\text{e} + \nu$ balances: $18 = 18 + 0$ and $9 = 8 + 1$.

## fy8-8-L2-01
**Given:** the levels of nickel-60 $E_\text{u} = 2505.8\,\text{keV}$ and $E_\text{l} = 1332.5\,\text{keV}$, $h = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s}$, $c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$, $1\,\text{keV} = 1000\,\text{eV}$, $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$
**Find:** the wavelength $\lambda$ of the gamma photon in picometres
**Principle:** The energy of a nucleus is quantised. Energy is conserved, so the energy of the photon that leaves in a transition is the difference between the levels, as in transitions of an atom, and the energy of a photon is $\tfrac{hc}{\lambda}$.
**Formula:** $\dfrac{hc}{\lambda} = E_\text{u} - E_\text{l}$, from which $\lambda = \dfrac{hc}{E_\text{u} - E_\text{l}}$
**Conversions:** $E_\text{u} - E_\text{l} = 2505.8\,\text{keV} - 1332.5\,\text{keV} = 1173.3\,\text{keV} = 1173.3 \cdot 1000 \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J} = 1.8796 \cdot 10^{-13}\,\text{J}$; finally $1\,\text{m} = 10^{12}\,\text{pm}$.
**Substitution:** The product $hc = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s} \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s} = 1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}$. $\lambda = \dfrac{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}}{1.8796 \cdot 10^{-13}\,\text{J}} = 1.0568 \cdot 10^{-12}\,\text{m} = 1.0568\,\text{pm}$
**Answer:** $1.06\,\text{pm}$, three significant figures; more precisely $1.057\,\text{pm}$, four figures like the energy difference $1173.3\,\text{keV}$.
**Reasonableness:** The measured gamma photon is $1173.2\,\text{keV}$, one of the two peaks of the gamma spectrum of cobalt-60; the difference of $0.1\,\text{keV}$ from the level difference $1173.3\,\text{keV}$ comes almost entirely from rounding of the levels (the exact level difference is $1173.24\,\text{keV}$), and the recoil of the nucleus takes only about $0.01\,\text{keV}$. The difference does not change the wavelength at three-figure precision. Its energy is a little smaller than the $1332.5\,\text{keV}$ of the other photon, so the wavelength is a little longer than its $0.931\,\text{pm}$, as it should be.

## fy8-8-L2-02
**Given:** the parent nucleus ${}^{214}_{83}\text{Bi}$; first beta minus decay, then alpha decay
**Find:** the atomic number $Z''$ of the final nucleus
**Principle:** The charge number is conserved in both decays. In beta minus decay a neutron changes into a proton, so $Z$ increases by one; the alpha particle takes two protons, so $Z$ decreases by two.
**Formula:** $Z' = Z + 1$ and $Z'' = Z' - 2$, so $Z'' = Z + 1 - 2$
**Conversions:** None are needed, because the atomic numbers are counts.
**Substitution:** $Z' = 83 + 1 = 84$ and $Z'' = 84 - 2 = 82$
**Answer:** $82$. The result is an exact integer. The nucleus is lead-210 ${}^{210}_{82}\text{Pb}$, because the mass number decreases only in the alpha decay: $214 - 4 = 210$.
**Reasonableness:** The intermediate step is polonium-214 ${}^{214}_{84}\text{Po}$, and this is exactly how the series goes in the uranium series of the simulation, from step $9$ to step $11$. The two decays together reduce the atomic number by one: $+1 - 2 = -1$.

## fy8-8-L2-03
**Given:** the energy of the gamma photon $E_\gamma = 661.7\,\text{keV}$, the wavelength of green light $\lambda = 550\,\text{nm}$, $h = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s}$, $c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$, $1\,\text{keV} = 1000\,\text{eV}$, $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$
**Find:** the ratio of the energies, how many times $E_\gamma$ is the energy $E_\text{g}$ of a green photon
**Principle:** The energy of each photon depends only on its wavelength or frequency. The energy of the green photon is calculated from the wavelength, and the comparison is made with one division in the same unit.
**Formula:** $E_\text{g} = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$, and the ratio asked for is $\dfrac{E_\gamma}{E_\text{g}} = \dfrac{E_\gamma \lambda}{hc}$
**Conversions:** $\lambda = 550\,\text{nm} = 550 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}$ and $E_\gamma = 661.7\,\text{keV} = 661.7 \cdot 1000\,\text{eV} = 661\,700\,\text{eV}$; the energy of the green photon is converted to electronvolts by substituting $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$.
**Substitution:** The product $hc = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s} \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s} = 1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}$. $E_\text{g} = \dfrac{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}}{550 \cdot 10^{-9}\,\text{m}} = 3.6118 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J} = 2.2545\,\text{eV}$ and $661\,700\,\text{eV} : 2.2545\,\text{eV} = 293\,500$
**Answer:** about $293\,000$, that is, $2.93 \cdot 10^{5}$ times greater, three significant figures like the wavelength $550\,\text{nm}$.
**Reasonableness:** An estimate with round numbers: $660\,000\,\text{eV} : 2\,\text{eV} \approx 330\,000$, the same order of magnitude. For this 662 keV line the difference between the levels of the nucleus is hundreds of thousands of times greater than the differences between the levels of the electrons of an atom, as the theory of the chapter says.

## fy8-8-L2-04
**Given:** the parent nucleus ${}^{232}_{90}\text{Th}$; alpha decay and after it two beta minus decays
**Find:** the neutron number $N$ of the final nucleus
**Principle:** The mass number and the charge number are conserved in every decay. Alpha decay decreases $Z$ by two and $A$ by four; beta minus decay increases $Z$ by one and does not change $A$.
**Formula:** $A_\text{final} = A - 4$, $Z_\text{final} = Z - 2 + 1 + 1$ and $N = A_\text{final} - Z_\text{final}$
**Conversions:** None are needed, because the numbers are counts.
**Substitution:** $A_\text{final} = 232 - 4 = 228$, $Z_\text{final} = 90 - 2 + 1 + 1 = 90$ and $N = 228 - 90 = 138$
**Answer:** $138$. The result is the exact number of neutrons. The nucleus is thorium-228 ${}^{228}_{90}\text{Th}$.
**Reasonableness:** The chain is ${}^{232}_{90}\text{Th} \to {}^{228}_{88}\text{Ra} \to {}^{228}_{89}\text{Ac} \to {}^{228}_{90}\text{Th}$. At the end we are in thorium again, but there are four fewer neutrons than at the start ($142 - 4 = 138$): the alpha particle took two, and each beta decay changed one neutron into a proton.

## fy8-8-L3-01
**Given:** initial nucleus ${}^{232}_{90}\text{Th}$, final nucleus ${}^{208}_{82}\text{Pb}$; the series has only alpha and beta minus decays
**Find:** the number of beta minus decays $n_\beta$
**Principle:** The mass number changes only in alpha decays, by four in each, so the change in the mass number gives the number of alpha decays $n_\alpha$. The atomic number decreases by two in every alpha decay and increases by one in every beta decay.
**Formula:** $A_\text{initial} - A_\text{final} = 4 n_\alpha$ and $Z_\text{final} = Z_\text{initial} - 2 n_\alpha + n_\beta$, from which $n_\beta = Z_\text{final} - Z_\text{initial} + 2 n_\alpha$
**Conversions:** None are needed, because the numbers are counts.
**Substitution:** $n_\alpha = \dfrac{232 - 208}{4} = 6$ and $n_\beta = 82 - 90 + 2 \cdot 6 = 4$
**Answer:** $4$. The number of beta minus decays is an exact integer.
**Reasonableness:** Check: $90 - 2 \cdot 6 + 4 = 82$ and $232 - 4 \cdot 6 = 208$. Alpha decays alone would take the atomic number to $78$, too low; beta decays raise it to lead. In the uranium series, too, there are fewer beta decays than alpha decays ($6$ and $8$).

## fy8-8-L3-02
**Given:** the levels of the nucleus $0$, $0.40\,\text{MeV}$ and $1.00\,\text{MeV}$, the nucleus at first on the level $1.00\,\text{MeV}$; $h = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s}$, $c = 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$, $1\,\text{MeV} = 10^{6}\,\text{eV}$, $1\,\text{eV} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$
**Find:** the shortest wavelength $\lambda_\text{min}$ seen in the gamma spectrum, in picometres
**Principle:** The energy of every gamma photon is the difference between two levels. The possible transitions are $1.00 \to 0$ ($1.00\,\text{MeV}$), $1.00 \to 0.40$ ($0.60\,\text{MeV}$) and $0.40 \to 0$ ($0.40\,\text{MeV}$). Because $\lambda = \tfrac{hc}{E}$, the shortest wavelength comes from the greatest energy, that is, from the direct transition to the ground state.
**Formula:** $\dfrac{hc}{\lambda_\text{min}} = E_\text{max}$, from which $\lambda_\text{min} = \dfrac{hc}{E_\text{max}}$
**Conversions:** $E_\text{max} = 1.00\,\text{MeV} = 1.00 \cdot 10^{6} \cdot 1.602 \cdot 10^{-19}\,\text{J} = 1.602 \cdot 10^{-13}\,\text{J}$; finally $1\,\text{m} = 10^{12}\,\text{pm}$.
**Substitution:** The product $hc = 6.626 \cdot 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s} \cdot 2.998 \cdot 10^{8}\,\text{m/s} = 1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}$. $\lambda_\text{min} = \dfrac{1.9865 \cdot 10^{-25}\,\text{J}\,\text{m}}{1.602 \cdot 10^{-13}\,\text{J}} = 1.2400 \cdot 10^{-12}\,\text{m} = 1.2400\,\text{pm}$
**Answer:** $1.24\,\text{pm}$, three significant figures like the energy $1.00\,\text{MeV}$.
**Reasonableness:** The spectrum shows three lines, as any three levels give three transitions. The other two wavelengths are longer: $0.60\,\text{MeV}$ gives $\tfrac{1.24}{0.60}\,\text{pm} \approx 2.1\,\text{pm}$ and $0.40\,\text{MeV}$ about $3.1\,\text{pm}$.

## fy8-8-L3-03
**Given:** the nucleus X decays first by beta minus decay and then by alpha decay; the end result is ${}^{222}_{86}\text{Rn}$
**Find:** the atomic number $Z_\text{X}$ of the nucleus X
**Principle:** The decays can be followed backwards, because the charge number is conserved in both. The alpha decay reduced the atomic number by two, so the atomic number of the intermediate nucleus was two greater; the beta minus decay increased it by one, so the atomic number of X was one smaller than that of the intermediate nucleus.
**Formula:** $Z_\text{Rn} = Z_\text{mid} - 2$ and $Z_\text{mid} = Z_\text{X} + 1$, from which $Z_\text{X} = Z_\text{Rn} + 2 - 1$
**Conversions:** None are needed, because the atomic numbers are counts.
**Substitution:** $Z_\text{mid} = 86 + 2 = 88$ and $Z_\text{X} = 88 - 1 = 87$
**Answer:** $87$. The result is an exact integer. The atomic number $87$ is francium, and the mass number is $222 + 4 = 226$, so X is ${}^{226}_{87}\text{Fr}$.
**Reasonableness:** Calculated forwards, ${}^{226}_{87}\text{Fr} \to {}^{226}_{88}\text{Ra} \to {}^{222}_{86}\text{Rn}$ balances: the mass number decreases only in the alpha decay, and the charge numbers are $87 + 1 = 88$ and $88 - 2 = 86$. The intermediate step radium-226 also decays to radon in the uranium series.
