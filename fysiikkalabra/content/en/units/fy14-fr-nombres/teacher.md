## Aim and level

The second lesson of the French chapter, for beginners who have done *Bonjour ! Introducing yourself*. The goal is
practical: students should leave able to count to 100, catch a price by ear and ask for it politely, not to learn
grammar. Instructions are in English. Every phrase can be heard with the browser's French voice. This voice is a
model, not a native speaker, so your own voice and the video are the main models. All prices in the lesson are
invented, and the market and the shop are a scenario.

## Before the lesson: check the videos

The spoken content of the two videos could not be played from the course's development environment. The tasks use
only what their YouTube metadata shows (title, channel, date, description and chapters, checked 1 November 2026).
Watch them once before the lesson and check:

- **Juancker´s Languages, *French NUMBERS 1-100*** (21 August 2023): does the speaker use the numbers of France
  (<span lang="fr">soixante-dix</span>, <span lang="fr">quatre-vingts</span>, <span lang="fr">quatre-vingt-dix</span>), and does the chapter structure (61–70 from
  2:31 … 91–100 from 3:55) still match? Is 71 said as <span lang="fr">soixante et onze</span>? Check also for adverts before or inside the
  video. If anything differs, play the chapters yourself and comment, or skip the video and use your own voice.
- **Learn French, *French vowel combinations*** (19 October 2022, optional): which combinations does it show? The
  extension task asks students only to note what the video shows and to find number words that use those combinations.

## Timing (75 minutes)

- **0–7 min, Think about it.** Read the hook. Pairs compare how Finnish, Swedish and English build numbers, explain *four score and seven*, and list ways to deal with a price they did not catch.
- **7–11 min, Predict.** Both predictions individually (70 in France, a price in euros). Their feedback stays closed until the sections *Counting to 100* and *Prices in euros* have been read.
- **11–22 min, Investigate: listen and watch.** Go through the number list in chorus (you say, the class repeats), then the video chapters 61–100 once. Each student notes the three hardest numbers.
- **22–28 min, Investigate: work out.** The number match individually, then *What did you notice?*
- **28–38 min, Theory summary.** Read the three sections together; drill the sound table and the price questions in chorus; back to the predictions, with one or two sentences on the Why question.
- **38–50 min, Apply: listening in pairs.** Partner A plays table A while B chooses the prices; then they swap. Then the receipt gap fill and the phrase match.
- **50–63 min, Role-play at the market.** Two rounds in pairs; two pairs perform for the class.
- **63–75 min, Check.** The three checks individually (about 5 minutes); start the text message (check 4) and finish it as homework.

**Fast pairs:** the phone-number extension (dictating an invented number in pairs), then the vowel-combination video.

**Running out of time:** watch only the chapters 71–80 and 91–100 of the video, let only one pair perform the
role-play, give the phrase match and the text message (check 4) as homework. Do not cut the listening in pairs or the
three checks: catching prices by ear is the core of the lesson.

## Pairs and organisation

- Pairs throughout. In *Listen in pairs* each partner needs a device, or one device and the listener turns away. Headphones are not needed: the listener should hear the speaker's device.
- If the browser has no French voice, the speaker reads the line aloud instead; this is also good practice.
- For the market role-play, students write their own price lists on paper before they start, so that the buyer really has to listen.

## Assessment (formative)

Use the criteria that the students see under the role-play and check 4:

- **Numbers right:** the prices in words match the digits, including 70–99 and the total.
- **Communicates the message:** greets, asks prices and the total, pays, says goodbye; asks for a repetition when needed.
- **Pronunciation understandable:** liaison with *<span lang="fr">euros</span>*, no *t* in <span lang="fr">quatre-vingt-un</span>, an attempt at the nasal vowels. A Finnish accent is fine.

The listening match gives each pair immediate feedback; walk around and note which numbers cause most trouble
(usually 60–99) for a short drill at the start of the next lesson.

## Common difficulties

- Writing 70–99 as Finns think them (*sept-dix*, *neuf-dix*) or adding up the parts wrongly (*quatre-vingt-dix-sept* heard as 87).
- <span lang="fr">Quatre-vingts</span> with and without *s*; <span lang="fr">vingt et un</span> but <span lang="fr">quatre-vingt-un</span>.
- Reading the decimal comma as a thousands separator or writing *€2.80*.
- Pronouncing every letter: *six euros* with a sharp *s* and a pause, *vingt* with a *t*.
- Students who know Belgian or Swiss French (<span lang="fr">septante</span>, <span lang="fr">nonante</span>) are right too; say where each form is used.
