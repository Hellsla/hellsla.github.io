## fy3-8-L1-01
**Given:** steel gutter, $l_0 = 6.0\,\text{m}$, $T_0 = 15\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, $T = 55\,{}^\circ\text{C}$, $\alpha = 12 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\tfrac{1}{\text{K}}$ (given in the problem)
**Find:** the change in length of the gutter $\Delta l$ in millimetres
**Principle:** The vibration of the particles of a solid gets stronger when it warms up and the average distance between the particles grows. The gutter is not jammed and its state of matter does not change, so the change in length is proportional to the initial length and the change in temperature.
**Formula:** $\Delta l = \alpha l_0 \Delta T$, where $\Delta T = T - T_0$. The quantity asked for, $\Delta l$, is already alone on the left side.
**Conversions:** The change in temperature is the same in degrees Celsius and in kelvins: $\Delta T = 55\,{}^\circ\text{C} - 15\,{}^\circ\text{C} = 40\,\text{K}$. The result comes out in metres, and $1\,\text{m} = 1000\,\text{mm}$.
**Substitution:** $\Delta l = 12 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\tfrac{1}{\text{K}} \cdot 6.0\,\text{m} \cdot 40\,\text{K} = 0.00288\,\text{m} = 2.88\,\text{mm}$
**Answer:** $\Delta l \approx 2.9\,\text{mm}$, two significant figures. The limiting data are the length $6.0\,\text{m}$ and the coefficient $12 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\tfrac{1}{\text{K}}$ (both have two figures); the temperature difference $\Delta T = 40\,\text{K}$, as a difference of whole degrees, also has two figures, so it does not limit the result any further.
**Reasonableness:** A rough estimate: $10^{-5}\,\tfrac{1}{\text{K}} \cdot 6\,\text{m} \cdot 40\,\text{K} \approx 2.4\,\text{mm}$, so the result is of the right order of magnitude. $\Delta l$ is positive because the gutter warms up. The change is only $\alpha \Delta T = 0.048\,\%$ of the length, but the fastenings must still let the gutter slide.

**Principle:** When it cools, the vibration of the particles gets weaker and their average distance decreases, so the wire gets shorter. **Formula:** $\Delta l = \alpha l_0 \Delta T$, where $\Delta T = T - T_0$ (final minus initial).
**Conversions:** The change in temperature is the same in kelvins: $\Delta T = -27\,{}^\circ\text{C} - 18\,{}^\circ\text{C} = -45\,\text{K}$. The result comes out in metres, and $1\,\text{m} = 1000\,\text{mm}$.
**Substitution:** $\Delta l = 23 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\tfrac{1}{\text{K}} \cdot 50.0\,\text{m} \cdot (-45\,\text{K}) = -0.05175\,\text{m} = -51.75\,\text{mm}$
**Answer:** $\Delta l \approx -52\,\text{mm}$, two significant figures. The limiting data are $\alpha = 23 \cdot 10^{-6}\,\tfrac{1}{\text{K}}$ and the difference of whole degrees $\Delta T = -45\,\text{K}$ (both have two figures); the length $50.0\,\text{m}$ has three figures and does not limit the result.
**Reasonableness:** A rough estimate: $2 \cdot 10^{-5}\,\tfrac{1}{\text{K}} \cdot 50\,\text{m} \cdot 45\,\text{K} \approx 45\,\text{mm}$. The sign is correct because the temperature falls. The wire gets shorter by about $0.10\,\%$, so it must be installed slack in summer so that it does not become too tight in winter.

In practice the gear is heated a little above $120\,{}^\circ\text{C}$ so that a clearance is left between the hole and the axle.
