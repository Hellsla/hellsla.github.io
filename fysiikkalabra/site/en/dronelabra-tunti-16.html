Lesson 16 · Your own drone project

Project presentation

Drone Challenge, presentation and self-assessment

The projects are presented, the shared Drone Challenge is flown and you assess your own work.

In this lesson you learn: You present the results of your project and assess your own work.

Needs the teacher's safety approval

Start right away (5–10 min)

Put your project on display and write one sentence on a card: what you investigated or solved and what the most important result was.

Project journal →

Try · Modify · Challenge yourself

  1. Try for everyone

    Present the project: the problem, the solution, the measurement results and what you learned. Answer one question and fill in the self-assessment.

  2. Modify your own ideas

    Take part in the Drone Challenge with your group in the role you have chosen and record the group's result.

  3. Challenge yourself optional

    Write a proposal for further development: what could the next group do with your project?

What did you make?

Basic level: A presented project, a finished project diary and a self-assessment.

Challenge yourself: A Drone Challenge result, or a proposal for further development.

Safety

There are spectators at the Drone Challenge: they stay behind the safety line, and every flight has a named observer and safety officer.

  • Drones: flight area and roles
  • Drones: emergency stop and abnormal situations
  • Drones: FPV flight and the observer
  • Drones: filming and privacy

Ahjo's safety instructions are still being prepared. Follow your teacher's induction.

For the teacher: preparation and timing

Timing: Exhibition and presentations 35 min, Drone Challenge 25 min, self-assessment and feedback 15 min.

Preparation: Build the Drone Challenge course and mark out the spectators' area. Print the self-assessment forms. Decide which tasks are flown on the course: precision landing is achievable for everyone, the obstacle course requires more flying experience.

Drone Challenge

At the end of the course, every group presents its own project and takes part in the shared Drone Challenge. The tasks are chosen so that every group can succeed at its own level.

  • Precision landing. Five landings on the mark. The result is the mean of the distances, not the best single hit.
  • Obstacle course. A lap through a marked course following the observer's instructions. The result is the time and the number of touches.
  • Steadiest hover. The Air:bit stays inside a marked area for as long as possible.
  • Best measurement. The group presents a measurement result whose reliability it can justify.

You can propose a task of your own once the teacher has approved its rules and safety.
