A puck on a string circles a stake on frictionless ice (top view) in uniform circular motion with radius r (0,5–3,0 m) and period T (2–10 s); the student sees the blue velocity arrow stay tangent and equally long while its direction turns (optionally the places and velocities at twelve equal steps over the last lap), reads f = 1/T, v = 2πr/T, the path s and the lap count, cuts the string and finds from the puck's own displacement that it leaves along the tangent (α = 90°) with unchanged speed, and in a second panel sees the velocity a twelfth of a lap ago and now from one point with their difference Δv̄, which is never zero on the circle and zero after the release — so a change of direction is a change of velocity, the motivation for the next chapter's acceleration (no a_n here).
