## fy4-1-L1-01
**Given:** $\Delta x = 120\,\text{m}$, $\Delta t = 15\,\text{s}$; the motion is uniform and in the direction chosen as positive
**Find:** the velocity $v$ of the runner in the unit $\text{m/s}$
**Principle:** The motion is uniform, so the velocity does not change along the whole distance. Then the velocity is the change in position divided by the time it took. The runner moves straight in the positive direction, so the displacement is equal to the distance.
**Formula:** $v = \dfrac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$. The formula is already solved for the velocity: $v = \dfrac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$
**Conversions:** The distance is in metres and the time in seconds, so the result comes directly in the unit $\text{m/s}$. No conversion is needed.
**Substitution:** $v = \dfrac{120\,\text{m}}{15\,\text{s}} = 8.0\,\text{m/s}$
**Answer:** $8.0\,\text{m/s}$, two significant figures like the time $15\,\text{s}$ (the distance $120\,\text{m}$ does not limit the accuracy further).
**Reasonableness:** Estimate: $\dfrac{120\,\text{m}}{15\,\text{s}}$ is $8\,\text{m}$ per second, and in $15\,\text{s}$ $8\,\text{m/s} \cdot 15\,\text{s} = 120\,\text{m}$, so the calculation can be checked backwards. The velocity $8.0\,\text{m/s} \approx 29\,\text{km/h}$ is the speed of a sprinter, so over a distance of $120\,\text{m}$ it is possible. The sign is positive, because the motion is in the positive direction.

## fy4-1-L1-02

**Formula:** $\Delta x = x_2 - x_1$. The formula is already solved for the displacement: $\Delta x = x_2 - x_1$
**Conversions:** Both positions are in metres, so no units need to be converted.
**Substitution:** $\Delta x = -5.0\,\text{m} - 12\,\text{m} = -17\,\text{m}$
**Answer:** $-17\,\text{m}$, two significant figures. The position $12\,\text{m}$ is known to the accuracy of a metre, so the difference has that accuracy.
**Reasonableness:** The cart moved in the negative direction, so the displacement must be negative. The cart first went $12\,\text{m}$ to the origin and then $5.0\,\text{m}$ past it, altogether $12\,\text{m} + 5.0\,\text{m} = 17\,\text{m}$. ## fy4-1-L1-04
**Given:** points of the line $(t_1;\, x_1) = (0\,\text{s};\, 4.0\,\text{m})$ and $(t_2;\, x_2) = (10\,\text{s};\, 24\,\text{m})$
**Find:** the velocity $v$ of the cart in the unit $\text{m/s}$
**Principle:** In uniform motion the velocity is constant, so the $(t, x)$ graph is a straight line. The slope of the line, that is the rise divided by the horizontal run, is the change in position divided by the change in time, that is the velocity.
**Reading the graph:** We use the two given points of the line: the position of the first is $x_1 = 4.0\,\text{m}$ at the moment $t_1 = 0\,\text{s}$ and the position of the second is $x_2 = 24\,\text{m}$ at the moment $t_2 = 10\,\text{s}$. The vertical rise is $x_2 - x_1$ and the horizontal run is $t_2 - t_1$. Their ratio is the velocity.
**Formula:** $v = \dfrac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$, where $\Delta x = x_2 - x_1$ and $\Delta t = t_2 - t_1$. So $v = \dfrac{x_2 - x_1}{t_2 - t_1}$
**Conversions:** The positions are in metres and the times in seconds, so no conversions are needed. The starting position $4.0\,\text{m}$ is not zero, but it cancels in the difference.
**Substitution:** $v = \dfrac{24\,\text{m} - 4.0\,\text{m}}{10\,\text{s} - 0\,\text{s}} = \dfrac{20\,\text{m}}{10\,\text{s}} = 2.0\,\text{m/s}$
**Answer:** $2.0\,\text{m/s}$, two significant figures, because the position $24\,\text{m}$ is given to the accuracy of a metre ($4.0\,\text{m}$ to a decimetre), so the difference $20\,\text{m}$ has two figures; the time $10\,\text{s}$ also has two.
**Reasonableness:** The graph rises, so the velocity is positive. Check: $4.0\,\text{m} + 2.0\,\text{m/s} \cdot 10\,\text{s} = 24\,\text{m}$, which is the other point of the line. If the velocity were calculated as $\dfrac{24\,\text{m}}{10\,\text{s}} = 2.4\,\text{m/s}$, the starting position would be forgotten.

## fy4-1-L1-06
**Find:** what a falling line in a $(t, x)$ graph tells about the motion.
**Principle:** The slope of a line in a $(t, x)$ graph is the velocity $v = \dfrac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}$. When the line falls, the position decreases as time passes, so the slope and the velocity are negative. The graph shows position as a function of time, not the shape of the path.
**Model answer:** The position decreases as time passes, so the velocity is negative and the object moves in the negative direction. The option in which the object moves downhill confuses the graph with a picture of the path: the object can move along a horizontal track even though the graph falls. A stopped object is at rest, and then the graph is horizontal. A straight line whose slope does not change describes a constant velocity, not a slowing motion. Time always increases as one goes to the right, so there is no object travelling backwards in time.
**Reading the graph:** In a falling line $x$ is smaller when $t$ is greater, so $\Delta x < 0$ and $\Delta t > 0$. Then $\dfrac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} < 0$.
